Bigelow Aerospace is a space technology company founded by [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) in 1998 and funded mostly by his own money, specialising in manufactured space equipment.<sup>1</sup> It is based in [Nevada Las Vegas](/places/nevada-las-vegas-usa).<sup>2</sup> [NASA](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) and other space companies have used the company's space habitats and other equipment in their explorations and experiments, citing their reliability and safety.<sup>3</sup>

Beyond its commercial work, Bigelow Aerospace was the contractor that ran the [Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon)'s UFO programme.<sup>4</sup> The [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) awarded the contract for the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap) to a subsidiary of Bigelow Aerospace owned by Robert Bigelow.<sup>5</sup> The programme was funded through the DIA and run using Bigelow Aerospace to investigate the [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) phenomenon.<sup>6</sup> Funding for the related [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) also went to Bigelow Aerospace, which hired subcontractors and solicited research on the programme's behalf.<sup>7</sup>

[Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) served as a senior scientist for Bigelow Aerospace, working on AATIP and examining activity from the DIA-funded programme.<sup>8</sup> On the company's behalf, he commissioned about 36 papers from leading experts worldwide on where their area of expertise would be by 2050, without disclosing any connection to extraterrestrials or [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap).<sup>9</sup> During a later period when the follow-on UAP programme went unfunded, its participants had support from Bigelow Aerospace and other places.<sup>10</sup>

One condition of Bigelow's contract with the DIA was that the company's Las Vegas plant be engineered to accept, store, and study certain exotic materials.<sup>11</sup> An alien logo is painted on the exterior of the building.<sup>12</sup> According to reporting in The New York Times, under Robert Bigelow's direction the company modified buildings in Las Vegas for the storage of metal alloys and other materials that [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) and AATIP contractors said had been recovered from unidentified aerial phenomena.<sup>13</sup> There were several attempts to divest materials to AAWSAP or to Bigelow Aerospace.<sup>14</sup> A plan for Bigelow Aerospace to build facilities in Las Vegas to house materials from Lockheed Martin Space Systems Company never materialised,<sup>15</sup> and under a related proposal known as Kona Blue, assets were to be transferred from [Lockheed Martin](/organisations/lockheed-martin), which held UFO material, to Bigelow Aerospace.<sup>16</sup> [Ralph Blumenthal](/people/ralph-blumenthal), the New York Times reporter who co-wrote the original story, later said he had learned that no such materials were in fact provided.<sup>17</sup>
