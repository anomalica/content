The Monroe Institute is a research and educational organisation founded by [Robert Monroe](/people/robert-monroe), who served as its founder and executive director.<sup>1</sup> The facility became active in 1972.<sup>2</sup> It is based in Virginia Nelliesford.<sup>3</sup> The Institute is known internationally for its work on the effects of sound wave forms on human behaviour,<sup>4</sup> and Robert Monroe remained an active participant in its work.<sup>5</sup>

Its work attracted the interest and co-operation of physicists, psychologists, biochemists, engineers, educators, psychiatrists, corporate presidents, and statisticians, many of whom served on its board of advisers.<sup>6</sup> By one account it had received over eleven thousand pieces of mail,<sup>7</sup> more than seven hundred people had participated in its research and experimental training programme,<sup>8</sup> and around fifty more were awaiting completion of their final indoctrination into the facility.<sup>9</sup> Its training programmes could qualify for college and university-level credit.<sup>10</sup> A core research group, the Monroe Institute Explorer Team, had six members<sup>11</sup> and, by one account, generated data faster than the Institute could process it.<sup>12</sup>

The Institute runs residential courses including the Gateway Voyage seminar and the Gateway Experience (Monroe Institute program), built around its Hemi-Sync audio technology and associated with claims of [Out-of-Body Experience (OBE)](/topics/out-of-body-experience-obe). The Institute championed the use of a particular form of Hemi-Sync meditation intended to connect with something else.<sup>13</sup> In the autumn of 1983, [Joseph McMoneagle](/people/joseph-mcmoneagle) attended a Gateway Voyage seminar at the Institute and afterwards asked Robert Monroe for help controlling his out-of-body experiences.<sup>14</sup>

[Ed Dames](/people/ed-dames) used Robert Monroe's programme on altered states and out-of-body experiences to evaluate potential recruits for [Project STARGATE](/projects/project-stargate).<sup>15</sup> McMoneagle, referred to within the programme as remote viewer number 001, lived in Nelliesford at the same location as the Institute.<sup>3</sup> [Chris Ramsay](/people/chris-ramsay) has said that McMoneagle was sent on weekend retreats to the Institute during his time on [The Stargate Project](/projects/the-stargate-project) to test whether leaving his body would make [remote viewing](/topics/remote-viewing-rv) sessions more accurate,<sup>16</sup> travelling from [Fort Meade](/places/maryland-fort-meade-usa) at weekends for the training.<sup>17</sup>

Nelson Delis undertook the Institute's Gateway Experience programme,<sup>18</sup> including a take-home exercise consisting of a 45-minute guided meditation.<sup>19</sup> On 2024-08-07, Graham Nichols used the Institute's Expand app, which features binaural beats and Monroe sound science technology.<sup>20</sup> [Jesse Mitchells](/people/jesse-mitchells) attended the Institute prior to 2024-11-13.<sup>21</sup> [Whitley Strieber](/people/whitley-strieber) has said he never met Robert Monroe but attended a Gateway programme at the Institute many years before 2026.<sup>22</sup> One participant underwent one week of intensive training under the guidance of Institute instructors as part of an evaluation of the Gateway process.<sup>23</sup> Julia Mossbridge has said that the Institute does great things in helping people and that its work can be very healing.<sup>24</sup>

Ramsay has said that his own first out-of-body experiences happened at home rather than at the Institute.<sup>25</sup> During a later out-of-body experience, he found he could not push his hand through a window as taught at the Institute, so he opened the window instead.<sup>26</sup> Ramsay has also described hearing a talk at the Institute about meditating on past trauma, in which participants would revisit a traumatic event or one with an unwanted outcome, imagine making the other decision, and live out the different outcome, from which they would feel better.<sup>27</sup>
