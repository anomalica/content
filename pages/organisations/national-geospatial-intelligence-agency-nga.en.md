The National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) is a US defence intelligence agency whose functions include processing, exploiting, and disseminating satellite imagery.<sup>1</sup> Within Anomalica's source base, the NGA's most prominent connection to UAP research runs through the career of [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch), described as a veteran of the NGA and the [National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)](/organisations/national-reconnaissance-office-nro).<sup>2</sup> He later worked as a senior intelligence officer at both agencies before becoming a whistleblower on US government [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) programmes.<sup>3</sup>

From November 2021 until April 2023, Grusch held the position of Senior Geospatial Intelligence (GEOINT) Capabilities Requirements Officer at the NGA.<sup>4</sup> Within that period, from late 2021 to July 2022, he served as the agency's co-lead for UAP analysis and its representative to the [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)](/projects/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uaptf).<sup>5</sup> At the NGA he also held the title of Senior Intelligence Capabilities Integration Officer, cleared at the Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information level,<sup>6</sup> and was separately described as the agency's Senior Technical Advisor for UAP analysis and Trans-Medium Issues.<sup>7</sup> Grusch's time at the agency is corroborated elsewhere: he is described as having worked for the NGA<sup>8</sup> and as the NRO and NGA's joint representative on the Pentagon's UAP Task Force.<sup>9</sup> By his own account, he spent 14 years as an intelligence officer, serving in the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) in active-duty and Air National Guard and Reserve capacities at the rank of Major, and most recently at the NGA at the GS-15 civilian level - the military equivalent of a full bird colonel.<sup>10</sup>

Grusch has said he tried to personally review every UAP report that came through during his leadership role at the NGA,<sup>11</sup> and told Congress that he personally reviewed multiple cases of satellite imagery relating to UAP events while working at the agency.<sup>12</sup> In 2022, while at the NGA, he filed a federal whistleblower complaint with the Office of the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG), an action later documented as David Grusch's federal whistleblower complaint to the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG).<sup>13</sup> He left the NGA in April 2023.<sup>4,14</sup> After fighting off administrative reprisals and maintaining his top secret clearance, he returned to work at the agency,<sup>15</sup> where he found he would not be allowed to do his job and that a minefield was being created for him.<sup>16</sup>

Other claims in Anomalica's source base situate the NGA within the wider US intelligence community's handling of UAP information. Christopher K. Mellon has said that the NRO, [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia), the NGA, [Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)](/organisations/federal-bureau-of-investigation-fbi), and [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa) appeared to hold important UAP data that they were not sharing with each other.<sup>17</sup> According to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) Historical Record Report (HR2), one line of effort was to partner with the archives of key intelligence and national security agencies, including the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia), the CIA, the Department of Energy, the NSA, and the NGA.<sup>18</sup> Separately, as part of its evaluation of the Department of Defense's handling of UAP, the U.S. Department of Defense Inspector General interviewed senior officials and requested UAP-related data, policies, and procedures from the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro), the military services, and the department's defense intelligence agencies.<sup>19</sup>
