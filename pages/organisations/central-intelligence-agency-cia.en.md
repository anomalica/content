The Central Intelligence Agency has intersected with the subject of [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) since the early 1950s. Following a surge in [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) sightings in 1952, including incidents over Washington, D.C., the CIA's Deputy Director for Intelligence tasked the [CIA Office of Scientific Intelligence (OSI)](/organisations/cia-office-of-scientific-intelligence-osi)'s Physics and Electronics Division to review UFO cases.<sup>1</sup> That same year, H. Marshall Chadwell sent a memo to the Director of the CIA titled "Flying Saucers",<sup>2</sup> and the CIA Director Walter Bedell Smith was privately advised that UAP sightings raised serious national security concerns and "were of such nature that they are not attributable to natural phenomenon or known types of aerial vehicles".<sup>3</sup> From 1953, the CIA convened the [Robertson Panel](/matters/robertson-panel) — formally the Scientific Advisory Panel on Unidentified Flying Objects — from 14 to 18 January 1953.<sup>4</sup> That panel recommended an active debunking effort using public media to steer the public away from reporting UFOs, reasoning that a persistent flood of reports could create mass hysteria to the benefit of the Soviet Union,<sup>5</sup> and specifically advised the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) to enlist the Walt Disney Company and mass media in a campaign to debunk UAP.<sup>6</sup> One assessment holds that the climate of hostility toward the UAP issue for decades afterward was a direct result of policies formulated by the Robertson Panel.<sup>7</sup> Separately, a CIA officer named Frederick Durant drafted a report for the CIA's Assistant Director of the OSI on the panel's work and findings.<sup>8</sup>

The CIA also ran classified aviation programmes whose secrecy contributed to the proliferation of UFO reports. President [Dwight D. Eisenhower](/people/dwight-d-eisenhower) approved the CIA-led Oxcart programme to develop a successor to the U-2 spy plane in 1958, which became fully operational in 1965.<sup>9</sup> By the time the [A-12](/objects/a-12) was deployed by the CIA in 1967, CORONA satellites were being used to collect imagery of denied areas with less provocation than aircraft overflights.<sup>10</sup> A declassified CIA assessment concluded that more than half of UFO reports investigated in the 1950s and 1960s were assessed to be US reconnaissance flights.<sup>11</sup> In 1964, Director of Central Intelligence John McCone tasked the CIA to update its evaluation of UFOs following high-level White House discussions about what to do if alien intelligence was discovered or there was a new outbreak of sightings,<sup>12</sup> and the CIA's scientific division officially acquired UFO-sighting case information from the director of the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP), a private organisation founded in 1956.<sup>13</sup>

The agency's relationship with key figures in UAP research and disclosure is extensive. [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) was a Stanford University researcher and an employee of the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa) when he and his colleague Russell Targ were approached by the CIA;<sup>14</sup> the CIA told Puthoff and Targ that Russia had a remote-viewing programme and that the US needed to develop one in response.<sup>15</sup> The [Stargate Program](/matters/stargate-program) was subsequently embedded in the federal government, run for years by the CIA and later the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia),<sup>16</sup> and became sufficiently successful that Puthoff reported directly to the Director of the CIA and the White House on a regular basis.<sup>17</sup> The programme was ultimately defunded and shut down; most who were informed believed it was killed because it was too effective, not for budget reasons.<sup>18</sup> [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) joined the CIA in 1982 and retired in 2007 after a twenty-five-year career,<sup>19</sup> serving multiple overseas and domestic tours and holding senior management positions at CIA headquarters;<sup>20</sup> at the time of his retirement he was a member of the CIA's Senior Intelligence Service.<sup>21</sup> After retiring, Semivan remained a CIA contractor and stayed connected to CIA efforts.<sup>22</sup> [Norm Kahn](/people/norm-kahn), an advisory board member of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa), is a 33-year CIA veteran decorated with the CIA's Intelligence Medal.<sup>23</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), who led classified UAP investigations worldwide with partners including the [FBI](/organisations/fbi) and the CIA,<sup>24</sup> worked with CIA officials from 2012 onwards,<sup>25</sup> and [James Woolsey](/people/james-woolsey), as CIA Director, was ordered by President Bill Clinton in 1993 to release more files on UAPs after lobbying from Laurance Rockefeller.<sup>26</sup>

The CIA's involvement in specific UAP incidents has also been documented. Following the [JAL Flight 1628 UAP Encounter](/events/jal-flight-1628-uap-encounter) in 1986, CIA agents confiscated all radar evidence at a meeting held at the Federal Aviation Administration's Washington office;<sup>27</sup> [John Callahan](/people/john-callahan) stated there was an order by the CIA to deny that meeting even happened.<sup>28</sup> At the 1964 Vandenberg UAP incident, Major Florenze Mansmann confirmed in 1982 that strangers present that day were CIA agents.<sup>29</sup> Elizondo also recounted that some servicemen involved in the 1980 Rendlesham Forest incident told him there was someone from the CIA in the room with personnel from the [Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI)](/organisations/air-force-office-of-special-investigations-afosi) on the day after the event.<sup>30</sup> In a significant operational context, the CIA and the NSA offered to provide assets for OPLAN Interloper and participated in weekly meetings in Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) spaces at the Pentagon and other agencies.<sup>31</sup> Elizondo held a meeting with high-level Navy officials, CIA representatives, and an NSA representative at which the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) was screened;<sup>32</sup> no one present believed the UAP was made by humans,<sup>33</sup> and a CIA representative confirmed afterwards that the aura visible around the object was not a camera artefact or lens flare but was real.<sup>34</sup>

The [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) has examined a range of allegations involving the CIA in its historical record investigation. Interviewees alleged that a former named senior CIA official quashed a proposal to remove off-world material from a corporation,<sup>35</sup> and that in approximately 2010 a separate individual attempted to set up a meeting to return such material to the US Government but the former CIA official stopped the transfer;<sup>36</sup> that official denied all allegations and signed a Memorandum for the Record attesting to the truthfulness of his statements.<sup>37</sup> AARO found no empirical evidence supporting claims that an organisation possessed twelve recovered spacecraft prior to 1970 or that the CIA had a partnership with a company that ended in 1989.<sup>38</sup> Separately, the CIA participated as a partner agency in AARO's archival lines of effort alongside the DIA, NSA, [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA)](/organisations/national-geospatial-intelligence-agency-nga), and other bodies.<sup>39</sup> Former CIA Director [John Brennan](/people/john-brennan) stated publicly that he had seen UAP videos he found eyebrow-raising and that some phenomena could involve a different form of life,<sup>40</sup> while [Steven Greer](/people/steven-greer) has claimed to have held a covert briefing on UFOs with CIA Director [James Woolsey](/people/james-woolsey) in 1993, under the guise of a dinner party.<sup>41</sup> Former senior CIA official [Devon Woods](/people/devon-woods) had an illustrious career with the CIA before joining the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI), where he became Elizondo's unofficial mentor.<sup>42</sup>
