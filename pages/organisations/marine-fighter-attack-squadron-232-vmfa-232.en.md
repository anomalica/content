Marine Fighter Attack Squadron 232 (VMFA-232) is a United States Marine Corps strike fighter squadron. During the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), it was one of the squadrons embarked in Carrier Air Wing 11 (CVW-11) aboard [USS Nimitz (CVN-68)](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68), flying the F/A-18C.<sup>1</sup> According to the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group (CSG-11) AAV Incident Report, the only units named as participants in the detection and intercept of the [Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV)](/topics/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav) were [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59), Carrier Airborne Early Warning Squadron 117 (VAW-117), VMFA-232, and [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41).<sup>2</sup> The wider air wing also included Strike Fighter Squadron 14 (VFA-14) flying the F/A-18E, Strike Fighter Squadron 94 (VFA-94) flying the F/A-18C, Electronic Attack Squadron 135 (VAQ-135) flying the EA-6B, VAW-117 flying the E-2C Hawkeye, Helicopter Antisubmarine Squadron 6 (Helicopter Anti-Submarine Squadron 6 (HS-6)) flying the H-60, and Fleet Logistics Support Squadron 30 Detachment 3 (VRC-30 Det 3) flying the C-2A.<sup>1</sup>

VMFA-232's Commanding Officer provided a statement on the events of 2004-11-14.<sup>3</sup> He launched from Nimitz at approximately 1030 local time flying a single-seat F/A-18C to conduct a Functional Check Flight of an aircraft that had recently undergone significant maintenance,<sup>4</sup> recalling the weather that day as blue skies, no clouds, and unlimited visibility.<sup>5</sup> Around 30 minutes into the flight he received a radio call from his air controller asking him to investigate an unidentified airborne contact, which he noted was not a standard request.<sup>6</sup> According to the incident report, the controller also asked whether he had ordnance onboard, a question the pilot considered odd since no controller had ever asked it of him before during identification of an unknown contact over US or international territory.<sup>7</sup> He had no ordnance aboard his aircraft.<sup>8</sup>

En route at approximately 460 kilometres per hour indicated and between roughly 4,600 and 7,600 metres altitude, he gained radar contact of what he believed to be two F/A-18Fs approaching the AAV from the west at low altitude, between approximately 150 and 1,500 metres,<sup>9</sup> and observed no other traffic on his radar besides them.<sup>10</sup> His controller instructed him to remain above approximately 3,000 metres owing to other fighter traffic investigating the AAV at low altitude.<sup>11</sup> As he descended through approximately 4,600 metres roughly 28 kilometres from the AAV, he saw a water disturbance on the ocean surface,<sup>12</sup> recalling the sea state as low and calm at the time.<sup>13</sup> At around 9 to 19 kilometres from the AAV the controller told him to "skip it" and return to his operating area,<sup>14</sup> but since he was already close he elected to fly over the disturbance to see what was causing it.<sup>15</sup> He judged it approximately 50 to 100 metres in diameter and close to round.<sup>16</sup>

According to the incident report, he considered the disturbance the only area of whitewater activity visible, and it reminded him of images of something rapidly submerging from the surface, like a submarine or ship sinking.<sup>17</sup> He also considered that it could resemble shoal water where the swell was breaking over a barely submerged reef or island.<sup>18</sup> After overflying the disturbance he turned back to the northwest and observed it clearing, no longer identifiable.<sup>19</sup> He did not see any object or vessel associated with it above, on, or below the surface,<sup>20</sup> and never made visual contact with either the other fighter aircraft vectored to the location or the AAV itself.<sup>21</sup> Per the report, he considered it possible that the disturbance was caused by an AAV that was "cloaked" or invisible to the human eye.<sup>22</sup>

He recovered aboard Nimitz at approximately 1200 local time.<sup>23</sup> Afterwards he reported to the Carrier Intelligence Center (CVIC), where his Intelligence Officer asked him whether he had seen the "supersonic Tic Tac".<sup>24</sup> Separately, in testimony to the House Oversight Committee, David Fravor stated that VMFA-232 flew the Red Air role during the training exercise associated with the encounter.<sup>25</sup>
