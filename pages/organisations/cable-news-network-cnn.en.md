On 1989-07-20, the day of George Herbert Walker Bush's Space Exploration Initiative announcement, CNN called [Richard C. Hoagland](/people/richard-c-hoagland) for an interview, an approach recorded as the 1989-07-20 Hoagland CNN Crossfire invitation.<sup>1</sup> Mike Bara said that Larry King fondly remembered Hoagland as a "great guest" from his appearance on King's CNN show in the early 1990s.<sup>2</sup>

As the launch of Mars Observer approached, Hoagland appeared live on CNN reminding viewers of the mission's controversial history as the spacecraft lifted off.<sup>3</sup> When Mars Observer later went missing, local and national television coverage of a demonstration against [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa)'s planned Cydonia secrecy dominated CNN's coverage of the story for the remainder of that day.<sup>4</sup>

The March 1996 press conference at the National Press Club in Washington DC, held on 1996-03-21, was attended by at least sixty members of the national and international media.<sup>5</sup> CNN and Cable-Satellite Public Affairs Network (C-SPAN) attended and filmed the National Press Club press conference but did not broadcast any footage of the proceedings.<sup>6</sup>

CNN's science anchor was John Holliman.<sup>7</sup> When Mars Pathfinder's panoramas were broadcast live on the network, Ron Nicks said he observed multiple geometric anomalies around the Mars Pathfinder Lander,<sup>8</sup> and viewers watching the coverage called in about strangely geometric rocks they saw in the images.<sup>9</sup> Discrepancies were repeatedly found between the original live CNN transmissions of Mars Pathfinder's landing on 1997-07-04 and the later electronic image versions released across the web by the [NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)](/organisations/nasa-jet-propulsion-laboratory-jpl).<sup>10</sup> Hoagland said that Nicks, a trained geologist, and thousands of other viewers of the initial Pathfinder data in real time on CNN reported seeing artifacts before he did.<sup>11</sup> Holliman said on 1998-04-06 that the independent researchers needed more time to properly evaluate the MIPL image.<sup>12</sup>

In 2017, [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) said he told CNN that he and his team had identified anomalous aircraft with no obvious flight surfaces or propulsion, manoeuvring with extreme manoeuvrability beyond healthy G-forces for a human or biological pilot, achieving hypersonic velocities, exhibiting low observability and positive lift, and seemingly defying the laws of aerodynamics.<sup>13</sup> When pressed by CNN's Erin Burnett on whether the objects were aliens, Elizondo said he wanted to let the data speak for itself and use it to inform senior Department of Defense leadership about the potential threat the technologies pose to national security, especially over controlled airspace.<sup>14</sup> Within a couple of weeks of the 2017 CNN interview with Luis Elizondo, astrophysicist Neil DeGrasse Tyson told CNN in January 2018 that he was not convinced by the [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/ufos-uaps) evidence.<sup>15</sup> Following the December 2017 news coverage, calls flooded Elizondo's home from CBS, ABC, National Broadcasting Company (NBC) News, CNN, PBS, [Fox News](/organisations/fox-news), and MSNBC.<sup>16</sup> While waiting in the greenroom at CNN before an on-camera interview, Elizondo and Jennifer were approached by [James R. Clapper](/people/james-r-clapper).<sup>17</sup>
