Politico's defense editor is [Bryan Bender](/people/bryan-bender).<sup>1</sup> On the afternoon of 2017-12-16, the [New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) broke online news of the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), followed seconds later by Politico and then the Washington Post,<sup>2</sup> in reporting that included the 2017-12 New York Times Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) disclosure story.

Dana White, a spokeswoman for [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon), confirmed to Politico that AATIP existed and was run by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo),<sup>3</sup> giving that confirmation before she left [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon).<sup>4</sup> [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa) posted the first two [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) UAP videos in mid-December 2017, timed for impact when Politico, the Washington Post and the New York Times broke the story of the AATIP investigations.<sup>5</sup>

Bender's later reporting on the subject included a 2019-04-23 story. According to 'In Plain Sight', Bender reported that the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) was drafting new guidelines for reporting UFOs, in what became the 2019-04 Politico story on new US Navy Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) reporting guidelines.<sup>6</sup> A further story, also according to 'In Plain Sight', reported on 2021-03-25 that military and intelligence agencies had been accused of stiff-arming investigators on UFO sightings, collected as the 2021-03 Politico story on US Air Force blocking Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) cataloguing.<sup>7</sup> Bender said that [UFOs](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) were now on the agenda.<sup>8</sup>

Mark Walker told Politico: "While I am encouraged the Under Secretary of the Navy confirmed that UAP encounters are fully investigated, there is frustration with the lack of answers to specific questions about the threat that superior aircraft flying in United States airspace may pose."<sup>9</sup>

According to 'In Plain Sight', a Politico story by Robert "Bob" Fish raised one possible theory that a foreign power, perhaps China or Russia, had developed next-generation technologies that could threaten the United States.<sup>10</sup>
