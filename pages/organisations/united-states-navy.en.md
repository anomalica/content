The United States Navy has played a prominent role in the emergence of [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) as a matter of formal military and governmental concern. Naval aviators and shipboard crews generated some of the most consequential early documentation of UAP, including encounters recorded on radar, infrared camera, and direct visual observation. The [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) off the coast of Southern California was documented by radar, by camera, and by four naval aviators,<sup>1</sup> with a whitish oval object approximately the size of a commercial plane observed being pursued by two Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) fighter jets.<sup>2</sup> A qualified Submarine Officer aboard the USS Louisville (SSN-724) in November 2004 reported that there were no unidentified sonar contacts in the vicinity of the aerial sightings or at any time during the operations off the coast of California.<sup>3</sup> The USS Princeton (CG-59), a Ticonderoga-class guided-missile cruiser, was part of the strike group involved in that incident.<sup>4</sup>

[David Fravor](/people/david-fravor), a retired Commander and former Commanding Officer in the United States Navy,<sup>5</sup> became one of the most prominent witnesses to emerge from the 2004 encounter. He had served as a Navy pilot for 18 years<sup>6</sup> and was, at the time of the Nimitz incident, the most junior commanding officer for an O-5 command in the Navy, having taken over command of his squadron only a month earlier.<sup>7</sup> In Navy fighter squadrons, officers fleet up such that the executive officer becomes the commanding officer,<sup>8</sup> and pilots were crewed with a primary and secondary weapon systems operator for the duration of a deployment.<sup>9</sup> [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves), a former US Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) pilot who served for a decade including two deployments in Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Inherent Resolve,<sup>10</sup> represented a further category of Navy witness. He testified that [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) objects had been detected essentially everywhere United States Navy operations are conducted across the world, according to [All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) reporting.<sup>11</sup>

Three unclassified Navy UAP videos became central to public debate about the phenomenon. One was recorded in November 2004 and two in January 2015.<sup>12, 13</sup> The videos had been circulating in the public domain after unauthorised releases in 2007 and 2017, and the Navy had previously acknowledged that they were indeed Navy videos.<sup>14</sup> On 2020-04-27, the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) authorised the formal release of all three, which are documented in the [Three unclassified US Navy Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) videos (2004, 2015)](/documents/2015-three-unclassified-us-navy-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-videos-2004).<sup>15</sup> The [Department of Defense (DoD) authorised release of three Navy Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) videos (2020)](/events/department-of-defense-dod-authorised-release-of-three-navy-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-videos-2020) formalised what had previously circulated without official sanction. The leaked footage comprised infrared images captured aboard the United States' most sophisticated fighter jets and Navy ships.<sup>16</sup> On the [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Super Hornet footage, Navy pilots can be heard attempting to understand what they are observing, with one exclaiming that there is a whole fleet of them.<sup>17</sup> Defence officials declined to release the location and date of a separate Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Super Hornet glowing-aura incident.<sup>18</sup>

The [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program) collected video and audio recordings of reported [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) incidents, including footage from a Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Super Hornet showing an aircraft surrounded by some kind of glowing aura travelling at high speed and rotating as it moved.<sup>19</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) took over the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program in 2010 and focused on the national security implications of UAP documented by US service members.<sup>20</sup> From 2012 onwards, [Luis D. Elizondo](/people/luis-d-elizondo) worked with officials from the United States Navy and the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia).<sup>21</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) asserted in his resignation letter that unusual aerial systems had menaced Navy pilots and posed a threat to national security, and that the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) was not taking that threat seriously.<sup>22</sup> The [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) was reviewing the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program history with the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia), the US Navy, and the [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force), and pursuing options to investigate [Luis D. Elizondo](/people/luis-d-elizondo)'s possible mishandling of sensitive information.<sup>23</sup> On 2017-12-18, the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) located a classified email generated by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) in August 2017, in which he requested assistance from a US Navy civilian employee to declassify one or more videos.<sup>24</sup> That civilian employee informed the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) that he had told Elizondo he was not the declassification release authority and took no further action.<sup>25</sup>

The [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force) and the United States Navy have been at the forefront of developing policies, procedures, and mechanisms for reporting [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena).<sup>26</sup> The Deputy Secretary of Defense, in consultation with the Director of National Intelligence, directed the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) to establish the [Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG)](/organisations/airborne-object-identification-and-management-synchronization-group-aoimsg) to succeed the Navy's Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force.<sup>27</sup> In August 2020, the Pentagon resurrected the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program, now called the UAP Task Force, and service members were then encouraged to report strange encounters.<sup>28</sup> The United States Navy implemented a Range Fouler Report that gathers information about [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) from pilots, though a service-wide reporting mechanism remained pending as of the [2023 House Oversight UAP Hearing](/events/2023-house-oversight-uap-hearing).<sup>29</sup> [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) believed the Navy took seriously the danger to his aircraft from the [2014 Virginia Beach UAP encounter](/events/2014-virginia-beach-uap-encounter) after it was reported.<sup>30</sup> Following the [Evaluation of the DoD's Actions Regarding Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (DODIG-2023-109)](/documents/evaluation-of-the-dods-actions-regarding-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-dodig-2023-109), the Director of Intelligence, Deputy Under Secretary of the Navy, Intelligence and Security, responding for the Secretary of the Navy, agreed with Recommendation 3 and stated that the United States Navy had begun developing interim guidance for UAP,<sup>31</sup> though the Secretary of the Navy did not provide official comments for inclusion in the final report as requested.<sup>32</sup> The [2017 New York Times Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) article](/documents/2017-new-york-times-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-article) accelerated broader scrutiny of the Navy's encounters, with a Navy commander contacting [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) shortly before its publication regarding a Freedom of Information Act request for material on the Nimitz incident.<sup>33</sup>

The Navy's historical engagement with [Anomalous Aerospace Threats](/topics/anomalous-aerospace-threats) also extends to earlier periods. The [E-2 Hawkeye](/objects/e-2-hawkeye), an American all-weather carrier-based tactical Airborne Early Warning (AEW) aircraft designed and developed in the 1950s by Grumman for the United States Navy as a replacement for the E-1 Tracer, featured in the operational context of the Nimitz strike group.<sup>34</sup> In naval aviation doctrine, the [A-6 Intruder](/objects/a-6-intruder) was identified by [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) as the only side-by-side aircraft in Navy service, while in the [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force) the [F-111 Aardvark](/objects/f-111-aardvark) held that distinction.<sup>35</sup> An earlier claim reported by [Frank Edwards](/people/frank-edwards) alleged that a US Navy jet had fired on a glowing disc near Washington, D.C.; however, the [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force) and the Navy found no incident report of weapons engagement with a UFO that summer and no Navy aircraft were confirmed present.<sup>36</sup> The [2004 USS Nimitz Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-encounter) and the [2004 USS Nimitz Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) incident](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-incident) remain the most thoroughly documented cases in the Navy's institutional record of UAP, underpinning much of the subsequent legislative and policy response to [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) within the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod).
