Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) recurs throughout UAP-related literature as the academic background of numerous researchers, engineers and physicists. One point of contact is the Abduction Study Conference at Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), 1992-06, held on its campus in June 1992, at which an abductee implant study was reported.<sup>1</sup> At that conference, MIT physicist David Pritchard described his attempts to identify a small foreign object recovered from Richard Price, a New York State abductee, in a case referred to as Richard Price's penile implant case.<sup>2</sup>

Several individuals associated with UAP research or investigation hold MIT credentials. [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell) earned a PhD in aeronautics and astronautics from MIT<sup>3</sup> and has been described by Luis Elizondo as an engineer trained there.<sup>4</sup> Guy Consolmagno, an American Jesuit, holds degrees from MIT and the University of Arizona.<sup>5</sup> Sara Seager, an astrophysicist at MIT, said that not knowing the origin of an object does not mean it originates from another planet or galaxy.<sup>6</sup> More broadly, research facilities associated with the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod), [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) and MIT have given the concept of warp drive serious consideration.<sup>7</sup>

MIT graduates named in these sources have gone on to other fields. Craig McPherson attended MIT.<sup>8</sup> Ron Levin, son of Gilbert Levin, is a physicist at MIT.<sup>9</sup> According to [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor), Chris Sato holds a senior position at Apple Inc. and is an MIT graduate.<sup>10</sup> Lex Fridman has said that his podcast's listeners include faculty at MIT and other major universities.<sup>11</sup> Fravor has separately named MIT, [California Institute of Technology (Caltech)](/organisations/california-institute-of-technology-caltech), [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university), Georgia Institute of Technology (Georgia Tech), Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, Carnegie Mellon University and the Naval Postgraduate School as among the top-tier universities from which leading-edge technology research typically emerges.<sup>12</sup>

MIT also appears in older material connecting fringe physics and government-adjacent scholarship. [Richard C. Hoagland](/people/richard-c-hoagland) has connected the geometry of Cydonia to the historical treatment of hyperspatial physics, drawing on independent researchers including Bruce DePalma, an MIT physicist, and [Thomas Bearden](/people/thomas-bearden), a nuclear engineer and physicist who had worked on the US Army's scalar weapons programmes.<sup>13</sup> In 1950 testimony given on behalf of [Donald Menzel](/people/donald-menzel) during loyalty hearings, Vannevar Bush stated that he had first known Menzel in either 1934 or 1935, while designing and building the differential analyzer at MIT, where Bush was then Vice President and Dean of Engineering.<sup>14</sup> According to Dark Mission, contributors to the Proposed Studies on the Implications of Peaceful Space Activities for Human Affairs (Brookings Report) included Curtis H. Barker of MIT, Jack C. Oppenheimer of NASA, and anthropologist Margaret Mead.<sup>15</sup>
