The Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) is a combat support agency within the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) responsible for providing military intelligence to warfighters, defence policymakers, and force planners. In the context of [unidentified anomalous phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) (UAP) research, the DIA is most significant as the administrative home of two closely related programmes: the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program](/matters/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program) (AAWSAP) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program). At the direction of Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), the Defense Appropriations Acts of Fiscal Years 2008 and 2010 appropriated 22 million US dollars for the DIA to assess long-term and over-the-horizon foreign advanced aerospace threats to the United States.<sup>1</sup> In coordination with the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), the DIA established the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap) in 2009, awarding the contract to a private sector organisation.<sup>2</sup>

The origins of the programme within the DIA trace to an approach by a senior DIA scientist — identified in secondary accounts as physicist [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski), whose worldview had been transformed by events he witnessed at [Skinwalker Ranch](/places/skinwalker-ranch) — who contacted [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) around 2007–08.<sup>3</sup> Bigelow relayed this to Reid,<sup>4</sup> and Reid subsequently met with DIA officials who indicated they wished to start a research programme on [unidentified flying objects (UFOs)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo).<sup>5</sup> The DIA issued an invitation to tender for the AAWSAP contract in August 2008; Bigelow was the sole bidder and secured US$10 million in funding for the first year with a five-year option.<sup>6</sup> The contract was executed by [LLC Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies](/organisations/llc-bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies), Bigelow's dedicated research entity. AAWSAP was described as a small but highly sensitive programme that reported directly to the director of the DIA and to Congress.<sup>7</sup> Early memorandums to and from DIA Director Lieutenant General [Michael D. Maples](/people/michael-d-maples) and Deputy Director Robert Cardillo initially referred to the AAWSAP effort in positive terms,<sup>8</sup> and Lacatski disclosed that the programme enjoyed the support of Maples and was funded through the efforts of Reid and Senators Ted Stevens and Daniel Inouye.<sup>9</sup>

Although investigating UFO/UAP was not specifically outlined in the AAWSAP contract's statement of work, the selected private sector organisation conducted UFO research with the support of the DIA programme manager, including reviewing new cases and older Project BLUE BOOK cases, operating debriefing and investigatory teams, and proposals to set up laboratories to examine any recovered UFO materials.<sup>10</sup> The DIA did not seek, nor specifically authorise, the paranormal work conducted at the Utah property (Skinwalker Ranch), though a DIA employee set up and managed the contract with the private sector organisation.<sup>11</sup> The US government also commissioned academic studies through the DIA that resulted in Defence Intelligence Research Documents (DIRDs), each focused on individual exotic technologies that might explain UAP observables.<sup>12</sup>

The DIA's institutional support for AAWSAP eroded over time. An extra US$12 million was appropriated in 2010, but a 2009 Pentagon review had determined the reports were of limited value, recommending the project be passed to another agency.<sup>13</sup> Within the DIA itself, an increasing number of detractors at the senior level actively tried to kill the programme.<sup>14</sup> When [James R. Clapper](/people/james-r-clapper) moved to become the Director of National Intelligence, he offered [Devon Woods](/people/devon-woods) a position at the DIA;<sup>15</sup> Woods accepted the role of deputy director,<sup>16</sup> and within 30 to 45 days of his reassignment, the atmosphere at the agency changed regarding the AAWSAP/AATIP work, with leadership beginning to question the programme.<sup>17</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) had been recruited in 2009 by [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) and a colleague to support an intelligence programme at the DIA requiring a senior intelligence officer to set up counterintelligence and security;<sup>18</sup> Pentagon spokeswoman Susan Gough later stated that Elizondo was never assigned to the DIA and did not lead AATIP, though he interacted with the DIA office managing the programme while it was still ongoing.<sup>19</sup> Elizondo, Stratton, and a colleague ultimately decided to move the remnants of AATIP away from the DIA and into Elizondo's portfolio of national programmes within the Office of the Secretary of Defense (OSD).<sup>20</sup> [Michael Higgins](/people/michael-higgins), who became the DIA's Director of Operations having previously worked sensitive programmes for several intelligence agencies, was contacted by Elizondo in an attempt to protect Lacatski from internal DIA forces opposed to the programme.<sup>21</sup>

The DIA cancelled AAWSAP/AATIP in 2012, citing lack of merit and concerns about the utility of the deliverables.<sup>22</sup> Supporters of the programme subsequently proposed to the [Department of Homeland Security (DHS)](/organisations/department-of-homeland-security-dhs) that it create and fund a new version under a Special Access Programme, codenamed KONA BLUE, though this effort ultimately failed.<sup>23</sup> The [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) later partnered with the DIA's archives — alongside those of the [CIA](/organisations/cia) and other national security agencies — as part of its effort to compile a historical record of US government involvement with UAP.<sup>24</sup> The [Wilson Davis Memo](/documents/wilson-davis-memo) also implicates the DIA in a separate thread of UAP-related claims: it alleges that on 2002-10-16, physicist [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) met retired DIA Director Vice Admiral [Tom Wilson](/people/tom-wilson) and that Wilson had been threatened with loss of promotion to DIA Director and early retirement if he persisted in seeking access to a certain programme.<sup>25</sup>
