The Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program was nestled inside the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA), a member of the US intelligence community, and drew its authority directly from the [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress).<sup>1</sup> Select members of Congress initiated and funded the programme to study Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) under DIA direction.<sup>2</sup> In 2009, in coordination with the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), the DIA established the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP), also known as the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip).<sup>3</sup> At the direction of Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) (D-NV), the Defense Appropriations Acts of Fiscal Years 2008 and 2010 appropriated 22 million US dollars for the DIA to assess long-term and over-the-horizon foreign advanced aerospace threats to the United States.<sup>4</sup> An invitation to tender for the contract was issued by the DIA in August 2008, and [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) was the sole bidder, securing 10 million US dollars in funding for the first year with a five-year option.<sup>5</sup> Contracted work under the programme began in 2008.<sup>6</sup>

The programme was initiated and managed for the DIA by [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski), a veteran intelligence analyst and rocket expert.<sup>7</sup> One condition of the contract was that [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace)'s plant in Las Vegas be engineered so it could accept, store, and study certain exotic materials.<sup>8</sup> [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) told George Knapp that, in the course of his work on the Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS) DIA contract, there were recovered extraterrestrial craft, information about which had not yet been made public.<sup>9</sup> Davis has also said his DIA-funded investigation concluded that the 2004 Tic Tac unidentified aerial phenomenon associated with the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) was a legitimate [UFO](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo), not a technology made on Earth by humans.<sup>10</sup> The DIA's [Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap) investigation was given an extra 12 million US dollars in 2010 appropriations to produce a dozen more reports.<sup>11</sup> The programme lasted only 27 months before it was ended, rather than the five-year operation the DIA had planned.<sup>12</sup> It was cancelled by the DIA in 2012 owing to a lack of merit and the utility of its deliverables.<sup>13</sup> Its supporters subsequently proposed that the [Department of Homeland Security (DHS)](/organisations/department-of-homeland-security-dhs) create and fund a successor under a special access programme codenamed KONA BLUE.<sup>14</sup>

Clapper](/people/james-r-clapper) personally hired Elizondo into the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, which acted as principal staff for the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) on sensitive intelligence-related matters and oversaw a cluster of DoD agencies including the DIA and the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa).<sup>18</sup> In 2010, Devon Woods, who had previously served as a senior leader at the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, became a senior director at the DIA.<sup>19</sup> Michael Higgins served as the DIA's Director of Operations before moving to a new assignment.<sup>20</sup>

[Thomas Wilson](/people/thomas-wilson) was Deputy Director of the DIA and Assistant Joint Chief of Staff for Intelligence (J-2) in 1997, serving under Director [Patrick Hughes](/people/patrick-hughes).<sup>21</sup> In April 1997, [Steven Greer](/people/steven-greer), as part of a group of UFO disclosure activists and former military servicemen, was escorted to the offices of the Deputy Director of the DIA.<sup>22</sup> In 1998, Greer was invited back to the DIA, again with Commander Willard Miller, to brief Hughes on UFOs.<sup>23</sup> Wilson left the DIA in July 2002 and was replaced by Admiral Lowell Jacoby.<sup>24</sup> He later became the 13th Director of the DIA.<sup>25</sup> [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) has said it is remarkable that Greer achieved being invited to brief senior DIA officials on UAP on at least two occasions, though he considers the claim that both a senior admiral and a general confirmed belief in a UFO cover-up at their first meeting to strain credibility for many who hear it.<sup>26</sup> Bobby Inman was a former vice director of the DIA.<sup>27</sup> According to Dark Mission, a 1993 White House press release stated that Inman won the DIA's Defense Superior Service Medal for achievements it described as "unparalleled in the history of intelligence".<sup>28</sup>
