Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41), nicknamed the Black Aces, is a US Navy squadron flying the F/A-18F Super Hornet.<sup>10</sup> According to David Fravor, the squadron consisted of twelve aircraft and several hundred personnel.<sup>11</sup> [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) commanded VFA-41,<sup>10</sup> flying the two-seat variant of the [F/A-18F Super Hornet](/objects/fa-18f-super-hornet) during his tenure.<sup>13</sup> Per Fravor's statement to the House Oversight Committee, he held command of the squadron in November 2004.<sup>16</sup> Fravor also told the committee that VFA-41 was attached to Carrier Air Wing 11 (CVW-11) and stationed aboard the [USS Nimitz (CVN-68)](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68) in 2004.<sup>19</sup> In earlier testimony to Congress, Fravor separately stated that in 2004 he was commanding officer of the squadron, known as the Black Aces, tasked to Carrier Air Wing 11 aboard the Nimitz.<sup>17</sup><sup>18</sup>

Fravor recounted that during his freshman cruise aboard USS Nimitz he observed Fighter Squadron 41 (VF-41) and Fighter Squadron 84 (VF-84), an F-14 Tomcat squadron, along with A-6 Intruder squadrons operating from the carrier.<sup>12</sup>

VFA-41 was one of the squadrons of CVW-11, which according to the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group (CSG-11) AAV Incident Report also included [Marine Fighter Attack Squadron 232 (VMFA-232)](/organisations/marine-fighter-attack-squadron-232-vmfa-232) flying F/A-18C, Strike Fighter Squadron 14 (VFA-14) flying F/A-18E, Strike Fighter Squadron 94 (VFA-94) flying F/A-18C, Electronic Attack Squadron 135 (VAQ-135) flying EA-6B, Carrier Airborne Early Warning Squadron 117 (VAW-117) flying E-2C Hawkeye, Helicopter Antisubmarine Squadron 6 (HS-6) flying H-60, and Fleet Logistics Support Squadron 30 Detachment 3 (VRC-30 Det 3) flying C-2A.<sup>8</sup> The same report states that the only participants in the events surrounding the detection and intercept of the [Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV)](/topics/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav) were [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59), VAW-117, VMFA-232, and VFA-41.<sup>9</sup>

On 2004-11-14, according to the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group (CSG-11) AAV Incident Report, F/A-18 pilots and weapon system operators from VFA-41 obtained both a visual and sensor contact with the AAV at approximately N31 20' W117 10', about 130 kilometres south of the US/Mexico border and 56 kilometres off the Baja California Mexico coast.<sup>1</sup> The same report identifies Fravor as pilot of FastEagle 01, one of two F/A-18Fs of VFA-41 involved in the intercept and visual contact with the AAV,<sup>2</sup> and [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) as pilot of FastEagle 02, the second aircraft.<sup>3</sup> It further states that Fravor was Commanding Officer of VFA-41 and flew the lead F/A-18F of FastEagle 01.<sup>4</sup> During the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), according to the book *In Plain Sight*, Fravor flew an FA-18F Super Hornet with a wingman, a serving female pilot who wished to remain anonymous, in another Super Hornet.<sup>15</sup>

[Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood) was a member of VFA-41 and the Weapon System Operator flying with another lieutenant, according to the incident report,<sup>5</sup> as part of the second F/A-18F section from the squadron airborne that day.<sup>6</sup> The same report states that all of the AAV activity was reported to D. C. Curtis by the commanding officers of USS Princeton and VFA-41.<sup>7</sup>
