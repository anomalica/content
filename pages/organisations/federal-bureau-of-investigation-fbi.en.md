The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) is the United States' principal domestic security and law-enforcement agency, responsible for handling federal crimes within the continental United States.<sup>1</sup> Across more than seven decades it has surfaced repeatedly in accounts of [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) and [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) research, sometimes as an investigating authority, sometimes as a source of records, and sometimes as the subject of allegations of secrecy or obstruction.

The Bureau's best-known document in the field is the 1950 memo written by Guy Hottel, then head of its Washington DC field office.<sup>2</sup> The 1950 Guy Hottel Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) memo is described as the most controversial and most popular file in the FBI's archive.<sup>3</sup> The Bureau's own website states that the Hottel report was never confirmed or investigated, and that the memo does not prove the existence of aliens.<sup>4</sup> The FBI also investigated the 1964 Lonnie Zamora UAP encounter at Socorro, New Mexico.<sup>5</sup> Author David Marler said that for decades the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf), the FBI and the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) maintained an official policy of denying, dispelling and debunking the UFO subject.<sup>6</sup>

A separate strand of the Bureau's involvement concerns the [cattle mutilations phenomenon](/topics/cattle-mutilations-phenomenon). Colorado Senator Floyd K. Haskell said he had discussed the mutilations with FBI Director Clarence Kelley and his chief assistant.<sup>7</sup> The Bureau concluded it had no jurisdiction to enter the case in the absence of evidence of interstate movement,<sup>8</sup> though it and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms were nonetheless pulled into the 1975 occult mutilation investigation.<sup>9</sup> The 1980 Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) cattle mutilation investigation conceded that some cases could not be prosaically explained, while concluding without evidence that most resulted from natural predation.<sup>10</sup> A separate 100-agent FBI investigation, demanded by three US State Attorneys-General, failed to solve the mystery.<sup>11</sup> The Bureau told an interviewer researching a book that there are 10,000 unsolved cattle mutilation cases across the United States.<sup>12</sup> It is a matter of public record, observed by police, FBI agents and local veterinarians, that thousands of physical evidence reports of suspicious mutilations have continued across a wide swathe of the country for decades.<sup>13</sup> The FBI has also, along with some UAP researchers, dismissed the Majestic-12 documents as an elaborate hoax,<sup>14</sup> and Ross Coulthart said the provenance of those documents is officially disputed by the FBI and the US military.<sup>15</sup>

The Bureau features prominently in the account of [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), who said FBI agents visited ranchers in the Roswell, New Mexico region door-to-door to threaten that anyone who spoke about the 1947 Roswell Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) crash would be killed,<sup>16</sup> and that Edgar Mitchell's family was among the Roswell families threatened by the FBI after the crash.<sup>17</sup> Elizondo also said the CIA and FBI have sometimes posted unclassified UAP documents on their websites for the public to search for and download.<sup>18</sup> He described leading classified investigations worldwide with partners including the FBI, the CIA and the Department of Homeland Security,<sup>19</sup> and said that he and [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) worked their network of friends in the US military branches, FBI, CIA and the Department of Energy.<sup>20</sup> Stratton handpicked members for a new UAP task force from agencies including the FBI, the [National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)](/organisations/national-reconnaissance-office-nro), NASA and the FAA.<sup>21</sup> Separately, as part of Task Force Dagger, Elizondo met FBI Director Robert Mueller in Kandahar, Afghanistan,<sup>22</sup> where the task force worked with the FBI and other US organisations to locate terrorist cells operating in Afghanistan.<sup>23</sup>
