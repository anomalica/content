[L. Ron Hubbard](/people/l-ron-hubbard) founded Dianetics and, subsequently, the Church of Scientology.<sup>1</sup> Before establishing the Church, Hubbard married Sara Northrup.<sup>2</sup>

[Jack Parsons](/people/jack-parsons), who founded the [NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)](/organisations/nasa-jet-propulsion-laboratory-jpl), was Hubbard's compatriot in numerous occult rites before Hubbard went on to found the Church.<sup>3</sup> Jacques Vallée has said that the inventor of Dianetics practised ritual magic with the rocket expert Parsons.<sup>4</sup> Vallée has also linked Parsons and Hubbard to the same process of belief in extraterrestrial superiority.<sup>5</sup>

According to Dark Mission (ebook), the Church has claimed that Hubbard was sent by the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) to infiltrate and break up a black magic cult operating in Los Angeles.<sup>6</sup> [Richard C. Hoagland](/people/richard-c-hoagland) has said that, after reviewing documentation supplied by the Church, he considers plausible the premise that Hubbard was an [Office of Naval Intelligence](/organisations/office-of-naval-intelligence) informant sent to infiltrate and destabilise the Ordo Templi Orientis (OTO) and Federation of American Scientists.<sup>7</sup> According to Dark Mission, the Federation of American Scientists was a left-wing group of disaffected [Manhattan Project](/projects/manhattan-project) veterans dismayed at the military uses of atomic weapons.<sup>8</sup> Hoagland has since said that he holds the weight of the evidence supports the Church's version of events regarding Hubbard's role at the OTO temple in [Pasadena](/places/california-pasadena-usa), over the less flattering alternative account.<sup>9</sup>

In October 2008, [Mike Bara](/people/mike-bara) was invited to visit the L. Ron Hubbard Life Exhibition in Hollywood and meet with several members of the Church.<sup>10</sup> Bara said that members of the Church sought to correct what they viewed as mischaracterisations in Dark Mission's treatment of Hubbard and Parsons at the JPL.<sup>11</sup> According to Bara, the Church was most concerned with conveying that none of its teachings are based on the Thelemic beliefs of [Aleister Crowley](/people/aleister-crowley).<sup>12</sup> Bara said he considers the Church deserving of the benefit of the doubt on that point.<sup>13</sup> Bara said the Church argued that Hubbard was sent to infiltrate and disrupt Parsons' activities at 1003 Orange Grove Avenue rather than being a willing participant in Parsons' pursuits within the OTO.<sup>14</sup> Bara said that documents provided by the Church inclined him to lean toward the infiltration interpretation.<sup>15</sup>

According to Dark Mission, a Bill of Sale produced by the Church shows that Parsons sold the sailing boat Harpoon (sailing boat) to Hubbard.<sup>16</sup> Hoagland has said that the Church remains a major influence in Hollywood today.<sup>17</sup>
