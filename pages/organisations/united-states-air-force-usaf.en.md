The United States Air Force (USAF) is a branch of the US armed forces and has been the principal government institution responsible for the formal investigation of [unidentified flying objects (UFOs)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) in the United States. Its involvement with [unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) spans from the late 1940s through to the present day, encompassing a succession of official study programmes, public communications strategies, and, more recently, a role in developing contemporary UAP reporting frameworks alongside the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn).<sup>1</sup><sup>26</sup>

The USAF's formal UAP investigation began in December 1947, when the Chief of Staff established [Project Sign](/projects/project-sign) to investigate early recorded observations of unidentified flying objects.<sup>3</sup> USAF personnel investigated 243 reported UAP sightings under Project Sign between 1947 and 1949.<sup>4</sup> Project Sign's findings were inconclusive but remained open to the possibility of extraterrestrial origins, while the subsequent [Project GRUDGE](/projects/project-grudge) adopted a position of debunking the phenomena as the result of natural causes.<sup>5</sup> A significant internal document produced during this period, the Estimate of the Situation, was rejected by USAF Chief of Staff General [Hoyt S. Vandenberg](/people/hoyt-s-vandenberg) as lacking proof.<sup>6</sup> After the original Project Grudge was terminated, the USAF continued collecting and analysing UFO reports within its existing intelligence processes,<sup>7</sup> before reorganising that mission in late 1951 into a new programme also named Project Grudge, led by Captain [Edward Ruppelt](/people/edward-ruppelt).<sup>8</sup>

In 1952, USAF Director of Intelligence Major General Charles P. Cabell established [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book) to investigate UFOs.<sup>9</sup><sup>11</sup> Between 1952 and 1969, USAF personnel investigated over 12,000 reported sightings under the programme.<sup>10</sup> [J. Allen Hynek](/people/j-allen-hynek) served as the lead scientist in charge of the Air Force's official UAP investigation team within Project Blue Book.<sup>12</sup> Captain Ruppelt later wrote that he had been expected to explain away every report and that the USAF sought press coverage aligned with its official position.<sup>13</sup> Hynek similarly stated that the USAF expected him to perform the role of debunker.<sup>14</sup> In parallel, the USAF implemented a public affairs campaign intended to persuade the public that UFOs were nothing unusual, with the stated goal of alleviating public anxiety.<sup>15</sup> In 1953, the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia)'s Robertson Panel advised the Air Force to enlist mass media in a campaign to debunk UAP reports.<sup>16</sup> An Air Force-funded study at the [University of Colorado](/organisations/university-of-colorado), chaired by physicist [Edward U. Condon](/people/edward-u-condon), subsequently declared the UAP topic devoid of scientific merit;<sup>17</sup> the resulting [Condon Report](/documents/condon-report) provided the Air Force with justification to close Project Blue Book.<sup>18</sup> The USAF formally concluded that no UFO reported, investigated, and evaluated under Project Blue Book had demonstrated any indication of a threat to national security.<sup>19</sup> Project Blue Book was terminated in 1969.<sup>20</sup>

The [Roswell incident (1947)](/events/roswell-incident-1947) became a central point of contention in assessments of USAF transparency. The [Government Accountability Office](/organisations/government-accountability-office) later commissioned an audit of the matter, and the USAF conducted a systematic search of archives and records centres in support of that inquiry.<sup>21</sup> In 1995, the USAF published [The Roswell Report: Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert (1995)](/documents/the-roswell-report-fact-versus-fiction-in-the-new-mexico-desert-1995), which concluded that the research did not locate information indicating the Roswell incident was a UFO event and found no evidence of a government cover-up.<sup>22</sup><sup>23</sup> A follow-on report, [The Roswell Report: Case Closed (1997)](/documents/the-roswell-report-case-closed-1997), published in 1997, supported the conclusion that the recovered debris had originated from a balloon-borne programme.<sup>24</sup>

In the contemporary period, the USAF designated [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) as a Special Interest Item, requiring aircrews to document and report UAP observations within 24 hours of engine shutdown and mission completion using the [Marauder Mission Reporting System](/objects/marauder-mission-reporting-system).<sup>25</sup> The [United States Army](/organisations/united-states-army) has used the same system because it has not developed its own UAP-specific processes. The USAF and USN have been described as being at the forefront of developing policies and mechanisms for UAP reporting.<sup>26</sup> The [Evaluation of the DoD's Actions Regarding Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (DODIG-2023-109)](/documents/evaluation-of-the-dods-actions-regarding-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-dodig-2023-109) recommended that the Secretary of the Air Force issue interim UAP guidance while awaiting [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) policy;<sup>27</sup> the Air Force's informal response did not provide specific actions or dates, leaving that recommendation resolved but open.<sup>28</sup>

Questions about the USAF's posture on UAP have persisted into recent congressional proceedings. The [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) found no evidence that the USAF maintained a policy intended to cover up extraterrestrial knowledge or materials, concluding instead that it had focused on concerns such as Soviet technology and US defence readiness.<sup>29</sup> Nevertheless, observers including [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) concluded that the Air Force was part of a broader effort to suppress UAP information,<sup>32</sup> and reporting indicated the Air Force had been less forthcoming than other agencies in this domain.<sup>30</sup> For decades, the USAF, [FBI](/organisations/fbi), and CIA were described as collectively maintaining a policy of denial towards UFO reports.<sup>31</sup> The Air Force also declined to support the congressionally funded Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) UAP investigation.<sup>33</sup> [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch), a former USAF officer who reached the rank of Major after 14 years of service,<sup>34</sup> became a significant public figure in UAP disclosure discussions, testifying before Congress in 2023 on matters relating to [Non-Human Craft Recovery Programs](/matters/non-human-craft-recovery-programs) and alleged government concealment of retrieved materials.
