The Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) has published public reports documenting [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap).<sup>1</sup> According to the Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena, Senate Report 116-233, accompanying the Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021, directed ODNI, in consultation with the Secretary of Defense, to submit an intelligence assessment of the threat posed by UAP and to report on the progress made by the [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)](/projects/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uaptf) in understanding that threat.<sup>2</sup> The Senate Intelligence Committee separately ordered the Director of National Intelligence and the Secretary of Defense to deliver a report on UAP sightings within a month,<sup>3</sup> and in December 2020, while serving as head of the [US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](/organisations/us-senate-select-committee-on-intelligence), [Marco Rubio](/people/marco-rubio) asked ODNI and the Pentagon to present Congress with an unclassified report on UAP within a month.<sup>4</sup>

In June 2021, the UAPTF and ODNI issued the congressionally mandated [Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena](/documents/preliminary-assessment-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-2021-06).<sup>5</sup> According to a [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) Inspector General summary of its evaluation of the department's actions on UAP, the assessment defines UAP as objects that are not immediately identifiable and lack a single explanation,<sup>6</sup> sorting them into one of five potential explanatory categories: airborne clutter, natural atmospheric phenomena, US government or industry developmental programmes, foreign adversary systems, and a catchall "other" category.<sup>7</sup>

Consistent with congressional direction, the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) provides quarterly reports, semiannual briefings, and an annual report to [Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) in coordination with ODNI.<sup>8</sup> According to the same historical-record report, ODNI submitted the 2022 Annual Report on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena to Congress on 2023-01-12.<sup>9</sup> That report's 510 total UAP records included the 144 reports covered by the 17 years of reporting in the earlier preliminary assessment, together with 247 new reports and 119 reports subsequently discovered or reported.<sup>10</sup>
