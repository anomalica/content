The Vatican, the government of the Holy See and central institution of the Catholic Church, recurs in UAP research chiefly through the Third Secret of Fatima and through claims of a Vatican intelligence-gathering role. Luis Elizondo has said the Vatican has formed alliances with UAP transparency advocates in Italy and the United States and confidentially shares centuries-old historic accounts that may be UAP-related.<sup>1</sup> Elizondo said he was told the Vatican has collected intelligence over the millennia drawn from eyewitness reports and anecdotes shared in the confessional,<sup>2</sup> and that the Catholic Church routinely polled priests to speak in general terms about what troubled their parishioners.<sup>3</sup> Researcher Jason Samosa has said he is certain the Vatican knows something about [UFOs](/topics/ufos-uaps),<sup>4</sup> and observes that the Vatican's role in the subject is hinted at by many researchers he respects but then drops away with no one focusing on it.<sup>5</sup>

The Vatican's engagement with the Fatima apparitions (1917) began cautiously. Michael Hesemann has said that Lúcia (Fatima seer)'s steps toward becoming a nun were guided by Church authorities who were at the time investigating the Fatima incidents and wanted her under their control so as to control the cult of Fatima itself.<sup>6</sup> After Lúcia left Aljustrel, all religious proclamations at the Cova da Iria chapel were made by priests appointed by the Church.<sup>7</sup> It had initially been difficult to find a priest willing to serve the chapel, because the apparitions had not yet been officially recognised by the Catholic Church.<sup>8</sup> Formal diplomatic relations between Portugal and the Vatican were established by Sidonio Pais in June 1918.<sup>9</sup>

According to The Fatima Secret, the Third Secret of Fatima would become the most zealously guarded secret in Vatican history,<sup>10</sup> and the sealed envelope was kept in the private quarters of [Pius XII](/people/pius-xii), the most secure part of the Vatican.<sup>11</sup> Bishop da Silva had wanted to send the envelope to Rome, but the Vatican instructed him to keep it at Leiria for the present.<sup>12</sup> Pius XII later ordered that Sister Lúcia could meet only people she was already in contact with, and that anyone else wishing to see her needed Vatican permission.<sup>13</sup> According to the same account, some within the Vatican opposed consecrating Russia because they wished to establish diplomatic relations with Moscow first, fearing the [Soviet Union](/organisations/soviet-union) would read a papal proclamation as interference in its internal affairs and endanger relief for Christians there<sup>14</sup>; despite being called the "pope of Fatima", Pius XII did not overcome this opposition to the consecration the apparition was said to have requested.<sup>15</sup> When the Portuguese bishops again petitioned Rome during the 1942 jubilee year, the Vatican remained silent until 1942-10-13.<sup>16</sup> [John XXIII](/people/john-xxiii) read the sealed envelope on 1959-08-21 in a closed room at Castel Gandolfo, in the presence only of a Vatican interpreter and his confessor, Monsignor Alfredo Cavagna.<sup>17</sup> A 1960 Vatican announcement stated that, given pressure both to publish and to keep the letter sealed for fear of alarming prophecies, it would likely remain sealed forever.<sup>18</sup> The Vatican maintained official silence on the Third Secret until the fiftieth anniversary of the Fatima events.<sup>19</sup> Swiss journalist Louis Emrich said the Vatican had taken every precaution to keep the document a papal secret.<sup>20</sup>

A separate, unofficial account known as the Diplomatic version of the Third Secret of Fatima (European News, 1963-10-15) had allegedly been sent by the Vatican to the governments of [D.C. Washington](/places/washington-dc-usa), [London](/places/london-united-kingdom) and Moscow at the height of the Cuban Missile Crisis in 1962-10, a year before its publication.<sup>21</sup> European News further reported that the 1963 agreement between the United Kingdom, Russia and the United States to halt atomic bomb tests was due to Vatican intervention.<sup>22</sup> Mrs Emrich, Louis Emrich's widow, told Hans Bender that her husband had worked on the story for some time before its 1963-10-15 publication and already held information about the document's probable contents,<sup>23</sup> and that a Vatican priest had visited Emrich, stressing that he had come unofficially and for his own reasons.<sup>24</sup> The Emrich version was never confirmed or denied by the Catholic Church.<sup>25</sup> Emrich himself later admitted that his diplomatic version had been constructed from rumours and information received from the Vatican, and was not meant to represent the original text.<sup>26</sup> Following the Vatican's 2000 press conference, Michael Hesemann spent a week in [Rome](/places/rome-italy) speaking to Vatican sources and conducting a long interview with Archbishop Tarcisio Bertone<sup>27</sup>; Bertone's research, according to the same account, found no evidence of a Vatican leak nor support for Emrich's claim that the Third Secret had been shown to Nikita Khrushchev or John F. Kennedy.<sup>28</sup> Father Nicholas Gruner accused the Vatican of whitewashing and watering down the Third Secret's message in the 2000 disclosure,<sup>29</sup> arguing that the Vatican's official interpretation raised far more questions than it answered.<sup>30</sup>

The Third Secret was formally revealed only at the end of a two-and-a-half-hour ceremony on 2000-05-13, after priests distributing the sacrament to the crowd had returned to their places.<sup>31</sup> In response to sceptics demanding publication of Lúcia's own handwritten notes, the Vatican released a facsimile of her handwriting alongside letters she had written three years earlier.<sup>32</sup> Weeks later, on 2000-06-05, newly elected Russian president Vladimir Putin visited the Vatican for a confidential meeting,<sup>33</sup> and the 2000-06-14 pardon of Mehmet Ali Ağca had been discussed with the Vatican beforehand and was supported by [John Paul II](/people/john-paul-ii).<sup>34</sup> Michael Hesemann, who travelled to Rome many times from Germany to speak with Vatican insiders about the Third Secret's contents,<sup>35</sup> considers it possible that the Fatima message influenced the Vatican's secret policy from Pius XII to John Paul II,<sup>36</sup> reflecting what he describes as the apparitions' deep impact on the Vatican's secret policies more broadly.<sup>37</sup> Among the Vatican insiders named in this research is Corrado Balducci, a Vatican official of fifty-two years' standing<sup>38</sup> who had worked for several years as an exorcist under Pope Paul VI.<sup>39</sup>
