The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is a US federal regulatory body whose filings requirements have surfaced in the corpus through its oversight of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa). TTSA was structured as a public company soliciting investment from potential investors as a public benefit corporation, a status prescribed under SEC laws and regulation.<sup>1</sup><sup>2</sup>

TTSA's disclosure obligations to the SEC produced a public record of the company's activities and personnel changes. It took [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) several months to formally disclose to the SEC that [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), and [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice) had departed the organisation.<sup>3</sup>

Two specific SEC disclosures document TTSA's technical and materials work. In a 2018 disclosure, TTSA revealed that it had retained [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff)'s firm EarthTech International to prepare plans on materials analysis and beamed energy propulsion launch systems, according to that filing.<sup>4</sup> In a 2019 disclosure, TTSA notified the SEC that Tom DeLonge had sold the same six Art's Parts samples to the company for $35,000, and that Hal Puthoff would be analysing them for TTSA, according to that filing.<sup>5</sup>
