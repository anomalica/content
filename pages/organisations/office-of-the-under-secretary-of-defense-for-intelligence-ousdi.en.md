Reid, who replaced Michael Higgins, came to OUSDI from the special operations community and was not related to Senator Harry Reid.<sup>32</sup> Neill Tipton was, at the time, among the top three senior officials in OUSDI, a rank equivalent to a three-star general;<sup>33</sup> Elizondo gave Tipton a full briefing, a folder of hard-copy Unidentified Aerial Phenomena reports several inches thick, and access to OUSDI's classified share drive.<sup>34</sup> Brad Byers was reportedly hesitant to brief [James Mattis](/people/james-mattis) on the subject until OUSDI had a permanent, Senate-confirmed appointee, since the position had been filled only by acting or temporary personnel since Michael Vickers had departed.<sup>35</sup> Elizondo and Jay Stratton also submitted Operation Plan Interloper through an Alternative Compensatory Control Measure (ACCM) process directly to the Joint Chiefs of Staff, bypassing OUSDI.<sup>36</sup> Employees filed charges with the [Department of Defense Inspector General](/organisations/department-of-defense-inspector-general) about Reid's conduct at OUSDI,<sup>37</sup> and Elizondo said colleagues loyal to him who remained at the office told him Reid planned to launch a criminal inquiry with AFOSI.<sup>38</sup> Elizondo also said that, according to a Freedom of Information Act response from the Pentagon, someone at OUSDI allegedly authorised the complete deletion of all his electronic files, folders and emails on the grounds that they had no historic value.<sup>39</sup> Reid was later reassigned from OUSDI,<sup>40</sup> and Elizondo has described the office as the same organisation that had previously underplayed and tried to kill AATIP and then the Pentagon's [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)](/projects/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uaptf).<sup>41</sup>

In Fiscal Years 2019 and 2020, Congress required the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD(I&S)) and the intelligence community to establish an interagency task force and develop a plan to investigate and address unidentified and unexplained airborne activity in sensitive areas.<sup>42</sup> The [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)](/projects/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uaptf) was led by the [United States Department of the Navy](/organisations/united-states-department-of-the-navy) under OUSD(I&S).<sup>43</sup> In a November 2021 memorandum, OUSD(I&S) was directed to establish the [Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG)](/organisations/airborne-object-identification-and-management-synchronization-group-aoimsg) as the successor to the UAPTF, to synchronise efforts across DoD and with other federal departments and agencies to detect, identify and characterise objects of interest in special use airspace.<sup>44</sup> The [Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG)](/organisations/airborne-object-identification-and-management-synchronization-group-aoimsg) operated under OUSD(I&S)'s auspices.<sup>45</sup> According to the Department of Defense's 2024 historical-record report, the Deputy Secretary of Defense, in coordination with the Director of National Intelligence, renamed AOIMSG as the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) and expanded its scope and mission, in response to the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022.<sup>46</sup> The DoD Inspector General's August 2023 evaluation states that the same Act assigns AARO, under OUSD(I&S), responsibility for synchronising and standardising the collection, analysis and identification of Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena incidents.<sup>47</sup> The Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security also noted that the findings informing that assessment largely predated the establishment of AARO - organisationally aligned under OUSD(I&S) - on 2022-07-20.<sup>48</sup>

DoD Instruction 5240.22 authorises OUSD(I&S) to establish policy for, and oversee, DoD counterintelligence support to counterterrorism and force protection, according to the U.S. Department of Defense Inspector General's August 2023 evaluation.<sup>49</sup> For that evaluation, the Inspector General interviewed senior officials at OUSD(I&S), the Military Services, the Military Department Counterintelligence Organizations, and Defense agencies to learn how their organisations detect, report, collect, analyse and identify UAP incidents.<sup>50</sup> It separately requested UAP-related data and organisational policies, procedures and guidance from OUSD(I&S), AARO, the Military Services, the Military Service Intelligence Components, the Military Department Counterintelligence Organizations, and defense intelligence agencies.<sup>51</sup> Responding for OUSD(I&S) and the AARO Director, the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security agreed with the Inspector General's recommendation to issue a Department of Defense policy on UAP.<sup>52</sup> The Inspector General will close that recommendation once OUSD(I&S), in coordination with AARO, provides the issued policy integrating UAP roles, responsibilities, requirements and coordination procedures into existing intelligence, counterintelligence and force protection policies and procedures.<sup>53</sup>
