The Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI), formally the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD[I&S]), is a senior component of the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) responsible for intelligence, counterintelligence, and security policy. The Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence position requires Senate confirmation.<sup>41</sup> Notable former holders of the position include [James R. Clapper](/people/james-r-clapper), who served as undersecretary of defense for intelligence before subsequently being appointed Director of National Intelligence by President Barack Obama.<sup>42</sup> The office has been organisationally significant in the history of US government UAP-related programmes, both as the administrative home of key personnel and as the parent body for successive UAP oversight structures.

[Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) was assigned to OUSDI from 2008-09-28 to 2017-10-04,<sup>1</sup> having returned to the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) in 2008 to work on an information-sharing operation between the DoD, the Department of Homeland Security, and state, local, and tribal law enforcement authorities.<sup>2</sup> According to testimony attributed to Kari DeLonge, when Elizondo took over the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program) (AATIP) as Director in 2010, he ran it out of the Office of the Secretary of Defense under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.<sup>3</sup> Separately, the AATIP -- also known as the Advanced Aerospace Weapons System Applications Program ([Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap)) -- was established in 2009 in coordination with OUSDI and the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia), with the contract awarded to a private sector organisation.<sup>4</sup>

The office became publicly associated with a dispute over [Luis D. Elizondo](/people/luis-d-elizondo)'s role following his departure in October 2017. Pentagon spokesperson Christopher Sherwood stated that Elizondo had no responsibilities with regard to AATIP while he worked in OUSDI, up until the time he resigned effective 2017-10-04.<sup>5</sup> Pentagon spokesperson Susan Gough later reiterated this position.<sup>6</sup> Christopher Sherwood stated he had spoken with OUSDI leadership, including individuals present from the time Elizondo started in the office, to establish this.<sup>7</sup> Elizondo, for his part, told [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton)'s colleague Garry Reid in a phone call on 2017-10-05 that he had for many years been secretly tasked by the Secretary of Defense as programme manager for investigating UFOs and other aerial threats, and that nobody in OUSDI had been cleared for this programme.<sup>8</sup> [Garry Reid](/people/luis-elizondo) served as Director for Defense Intelligence within OUSDI.<sup>9</sup>

Elizondo's departure generated an internal review. His first resignation letter, dated 2017-10-03, submitted his resignation effective 2017-10-04 in his capacity as Director, National Programs Special Management Staff, OUSDI, and requested a deferred retirement.<sup>10</sup> He was cleared out of OUSDI the day after submitting this letter and provided no reason for the sudden departure.<sup>11</sup> On or about 2017-10-04, a second resignation letter was delivered to the OUSDI Chief of Staff office by someone other than Elizondo, formatted as a Memorandum for Record and citing concerns over [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) and [Anomalous Aerospace Threats](/topics/anomalous-aerospace-threats) as the basis for his resignation.<sup>12</sup> Given uncertain provenance, OUSDI retained a copy but did not forward it to the Secretary of Defense's office.<sup>13</sup> OUSDI referred the timing and circumstances of Elizondo's resignation to the [Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI)](/organisations/air-force-office-of-special-investigations-afosi), whose informal assessment did not find any wrongdoing and declined to open a formal investigation absent indicators of misconduct.<sup>14</sup> The office was also reviewing AATIP history with the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia), the [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy), and the US Air Force, and pursuing options to investigate Elizondo's possible mishandling of sensitive information.<sup>15</sup> On 2017-12-15, Garry Reid notified the OUSDI Security Officer that Elizondo may have misused government systems and may intend to release US Government footage or information obtained during his employment.<sup>16</sup> On 2017-12-18, OUSDI located a classified email generated by Elizondo in August 2017 in which he had requested assistance from a [United States Department of the Navy](/organisations/united-states-department-of-the-navy) civilian employee to declassify one or more videos.<sup>17</sup> According to a Freedom of Information Act response, Elizondo's electronic files, folders, and emails were subsequently deleted by someone in OUSDI under the justification that they had no historic value.<sup>18</sup>

Accounts from Elizondo's memoir describe internal tensions within OUSDI during this period. [James R. Clapper](/people/james-r-clapper) had been a senior supervisor of Elizondo within the office.<sup>19</sup> [Neill Tipton](/people/neill-tipton) was described as among the top three senior officials in OUSDI, equivalent in rank to a three-star general.<sup>21</sup> Elizondo and [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) reportedly attempted to circumvent the office's bureaucracy by submitting OPLAN Interloper to the Joint Chiefs of Staff via an Alternate Compensatory Control Measures (ACCM) process, though they were ultimately unable to bypass OUSDI to escalate UAP concerns.<sup>20</sup> [Brad Byers](/people/brad-byers) was described as hesitant to brief the Secretary of Defense until OUSDI had a permanent appointment to the Under Secretary position.<sup>40</sup>

In the years following Elizondo's departure, OUSDI -- by then redesignated as the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD[I&S]) -- became the parent body for successive UAP investigation structures. The United States Congress, in its Fiscal Year 2019 and Fiscal Year 2020 legislation, required OUSD[I&S] and the intelligence community to establish an interagency task force and develop a plan to investigate unidentified airborne activity in sensitive areas.<sup>22</sup> The [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)](/organisations/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uaptf) was subsequently led by the [United States Department of the Navy](/organisations/united-states-department-of-the-navy) under the cognizance of OUSD[I&S].<sup>23</sup> In a November 2021 memorandum, the Deputy Secretary of Defense directed OUSD[I&S] to establish the [Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG)](/organisations/airborne-object-identification-and-management-synchronization-group-aoimsg) as the successor to the UAPTF, to synchronise efforts across the DoD and with other federal departments and agencies to detect, identify, and characterise objects of interest in special use airspace.<sup>24</sup> In response to the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022, the Deputy Secretary of Defense directed OUSD[I&S] to rename AOIMSG as the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) and expand its scope and mission, effective 2022-07-15.<sup>25</sup> The Fiscal Year 2022 National Defense Authorization Act assigns the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)))](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) responsibility, under OUSD[I&S], for synchronising and standardising the collection, analysis, and identification of UAP incidents.<sup>26</sup> The [Evaluation of the DoD's Actions Regarding Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (DODIG-2023-109)](/documents/evaluation-of-the-dods-actions-regarding-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-dodig-2023-109), produced by the [U.S. Department of Defense Office of Inspector General](/organisations/us-department-of-defense-office-of-inspector-general), recommended that the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, in coordination with the AARO Director, issue a DoD policy to integrate UAP roles, responsibilities, and coordination procedures into existing intelligence, counterintelligence, and force protection policies and procedures.<sup>27</sup>
