SRI International, formerly the Stanford Research Institute, is an independent nonprofit research institute in California Menlo Park, where [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee) worked with affiliated scientists.<sup>1</sup> [Diana Walsh Pasulka](/people/diana-walsh-pasulka)'s research found that, like other scientists she had interviewed, [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell) had also been involved with the institute.<sup>2</sup>

SRI became linked to research into [Remote Viewing (RV)](/topics/remote-viewing-rv), precognition, and extrasensory perception, studied at the institute at the same time and place as Vallée's early internet research.<sup>3</sup> According to American Cosmic, declassified documents from the 1970s and 1980s indicate SRI International was a research site for extraordinary research.<sup>4</sup> Remote viewing and related esoteric skills were also studied there under a classified programme called [The Stargate Project](/projects/the-stargate-project), funded by the US military in partnership with SRI.<sup>5</sup> When organisers later set up [Project STARGATE](/projects/project-stargate), they turned to SRI, where remote viewing had already been studied for nearly five years.<sup>6</sup> Researchers at the institute are credited with coining the term "remote viewing" in the early 1970s.<sup>7</sup> Its [Cognitive Sciences Laboratory](/organisations/cognitive-sciences-laboratory-the-laboratories-for-fundamental-research) received considerable funding, carried out and supported extensive experimentation, and provided support to intelligence operations from 1972 through 1995.<sup>8</sup>

The remote-viewing programme was led by [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) and [Russell Targ](/people/russell-targ). Puthoff's career spanned decades of research at General Electric, Sperry Corporation, the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa), [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university), and SRI International.<sup>9</sup> Most of the subjects Puthoff and Targ worked with displayed some degree of remote-viewing talent from the outset<sup>10</sup> and, in a form of self-selection, voluntarily continued their involvement out of strong personal interest.<sup>11</sup> Puthoff and Targ's report, as recorded in Remote Viewing Secrets, held that subjects who trained over a one-year period performed excellently under a variety of experimental conditions,<sup>12</sup> and that accumulated data indicated both specially selected and unselected people could be assisted in developing remote-perceptual abilities to a level of useful information transfer.<sup>13</sup> An IEEE report on the research, cited in Remote Viewing Secrets, stated that the drawings subjects produced were generally more accurate than their verbal descriptions.<sup>14</sup> [Joseph McMoneagle](/people/joseph-mcmoneagle) later said that "perform well" in Puthoff and Targ's report did not imply world-class remote viewing, but rather the ability to display psychic functioning under controlled conditions to a better-than-chance result.<sup>15</sup>

According to Puthoff and Targ's report, as recorded in Remote Viewing Secrets, viewers should be self-selected,<sup>16</sup> able to learn by example,<sup>17</sup> and open to the possibility of paranormal information transfer,<sup>18</sup> while being able to withstand public scrutiny, ridicule, and failure.<sup>19</sup> The same report held that subjects should have artistic talent capable of expressing their perceptions through drawing or sketching,<sup>20</sup> and should be able to process information within a high-stimulation environment such as the Ganzfeld procedure.<sup>21</sup>

[Ingo Swann](/people/ingo-swann) was a well-known psychic and remote viewer who worked with the laboratory at SRI.<sup>22</sup> Swann developed Controlled Remote Viewing (CRV) while working at the institute,<sup>23</sup> and McMoneagle said that Swann's suggestion to use map coordinates to identify a target gave rise to the [Coordinate Remote Viewing](/topics/coordinate-remote-viewing) system.<sup>24</sup> The Outbounder Remote Viewing protocol was initially used exclusively by scientists at SRI.<sup>25</sup> McMoneagle himself was formally trained in dowsing at the Cognitive Sciences Laboratory by an internationally known dowser,<sup>26</sup> and consulted for SRI and Science Applications International Corporation (SAIC) from 1984 through 1995.<sup>27</sup> He later observed that, over the course of their careers, every exceptional viewer studied at SRI's Cognitive Sciences Laboratory and SAIC essentially produced a near flat-line in ability, with the best performing about as well at the start of their careers as at the end.<sup>28</sup>
