The United States Navy (USN) is a branch of the US armed forces that has generated and retained some of the most consequential first-hand evidence relating to [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap), principally through encounters by its aviators and the sensor systems aboard its vessels and aircraft. Two of its officers - [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor), a retired Commander and former Commanding Officer who served as a naval aviator for 18 years,<sup>1</sup> and [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves), a former [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) pilot with a decade of Navy service including two deployments in Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Inherent Resolve<sup>2</sup> - became among the most prominent witnesses to testify publicly about UAP encounters. Both drew on direct operational experience, with [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) noting that at the time of the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) he had only recently assumed command of his squadron.<sup>3</sup>

The [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) stands as the most extensively documented Navy UAP case. The incident off Southern California was recorded by radar, by camera, and by four naval aviators.<sup>3</sup> The USS Princeton (CG-59), a Ticonderoga-class guided-missile cruiser serving in the Navy, was among the assets involved.<sup>4</sup> Footage subsequently released showed a whitish oval object chased by two Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18)F fighter jets off the coast of San Diego.<sup>5</sup> The infrared imagery in that and related cases was captured on board the Navy's most sophisticated fighter jets and ships.<sup>6</sup> The Navy also recorded two further encounters in January 2015,<sup>8</sup> and these videos had been circulating in the public domain following unauthorised releases in 2007 and 2017.<sup>9</sup> The Navy had previously acknowledged that the circulating videos were genuine Navy recordings<sup>10</sup> before the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) authorised their formal release on 2020-04-27.<sup>11</sup>

The [Three unclassified US Navy Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) videos (2004, 2015)](/documents/2015-three-unclassified-us-navy-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-videos-2004) formed part of the evidence gathered by the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), which collected video and audio recordings of reported [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) incidents, including footage from a Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Super Hornet showing an aircraft surrounded by a glowing aura travelling at high speed and rotating as it moved.<sup>12</sup> Defence officials declined to release the location and date of the glowing-aura incident.<sup>13</sup> From 2012 onwards, [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) worked with officials from the Navy and the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) as part of the programme's work.<sup>14</sup> In August 2017, [Luis D. Elizondo](/people/luis-d-elizondo) generated a classified email requesting that a Navy civilian employee assist with declassifying one or more videos; that employee informed the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) he was not the declassification release authority and took no further action.<sup>16</sup> The OUSDI subsequently reviewed AATIP history in consultation with the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia), the Navy, and the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf), while pursuing options to investigate Elizondo's possible mishandling of sensitive information.<sup>15</sup>

Wider Navy UAP activity extended beyond the Nimitz strike group. A US Navy aircrew struggled to lock onto a fast-moving object off the US Atlantic coast in 2015,<sup>7</sup> and [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) reported that UAP had been detected essentially everywhere Navy operations are conducted across the world, according to [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) reporting.<sup>17</sup> [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) also testified that the Navy took the danger to his aircraft from the [2014 Virginia Beach UAP encounter](/events/2014-virginia-beach-uap-encounter) seriously after it was reported.<sup>23</sup> The Navy introduced a Range Fouler Report to gather UAP information from pilots, though a service-wide reporting mechanism remained pending as of his testimony at the [2023 House Oversight UAP Hearing](/events/2023-house-oversight-uap-hearing).<sup>18</sup> In August 2020 the Pentagon reconstituted its programme under the name UAP Task Force, with service members encouraged to report strange encounters.<sup>24</sup>

Alongside the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf), the Navy has been identified as being at the forefront of developing policies, procedures, and mechanisms for UAP reporting.<sup>19</sup> Following the [Evaluation of the DoD's Actions Regarding Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (DODIG-2023-109)](/documents/evaluation-of-the-dods-actions-regarding-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-dodig-2023-109), Recommendation 3 called on the Secretary of the Navy to issue interim UAP guidance pending overarching Department of Defense policy, covering integration of existing intelligence, counterintelligence, and force protection procedures, coordination with geographic combatant commands, and defined roles and responsibilities for Military Department Counterintelligence Organisations.<sup>20</sup> The Director of Intelligence, Deputy Under Secretary of the Navy, responding on behalf of the Secretary of the Navy, agreed with the recommendation and stated that the Navy had begun developing interim guidance.<sup>21</sup> The [Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG)](/organisations/airborne-object-identification-and-management-synchronization-group-aoimsg) was subsequently established by direction of the Deputy Secretary of Defense to succeed the Navy's UAP Task Force.<sup>22</sup> Earlier historical claims involving the Navy - such as [Frank Edwards](/people/frank-edwards)'s account of a Navy jet engaging a disc-shaped object near Washington, D.C. - were investigated and found to lack corroborating incident reports; the USAF and Navy found no record of any weapons engagement with a UAP that summer, and no Navy aircraft were recorded as present.<sup>25</sup>
