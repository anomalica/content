Fox News is an American cable television news network. According to the book *Dark Mission*, on the Saturday before the 2000 United States presidential election the network broke a story about [George W. Bush](/people/george-w-bush)'s arrest 25 years earlier for drunk driving.<sup>1</sup>

The network later featured coverage relating to [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap). Days after [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) broke the story on the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) in December 2017, Brett Baer said on Fox News that a lot of people were taking the revelation seriously.<sup>2</sup> Following that coverage, calls flooded [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo)'s home from CBS, ABC, NBC, [CNN](/organisations/cable-news-network-cnn), PBS, Fox and MSNBC.<sup>3</sup>

In May 2019, Fox News host [Tucker Carlson](/people/tucker-carlson) interviewed Elizondo, a conversation later published as the 2019-05 Fox News interview with Luis Elizondo on recovered Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) debris. Elizondo said it was an absolute fact that unidentified aerial phenomena objects were there, but that what they are, where they are from, and who is behind the wheel was not known.<sup>4</sup>

In a separate appearance, [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) told Carlson that the Tic Tac craft he encountered was white, about 12 metres long, hovering over the water, and moved forward, back, left and right with no rotor wash and no wings.<sup>5</sup>

When Fox & Friends, a Fox News programme, covered the documentary Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation, it did not mention that [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) works for [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa), nor that the documentary was made by the company that employs Elizondo and Mellon.<sup>6</sup>
