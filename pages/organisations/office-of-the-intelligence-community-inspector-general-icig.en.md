The Office of the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG) is a US federal oversight body responsible for receiving and evaluating whistleblower complaints from within the United States Intelligence Community. It became a focal point of public UAP discourse in 2023 after it was reported to have assessed a complaint concerning unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) as both credible and urgent.

In 2022, while employed at the [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA)](/organisations/national-geospatial-intelligence-agency-nga), [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch) filed a federal whistleblower complaint with the ICIG.<sup>1</sup> The complaint was found credible and urgent for reporting to the Congressional intelligence committees.<sup>2</sup> This filing forms the basis of the event known as David Grusch's federal whistleblower complaint to the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG), and the underlying document is catalogued as the David Grusch 2022 whistleblower complaint to the Intelligence Community Inspector General. The managing partner of Compass Rose Legal Group, the law firm that formerly represented Grusch, reportedly co-signed the complaint submitted to the ICIG.<sup>3</sup>

According to The Debrief article "Intelligence Officials Say U.S. Has Retrieved Non-Human Craft" (2023-06-05) and a subsequent Compass Rose press release (2023-06-09), the ICIG found the whistleblower's assertion that UFO-related information was inappropriately concealed from [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) to be "urgent and credible."<sup>4</sup> In the same press release, Compass Rose Legal Group clarified that it "took no position and takes no position" on the substance of the information Grusch provided to the ICIG.<sup>5</sup> Commenting on the matter, Marik von Rennenkampff said that Grusch and other individuals who spoke to investigators appeared to have little incentive to lie, given the legal risk of making false statements to the ICIG.<sup>6</sup> Making knowingly and willfully false statements to the ICIG carries the risk of financial penalties and imprisonment.<sup>7</sup>

The ICIG has also been named in connection with testimony from other figures associated with UAP-related whistleblowing. [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) has stated that he and many of his former colleagues have provided classified testimony to both the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) and the ICIG.<sup>8</sup> Elizondo further states that many of the colleagues who provided such classified testimony have subsequently been targeted with threats to their careers, security clearances, and lives.<sup>9</sup>
