The Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) was a classified US Department of Defense programme established in 2009 by the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) in coordination with the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.<sup>1</sup> Its creation was enabled by funding appropriated under the Defence Appropriations Acts of Fiscal Years 2008 and 2010, which directed a total of $22 million to the DIA to assess long-term and over-the-horizon foreign advanced aerospace threats, at the direction of Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid).<sup>2</sup> The primary contract was awarded to [Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies), a private sector organisation with ties to aerospace research.<sup>3</sup> According to the [All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro)'s 2024 historical record report, there had been approximately a 40-year gap in US government [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) investigation programmes between the termination of [Project Blue Book](/documents/project-blue-book) in 1969 and the establishment of AAWSAP.<sup>17</sup>

The stated primary purpose of AAWSAP was to investigate potential next-generation aerospace technologies across 12 specific areas, including advanced lift, propulsion, the use of unconventional materials and controls, and signature reduction.<sup>4</sup> According to accounts attributed to [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski), a key DIA programme manager, the programme was described as small but highly sensitive, reporting directly to the director of the DIA and to Congress.<sup>5</sup> It employed outside contractors alongside a small cadre of handpicked intelligence officers tasked with managing and overseeing contractor work.<sup>6</sup> The programme enjoyed the support of DIA Director Lieutenant General [Michael D. Maples](/people/michael-d-maples) and was funded through the efforts of Senators [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens), and [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye).<sup>7</sup>

Although investigating UFO/UAP was not specifically outlined in the AAWSAP contract's statement of work, the selected private sector organisation conducted UFO research with the support of the DIA programme manager.<sup>8</sup> AAWSAP and its associated contractors also investigated an alleged hotspot of UAP and paranormal activity at a property in Utah — identified in related accounts as [Skinwalker Ranch](/places/skinwalker-ranch) — owned by the head of the private sector organisation, including reported phenomena described as "shadow figures," "creatures," "remote viewing," and "human consciousness anomalies."<sup>9</sup> The DIA itself did not seek nor specifically authorise this paranormal and UFO work by the contractor, though a DIA employee set up and managed the contract.<sup>10</sup> Numerous researchers and advisers worked with and supported both AAWSAP and the subsequent [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), including [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis), [Garry Nolan](/people/garry-nolan), [Colm Kelleher](/people/colm-kelleher), and [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow).

On 2009-06-24, Senator [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) wrote to then Deputy Secretary of Defense [William Lynn III](/people/william-lynn-iii) requesting that AAWSAP and AATIP be designated as a DoD Special Access Programme (SAP).<sup>11</sup> This request was declined by [William Lynn III](/people/william-lynn-iii) on the recommendation of then-Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence James R. Clapper Jr., who judged that such a designation was not justified.<sup>12</sup> The programme ran until 2012, when the DIA cancelled it citing lack of merit and concerns about the utility of its deliverables.<sup>13</sup> According to AARO's assessment, unlike AAWSAP, AATIP was never an official DoD programme; following AAWSAP's cancellation, the AATIP name was used informally by certain individuals within the DoD who continued researching UAP sightings from military observers as an ancillary duty, with no dedicated personnel or budget.<sup>14</sup>

Following cancellation, AAWSAP's supporters proposed to the [Department of Homeland Security](/organisations/department-of-homeland-security) that a new version of the effort be created and funded under a Special Access Programme codenamed [KONA BLUE](/matters/kona-blue), which would have restarted UAP investigations, paranormal research, and the anticipated reverse-engineering of recovered off-world spacecraft.<sup>15</sup> This proposal was ultimately unsuccessful. AARO's 2024 report further assessed that modern allegations that the US government is concealing off-world technology and beings largely originate from the same group of individuals who had ties to the cancelled AAWSAP/AATIP programme.<sup>16</sup> The public profile of AAWSAP was complicated by the [2017 NYT Pentagon UAP Article](/documents/2017-nyt-pentagon-uap-article) and subsequent reporting, with [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) later stating that those articles did not adequately explain the distinction between AAWSAP and AATIP, which he believed caused confusion for years afterwards.<sup>18</sup> Questions also arose regarding the precise roles played by individuals such as [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) within the programme: AAWSAP contracted work began in 2008 at the DIA, while Elizondo's alleged leadership of the effort has been dated to 2010 at the Office of Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSD(I)), after the programme was transferred.<sup>20</sup> [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) has stated that, as an AAWSAP/AATIP contractor and senior adviser, he continued to attend meetings, provide briefings, and gain access to videos under Elizondo's leadership during this later period.<sup>19</sup>
