Its involvement with [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) dates to at least 1952, when CIA Director Walter Bedell Smith was privately advised that UAP sightings raised serious national security concerns and were "of such nature that they are not attributable to natural phenomenon or known types of aerial vehicles."<sup>1</sup> That same year, on 2 October 1952, the CIA's H. Marshall Chadwell sent a memo to the CIA Director titled "Flying Saucers."<sup>2</sup> The following January, the CIA Office of Scientific Intelligence convened the [Robertson Panel](/matters/robertson-panel) -- the Scientific Advisory Panel on Unidentified Flying Objects -- which sat from 14 to 18 January 1953.<sup>3</sup> The panel advised the Air Force to enlist the Walt Disney Company and mass media in a campaign to debunk UAP, citing concerns that US air defence communications might be overwhelmed by UAP reports.<sup>4</sup> According to former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense Christopher Mellon, the climate of institutional hostility toward the UAP issue that persisted for decades was a direct result of the government policies formulated by the Robertson Panel.<sup>5</sup>

In the years following the Robertson Panel, the CIA's relationship with the UAP subject was characterised by prominent observers as one of active concealment. Harvard astronomer Donald Menzel, who publicly presented himself as a UFO sceptic, held a clandestine association with the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa), CIA, and Navy, holding a top-secret-ultra clearance that was revealed only after his death.<sup>6</sup> The National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP), founded in 1956, attracted support from former military and intelligence officials including former CIA Director Vice Admiral [Roscoe Hillenkoetter](/people/roscoe-hillenkoetter), who had himself reportedly stated -- along with other intelligence officers -- that the US government knew UFOs were extraterrestrial and was withholding this fact from the public.<sup>7,8</sup> According to [Daniel Sheehan](/people/daniel-sheehan), before [Jimmy Carter](/people/jimmy-carter)'s January 1977 inauguration, Carter sought a meeting with CIA Director George Herbert Walker Bush to receive a briefing on UFOs, but Bush refused, stating that Carter had no need to know.<sup>9</sup> Major Florenze Mansmann confirmed in 1982 that strangers present at the 1964 Vandenberg UAP incident were CIA agents.<sup>39</sup> [John Callahan](/people/john-callahan) stated that following the [JAL Flight 1628 UAP Encounter](/events/jal-flight-1628-uap-encounter), CIA agents confiscated all radar evidence at a meeting held at the Federal Aviation Administration's Washington office,<sup>13</sup> and that an order was subsequently made to deny that the meeting had ever taken place.<sup>14</sup> The [US Air Force](/organisations/us-air-force), [FBI](/organisations/fbi), and CIA were all characterised as maintaining a policy of denial towards UFOs for decades.<sup>35</sup>

Upon entering the White House in 1993, President Bill Clinton ordered CIA Director [James Woolsey](/people/james-woolsey) to release more files on the UAP subject after lobbying from Laurance Rockefeller.<sup>10</sup> That same year, [Steven Greer](/people/steven-greer) claims to have held a covert briefing on UFOs with Woolsey under the guise of a dinner party.<sup>11</sup> Separately, John Petersen visited Woolsey at the CIA and raised the existence of heavily redacted documents that the UFO community believed related to government knowledge of alien material; Woolsey offered to investigate whether the redacted portions concerned UAPs.<sup>12</sup>

The CIA was also the originating patron of the [Stargate Program](/matters/stargate-program), the US government's remote-viewing research effort. [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) was a [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university) researcher and employee of the NSA when he and his colleague [Russell Targ](/people/russell-targ) were approached by the CIA,<sup>17</sup> which informed them that the Soviet Union had a [Remote Viewing](/concepts/remote-viewing) programme and that the United States needed to develop one in response.<sup>16</sup> The Stargate Programme was embedded in the federal government, run for years by the CIA and later the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia).<sup>15</sup> It became sufficiently successful that Puthoff reported directly to the director of the CIA and the White House on a regular basis.<sup>18</sup> The programme was ultimately defunded and shut down; according to [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), most of those informed believed it was terminated because it was too effective, not for budgetary reasons.<sup>19</sup> Witnesses to the [Rendlesham Witness Drugging and Hypnosis Event](/events/rendlesham-witness-drugging-and-hypnosis-event) told Elizondo that a CIA officer was present in the room alongside [Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI)](/organisations/air-force-office-of-special-investigations-afosi) personnel on the day after the 1980 Rendlesham Forest incident.<sup>20</sup> The CIA also maintained what Elizondo describes as a "weird desk," presided over by a physician named William Livingston, which was responsible for investigating unusual medical issues, implants, and abductions connected to UAP encounters.<sup>21</sup>

The CIA has been represented within the principal UAP advocacy and research organisations by a number of former senior officers. [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) joined the CIA in 1982 and retired in 2007 after a twenty-five-year career,<sup>22</sup> at which time he was a member of the CIA's Senior Intelligence Service.<sup>23</sup> He subsequently became one of two co-founders of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) (TTSA),<sup>24</sup> having served multiple overseas and domestic tours and held senior management positions at CIA headquarters. Puthoff, who had led CIA and Defence Department research into remote viewing, became the third co-founder of TTSA.<sup>25</sup> [Norm Kahn](/people/norm-kahn), a 33-year CIA veteran decorated with the CIA's Intelligence Medal, served on the TTSA advisory board.<sup>26</sup> [Devon Woods](/people/devon-woods) had an illustrious career with the CIA before joining the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, where he became Elizondo's unofficial mentor;<sup>36</sup> within 30 to 45 days of Woods's subsequent reassignment to the DIA, the atmosphere at that agency changed regarding the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) and Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) work, with Woods bringing in CIA colleagues and leadership beginning to question the programme.<sup>37</sup> Elizondo himself led classified investigations with partners including the FBI, CIA, and the Department of Homeland Security.<sup>27</sup>

CIA participation in more recent UAP evaluation is documented in accounts of inter-agency UAP assessments. The NSA and CIA offered to provide assets for OPLAN Interloper, with weekly meetings launched in Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) spaces at the Pentagon and other agencies.<sup>28</sup> Elizondo held a meeting with senior Navy officials, CIA representatives, and an NSA representative at which the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) was screened;<sup>29</sup> no one present believed the object was of human manufacture,<sup>30</sup> and a CIA representative confirmed that the aura visible around the object was not a camera artefact or lens flare.<sup>31</sup> Elizondo also recounts that several senior officials informed him that a colleague who had worked at the CIA some decades earlier was given an official report and autopsy of a dissected nonhuman body recovered from an unspecified crashed UAP,<sup>32</sup> a claim touching on the [Biological Automaton Hypothesis](/concepts/biological-automaton-hypothesis) and [UAP Alien Implants](/matters/uap-alien-implants). Former CIA Director [John Brennan](/people/john-brennan) stated publicly that he had seen UAP videos he found eyebrow-raising and that some phenomena could involve a different form of life.<sup>33</sup> Christopher Mellon has observed that the CIA, alongside the NSA, the FBI, and other agencies, appeared to hold important UAP data that was not being shared with other parts of the government.<sup>34</sup>
