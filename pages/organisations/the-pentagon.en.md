The Pentagon is the headquarters of the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod), constructed by the US Department of War at the beginning of World War II.<sup>1</sup> It has a total floor area of approximately 6.5 million square feet<sup>2</sup> and employs approximately 22,000 people.<sup>2</sup> Its eleven corridors are arranged like the spokes of a wheel, allowing any location within the building to be reached on foot in five minutes.<sup>3</sup> In the context of [unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP)](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) research, the building has served as the administrative centre for the US government's principal classified UAP investigation programmes, and its internal politics have significantly shaped the history of those programmes.

The [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program) began as part of the Defense Intelligence Agency and was funded through the Pentagon budget for classified programmes.<sup>4</sup> Approximately $22 million (US) was spent on the programme, an amount described as almost impossible to locate within the DoD budget.<sup>5,6</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) ran the programme from office 3C503A — third floor, C Ring, fifth corridor, alpha suite — within the building,<sup>7,8</sup> and a 2009 Pentagon briefing summary prepared by the programme's director at the time asserted that the United States was incapable of defending itself against some of the technologies the programme had discovered.<sup>9</sup> Backers of the programme state that the DoD ended its formal funding in 2012,<sup>10</sup> with a Pentagon spokesman stating at the time that other higher-priority issues merited funding.<sup>11</sup> A separate 2009 Pentagon review of the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program had already determined that its reports were of limited value, recommending the project be passed to another agency.<sup>12</sup>

[Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) wrote to the US Secretary of Defense in 2009-06 requesting that AATIP be given Special Access Program status, citing substantial progress and unconventional aerospace-related findings.<sup>13</sup> The Pentagon rejected that request.<sup>14</sup> Within the building, [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) visited and met with Elizondo and other AATIP team members in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF),<sup>15</sup> and the National Security Agency and the CIA offered assets for a planned operation known as OPLAN Interloper, with weekly meetings held in SCIF spaces at the Pentagon and other agencies.<sup>16</sup> OPLAN Interloper was ultimately rejected, with Elizondo attributing this to Pentagon leadership's reluctance to be associated with the historical stigma surrounding [UFOs](/topics/ufo).<sup>17</sup> Elizondo, [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton), and [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) concluded that the only way to change the Pentagon's handling of UAP was to compel congressional action by involving the press.<sup>18</sup>

Elizondo resigned from the Pentagon in 2017-10, characterising his departure as a protest against excessive secrecy and internal opposition to investigating UAP.<sup>19</sup> Prior to leaving, he arranged for the Pentagon to release videos taken from cockpit cameras of F-18 fighter jets.<sup>20</sup> These included the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video), the [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video), and the [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video). Following the public disclosure of AATIP in December 2017 by [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times), the [Pentagon Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) Denial Campaign](/matters/pentagon-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-denial-campaign) began: Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) publicly denied that Elizondo had any involvement in AATIP,<sup>21</sup> despite [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) having told the Pentagon's public affairs office that such a claim would be wrong and untrue — a warning the Pentagon disregarded.<sup>22</sup> The [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) was identified as a source of internal resistance to Elizondo's work. Mellon had served as US Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security and Information Operations, described as the third-highest intelligence position at the Pentagon, and returned as an unpaid consultant to the Office of Naval Intelligence in 2017 before meeting Elizondo.<sup>27</sup>

The Pentagon's engagement with the UAP subject predates the AATIP era. In 1997-04, [Steven Greer](/people/steven-greer), Edgar Mitchell, and companions were escorted into the Pentagon to meet with Admiral Thomas Wilson, Deputy Director of Intelligence for the Joint Chiefs of Staff.<sup>28</sup> That meeting took place at 10:00 on 1997-04-10 and included a small group of attendees.<sup>28</sup> Separately, in 1993-07, [Nat Kobitz](/people/nat-kobitz) confirmed that a Pentagon meeting with [Gordon Novel](/people/gordon-novel) and his son [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) took place, an event now recorded as the [Novel Kobitz Pentagon Meeting 1993](/events/novel-kobitz-pentagon-meeting-1993).<sup>29</sup> A 2009 request by Reid — the [2009 Harry Reid SAP Request](/events/2009-harry-reid-sap-request) — and the [1997 Pentagon UAP Briefing](/events/1997-pentagon-uap-briefing) are among the documented interactions between senior officials and the UAP subject within the building's history.

On 2020-04-27, the US Department of Defense officially released three previously classified UAP videos taken by US Navy pilots in 2004 and 2014-2015, acknowledging that the aerial phenomena depicted remained characterised as unidentified.<sup>23</sup> The Pentagon states that the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video), which records a 2014 or 2015 encounter by a Navy fighter jet from the USS Theodore Roosevelt, is real and officially unexplained.<sup>30</sup> In 2022-05, Pentagon officials Ronald S. Moultrie and Scott W. Bray publicly acknowledged that UAP posed potential flight safety and general security risks, and stated that the Pentagon's UAP team was investigating 400 unresolved UAP incidents recorded since the preliminary UAP report issued in mid-2021.<sup>24</sup> In 2023, the Pentagon released a further public report on UAP activity documenting more than 300 UAP events occurring since 2021.<sup>25</sup> Section 1673 of the FY2023 National Defense Authorization Act, headed "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Reporting Procedures", created a secure method allowing current or former government employees or contractors to submit UAP-related information to the Pentagon UAP office and to key congressional committees.<sup>26</sup>
