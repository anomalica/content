The Pentagon is the headquarters of the [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) of the United States. Constructed by the US Department of War at the beginning of World War II,<sup>1</sup> the building comprises approximately 6.5 million square feet of floor space and houses approximately 22,000 personnel.<sup>2</sup> Its eleven corridors are arranged like the spokes of a wheel, such that any point within the building can be reached on foot in around five minutes.<sup>3</sup> In the context of [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) (UAP) research, the Pentagon has been the site of numerous investigations, briefings, and institutional disputes relating to the UAP subject over several decades.

Early UAP-related activity at the Pentagon included a secret UFO Working Group initiated by a former US Army special forces and military intelligence Colonel, John Alexander, who had previously worked on a remote viewing programme in the 1970s and 1980s.<sup>4</sup> A notable briefing occurred on 1997-04-10, when [Steven Greer](/people/steven-greer) and former NASA astronaut Edgar Mitchell were escorted into the Pentagon to meet with Admiral Thomas Wilson, then Deputy Director of Intelligence for the Joint Chiefs of Staff.<sup>5, 6</sup> Separately, [Nat Kobitz](/people/nat-kobitz) confirmed that a meeting with [Gordon Novel](/people/gordon-novel) and his son took place at the Pentagon in July 1993.<sup>7</sup>

The [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) represented the Pentagon's most extensively documented modern UAP investigation effort. [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) joined AATIP at the Pentagon in late 2008,<sup>8</sup> working from office 3C503A on the third floor.<sup>10</sup> The programme was part of a $22 million effort buried within the Pentagon's bureaucratic structure.<sup>9</sup> In June 2009, Senator [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) wrote to the Secretary of Defence requesting that AATIP be granted Special Access Program status, citing significant findings; the Pentagon rejected this request.<sup>11, 12</sup> Much of the programme's funding was directed to Robert Bigelow's company, Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies.<sup>13</sup> When Congressional funding for the associated Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) lapsed in 2012, [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) maintained that the Pentagon continued its UAP investigations in a covert capacity.<sup>14</sup> [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) visited the Pentagon during this period and met with the AATIP team in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF).<sup>15</sup> Pentagon leadership's reluctance to engage formally with the UAP subject was reflected in the rejection of a proposed operational plan known as OPLAN Interloper, which Elizondo attributed to an unwillingness to be associated with the historical stigma surrounding UFOs.<sup>16</sup>

[Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) resigned from the Pentagon on 2017-10-04, citing internal obstruction of his UAP investigative efforts.<sup>17</sup> Before departing, he obtained declassification of three UAP videos.<sup>18</sup> In December 2017, [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) reported the existence of the Pentagon's secret UAP investigation unit, marking the programme's first public disclosure.<sup>19</sup> Following the publication of the story, Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) publicly denied that Elizondo had any involvement with AATIP.<sup>20</sup> [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) disputed this characterisation in a reply to the Pentagon's public affairs office, stating it would be wrong and untrue; the Pentagon nonetheless maintained the denial.<sup>21</sup> [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), a former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence who had served in the [Office of Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSD(I))](/organisations/office-of-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), worked alongside Elizondo to bring UAP concerns to the attention of Congress and the public, with the stated aim of compelling the Department of Defense to treat the subject seriously.<sup>21</sup>

In April 2020, the DoD made an official public acknowledgement that videos filmed by US Navy pilots depicted unidentified objects.<sup>22</sup> On 2020-04-27, the Pentagon formally released three UAP videos — including the [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video), the [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video), and the [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video) — stating that the aerial phenomena depicted remained unidentified.<sup>23</sup> The [UAP Task Force](/organisations/uap-task-force) was established in August 2020 as the successor body to AATIP within the Pentagon.<sup>24</sup> In May 2022, senior Pentagon officials Ronald S. Moultrie and Scott W. Bray publicly acknowledged that UAPs represented potential flight safety and national security risks, and confirmed that 400 unresolved UAP incidents were under investigation.<sup>25</sup> In 2023, the Pentagon published a further UAP report documenting over 300 UAP events recorded since 2021 alone, covering only unclassified incidents.<sup>26</sup> Legislation enacted as Section 1673 of the FY2023 National Defense Authorisation Act (NDAA) created a secure reporting mechanism allowing current and former government employees and contractors to submit UAP-related information to the Pentagon UAP office and key congressional committees.<sup>27</sup> The [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video), recorded during a 2014 or 2015 encounter by a US Navy fighter jet operating from the USS Theodore Roosevelt, was confirmed by the Pentagon to be genuine and officially unexplained.<sup>28</sup>
