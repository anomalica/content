The F/A-18 is a carrier-based fighter jet operated by the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn), flown by naval aviators involved in several reported encounters with Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP). Each aircraft carries a pilot, referred to informally as the "front seater", and a Weapon Systems Officer (WSO), known as the "back seater".<sup>7</sup>

The type is most closely associated with the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter). [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) commanded the F/A-18 squadron aboard the [USS Nimitz (CVN-68)](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68) at the time,<sup>4</sup> and [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) piloted the second F/A-18 Super Hornet involved in the intercept.<sup>8</sup> According to Christopher K. Mellon, the [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) contacted two already-airborne F/A-18s to intercept the unidentified object.<sup>18</sup> Two US Navy F/A-18s from the Nimitz intercepted one of the objects at close quarters in conditions of perfect visibility.<sup>16</sup> Fravor observed that the object, later described as Tic Tac-shaped, was about the size of his F/A-18, with no markings, no wings, and no exhaust plumes.<sup>5</sup> According to the source record, Fravor observed a wingless white craft approximately 14.6 metres long from the cockpit of his F/A-18.<sup>17</sup> Mellon said the four aviators involved observed that the object had no wings or exhaust.<sup>19</sup>

During the encounter, Fravor circled down to confront the Tic Tac-shaped object off the coast of [California San Diego](/places/california-san-diego-usa),<sup>14</sup> and the object glided over the top of his F/A-18's nose before disappearing.<sup>15</sup> Mellon said the object then reoriented itself toward the approaching F/A-18 and performed a series of discrete tumbling manoeuvres, taking a position directly behind the aircraft.<sup>20</sup> Separately, the object somehow knew the F/A-18s' intended combat air patrol meeting point despite it being sixty miles away, and reached that location within seconds of departing Fravor and [Jim Slaight](/people/jim-slaight).<sup>9</sup> Fravor said that, low on fuel, he decided to end the exercise, and both F/A-18 jets returned to the carrier.<sup>10</sup> Mellon likewise said both F/A-18s returned to the Nimitz due to safety concerns and low fuel,<sup>21</sup> and that additional F/A-18s were subsequently dispatched but achieved similarly unsuccessful results against the object.<sup>22</sup> Two F/A-18 Super Hornets conducting training exercises were also asked to go and get eyes on the wider UAP fleet spotted during the encounter.<sup>6</sup>

[Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) described a separate incident aboard the USS Roosevelt, saying that during an exercise requiring two F/A-18 Hornets to fly within approximately 30 metres of each other, a UAP flew between the aircraft's wings and disappeared, splitting their formation.<sup>11</sup> Elizondo said the object came close enough that both pilots clearly saw it was a transparent orb or sphere containing a cube, with the cube's four points touching the inner circumference of the sphere.<sup>12</sup> He also said the F/A-18 Hornets aboard the Roosevelt had recently undergone radar system upgrades, replacing some systems dating to the 1980s.<sup>13</sup>

F/A-18 squadrons have also been linked to sightings beyond 2004. [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) said his F/A-18 squadron began seeing UAP hovering over restricted airspace southeast of Virginia Virginia Beach, from 2014.<sup>3</sup> Years later, [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) arranged for the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) to release cockpit videos taken from F/A-18 fighter jets, shortly before he left the department.<sup>1</sup> Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) said the videos were released "for research purposes... and not for general public release."<sup>2</sup>
