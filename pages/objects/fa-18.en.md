The F/A-18 Hornet and Super Hornet are twin-engine, carrier-based multirole fighter aircraft operated by the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn). Each aircraft carries a pilot, referred to as the "front seater", and in two-seat variants a Weapon Systems Officer (WSO), colloquially known as "Wizzo" and referred to as the "back seater".<sup>1</sup>

The type is most closely associated with the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter). [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) commanded the F/A-18 squadron aboard the [USS Nimitz (CVN-68)](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68) at the time.<sup>2</sup> Two US Navy F/A-18 Super Hornets conducting training exercises were asked to go and get eyes on a fleet of UAP that had been spotted.<sup>3</sup> According to Christopher K. Mellon, the [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) contacted two F/A-18s already airborne to intercept the object.<sup>4</sup> [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich), then a Lieutenant Junior Grade, piloted the second F/A-18 involved in the encounter.<sup>5</sup> The two F/A-18s intercepted one of the objects at close quarters in conditions of perfect visibility.<sup>6</sup>

Fravor observed that the object was approximately the size of his F/A-18, with no markings, no wings, and no exhaust plumes.<sup>7</sup> According to Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs, the wingless white craft was approximately 14.6 metres long.<sup>8</sup> Fravor flew his F/A-18 down to confront the Tic Tac-shaped object off the coast of [California San Diego](/places/california-san-diego-usa),<sup>9</sup> which then glided over the nose of his aircraft and disappeared.<sup>10</sup> According to Christopher K. Mellon, the object then reoriented itself toward the approaching F/A-18 and performed a series of discrete tumbling manoeuvres before taking up a position directly behind the aircraft.<sup>11</sup> The Tic Tac somehow knew the intended combat air patrol meeting point of Fravor and [Jim Slaight](/people/jim-slaight), sixty miles away, and reached that location within seconds of leaving them.<sup>12</sup> Fravor said that, low on fuel, he decided to end the exercise, and both F/A-18s returned to the carrier.<sup>13</sup> According to Christopher K. Mellon, additional F/A-18s were subsequently dispatched but achieved similarly unsuccessful results against the object.<sup>14</sup>

[Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) said that during a separate exercise requiring two F/A-18 Hornets to fly within approximately 30 metres of each other, a UAP flew between the aircraft's wings and disappeared, splitting their formation.<sup>15</sup> Elizondo said the object came close enough for both pilots to see that it was a transparent sphere containing a cube, its four points touching the sphere's inner circumference.<sup>16</sup> Elizondo said the F/A-18 Hornets aboard the USS Roosevelt involved had recently undergone radar system upgrades, replacing some systems dating to the 1980s.<sup>17</sup>

[Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves)' F/A-18 squadron began seeing what he described as [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) hovering over restricted airspace southeast of Virginia Virginia Beach, from 2014.<sup>18</sup>

Elizondo arranged for the Pentagon to release videos taken from F/A-18 cockpit cameras, shortly before he resigned from the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod).<sup>19</sup> Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) said the F/A-18 cockpit videos were released "for research purposes... and not for general public release."<sup>20</sup>
