The F/A-18 Hornet and Super Hornet is a twin-engine fighter jet flown by the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn). Each aircraft carries a pilot, known as the "front seater", and, in two-seat variants, a Weapon Systems Officer (WSO) - pronounced "Wizzo" and known as the "back seater".<sup>1</sup>

During the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) commanded the F/A-18 squadron aboard the [USS Nimitz (CVN-68)](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68).<sup>2</sup> Two US Navy F/A-18 Super Hornets conducting training exercises were asked to go and get eyes on a fleet of unidentified objects that had been spotted.<sup>3</sup> Lieutenant Junior Grade [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) piloted the other F/A-18 involved.<sup>4</sup> Fravor flew down and circled to confront a Tic Tac-shaped object off the coast of [California San Diego](/places/california-san-diego-usa), in November 2004.<sup>5</sup> According to Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs, Fravor observed from the cockpit a wingless white craft approximately 14.6 metres long.<sup>6</sup> The object was about the size of Fravor's F/A-18, with no markings, no wings, and no exhaust plumes.<sup>7</sup> It then glided over the nose of Fravor's aircraft and disappeared.<sup>8</sup> The object subsequently reappeared at the two jets' intended combat air patrol point, around sixty miles away, within seconds of leaving Fravor and [Jim Slaight](/people/jim-slaight).<sup>9</sup> Fravor said that, low on fuel, he ended the exercise and both F/A-18s returned to the carrier.<sup>10</sup> The two Nimitz F/A-18s had intercepted the object at close quarters in conditions of perfect visibility.<sup>11</sup>

A separate account of the encounter, given by Christopher K. Mellon, describes the [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) contacting two F/A-18s that were already airborne to intercept the object.<sup>12</sup> Mellon said the four aviators involved observed that the object had no wings or exhaust.<sup>13</sup> He said the object suddenly reoriented itself toward the approaching F/A-18 and performed a series of discrete tumbling manoeuvres before taking up a position directly behind the aircraft.<sup>14</sup> Mellon said both F/A-18s returned to the USS Nimitz owing to safety concerns and low fuel.<sup>15</sup> Additional F/A-18s were dispatched but, according to Mellon, achieved similarly unsuccessful results against the object.<sup>16</sup>

In a separate incident, [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) said that, during an exercise requiring two F/A-18 Hornets to fly within approximately 30 metres of each other, a UAP flew between the aircraft's wings and disappeared, splitting their formation.<sup>17</sup> He said both pilots saw the object clearly enough to identify it as a transparent orb or sphere containing a cube, its four points touching the inner circumference of the sphere.<sup>18</sup> Elizondo said the F/A-18 Hornets involved, aboard the USS Roosevelt, had recently undergone radar system upgrades, with some of the previous systems dating to the 1980s.<sup>19</sup>

Separately, [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves)' F/A-18 squadron began seeing [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) hovering over restricted airspace southeast of Virginia Virginia Beach, from 2014.<sup>20</sup> F/A-18 cockpit-camera footage entered the public record after Elizondo arranged for the Pentagon to release videos taken from cockpit cameras of F/A-18 fighter jets, shortly before he quit the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod).<sup>21</sup> Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) said the videos were released "for research purposes... and not for general public release."<sup>22</sup>
