USS Nimitz (CVN-68) is a nuclear-powered aircraft carrier of the United States Navy and the lead vessel of the [Nimitz Carrier Strike Group](/organisations/nimitz-carrier-strike-group).<sup>1</sup> In November 2004, the carrier served as the primary platform for the series of events now widely referred to as the [2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter), making it central to one of the most extensively documented UAP cases in US military history. The carrier's strike group for that deployment comprised [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) (CG-59), USS Chafee (DDG-90), [USS Higgins](/objects/uss-higgins) (DDG-76), and [USS Louisville](/objects/uss-louisville) (SSN-724).<sup>2</sup>

[Carrier Air Wing 11](/organisations/carrier-air-wing-11) (CVW-11), embarked aboard USS Nimitz at the time, included [Marine Fighter Attack Squadron 232 (VMFA-232)](/organisations/marine-fighter-attack-squadron-232-vmfa-232) flying F/A-18C aircraft, [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41) flying F/A-18F aircraft, [Electronic Attack Squadron 135 (VAQ-135)](/organisations/electronic-attack-squadron-135-vaq-135) flying EA-6B aircraft, [Carrier Airborne Early Warning Squadron 117 (VAW-117)](/organisations/carrier-airborne-early-warning-squadron-117-vaw-117) flying [E-2C Hawkeye](/objects/e-2c-hawkeye) aircraft, and [Fleet Logistics Support Squadron 30 (VRC-30) Det 3](/organisations/fleet-logistics-support-squadron-30-vrc-30-det-3) flying C-2A aircraft, among other units.<sup>3</sup> In November 2004, the strike group was at the beginning of a workup cycle in preparation for a combat deployment to the Persian Gulf to support ground forces in Iraq.<sup>4</sup>

On 2004-11-14, the USS Nimitz was conducting training exercises off the Southern Californian coastline ahead of its planned deployment.<sup>5</sup> The carrier strike group was operating approximately 160 kilometres southwest of San Diego.<sup>6</sup> [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41) was attached to [Carrier Air Wing 11](/organisations/carrier-air-wing-11) and stationed aboard the Nimitz at that time, with [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) serving as the squadron's commanding officer.<sup>7</sup> The planned exercise involved launching aircraft from the Nimitz to simulate high-fidelity air defence training scenarios.<sup>8</sup> When radar operator [Kevin Day](/people/kevin-day) aboard [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) again detected a cluster of unidentified objects and confirmed that the USS Nimitz's own radar was observing the same contacts, he recommended to Captain [James Smith](/people/james-smith) of the Nimitz that aircraft be dispatched to investigate.<sup>9,10</sup> An [E-2C Hawkeye](/objects/e-2c-hawkeye) launched from the Nimitz also detected the closest UAP on its radar, providing further corroboration.<sup>11</sup>

Following the captain's concurrence, Fravor's flight launched from the Nimitz and, after checking in with the air controller aboard [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton), was redirected from training to real-world tasking associated with the unidentified contacts.<sup>12</sup> Radar tracking of the [Tic Tac Sighting](/events/tic-tac-sighting) was achieved by [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton), the USS Nimitz, and the [E-2C Hawkeye](/objects/e-2c-hawkeye), though the F/A-18 fire control radars did not acquire the object.<sup>13</sup> Upon returning to the Nimitz, Fravor described what he had witnessed to a crew preparing to launch; that crew subsequently obtained the approximately 90-second forward-looking infrared (FLIR) video of the Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV) that was later released by the US government in 2017.<sup>14</sup> Shortly after the incident, two unidentified individuals arrived by naval helicopter aboard the Nimitz, and Petty Officer Patrick Hughes was ordered to hand over data bricks from the [E-2C Hawkeye](/objects/e-2c-hawkeye) to two individuals described as air force personnel.<sup>15</sup>

In the immediate aftermath of the encounter, very little formal follow-up was conducted once pilots returned to the Nimitz.<sup>16</sup> Fravor later testified that neither the ship's captain, the admiral, nor any other commanding officers spoke to him about the incident following his return.<sup>16</sup> Nevertheless, the video footage and associated radar data circulated among crewmen aboard the Nimitz and [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) via the government's classified email system.<sup>17</sup> The [2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter) was later documented in the [Navy F/A-18F Nimitz Encounter Video](/documents/navy-fa-18f-nimitz-encounter-video) and the [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video), and studied by officials associated with the Pentagon's UAP programme, who described footage of a whitish oval object chased by two Navy F/A-18F Super Hornets from the carrier off the coast of San Diego.<sup>18</sup> The [2017 NYT Pentagon UAP Article](/documents/2017-nyt-pentagon-uap-article) identified the 2004 Nimitz encounter as a key case, and the encounter was subsequently the subject of the [2004 Nimitz UAP Incident](/matters/2004-nimitz-uap-incident) and Fravor's [Fravor House Oversight Committee Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) Testimony, 2023](/events/2023-fravor-house-oversight-committee-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-testimony). The 2004 encounter was documented by radar, by camera, and by four naval aviators.<sup>19</sup>

Luis Elizondo, former head of the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP), later assessed that the UAP incidents involving [USS Theodore Roosevelt](/objects/uss-theodore-roosevelt) bore all the hallmarks of the Nimitz and Princeton case from 2004, noting that both the USS Theodore Roosevelt and the USS Nimitz were nuclear-powered vessels.<sup>20</sup> The Nimitz encounter has remained a reference point in UAP research and congressional oversight, with [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) publishing associated video footage in December 2017 and Christopher Mellon publicly stating at [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science)'s launch that the US government could verify the events involving the USS Nimitz on 2004-11-14.
