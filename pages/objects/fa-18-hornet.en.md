The F/A-18 Hornet is a fighter-attack aircraft; its designation, "F/A", stands for fighter attack.<sup>1</sup> [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves), a former [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) pilot, flew the F/A-18 Hornet during a decade of service that included two deployments, in Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Inherent Resolve.<sup>2</sup>

[David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) moved to the F/A-18 Hornet after flying the A-6 Intruder, a bomber, and had to learn dogfighting for the first time as part of the Hornet's fighter-attack mission, having lacked significant air-to-air combat experience beforehand.<sup>3</sup> Fravor said that the A-6 Intruder could travel considerably further on its fuel than the F/A-18 Hornet.<sup>4</sup> Legacy F/A-18 Hornet aircraft carry less fuel than the [F/A-18F Super Hornet](/objects/fa-18f-super-hornet).<sup>5</sup> Fravor continued to fly single-seat F/A-18 Hornets until the end of 2001.<sup>6</sup>

During the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) on 2004-11-14, Fravor's F/A-18 Hornet fire-control radar never picked up the object later referred to as the Tic Tac.<sup>7</sup> His wingman's aircraft radar briefly locked onto the object before it jammed the radar and the lock was lost, prompting the pilot to switch to the targeting pod.<sup>8</sup> The object took evasive manoeuvres to maintain distance from Fravor's closing F/A-18F Super Hornet, showing no difficulty keeping pace with the aircraft.<sup>9</sup> Fravor's aircraft was not armed during the encounter, and he never felt threatened.<sup>10</sup> He estimated the object's length at about 12 metres, drawing on 16 years of experience flying against other aircraft and comparing its size to that of an F/A-18 Hornet.<sup>11</sup> Other witnesses who observed the object returned a consistent estimate that it was about the size of an F/A-18 Hornet.<sup>12</sup>

The F/A-18 Hornet can be fitted with an Advanced Targeting Forward Looking Infrared Radar (ATFLIR) camera, which detects the heat signatures of a combatant aircraft.<sup>13</sup>
