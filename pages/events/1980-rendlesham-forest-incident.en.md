In December 1980, an American military patrol near RAF Woodbridge, a base in Suffolk, England, saw lights descending into the adjacent [Rendlesham Forest](/places/england-rendlesham-forest-uk).<sup>1</sup> Security policemen [John Burroughs](/people/john-burroughs) and [Jim Penniston](/people/jim-penniston) found a landed unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) in the forest, near a joint UK-US military facility where the two allies had stored nuclear weapons in a secret bunker.<sup>2</sup> The object was reported to have hovered over that underground bunker.<sup>3</sup> Raymond E. Fowler said that a document he obtained describes the object as triangular in shape, landing at the back gate of RAF Woodbridge, then a joint [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf)/Royal Air Force base.<sup>4</sup> According to the book In Plain Sight, Penniston and Burroughs said they saw the craft land on 1980-12-26.<sup>5</sup>

Penniston described a small craft roughly three metres wide, composed of what looked like a black, smoky glass-like material, with unfamiliar symbols inscribed on its upper side.<sup>6</sup> According to Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs, witnesses said the symbols resembled those reported at other UAP encounters.<sup>7</sup> Burroughs saw flashing red, green and blue lights in a clearing that distorted the image of a craft, though he said he never saw anything metallic or hard.<sup>8</sup> Penniston later said he touched the craft and made sketches of the inscriptions in a notebook.<sup>9</sup> One such sketch survives.<sup>10</sup> Fowler said investigators found depressions in the ground where three landing gear had rested the object,<sup>11</sup> and that they also found radiation at the landing site.<sup>12</sup> Nick Pope said that radiation levels tested the day after the incident were many times higher than normal background, a finding challenged by sceptics.<sup>13</sup> Fowler's document also describes armed security police advancing toward the object before it took off into the woods, lit by blue lights underneath and a red light on top,<sup>14</sup> and records wild animals, including rabbits, running out of the woods after the object departed.<sup>15</sup>

The watches worn by Burroughs and Penniston were later found to have lost forty-five minutes compared with the timepieces of airmen who had stayed on base.<sup>16</sup> Penniston said that he and his colleagues suffered "missing time" during the encounter, with their memories deliberately confused and blocked in some way.<sup>17</sup> Luis Elizondo said that [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) calls this phenomenon of lost time associated with UAP encounters, including at Rendlesham, the "Rip Van Winkle Effect".<sup>18</sup> After the incident, Burroughs experienced worsening health effects, including vision problems, white gums, heart murmurs and heart tissue scarring, culminating in surgery to correct damaged leaflets in his mitral valve.<sup>19</sup> [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) said that United States military personnel who witnessed the event received radiation burns.<sup>20</sup> Two of the witnesses were later awarded permanent disability by the US government for their involvement and injury.<sup>21</sup> Fravor also said that one witness was medically disabled, and that [John McCain](/people/john-mccain)'s office helped him get his disability status reestablished after it had initially been denied.<sup>22</sup> McCain separately had the service records of one of the servicemen involved declassified.<sup>23</sup>

Two nights after the initial sighting, US Air Force Colonel Charles Halt, deputy commander of the nearby RAF Bentwaters base, led a patrol back into the forest following another reported landing, and later set out what he saw in a detailed memorandum.<sup>24</sup> Halt's patrol witnessed what he called an "intelligently-controlled" object moving through the forest.<sup>25</sup> He made an audiotape of his live commentary, describing the object as looking "like an eye winking at you".<sup>26</sup> He and his patrol watched the object, which "danced about in the forest", shed sparks that looked like molten metal, amid a heavy field of static electricity in the air.<sup>27</sup> The object shone beams of light into the nuclear weapons storage area on the RAF Bentwaters base.<sup>28</sup> Halt has said the nearby lighthouse was easily visible and quite distinct, at least 30 degrees away from what he saw.<sup>29</sup> He has said he believed the vehicle was probably extra-terrestrial, having watched it hover at close range.<sup>30</sup> He remains adamant that the objects he saw were intelligently controlled and probably non-human in origin.<sup>31</sup> Halt has also said he believes something was controlling what he saw, describing the experience as "a bit scary".<sup>32</sup>

Ian Ridpath and other sceptics have said that all the men saw was the nearby lighthouse, a misperception of distant lights.<sup>33</sup> The [Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI)](/organisations/air-force-office-of-special-investigations-afosi) attacked the credibility of Halt's sighting.<sup>34</sup> Halt has said he believes there was a cover-up regarding the incident.<sup>35</sup> According to the book In Plain Sight, radar operators have since testified that an unknown object was tracked in the area on the night of the incident.<sup>36</sup> The same source reports that British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher was privately quoted as saying "you can't tell the people" regarding claims of an official cover-up, a phrase later used as the title of the book You Can't Tell the People: The Definitive Account of the Rendlesham Forest Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) Mystery.<sup>37</sup> Jonathan Caplan has described the incident as an interesting UK UAP case.<sup>38</sup>
