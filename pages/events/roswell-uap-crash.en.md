The Roswell incident began on 1947-07-05, when ranch foreman Mac Brazel found crash debris scattered across a cattle ranch approximately 120 kilometres from [New Mexico Roswell](/places/new-mexico-roswell-usa).<sup>1</sup> On 1947-07-08, Colonel William Blanchard ordered Walter Haut to issue a press release announcing that the US Army had recovered a flying disc,<sup>2</sup> and the Roswell 1947 Press Release stated that the intelligence office of the 509th Bomb Group had gained possession of a disc.<sup>3</sup> The *Roswell Daily Record* ran the headline "RAAF Captures Flying Saucer on Ranch in Roswell Region" on that same date.<sup>4</sup> Within hours, however, General Ramey issued a statement asserting the recovered object was simply a weather balloon.<sup>5</sup> Intelligence officer Jesse Marcel, who examined the original debris field -- which he described as approximately 1,200 metres long and 60 to 90 metres wide, with a gouge extending up to 150 metres long<sup>6</sup> -- later claimed the genuine material had been substituted by General Ramey with weather balloon wreckage, and stated his belief that what crashed was extra-terrestrial in origin.<sup>7</sup> Three decades after the event, Marcel told researcher Stanton Friedman the same, prompting Friedman to allege a "cosmic Watergate cover-up".<sup>8</sup>

The official explanation shifted over subsequent decades. The US Air Force's Roswell Air Force 1994 Report, released on 1994-07-27,<sup>9</sup> admitted the 1947 weather balloon story was false and instead attributed the wreckage to the top-secret Project Mogul, described as an early attempt to detect Soviet nuclear tests by affixing microphones to high-altitude balloons.<sup>17</sup> A separate assessment by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) holds that the government revised its Roswell cover story at least twice in the seventy-odd years since the incident.<sup>18</sup> A 1995 review ordered by President [Bill Clinton](/people/bill-clinton)<sup>34</sup> -- the Clinton Roswell Records Review 1995 -- found that not a single document from the Roswell Army Airfield in the crash period survived; every record relating to the 1947 incident had been destroyed with no apparent authorisation.<sup>10</sup> Clinton has since stated that when he was president, every attempt was made to find out everything about Roswell, but that many mysteries remain.<sup>38</sup>

A significant body of testimony concerns recovered material and occupants. The Walter Haut Roswell Affidavit 2002, signed on 2002-12-26,<sup>15</sup> described an apparent craft in a hangar approximately 3.6 to 4.6 metres in length, about 1.8 metres high, and described as more egg-shaped,<sup>12</sup> and further testified that Haut was taken by Colonel Blanchard to view recovered child-sized bodies with abnormally large heads lying under a tarpaulin.<sup>13</sup> Researcher Don Schmitt stated that he and his co-author drafted the affidavit based on information Haut had provided over the years, and that Haut read and approved every word in the presence of his daughter, a notary, and another witness.<sup>14</sup> The Art's Parts Letters separately allege that crash debris including corpses of occupants was sent to Wright Patterson Air Force Base.<sup>32</sup> Numerous witnesses associated with the crashes reportedly told government officials they observed hieroglyphics on various pieces of the debris, but were instructed not to discuss this publicly.<sup>26</sup> Physicist [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) told Elizondo that four deceased nonhuman bodies were recovered from the crash<sup>19</sup> and that the Roswell event was real,<sup>20</sup> and Puthoff was separately reported to be in possession of material allegedly recovered from the site: an intricate piece with multiple microscopic layers of interlacing bismuth and magnesium and what appeared to be a bevelled edge.<sup>25</sup>

The Roswell EMP Crash Hypothesis represents one explanatory framework advanced within UAP research circles. Elizondo has stated the craft fell near a government test facility and broke into two crash sites.<sup>21</sup> Puthoff presented the theory to Elizondo that the crash was caused by Department of Defense experiments with Electromagnetic Pulse (EMP) energy -- a by-product of the atomic bomb -- generated at a nearby test range,<sup>23</sup> and hypothesised that if the aircraft was downed by an EMP, it would suggest a vulnerability in its propulsion, avionics, or life-support systems.<sup>24</sup> Elizondo has similarly opined that the two Roswell craft crashed because a primitive EMP device disrupted their propulsion bubble.<sup>22</sup> Elizondo also learned from Puthoff that at the dawn of the nuclear age, UAP began appearing in greater numbers and sometimes crashed, with Roswell cited as one such incident.<sup>22</sup> [Kenneth Arnold](/people/kenneth-arnold)'s UAP sighting in 1947 occurred a couple of weeks before the Roswell crashes.<sup>37</sup>

Several alternative and contested claims have circulated in the decades since 1947. Annie Jacobsen, in her 2011 history of Area 51, reported that US Signal Corps engineers tracked two anomalous objects flying across the south-western United States in July 1947,<sup>27</sup> and that the office of the Joint Chiefs of Staff took command of the recovery, retrieving the airframe, propulsion equipment, and the crashed craft's power plant.<sup>28</sup> Jacobsen also asserted that what crashed was Russian, built by captured Nazi scientists,<sup>29</sup> and that the bodies found aboard were grotesquely deformed children with unusually large heads and oversize eyes.<sup>30</sup> [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge), in his DeLonge Joe Rogan Interview, similarly described the crash as a Nazi-built Russian flying saucer -- a claim overlapping with Jacobsen's account.<sup>31</sup> [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell)'s hometown was Artesia, New Mexico, near the crash site,<sup>33</sup> and Mitchell reportedly confided to Puthoff and [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) that his family was among Roswell families threatened by the [FBI](/organisations/fbi) after the crashes, with agents visiting ranchers door-to-door to warn that speaking about the events would have fatal consequences.<sup>33</sup> A letter attributed to Whitley Strieber claimed that retired US Army Brigadier General Arthur Exon stated in 1989 that there was a "completely unequivocal cover-up" of a retrieved alien craft, running from President Truman downward.<sup>35</sup> At the Disclosure Project 2001 Press Club Hearing in May 2001, aerospace illustrator Mark McCandlish testified that the US government had successfully reverse-engineered a spacecraft retrieved from the Roswell crash site to build three alien reproduction vehicles, later referred to as the ARV Fluxliner.<sup>36</sup>

Within the broader UAP research community, the Roswell event is frequently referenced as a foundational case. Sources connected to the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) told Elizondo of several historic UAP crashes from which nonhuman bodies were recovered by the United States, with Roswell cited among them.<sup>34</sup> Anti-Gravity Propulsion has been invoked in connection with the alleged craft's mechanism. Friedman's decades of advocacy kept the case prominent in public discourse, and the event has been discussed by figures including [DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge), Elizondo, Puthoff, Davis, and Mitchell in the context of more recent UAP disclosures. The Roswell incident continues to occupy a central position in UAP literature, with competing explanations -- ranging from a classified balloon programme to a non-human craft -- remaining unresolved in any publicly accessible official record.
