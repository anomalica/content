In December 1965, something crashed near the town of Pennsylvania Kecksburg, prompting a large military response.<sup>1</sup> The incident has since become one of the most cited crash-retrieval cases in American UFO history, though the identity of the object involved has never been officially settled beyond the conclusion reached by the US Air Force's investigative body at the time.

Witnesses at the scene described the object as acorn-shaped, roughly 2.74 to 3.66 metres in diameter.<sup>2</sup> Among those who reported seeing it up close were Jim Romansky and Bill Bulebush, who said it carried a gold band around its base bearing markings that resembled Egyptian hieroglyphics.<sup>3</sup> According to "In Plain Sight", this claim was made by the two witnesses but its origin was not independently verified.

The recovery of the object was, according to multiple witnesses cited in "In Plain Sight", a major military operation carried out under extremely tight secrecy.<sup>4</sup> This account of a large-scale, tightly controlled retrieval effort is a recurring feature of descriptions of the incident, contributing to the case's prominence in later discussions of alleged crash retrievals.

[Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book), the US Air Force programme responsible for investigating UFO reports through the 1950s and 1960s, officially attributed the crashed object to a meteorite, according to "In Plain Sight".<sup>5</sup> That conclusion has not resolved public debate over the case; it has been suggested that the object was instead a satellite belonging to either the [Soviet Union](/organisations/soviet-union) or the United States, though according to "In Plain Sight" what actually crashed at Kecksburg remains an open question.<sup>6</sup>

The case resurfaced in efforts to obtain official records decades after the event. In 2002, shortly after leaving the Clinton White House, [John Podesta](/people/john-podesta) supported a lawsuit seeking the release of government records concerning the 1965 incident.<sup>7</sup> Separately, [Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean) won a court case against the [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) over the release of documents relating to the Kecksburg incident, as recounted in "Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs".<sup>8</sup> These legal actions reflect the case's continued status as a focal point for those seeking greater transparency around historical UFO-related government records, even as the underlying question of what crashed near Kecksburg in December 1965 remains unresolved.
