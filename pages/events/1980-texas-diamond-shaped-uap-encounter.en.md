On the evening of 29 December 1980, a UAP encounter took place near Texas Dayton, USA.<sup>1</sup> The case is notable within UAP literature for the physical after-effects reported by those involved, and for the legal action one of the witnesses subsequently brought against the US government. The event is documented in *In Plain Sight* (2021) and, in greater detail, in *Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs* (2024).

According to these accounts, two women and a boy were driving along a road in Texas when they observed a diamond-shaped object descend from the sky and come to a hover directly over a tree near the roadside.<sup>2</sup> As the object hung in place, the two women reported sensing a massive amount of heat radiating from it, a sensation strong enough that they later described it as a defining feature of the encounter.<sup>3</sup> No further description of the object's behaviour or eventual departure is recorded in the available sources; the accounts move directly from the sighting itself to its aftermath.

In the period following the encounter, the two women developed a cluster of physical symptoms. These included headaches, severe skin burns, nausea, diarrhoea, eye damage, lesions, exhaustion, hair loss, and shedding fingernails.<sup>4</sup> The boy, who had remained inside the car throughout the sighting rather than being directly exposed alongside the two women, nonetheless went on to develop eye problems of his own, later requiring glasses to manage schoolwork.<sup>5</sup> The distribution of symptoms — more severe and more varied among the two women than the boy — has been noted in the retelling of the case as consistent with differing degrees of exposure to whatever the witnesses encountered, though the sources themselves stop short of explaining the mechanism.

The health effects reported did not end with the immediate aftermath. In the longer term, one of the two women went on to develop severe cataracts, while the other developed breast cancer.<sup>6</sup> These outcomes are presented in the sources as continuations of the same injuries first noted in the days following the encounter, rather than as unrelated later developments, though no further clinical detail — diagnosis dates, treatment, or medical assessment of causation — is given in the material available.

The case also has a legal dimension. In December 1980, the person in Dayton, Texas who had developed cancer following the encounter brought a lawsuit against the United States government.<sup>7</sup> The claim was unsuccessful: it was rejected at first instance, and the rejection was upheld on appeal to a higher court.<sup>7</sup> No further detail is recorded regarding the specific grounds of the suit, the government's response, or the reasoning behind the appellate court's decision.

Taken together, the available material describes the 1980 Texas diamond-shaped UAP encounter as a single, geographically localised incident near Texas Dayton involving three witnesses, a sighting of short but intense character, a subsequent pattern of acute and then chronic health effects among those involved, and an attempt — ultimately unsuccessful — to seek legal redress from the US government. It remains one of the relatively few UAP cases in which claimed physical harm to witnesses was carried through to formal litigation.
