The Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) was a classified US government programme administered by the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) to investigate unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) and related topics. Select members of Congress initiated and funded AAWSAP under the direction of the DIA, representing the Department of Defense's return to formal UAP investigation.<sup>1</sup> The [DIA](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) issued an invitation to tender in August 2008; [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) was the sole bidder and secured US$10 million in funding for the first year, with a five-year option.<sup>2</sup> In total, the programme drew on approximately US$22 million in [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) funds earmarked by Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid).<sup>3</sup> The original funding was programmed to run from 2008 to 2012.<sup>4</sup>

The prime contract was awarded to [Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies) (BAASS), owned by [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow).<sup>5</sup> [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) served as the programme's chief scientist.<sup>6</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), after joining the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSD(I)), was tasked to join AAWSAP and subsequently led the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), a closely related effort.<sup>7</sup> Shortly after accepting his role, Elizondo attended the [Roslyn Virginia Hotel Dinner](/events/roslyn-virginia-hotel-dinner), a gathering at which the programme's leadership and Nevada-based contractors met for the first time.<sup>8</sup> At that juncture, AAWSAP was not sharing UAP data with other nations, owing to national security concerns.<sup>9</sup> [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) commissioned 38 [Defence Intelligence Research Documents](/documents/defence-intelligence-research-documents) (DIRDs) — academic theoretical papers from leading scientists covering topics such as traversable wormholes, antigravity, invisibility cloaking, and warp drives.<sup>10</sup> These documents were framed as applicable to any conceivable weapon system in order to obscure the programme's true focus on UAP.<sup>11</sup>

AAWSAP's remit extended beyond conventional UAP analysis to include an investigation of [Skinwalker Ranch](/places/skinwalker-ranch), a property in Utah previously purchased by [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) in the mid-1990s to study unusual occurrences associated with the site.<sup>12, 13</sup> From the programme's inception in 2008, teams of researchers visited the ranch to collect data on anomalous activity, including UAP sightings.<sup>14</sup> The programme's manager, [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski), maintained that the paranormal and UAP investigations were interrelated and resisted internal pressure to narrow AAWSAP's scope to UAP threats alone.<sup>23, 24</sup>

A significant strand of AAWSAP's work concerned [UAP biological effects](/matters/uap-biological-effects). Elizondo reviewed bio-effects reports documenting medical consequences experienced by individuals who had encountered UAP technology.<sup>16</sup> A physician identified only as [Will](/people/will-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap-physician) served as medical adviser to the programme and to Bigelow's National Institute for Discovery Science.<sup>17</sup> Multiple members of the AAWSAP/AATIP team suffered severe biological effects resulting in life-threatening medical conditions.<sup>18</sup> Medical researchers on the programme concluded that the severity of symptoms correlated with a subject's proximity to a UAP and their individual genetic circumstances.<sup>15</sup> To investigate alleged UAP materials, the programme routinely submitted samples to external scientists framed as recovered foreign technology, using blind-study methodology to obtain unbiased analysis.<sup>19</sup> The programme also obtained photographs of alleged [UAP alien implants](/matters/uap-alien-implants) removed from living foreign military pilots.<sup>20</sup> [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) produced a detailed AAWSAP/AATIP report on the [2004 Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter), which was independently characterised as thorough.<sup>27</sup>

AAWSAP faced substantial institutional opposition throughout its existence. Senior figures within the [DIA](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) actively worked against the programme, and a 2009 [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon) review determined the reports were of limited value.<sup>21</sup> The US Air Force and most components of the US intelligence community declined to support the congressionally funded investigation.<sup>22</sup> In the autumn of 2009, Elizondo and [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) met with [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski) to discuss refocusing the programme exclusively on UAP threats, a scope reduction that Lacatski ultimately refused.<sup>23, 24</sup> [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid)'s congressional funding for AAWSAP ran out in 2012, though Elizondo has maintained that the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon) secretly continued UAP investigations thereafter.<sup>25</sup> A successor initiative that Elizondo designated the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) was reported to remain active as late as 2017.<sup>26</sup>
