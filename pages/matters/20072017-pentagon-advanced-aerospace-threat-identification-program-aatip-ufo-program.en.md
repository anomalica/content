The Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) was a covert US [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) programme that investigated reports of unidentified flying objects, according to DoD officials, interviews with programme participants, and records obtained by [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times).<sup>3</sup> The programme began in 2007<sup>7</sup> under the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia)<sup>10</sup> and was run by military intelligence official [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) on the fifth floor of the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon)'s C Ring.<sup>2</sup> The [DoD](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) has stated that the programme was formally shut down in 2012,<sup>5</sup> though programme backers assert that while the Pentagon ended funding that year, officials continued to investigate episodes brought by service members while carrying out other DoD duties.<sup>6</sup> The DoD had not previously acknowledged the programme's existence prior to December 2017, when [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon) officials confirmed it in response to questions from [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times).<sup>11</sup>

The programme was initially largely funded at the request of [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), then the Nevada Democrat Senate majority leader, who had a long-standing interest in space phenomena.<sup>8</sup> In 2007, [Reid](/people/harry-reid) met with [DIA](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) officials after a meeting with entrepreneur [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) and learned they wished to start a UFO research programme.<sup>15</sup> He subsequently summoned senators [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens) and [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye) to a secure room in the Capitol to discuss the proposal.<sup>16</sup> None of the three senators sought a public debate about the funding; [Reid](/people/harry-reid) described it as "black money" drawn from the Pentagon budget for classified programmes.<sup>17</sup> Contracts obtained by [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) show a congressional appropriation of just under $22 million beginning in late 2008 through 2011,<sup>18</sup> representing a modest allocation within the approximately $600 billion annual DoD budget.<sup>1</sup> The funds were directed towards programme management, research, and assessments of the threat posed by the objects studied.<sup>19</sup>

Most of the money went to [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace), the Las Vegas-based company owned by [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow), a billionaire entrepreneur and longtime friend of [Reid](/people/harry-reid), which hired subcontractors and solicited research on behalf of the programme.<sup>9, 20</sup> Under [Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow)'s direction, [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace) modified buildings in [Las Vegas](/places/nevada-las-vegas-usa) for the storage of metal alloys and other materials that [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) and programme contractors said had been recovered from unidentified aerial phenomena.<sup>21</sup> Programme researchers also studied individuals who reported physical effects from encounters with unidentified objects, examining them for physiological changes,<sup>22</sup> and spoke with military service members who had reported sightings of strange aircraft.<sup>23</sup> Contractors included [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), an engineer who had previously conducted research on extrasensory perception for the [CIA](/organisations/cia).<sup>24</sup> Working with [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace), the programme produced documents describing sightings of aircraft that appeared to move at very high velocities with no visible signs of propulsion, or that hovered with no apparent means of lift.<sup>46</sup> Officials also studied video footage of encounters between unknown objects and American military aircraft, including footage relating to the 2004 encounter involving the [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz), in which a whitish oval object approximately the size of a commercial aircraft was observed being chased by two Navy F/A-18F Super Hornet fighter jets off the coast of San Diego.<sup>25</sup> The programme additionally collected footage from a Navy F/A-18 Super Hornet showing an object surrounded by a glowing aura travelling at high speed.<sup>24</sup>

By 2009, [Reid](/people/harry-reid) concluded the programme had made sufficiently extraordinary discoveries to warrant heightened security, citing "highly sensitive, unconventional aerospace-related findings."<sup>26</sup> He wrote to Deputy Defense Secretary [William Lynn III](/people/william-lynn-iii) requesting that the programme be designated a restricted [Special Access Programme](/matters/special-access-programs).<sup>27</sup> A [2009 Pentagon briefing](/documents/2009-pentagon-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-briefing) summary prepared by the programme's director at the time asserted that "what was considered science fiction is now science fact," and that the United States was incapable of defending itself against some of the technologies discovered.<sup>28</sup> [Reid](/people/harry-reid)'s request for the restricted designation was denied.<sup>29</sup> Pentagon spokesman [Thomas Crosson](/people/thomas-crosson) later stated publicly that the programme ended because "it was determined that there were other, higher priority issues that merited funding."<sup>12</sup> [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), however, maintained that only the government funding had ended in 2012, and that he had continued working with officials from the Navy and the [CIA](/organisations/cia) on the programme thereafter.<sup>13, 14</sup>

[Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) resigned from the programme in October 2017, citing what he characterised as excessive secrecy and internal opposition.<sup>33</sup> His [resignation letter](/documents/elizondo-resignation-letter), dated 2017-10-04, called for more serious attention to "the many accounts from the Navy and other services of unusual aerial systems interfering with military weapon platforms and displaying beyond-next-generation capabilities," and informed Secretary of Defense [Jim Mattis](/people/jim-mattis) that "there remains a vital need to ascertain capability and intent of these phenomena for the benefit of the armed forces and the nation."<sup>30, 31</sup> Following his departure, [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) joined [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) and former DoD official [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) in a commercial venture called [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science).<sup>32</sup> In the period that followed, Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) denied that [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) had any involvement in [AATIP](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip).<sup>34</sup> [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) stated that he believed the programme's effort had continued and that he had a successor, whom he declined to name. [Reid](/people/harry-reid), reflecting on the programme, stated that he did not know where the objects had come from: "If anyone says they have the answers now, they're fooling themselves. We do not know."
