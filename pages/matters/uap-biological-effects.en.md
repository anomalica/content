UAP biological effects refers to the range of reported physical and physiological harms — including radiation-like burns, neurological injury, and systemic illness — said to have been experienced by individuals following close proximity to unidentified anomalous phenomena. The subject has been studied within US government-affiliated programmes, most notably the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program](/matters/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program) (AAWSAP) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), which examined witnesses for physiological changes resulting from UAP encounters.<sup>1</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), a former US government official associated with both programmes, has stated that he reviewed bio-effects reports going back many years documenting medical consequences experienced by humans who encountered UAP technology,<sup>2</sup> and that reports, photographs, and medical records collected over decades from across the world documented consistent effects on witnesses.<sup>3</sup> The documented spectrum of effects reportedly touches all five senses and includes sleep disturbances, neurological symptoms, time distortion, and other phenomena.<sup>4</sup> Researcher John F. Schuessler, associated with [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow), is said to have compiled examples of civilian UAP biological effects dating back to 1950.<sup>5</sup>

Among the most extensively documented historical cases is the [Colares Incidents](/events/colares-incidents) of 1977 in [Brazil Colares](/places/colares-brazil). [Dr. Wellaide Cecim Carvalho](/people/wellaide-cecim-carvalho) treated approximately forty victims, most presenting with burns consistent with thermal or directed-energy exposure.<sup>6</sup> In twenty-three fresh-wound cases she examined, two puncture wounds were found at the centre of each burn,<sup>7</sup> and all twenty-three tested individuals had low haemoglobin levels.<sup>8</sup> Journalist [Robert Pratt](/people/robert-pratt) documented that thirty-four residents reported irritated eyes, five experienced temporary blindness, and eight reported lasting vision changes;<sup>9</sup> forty-one individuals suffered prolonged nausea and fifty-five reported extreme headaches.<sup>10</sup> Fourteen victims entered a catatonic state for short periods and fifty-four suffered temporary paralysis;<sup>11</sup> thirty-six reported chronic illness for years after their encounter, and ten of that group subsequently died.<sup>12</sup>

Civilian cases from North America have also featured in government-adjacent research. Following what Elizondo described as a December 1980 road incident in Texas — consistent with the [Cash-Landrum Incident](/events/cash-landrum-incident) — two women's symptoms reportedly progressed from headaches to severe skin burns, nausea, eye damage, lesions, exhaustion, hair loss, and loss of fingernails.<sup>13</sup> One later developed severe cataracts and the other developed breast cancer.<sup>14</sup> In a separate incident in August 2007 near Davis, California (see [Davis California UAP Orb Incident 2007](/events/davis-california-uap-orb-incident-2007)), a mother and daughter encountered three blue orbs on the road, two of which allegedly penetrated their vehicle;<sup>15</sup> one orb reportedly passed directly through the older woman's upper chest and exited her upper right arm.<sup>16</sup> The woman subsequently experienced premature ageing, skin rashes, hair loss, blurry vision, hearing loss, osteoarthritis, and was diagnosed with breast cancer, undergoing a bilateral mastectomy.<sup>17</sup> Military cases are also on record: [John Burroughs](/people/john-burroughs), a US Air Force serviceman present during the [Rendlesham Forest Incident](/events/rendlesham-forest-incident), experienced a series of worsening health effects including vision problems, heart murmurs, and cardiac tissue scarring, culminating in heart surgery to correct damaged leaflets in his mitral valve.<sup>18</sup>

Within the AAWSAP/AATIP programmes, biological effects were not confined to the subjects studied. Elizondo stated that two of his colleagues were under medical care for both cutaneous and visceral injuries sustained from direct interactions with UAP,<sup>19</sup> and that the injuries appeared to stem from a form of directed-energy exposure, resembling radiation.<sup>20</sup> Multiple programme team members experienced severe biological effects resulting in life-threatening medical issues,<sup>21</sup> with effects reportedly extending to their family members, including children.<sup>22</sup> Neuroimaging analysis conducted in association with the programme found that UAP experiencers — including military and intelligence personnel — exhibited brain scarring consistent with white-matter disease, a finding with which [Garry Nolan](/people/garry-nolan) concurred.<sup>23</sup> AAWSAP/AATIP medical researchers concluded that the severity of symptoms was influenced by the individual's genetic circumstances and their proximity to the UAP at the time of the encounter.<sup>24</sup> Research led by [Will (AAWSAP physician)](/people/will-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap-physician) and Nolan additionally found that nearly all subjects in the AAWSAP studies shared Native American DNA, specifically Cherokee heritage — a finding discussed in relation to the [Cherokee UAP Experiencer Connection](/matters/cherokee-uap-experiencer-connection) and explored further through the [UAP Caudate-Putamen DNA Study](/matters/uap-caudate-putamen-dna-study).<sup>25</sup>

Biological effects have been formally categorised within UAP research frameworks as a distinct observable. [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) listed biological effects as one of six UAP observables alongside hypersonic velocities, instantaneous acceleration, low observability, transmedium travel, and [anti-gravity propulsion](/concepts/anti-gravity-propulsion);<sup>26</sup> this categorisation is also discussed in the context of [UAP Sixth Observable Biological Effects](/matters/uap-sixth-observable-biological-effects). One theoretical explanation, drawn from the [UAP Warp Bubble Theory](/matters/uap-warp-bubble-theory), holds that a UAP's propulsion system blueshifts radiation outside the craft's warp bubble, potentially exposing nearby witnesses to ultraviolet radiation, soft X-rays, or gamma rays.<sup>27</sup> Elizondo has stated his opinion that UAP harm to humans is generally unintentional, analogising the phenomenon to the hazard of standing behind a jet engine during takeoff.<sup>28</sup> On a policy level, the US government has reportedly awarded multiple servicemen 100 per cent disability status in writing due to medical issues arising from close encounters with UAP,<sup>29</sup> and the late Senator [John McCain](/people/john-mccain) was described as one of the first political figures to champion assessment and assistance for affected military personnel.<sup>30</sup> Elizondo identified biological effects as a [sixth UAP observable](/matters/uap-sixth-observable-biological-effects) that had not been publicly discussed by the US government at the time of his writing, encompassing radiation burns, internal organ damage, and other documented medical issues suffered by service personnel and intelligence officials.<sup>31</sup>
