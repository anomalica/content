Roswell is a city in south-eastern New Mexico, USA, with a population of about 50,000, up from roughly 15,000 in the late 1940s.<sup>1</sup> Since the 1930s, the high plains of south-eastern New Mexico have served as a test area for the United States' space and aerospace technology,<sup>2</sup> and the White Sands testing ground lies a two-hour drive to the west of the crash site associated with the city.<sup>3</sup> Robert Goddard worked alone in the deserts outside Roswell, corresponding frequently with [Jack Parsons](/people/jack-parsons) and his associates.<sup>4</sup> Roswell now hosts an annual four-day UFO festival.<sup>5</sup>

Local New Mexico newspapers carried many reports of odd lights throughout 1947,<sup>6</sup> including a disc-shaped object seen zigzagging across the sky on 1 July<sup>7</sup> and something brightly lit that crossed the skies over Roswell in a north-westerly direction on the evening of 2 July, witnessed by members of the public and reported locally.<sup>8</sup> On 5 July 1947, ranch foreman Mac Brazel said he found crash debris scattered across a cattle ranch 120 kilometres from Roswell,<sup>9</sup> and that he then drove into town to report the find,<sup>10</sup> in what became known as the [Roswell incident (1947)](/events/roswell-incident-1947). The 1947 Roswell Army Air Field press release July 8 stated that the object had landed on a ranch near Roswell the previous week, and that the rancher, lacking phone facilities, stored the debris until he could contact the sheriff's office, which in turn notified Major [Jesse Marcel](/people/jesse-marcel) of the 509th Bomber Group Intelligence Office.<sup>11</sup> A later report was issued stating that the Roswell disc was a crashed weather balloon.<sup>12</sup> The following day, the Irish Times reported that the US War Department said it was without news of the New Mexico flying saucer and was checking with Roswell.<sup>13</sup>

Drawing on later reporting, [Annie Jacobsen](/people/annie-jacobsen) wrote that in July 1947 United States Signal Corps engineers tracked two anomalous objects flying across the south-western United States, occasionally hovering before continuing on.<sup>14</sup> According to Jacobsen, one of the two objects crashed near Roswell, and the office of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](/organisations/joint-chiefs-of-staff) took command and recovered the airframe and propulsion equipment, including the craft's power source, in what she associated with the 1947 Roswell crash and debris retrieval.<sup>15</sup> Jacobsen wrote that the craft was nothing like a conventional aircraft, with no wings or tail, a round fuselage and a dome on top.<sup>16</sup> According to In Plain Sight, secret US Army memos declassified in 1994 referred to the object as a "flying disc".<sup>17</sup> The 1947 crash occurred in the vicinity of a government test facility in New Mexico and broke into two crash sites, in an event referred to as the 1947 Roswell Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) crash.<sup>18</sup> According to the Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP), Volume I, a crashed balloon associated with Project Mogul outside Roswell is assessed to be the source of early UFO claims related to the incident.<sup>19</sup>

Truman](/people/harry-s-truman) on down".<sup>23</sup> Exon was allegedly heavily involved in the retrieval of the Roswell craft in 1947.<sup>24</sup> Philip Corso said that an alien spacecraft crashed at Roswell in 1947 and was recovered by the US government, in what is also known as the 1947 Roswell UFO crash.<sup>25</sup> Luis Elizondo said [FBI](/organisations/federal-bureau-of-investigation-fbi) agents visited ranchers in the Roswell area door-to-door to warn that anyone who spoke about the crash would be killed.<sup>26</sup> President Bill Clinton said that, as far as he knew, an alien spacecraft did not crash in Roswell in 1947, that if the US Air Force had recovered alien bodies they had not told him, and that he wanted to know.<sup>27</sup>

Rumours of recovered flying saucers persisted into 1950: a 9 January 1950 issue of Time magazine reported claims that crashed "flying saucers" and "small humanoid looking bodies" had been recovered from crashes in New Mexico,<sup>28</sup> and in March 1950 the 1950 Guy Hottel FBI memo told J.
