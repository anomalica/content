Sydney, in the Australian state of New South Wales, is the setting for the Peter Khoury alien abduction case, centred on [Peter Khoury](/people/peter-khoury). Khoury's family migrated from Lebanon to Australia in 1973, following his father, who had already moved there.<sup>1</sup> By 1988, Khoury was twenty-four and living with his parents in a Sydney suburb.<sup>2</sup> Some years before that, at the family's Sydney family home footsteps and 1988 encounter house, his mother reportedly saw an unknown man walking into a room that was later found empty.<sup>3</sup> According to [Bill Chalker](/people/bill-chalker), Khoury described his 1988 abduction experience to him.<sup>4</sup>

The case's central episode, the 1992-07-23 Peter Khoury bedroom encounter, is reported to have involved two near-human females who appeared suddenly to Khoury in daylight in his Sydney bedroom.<sup>5</sup> One of the two alleged visitors was reported to be Asian in appearance.<sup>6</sup> Hair was collected from a Sydney suburb in July 1992 following the encounter,<sup>7</sup> including a clear, thin head hair 15 centimetres long recovered on 23 July 1992.<sup>8</sup> Peter Khoury has said that Brian Woolch, a former Sydney radio station operator with an interest in paranormal topics, described a mark on his head as looking like a bullet hole and said Khoury could not have faked it.<sup>9</sup> Brian Woolch is otherwise not linked to the case.

Laboratory analysis of the hair produced the Chinese Mongoloid mitochondrial Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) substitution found in Peter Khoury's alien hair shaft. According to Hair of the Alien, a chief biochemist who examined the sample considered it possible that Khoury had had contact with a fair-skinned, nearly albino woman from the Sydney area, but concluded that explanation was ruled out because the mitochondrial Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) fitted only a Chinese Mongoloid donor.<sup>10</sup> The same source states that the rare substitution, found in 4% of Taiwanese women, made it implausible that a Chinese woman with matching thin, clear hair would be found in Sydney.<sup>11</sup> The source further states that the DNA results support Khoury having been in the presence of two people carrying rare mitochondrial DNA not easily found in the Sydney region,<sup>12</sup> and that the results do not support prosaic explanations such as processing errors or contamination.<sup>13</sup>

Sydney also hosted the UFO Experience Support Association (UFOESA), described as one of the most notable manifestations of the support-group concept for [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) experiencers in Australia.<sup>14</sup> Jamie Leonarder worked as a welfare worker with a Sydney agency supporting destitute people and those with drug and other dependency problems.<sup>15</sup> Chalker separately corresponded with and later interviewed, in Sydney, the 1976 New South Wales woman (Communion letter case),<sup>16</sup> whose reported encounter took place in late 1976 in New South Wales, in the middle of the day, while she was vacuuming her home.<sup>17</sup> Chalker has also related that [Whitley Strieber](/people/whitley-strieber) found only one case, among many worldwide alien-encounter accounts, that closely matched his own experience regarding hair, and that it came from Australia.<sup>18</sup> Harvard psychiatrist [John Mack](/people/john-mack), of [Harvard University](/organisations/harvard-university), visited Australia in 1996 to research the subject of [alien abduction](/topics/alien-abduction).<sup>19</sup> Chalker has said that, to his knowledge, only one other account of possible alien hair emerged in Australia, in 1992 and 2001.<sup>20</sup>

Sydney's connection to Khoury's biography extends beyond the abduction case: he has said that during roughly five years living alone, he associated with a hardcore Sydney standover man who was later killed.<sup>21</sup> Separately, the Nugan Hand Bank collapsed in scandal in Sydney in 1980, amid allegations of money laundering and drug smuggling, an episode recorded as the 1980 Nugan Hand Bank collapse.<sup>22</sup> [James MacDonald](/people/james-macdonald) has said that experienced private UFO investigating groups operated in Sydney as well as in [Australia, Victoria, Melbourne](/places/victoria-melbourne-australia) and Australia, Tasmania, Hobart.<sup>23</sup>
