Alex Dietrich is a former [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) aviator best known, alongside [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor), for her role in the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter). According to the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group (CSG-11) AAV Incident Report, she was the pilot of FastEagle 02, one of two F/A-18Fs of [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41) involved in the intercept and visual contact with the [Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV)](/topics/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav) on 2004-11-14.<sup>1</sup> At the time she held the rank of Lieutenant Junior Grade and flew the other US Navy [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Super Hornet involved in the encounter.<sup>2</sup>

On 2004-11-14, Dietrich and Fravor, each with a weapon systems officer in the back seat, were diverted to investigate radar contacts reported by [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59).<sup>3</sup> Fravor detached Dietrich's F/A-18F, which held at approximately 6,100 metres, while his own aircraft descended to between approximately 3,700 and 4,900 metres.<sup>4</sup>

According to Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs, the two pilots, flying at approximately 6,100 metres, looked down and saw the sea below churning in one spot, with whitecaps and a patch of bubbles rising from the water.<sup>5</sup> Dietrich circled above while Fravor descended for a closer look at the object.<sup>6</sup> The same source states that all four pilots noticed a bizarre object darting back and forth over the whitecaps, about 15 metres above the water.<sup>7</sup> Fravor's weapon systems officer maintained a running dialogue over the interflight radio with Dietrich's FastEagle 02 and reported what they were seeing to USS Princeton.<sup>8</sup> Dietrich's own weapon systems officer was known by the call sign "Noodle"; together they made up FASTEAGLE 02.<sup>9</sup>

Dietrich said she initially tried to categorise the unidentified object as possibly a helicopter or a drone.<sup>10</sup> She has described feeling vulnerable during the encounter because they had nothing with which to defend themselves.<sup>11</sup> Dietrich said that had she witnessed the object alone, she might not have reported it because of how implausible it would sound.<sup>12</sup> She described the Tic Tac object encountered during the 2004 USS Nimitz Unidentified Aerial Phenomena / Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) encounter as manoeuvring in a "playful" manner.<sup>13</sup>

Dietrich flew at Fravor's wing during the encounter and had never spoken publicly about it before a 2021 interview.<sup>14</sup> She has said she feels a responsibility to share her account because she was in a government aircraft, was on duty, and the material is unclassified.<sup>15</sup> Years afterwards, Dietrich contacted Fravor to ask whether he had been contacted about the incident.<sup>16</sup> Fravor, who had by then retired from the Navy, told her he had not been contacted since 2009, but that he was happy to meet with anyone who wanted to talk.<sup>17</sup> Dietrich told Fravor that she had been invited to [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) multiple times to discuss the encounter and to view other [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) videos.<sup>18</sup>

According to [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), neither Fravor nor Dietrich displayed any propensity to exaggerate, and their crews perceived them as the best of the best.<sup>19</sup> Elizondo said that Dietrich, whose call sign was "New Girl", was more skilled and deadly as a pilot than the call sign suggested.<sup>20</sup> He said that, fresh out of training, she flew circles around her peers, which was probably the reason she was handpicked for her assignment with the Black Aces squadron.<sup>21</sup> Elizondo said he later heard a story that Dietrich had more confirmed kills in a period of time than the entire [United States Marine Corps (USMC)](/organisations/united-states-marine-corps-usmc).<sup>22</sup> Brad Byers and Shari Smith requested extensive data and asked to speak with the pilot witnesses Fravor and Dietrich, along with a radar operator, in connection with briefing James Mattis on UAP.<sup>23</sup> Elizondo also arranged for [Christopher Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) to take part in official debriefings with Fravor, Dietrich, and other US Navy personnel who had seen UAP up close or on military sensor systems.<sup>24</sup>
