Ronald Reagan's conservative Republican presidency followed the liberal administration of [Jimmy Carter](/people/jimmy-carter) in 1981.<sup>1</sup> Luis Elizondo has said he believes that [Harry S. Truman](/people/harry-s-truman), [Dwight D. Eisenhower](/people/dwight-d-eisenhower), [John Fitzgerald Kennedy](/people/john-fitzgerald-kennedy), [Lyndon B. Johnson](/people/lyndon-b-johnson), Carter, Reagan, [George Herbert Walker Bush](/people/george-herbert-walker-bush) and [Donald Trump](/people/donald-trump) were each briefed in some way on [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap).<sup>2</sup> Elizondo has also said he has reason to believe that the Strategic Defense Initiative, nicknamed Star Wars, which Reagan championed, was concerned with UAP and not solely with nuclear weapons.<sup>3</sup> Separately, Reagan once suggested to his Soviet counterpart [Mikhail Gorbachev](/people/mikhail-gorbachev) that the United States and the [Soviet Union](/organisations/soviet-union) should work together in the event of an alien invasion.<sup>4</sup> In a speech to the United Nations, Reagan said he occasionally thought about how quickly the world's differences would vanish if humanity were facing an alien threat from outside the world, and asked whether such a force might not already be among us.<sup>5</sup>

Ross Coulthart has said that [Robert Fish](/people/robert-fish) had extensive experience working on classified programmes, including Reagan's "Trust but Verify" nuclear missile disarmament treaty with the Soviets.<sup>6</sup>

James Baker served as staff director in the Reagan administration.<sup>7</sup> According to Jason Samosa, citing Nick Redfern, Baker was involved in a [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) briefing given to Reagan.<sup>8</sup> Samosa has said this briefing included attendees from the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T), whom he presumed to be Christopher Green and his staff, along with the [Collins Elite](/organisations/collins-elite) and a third group who also briefed.<sup>9</sup> Samosa's research, in his own account, indicates that Richard Nixon, Carter, Reagan and Bush all appear to have received briefings on the UFO subject.<sup>10</sup> Samosa has further said that, in his view, [Bill Clinton](/people/bill-clinton) and [Barack Obama](/people/barack-obama) came into office without ties to the American establishment, unlike Reagan, Nixon and the Bush family.<sup>11</sup> Samosa has also said that The Family (The Fellowship) maintained a consistent presence at the centre of executive-branch power across the transition from the Carter administration to the Reagan administration.<sup>12</sup>

Ross Coulthart has said that an account of a remark Reagan made about Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977 film) was confirmed to him by an unnamed person who was present in the room at the time, someone other than Steven Spielberg or Reagan himself.<sup>13</sup> Coulthart has related that when the film was watched in [The White House](/organisations/the-white-house) movie room with Spielberg present, Reagan told those in the room that there was a lot in the film and that a few people present knew that everything in it was true.<sup>14</sup>

[Jesse Mitchells](/people/jesse-mitchells) has said that the biggest fallacy in [ufology](/topics/ufology) is treating figures such as Reagan as though they spent most of their time on UFOs, when the subject occupied under one per cent of their attention and their real focus was geopolitics.<sup>15</sup>
