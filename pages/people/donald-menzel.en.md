Donald Menzel was an American astronomer at [Harvard University](/organisations/harvard-university), publicly known during his lifetime as one of the leading sceptics of UFO reports, and posthumously revealed to have held a clandestine security clearance and undisclosed ties to US intelligence and military agencies.

Benjamin Simon said Menzel described weather balloons and other aerial phenomena that, under certain weather conditions, can resemble [UFOs](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo).<sup>1</sup> Before the Second World War, Menzel worked with [Vannevar Bush](/people/vannevar-bush) on the differential analyzer, an early analog computer.<sup>2</sup> In 1950 testimony given on Menzel's behalf during loyalty hearings, Bush stated he had first known Menzel in 1934 or 1935, when Menzel was an assistant or associate professor of astronomy at Harvard interested in applying the analyzer to astrophysical problems.<sup>3</sup><sup>4</sup>

Menzel's public scepticism was expressed in his 1953 book Flying Saucers, in which he explained a major sighting as an atmospheric lensing effect.<sup>5</sup> He applied this reasoning to the 1949 Charles B. Moore balloon and unidentified object sighting April 24, claiming the observers had seen a mirage of the balloon moving downward and to one side of it.<sup>6</sup> Bruce Maccabee said Menzel made critical errors comparing his lensing theory with the data reported by observers of that sighting.<sup>7</sup> [Whitley Strieber](/people/whitley-strieber) found that Menzel's description of the event in Flying Saucers did not match the official report,<sup>8</sup> and Menzel did not mention the sighting angles reported by Charles B. Moore anywhere in the book.<sup>9</sup> In 1966, Menzel poured benzine over a tankful of acetone during a televised documentary to demonstrate optical properties, in an attempt to convince the audience that UFOs were nothing but mirages.<sup>10</sup>

Menzel was also known for leading public attacks on Immanuel Velikovsky,<sup>11</sup> and for mentoring [Carl Sagan](/people/carl-sagan) at Harvard.<sup>12</sup> When Menzel's name appeared as a founding member of [Majestic 12 (MJ-12)](/projects/majestic-12-mj-12), alongside Bush, in the memo mailed to Jaime Shandera,<sup>13</sup> debunkers initially cited his presence as evidence the documents were forgeries, given his record as a public UFO debunker who had written three books on the subject.<sup>14</sup> [Stanton Friedman](/people/stanton-friedman) later went through Menzel's papers while researching the MJ-12 documents and found he had led an elaborate double life,<sup>15</sup> including references to his participation in top-secret weapons programmes and intelligence committees.<sup>16</sup>

It emerged only after Menzel's death that he had a clandestine association with the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa), the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia), and the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn), holding a top-secret-ultra security clearance.<sup>17</sup> He never disclosed this relationship, even to Harvard.<sup>18</sup> Friedman said Menzel's undisclosed ties amounted to those of a deep-cover disinformation agent tasked with shutting down [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) stories.<sup>19</sup>
