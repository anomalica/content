Nat Kobitz was the former Director of Science and Technology Development for the [US Navy Science and Technology Development](/organisations/us-navy-science-and-technology-development), a position he held for nearly 30 years before departing in 1994.<sup>3</sup> Described by journalist [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) as one of the most senior [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) insiders ever to make public claims about retrieved alien technology,<sup>2</sup> Kobitz had a distinguished career that included work on the launch vehicle air frame for the Gemini space programme and numerous Navy surface ship and submarine projects.<sup>4</sup> He was also involved in developing the [Sea Scout UAV Program](/matters/sea-scout-uav-program), an unmanned aerial vehicle project whose prototype was lost after straying into a White House security jamming zone near a presidential family wedding in Texas.<sup>5</sup>

In early 2020, Kobitz contacted Coulthart by telephone from his home in [Maryland Baltimore](/places/maryland-baltimore-usa), following receipt of a letter from the journalist.<sup>7</sup> During subsequent conversations, Kobitz confirmed that he had attended a meeting at the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon) in July 1993 with CIA-linked private investigator [Gordon Novel](/people/gordon-novel) and his son [Sur Novel](/people/sur-novel).<sup>6</sup> Gordon Novel had written an account of that [meeting](/events/novel-kobitz-pentagon-meeting-1993) claiming that Kobitz — referred to under the pseudonym "Wally Katz" — admitted knowledge of alien reproduction vehicles reverse-engineered from recovered extraterrestrial craft.<sup>7</sup> [Sur Novel](/people/sur-novel) separately confirmed to Coulthart that he had witnessed the meeting and seen his father present Kobitz with a detailed drawing of the [ARV Fluxliner](/objects/arv-fluxliner), a craft design attributed to artist [Mark McCandlish](/people/mark-mccandlish).<sup>8</sup> Sur Novel recalled Kobitz's immediate reaction as "what are you doing with this" — a response he characterised as neither dismissive nor alarmed.<sup>10</sup> Kobitz himself told Coulthart that upon being shown the [ARV Fluxliner](/objects/arv-fluxliner) graphic he said: "Okay, I've seen that before... And my conclusion is that it's a hoax."<sup>9</sup>

When Coulthart asked Kobitz directly whether he had ever been read into any programmes involving crashed UFOs or UAPs, Kobitz replied: "Yes, I was. I was never read out of it. So, I really don't think it would help but I really can't speak about it."<sup>11</sup> On the question of recovered alien spacecraft, Kobitz said "I only have hearsay information" and declined to confirm or deny the matter directly.<sup>12</sup> He nonetheless stated that what he had heard was "That we had recovered, several times, alien spacecraft. Or what was thought to be,"<sup>13</sup> and when pressed on whether the United States had been attempting to develop recovered alien technology, he responded: "Yes, I can say that's so."<sup>14</sup> Kobitz also reacted dismissively to the official [Project MOGUL](/matters/project-mogul) explanation for the 1947 [Roswell UAP Crash](/events/roswell-uap-crash), remarking that "More than one person reported that it was not soft material. It was hard metal pieces."<sup>15</sup>

Kobitz acknowledged that the US Navy had conducted research into anti-gravity propulsion, though he characterised his own involvement as relatively unremarkable, describing it as "a propulsion system, very low energy ion propulsion."<sup>16</sup> He stated his view that the United States had not succeeded in replicating the principle of anti-gravity despite years of classified research.<sup>17</sup> He also made separate inquiries regarding Navy scientist [Salvatore Pais](/people/salvatore-pais) and reported that neither he nor his colleagues were aware of any working prototypes of Pais's patents within the Navy.<sup>22</sup>

After leaving the Navy in 1994, Kobitz established [NKA Science](/organisations/nka-science), a private consultancy specialising in advanced manufacturing technology with a particular focus on electron beam welding.<sup>18</sup> During this period he was invited to [Wright Patterson Air Force Base](/places/wright-patterson-air-force-base) to examine metal fragments held under high security as part of what became known as the [Kobitz Wright-Patterson Metal Examination](/matters/kobitz-wright-patterson-metal-examination).<sup>19</sup> He described examining a piece of material approximately 0.9 by 1.2 metres, reported to him as a titanium alloy unknown to the US Air Force, which displayed a bulkhead-to-skin bond with no visible join — appearing as if the surfaces had been cast as a single integral unit.<sup>20</sup> In his expert opinion, no known industrial process could account for the bond he observed on what has been termed the [Wright Patterson Foreign Technology Metal Fragment](/objects/wright-patterson-foreign-technology-metal-fragment).<sup>21</sup> Nat Kobitz died on 2020-04-05, shortly after his conversations with Coulthart were recorded.<sup>23</sup>
