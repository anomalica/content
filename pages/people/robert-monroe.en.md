Robert Monroe was an American businessman who conducted a systematic investigation of [out-of-body experience (OBE)](/topics/out-of-body-experience-obe) travel.<sup>1</sup> He was the founder of The Monroe Institute,<sup>2</sup> where he set up learning centres using training tapes intended to help people leave their bodies.<sup>3</sup> His account of the work, which spanned hundreds of experiments over twelve years, appeared in his book Journeys Out of the Body.<sup>4</sup> According to Joseph McMoneagle, Monroe had been experiencing OBEs since 1958 and had already demonstrated control over them.<sup>5</sup> Monroe determined that there was no physiological cause for his condition<sup>6</sup> and began experimenting systematically, keeping notes of each event.<sup>7</sup> Wary of being classified as a patient, he avoided discussing it with his psychologist and psychiatrist friends,<sup>8</sup> confiding instead in occasional meetings with Foster Bradshaw, his only outlet at the time.<sup>9</sup> It was Bradshaw who first suggested that what Monroe was experiencing was astral projection, though Bradshaw himself had only heard of such things at second hand.<sup>10</sup> Monroe's wife accepted his condition, if reluctantly, having no other real choice.<sup>11</sup> An early example of his self-experimentation was the 1959-05-05 three-way device experience, during which he floated up, rolled in the air, dove through the floor, and found himself standing in an unfinished house overlooking a valley.<sup>12</sup>

Monroe developed a distinctive vocabulary for his experiences, preferring the terms Second Body and Second State to "astral," saying he did not know what that word meant.<sup>13</sup> Prompted by a discussion with Mrs. Bradshaw about whether a "cord" connected the physical and Second Body, he reported feeling, in an experiment on 1961-01-09, an object extending from a spot between his shoulder blades with a base like the spread-out roots of a tree.<sup>14</sup> He observed that in the Second State there was no evidence of male-female interpenetration, and that the physical form of a sex counterpart was entirely absent.<sup>15</sup> Separation from the body was preceded by what he called the State of Vibration: in a hole-and-180-degree-rotation technique he performed at least eleven times, he followed a repeatable formula of first establishing the vibration condition, then rotating 180 degrees, at which point a hole would appear.<sup>16</sup> Among the basic fears he associated with The Barrier was fear of the unknown, arising because out-of-body existence follows a completely different set of rules from the physical world.<sup>17</sup> Of the environments he distinguished, Locale I consisted of people and places that actually existed in the material world at the moment of the experiment.<sup>18</sup>

Monroe treated a small number of visits as evidential test cases, several occurring during what he later grouped as the middle stage of his research. On the afternoon of 1958-09-10, intending to visit Foster Bradshaw and his wife, whom he knew to be ill in bed with a cold,<sup>19</sup> he instead found the couple outside walking toward a smaller building resembling a garage, Bradshaw in a light overcoat and hat and Mrs. Bradshaw in a dark coat<sup>20</sup> - which puzzled him, since he had expected to find Bradshaw in bed.<sup>21</sup> Monroe recounted that when he telephoned Mrs. Bradshaw that evening, she confirmed the pair had indeed been walking toward the garage at approximately 4:25pm, she heading to the post office and he accompanying her for fresh air despite being ill,<sup>22</sup> and that neither of them had been aware of his presence at the time.<sup>23</sup> A similar pattern followed on the morning of 1959-03-05: staying in a motel in Winston-Salem, Monroe saw a boy tossing a baseball, a man loading an awkward device into a car, and people at a table dealing large white cards,<sup>24</sup> then visited Agnew Bahnson and his wife that evening in connection with the vision.<sup>25</sup> Monroe said that Bahnson's son later confirmed tossing a baseball while walking to school that morning;<sup>26</sup> that Bahnson himself confirmed loading a Van de Graaff generator into his car at the time;<sup>27</sup> and that Mrs. Bahnson confirmed that, for the first time in two years, she had brought the morning mail to the breakfast table and passed out large white envelopes.<sup>28</sup> Monroe also wrote to [Richard Gordon](/people/richard-gordon), describing his out-of-body experiences and a technique for leaving the physical body; the letter was read to Gordon repeatedly in hospital, at his own request, by his wife.<sup>29</sup>

A more extended evidential episode centred on R.W., a businesswoman and close friend. Attempting an out-of-body visit to her while she was on vacation on the New Jersey coast, without knowing her exact location,<sup>30</sup> Monroe perceived her seated in a kitchen holding a glass, with two teenage girls, one blonde and one brunette, also present holding drinks.<sup>31</sup> Asking mentally whether she knew he was there, he perceived she replied that she did, while simultaneously continuing an oral conversation with the girls.<sup>32</sup> During the 1960-10-10 out-of-body visit to R.W., Monroe pinched her side, above the hips and below the rib cage, to test whether she would remember the visit;<sup>33</sup> she let out a loud cry, and he backed away, surprised that he was able to physically pinch her.<sup>34</sup> He recorded a return time of three thirty-five and, since R.W. would not be back until Monday, could not verify the contact until then.<sup>35</sup> Questioned afterward without being told the reason, R.W. showed complete astonishment on being asked whether she remembered a pinch that Saturday afternoon, asking "Was that you?"<sup>36</sup> and showed Monroe two brown and blue marks on her left side, at exactly the spot where he had pinched her.<sup>37</sup> Monroe apologised, promising that if he tried anything like it again he would use something less painful.<sup>38</sup> He assessed the episode's timing as coinciding with the actual events and categorised it as an indeterminate autosuggestion hallucination involving wilful desire and [preknowledge](/topics/precognition) of R.W.'s general location,<sup>39</sup> and listed six points of correspondence between his impressions and the actual events: the indoor location, the number of people present, the description of the girls, their actions, the acknowledgment of pinching, and the resulting physical marks.<sup>40</sup>

Some of Monroe's experiences involved apparent encounters with non-human entities. On one occasion he had the sensation of fighting, outside his body, with two small beings;<sup>41</sup> he recounted trying the idea of fire against them, which seemed to help a little, though he sensed the beings were amused, as if nothing he did could harm them, and he was sobbing for help.<sup>42</sup> He recounted that during this experience a man in a dark robe reaching to his ankles stopped a short distance away and watched with a serious expression, without Monroe being able to see his feet.<sup>43</sup> Jacques Vallée, comparing this to the case of José Antonio, said that in both instances the apparition of a taller robed man marked the end of the ordeal.<sup>44</sup> Vallée also described Monroe, whom he called a psychic experimenter, as having recorded a beam-of-light phenomenon during his investigations of out-of-body consciousness.<sup>45</sup> Lying on his bed on 1960-09-09, Monroe felt bathed in and transfixed by a powerful beam that seemed to come from the north, about 30 degrees above the horizon;<sup>46</sup> he felt completely powerless, as if in the presence of a very strong force in personal contact with him,<sup>47</sup> and perceived the force behind the beam as an intelligence beyond his comprehension that came into his head searching every memory in his mind.<sup>48</sup> On 1960-09-16 he experienced the same impersonal probing beam, from the same angle;<sup>49</sup> during it he received the impression that he was bound by loyalty to the intelligent force and had a job to perform on earth,<sup>50</sup> along with an impression of huge, ancient, rust-covered pipes through which something like oil, but higher in energy, was passing.<sup>51</sup> As part of the 1960 out-of-body beam experiences, on 1960-09-30 he experienced the same pattern again, perceiving beings that seemed to soar up into the sky while he pleaded after them.<sup>52</sup> He concluded that the intelligence behind the beam was far beyond his understanding, describing it as impersonal and cold, and speculated that such visits might underlie humanity's religious beliefs;<sup>53</sup> afterward he cried and concluded that the God of his childhood and of organised religion was not as he had been taught to worship.<sup>54</sup>

In an attempt at scientific verification, Monroe took part in Electroencephalogram (EEG) Experiment #EEG-5 on 1966-07-19, during what he later grouped as the later stage of his research. He drove approximately 110 kilometres from Richmond, Virginia to a hospital's electroencephalography laboratory on a major university campus, arriving at 9:00 p.m.,<sup>55</sup> where a technician attached electrodes to him by 9:30 p.m. while he reclined on a cot in a semi-darkened room.<sup>56</sup> After about forty-five minutes of a fractional relaxation technique, unable to attain full relaxation, he took a break to smoke a cigarette and talk with the technician,<sup>57</sup> before attempting the "roll-out" method of separation.<sup>58</sup> On a second attempt he let himself float down to the floor beside the cot, feeling himself pass through the various EEG wires on the way down,<sup>59</sup> then floated horizontally toward the outer rooms in search of the technician,<sup>60</sup> whom he found in a brightly lit room, not alone, but with a man standing beside her<sup>61</sup> who was about her height, with curly hair.<sup>62</sup> Afterward, Monroe told the technician he had seen her together with a man, and she confirmed the man was her husband;<sup>63</sup> going outside with her once the electrodes were removed, he met a man matching that description.<sup>64</sup> Monroe had been unaware of the husband's presence beforehand, which he considered the only supporting evidence beyond whatever might have appeared on the EEG recording itself,<sup>65</sup> and, according to Journeys Out of the Body, the technician herself later confirmed that Monroe had not known of her husband's presence and had not met him previously.<sup>66</sup> Monroe's influence extended beyond his own experiments: by 1983, Joseph McMoneagle regarded him as the one person in the world he trusted who claimed to control out-of-body experiences,<sup>67</sup> and that autumn, after attending a Gateway Voyage seminar at the Monroe Institute in Virginia, McMoneagle asked Monroe for help controlling his own OBE ability.<sup>68</sup> The two worked together in Monroe's lab for nearly fourteen months, and McMoneagle eventually learned some control, though not as much as Monroe;<sup>69</sup> together they visited a number of targets both out-of-body and through remote viewing,<sup>70</sup> and McMoneagle found the training produced a substantial increase in control over his remote-viewing ability.<sup>71</sup> Decades later, the writer Whitley Strieber said he had never met Monroe but had attended a gateway programme at The Monroe Institute.<sup>72</sup>
