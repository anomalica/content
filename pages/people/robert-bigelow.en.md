Robert Bigelow is an American aerospace entrepreneur and hotelier whose companies have carried out privately funded and government-contracted research into unidentified aerial phenomena. George Knapp described meeting Bigelow in 1989, characterising him as a billionaire businessman who owned real estate and hotel properties in multiple states.<sup>1</sup> Knapp said Bigelow subsequently began funding private UFO investigations as well as the work of UFO organisations.<sup>2</sup> Bigelow's fortune derives from the Budget Suites of America hotel chain.<sup>3</sup>

In 1995, Bigelow self-funded the creation of the [National Institute for Discovery Science](/organisations/national-institute-for-discovery-science) (NIDS),<sup>4</sup> which George Knapp described as founded in 1996.<sup>5</sup> The following year, in 1996, Bigelow paid 200,000 US dollars to purchase Skinwalker Ranch in Utah,<sup>6</sup> funding full-time surveillance of the property as part of an investigation into paranormal claims associated with it.<sup>7</sup> [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) began working for NIDS in July 1996,<sup>8</sup> and Bigelow later invited [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell) to serve on the organisation's science advisory board,<sup>9</sup> where Mitchell sat while NIDS conducted research that remained secret.<sup>10</sup> None of the scientists Bigelow brought in to study the Skinwalker Ranch phenomenon were UFO believers by background.<sup>11</sup> The scope of NIDS's work extended beyond the ranch, and much of what it uncovered remains confidential;<sup>12</sup> the organisation conducted investigations across the United States from 1995 to 2004.<sup>13</sup> Luis Elizondo later described NIDS as having studied Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) and the paranormal during the 1990s.<sup>14</sup>

In 1998, Bigelow founded [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace), a company specialising in manufactured space equipment, largely with his own funding.<sup>15</sup> The company has since worked with NASA to produce expandable craft for use in space.<sup>16</sup> [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) said his own interest in UFOs originated with Bigelow,<sup>17</sup> and Luis Elizondo counted Bigelow among Reid's friends.<sup>18</sup> Reid said he visited Bigelow's Skinwalker Ranch, and that what a Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) scientist saw there convinced him the phenomenon warranted investigation.<sup>19</sup> In August 2008, the DIA issued an invitation to tender for the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap); Bigelow was the sole bidder, securing 10 million US dollars in funding for the first year with a five-year option.<sup>20</sup> The contract was awarded to a subsidiary of Bigelow Aerospace.<sup>21</sup> The programme's investigations began in late 2008 and reportedly ended in 2012,<sup>22</sup> though most of the AAWSAP team were terminated by mid-2010.<sup>23</sup>

Most of the funding for the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) also went to Bigelow's aerospace research company.<sup>24</sup> That funding flowed to Bigelow Aerospace, which hired subcontractors and solicited research for the programme.<sup>25</sup> Working with Bigelow's Las Vegas-based company, AATIP produced documents describing sightings of aircraft that appeared to move at very high velocities with no visible signs of propulsion.<sup>26</sup> Much of the Pentagon funding for the UAP investigation went to [LLC Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies](/organisations/llc-bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies) (BAASS), owned by Bigelow,<sup>27</sup> which served as the prime contractor for the programme [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) joined<sup>28</sup> and on which Bigelow was described as the lead contractor.<sup>29</sup> Bigelow hired several of the same scientists who had worked on his earlier NIDS investigations to work on the BAASS programme.<sup>30</sup> Elizondo described Bigelow as a billionaire hotelier, developer, and aerospace magnate,<sup>31</sup> and as the contractor running the investigation at Utah Skinwalker Ranch.<sup>32</sup> Bigelow had bought the ranch land in the mid-1990s so his scientists could study unusual occurrences long associated with the property,<sup>33</sup> and he still owned it at the time of AAWSAP.<sup>34</sup> Elizondo said Bigelow spent much of his own money funding some of the programme's costs.<sup>35</sup> According to reporting on the programme, under Bigelow's direction Bigelow Aerospace modified buildings in [Nevada Las Vegas](/places/nevada-las-vegas-usa) for the storage of metal alloys and other materials that Elizondo and AATIP contractors said had been recovered from unidentified aerial phenomena.<sup>36</sup> An alien logo is painted on the exterior of the Bigelow Aerospace building in Las Vegas.<sup>37</sup>

Bigelow has spoken publicly about his views on the [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) and [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) issue. He has said that internationally, the United States is the most backward country in the world on the UFO issue.<sup>36</sup> He has said American scientists are scared of being ostracised and that American media is scared of the stigma surrounding the subject,<sup>37</sup> while, in his view, China and Russia are much more open about it, working on it through large state organisations.<sup>38</sup> Bigelow has said he believes UFOs have visited Earth.<sup>39</sup> In an interview with 60 Minutes correspondent Lara Logan, he said he was "absolutely convinced" that aliens exist,<sup>40</sup> and he has said he probably spent more as an individual researching an extraterrestrial presence than anyone else in the United States ever has.<sup>41</sup> Bigelow is described as notoriously media shy, making the 60 Minutes interview a notable exception.<sup>42</sup> He has also described his grandparents having a close encounter with a UAP that filled the windscreen of their car before shooting away into the distance.<sup>43</sup>
