Ross Coulthart is an Australian investigative journalist and author who has won five Walkley Awards, including the Gold Walkley, and previously worked as an investigative reporter on 60 Minutes on Channel Nine and as chief investigations reporter for the Sunday Night programme.<sup>1</sup> He is best known in the context of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) research for his 2021 book *In Plain Sight*, a detailed survey of UAP cases, government responses, and the accounts of military and intelligence insiders. His reporting spans Australian, American, and international dimensions of the phenomenon.

Coulthart's interest in UAP began in the early 1990s when he interviewed serving senior Royal Australian Air Force officers who spoke openly of UAP sightings during their careers and encouraged him to take the subject seriously.<sup>2</sup> One anonymous officer told him that pilots routinely avoid reporting such encounters because doing so is career-ending, adding that "there is something weird happening that can't be dismissed".<sup>3</sup> These conversations shaped the investigative approach he would later apply in *In Plain Sight*.

A substantial portion of Coulthart's Australian research concerns the Australia, Western Australia, Harold Holt Naval Communications Station, located near Australia, Western Australia, Exmouth on the North West Cape. He found that two Royal Australian Air Force sighting reports document a UAP incident at the US Naval Communication Station near Exmouth on 25 October 1973,<sup>4</sup> and that declassified files held by the National Archives of Australia reveal anomalous sightings at North West Cape have been officially reported to the [Australian Department of Defence](/organisations/australian-department-of-defence) for decades by soldiers, tourists, a senior American officer at the base, and a local fireman.<sup>5</sup> He also established that no records of the 1973 sighting appear in declassified Royal Australian Air Force records from that year held by the National Archives of Australia.<sup>6</sup> Separately, he documented the account of [Annie Farinaccio](/people/annie-farinaccio), who in 1991 witnessed a UAP near the Harold Holt Naval Communications Station in the company of two Australian Federal Police officers; she described a craft with lights on its underside that performed extreme manoeuvres at speeds far beyond acknowledged human technology, an event catalogued in the 1991 Exmouth Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) sighting (Annie Farinaccio).<sup>7</sup> Coulthart also conducted original on-site research into the station itself, noting that its 12 powerful antenna tower arrays reach a highest point of 388 metres, making it the most powerful transmission station in the Southern Hemisphere.<sup>12</sup>

In the domain of Australian UAP history more broadly, Coulthart interviewed Joy Clarke and Colin Kelly on site at Westall High School on 2019-11-16 regarding the Westall Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) sighting 1966.<sup>8</sup> His book addresses that case alongside numerous other historical and contemporary incidents, drawing on his interviews with former officials at organisations including Australian Broadcasting Corporation Four Corners and Seven Network (7NEWS), where he worked, as well as his engagement with international sources.

At the international level, *In Plain Sight* gives extensive coverage to American UAP programmes and the individuals associated with them. Coulthart documents the career of [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), whom he describes as having led the Pentagon's secret Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) before resigning from the Department of Defense in 2017 in protest at excessive secrecy and internal opposition to UAP investigation.<sup>11</sup> He also references Nat Kobitz, described in his book as one of the most senior US Defense Department insiders ever to go public with claims of retrieved non-human technology,<sup>10</sup> and engages with the broader matter of Non-Human Craft Recovery Programs as alleged by multiple sources he interviewed. Coulthart states that US government and military insiders he spoke with admitted to knowledge of technology operating in skies, oceans, and orbit that far exceeds known human science.<sup>9</sup>

*In Plain Sight* was published in 2021 and positioned Coulthart as one of the more prominent journalists working in the UAP space from outside the United States. His work draws on declassified government documents, firsthand interviews with military personnel and intelligence officials, and his own examination of physical sites and archival records, including those held at the National Archives of Australia and in the archives of figures such as Edgar Mitchell. He has continued reporting on UAP matters through broadcast media following the book's publication, with appearances connected to 60 Minutes and other outlets in Australia and internationally. His location base for much of his research has included USA, Maryland, Baltimore alongside Australian locations.
