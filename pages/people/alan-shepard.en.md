Alan Shepard was an American astronaut who piloted the Freedom 7 capsule during the 1961-05-05 Freedom 7 sub-orbital flight. A subsequent inner-ear problem took him off flight status for six years, from 1964 until he was reinstated in 1969.<sup>1</sup> He underwent a secret and then-innovative inner-ear surgical procedure that allowed him to command the [Apollo 14](/projects/apollo-14) mission and complete the 1971 Apollo 14 landing on the Moon.<sup>2</sup>

During the final extra-vehicular activity of [Apollo 14](/projects/apollo-14), Shepard surprised NASA and the nation on live television by hitting the first golf shot taken on the Moon, on 1971-02-05.<sup>3</sup> He boasted during the live radio transmission that the ball "went miles and miles."<sup>4</sup>

Shepard took part in a formal in-flight study of cosmic ray visual phenomena (light flashes) conducted during Apollo 14, alongside crewmate [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell). He reported 10 light flashes in his right eye and 4 in his left.<sup>5</sup> On one occasion, Mitchell reported a flash at the same moment Shepard reported one in his right eye;<sup>6</sup> Shepard considered the two reports to have occurred within a fraction of a second of each other.<sup>7</sup> He never observed two light-flash streaks occurring simultaneously,<sup>8</sup> did not notice a halo effect following the flashes,<sup>9</sup> and did not notice a preferred direction for them.<sup>10</sup> Shepard felt that the flashes he observed appeared generally random in direction, without a consistent correlated pattern.<sup>11</sup> Some flashes spanned as much as 50 to 60 degrees of his peripheral field of view,<sup>12</sup> which he estimated at roughly 50 to 160 degrees horizontally.<sup>13</sup> In some cases a flash appeared broken in the centre, giving the impression of two dots, one immediately following the other.<sup>14</sup> He noted that light could seep in around the edges of the window shades when the spacecraft rolled so the sun struck some of the windows,<sup>15</sup> and he generally kept his eyes open during the observation period except when avoiding this sunlight seepage.<sup>16</sup> The crew closed their eyes when Mitchell turned on his flashlight during the sessions.<sup>17</sup> Shepard considered that setting aside a defined period of time for observation was necessary, since without it, other distracting thoughts made the phenomenon too random to quantify.<sup>18</sup>

Only two Apollo 14 astronauts, Shepard and Mitchell, landed on the Moon, and each carried one Hasselblad camera.<sup>19</sup> Early in the mission's first EVA, Shepard photographed Mitchell deploying the television camera just north of Fra Mauro (Apollo 14 landing site), an image from the Ken Johnston collection known as AS14-66-9301 "Mitchell Under Glass" (Apollo 14 photograph).<sup>20</sup> Administrative records catalogue it as a genuine Apollo 14 photograph.<sup>21</sup> Shepard also took the Apollo 14 landing site panorama held in the Ken Johnston Collection.<sup>22</sup> Photograph AS14-66-9279 (Apollo 14 photograph), taken by Shepard while standing on the east side of the Lunar Module Antares, contains major elements of the same scattered-light geometry seen in Johnston's version of AS14-66-9301.<sup>23</sup>

[Richard C. Hoagland](/people/richard-c-hoagland) said that he found a deliberately faked photograph in the middle of Shepard's own account of his journey to the Moon in the photographic section of Moon Shot (book).<sup>24</sup> The image, catalogued as the Alan Shepard fake golf shot photograph (Moon Shot), was published as a two-page spread showing both Shepard and Mitchell simultaneously in the same frame, something no single Hasselblad camera could have captured.<sup>25</sup> Mitchell had erected a colour television camera at the landing site two days before Shepard's golf shot, which broadcast a running sequence of images to Earth in parallel with Shepard's live radio transmission.<sup>26</sup> In the faked image, the flag pointed in the opposite direction and Shepard's body position differed from the real event.<sup>27</sup> The faked centrefold copies and reverses portions of the Lunar Module Antares, adding astronauts, a golf ball, a TV antenna, shadows and a flag as cutouts from other Apollo 14 imagery, with the flag pointing in the opposite direction from the actual Hasselblad photograph and live television downlink.<sup>28</sup>

According to Dark Mission, there have been persistent rumours that [Neil Armstrong](/people/neil-armstrong) and Shepard were also Freemasons, though this has never been confirmed.<sup>29</sup> Hoagland separately speculated that Armstrong and [Buzz Aldrin](/people/buzz-aldrin) may have been pre-selected for the Apollo 11 mission for the same reasons he believed applied to Shepard and the Freedom 7 flight.<sup>30</sup>
