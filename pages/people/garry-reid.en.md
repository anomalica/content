Garry Reid was a US Department of Defense official who, within the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), ran security and law enforcement for the department<sup>1</sup> and controlled security clearances across the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod), including that of [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo)<sup>2</sup>. He replaced Michael Higgins in the post, having been brought into OUSD(I) from the special operations community; he is not related to Senator [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid)<sup>3</sup>.

Elizondo was assigned to OUSDI from 2008-09-28 to 2017-10-04<sup>4</sup>, initially as an action officer in Partner Engagement<sup>5</sup> before becoming a branch chief managing classified information in support of the Office of Military Commissions<sup>6</sup>. Reid said that, to the best of his knowledge, Elizondo had no job responsibilities related to the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip)<sup>7</sup>, a programme that had been terminated in 2012<sup>8</sup>, and said Elizondo, a former Department of Defense employee, aggrandised his role in it in public statements<sup>9</sup>. For his part, Elizondo had kept Reid out of the loop on anything pertaining to AATIP<sup>10</sup>.

On 2017-10-03, Elizondo submitted a resignation letter requesting immediate termination of his employment<sup>11</sup>, telling his direct supervisor, according to Reid, that he was resigning because a fleeting job opportunity required his immediate availability<sup>12</sup>. He completed all administrative and security out-processing, including formal debriefs for Special Access Programs and Sensitive Compartmented Information, within 24 hours of submitting the letter<sup>13</sup>, and was cleared out of OUSDI the following day<sup>14</sup>; Reid said Elizondo gave no reason for the sudden departure<sup>15</sup>. In a phone call three days later, on 2017-10-06, Elizondo told Reid the job had been too stressful on his family, according to Reid<sup>16</sup>, though Reid's second memorandum on the matter states Elizondo gave three different explanations for the resignation<sup>17</sup>. By Elizondo's account, John Garrity, his immediate supervisor on the Guantanamo Bay portfolio, telephoned him after his resignation to say Reid wanted to see him immediately<sup>18</sup>; Reid asked him what he was supposed to do with the resignation letter<sup>19</sup>, and told him that if he did not come in, Reid would have no option but to tell people he was acting crazy, warning it could affect his security clearance<sup>20</sup>.

A second resignation letter was delivered to the OUSDI Chief of Staff's office on or about 2017-10-04, after Elizondo had already left<sup>21</sup>, hand-delivered by someone other than Elizondo himself<sup>22</sup>. It was inconsistent with Elizondo's first resignation letter, which Elizondo had personally handed to his supervisor<sup>23</sup>, and was formatted as a Memorandum for Record despite using the salutation "Mr. Secretary"<sup>24</sup>; given its uncertain provenance, OUSDI retained a copy but did not forward it to the Secretary of Defense's office<sup>25</sup>. On 2017-12-18, OUSDI located a classified email chain involving Reid's office on video declassification, generated by Elizondo in August 2017, in which he had asked a Navy civilian employee for help declassifying one or more videos<sup>26</sup>; the video attached was the same footage later broadcast on national news on 2017-12-17<sup>27</sup>, though it had not been determined whether it was properly declassified, what process was followed, or how it reached the media<sup>28</sup>. On 2017-12-15, acting on information contained in a [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) inquiry, Reid notified the OUSDI Security Officer that Elizondo may have misused government systems and might intend to release government footage or information obtained during his employment<sup>29</sup>.

OUSDI referred the timing and circumstances of Elizondo's resignation to the [Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI)](/organisations/air-force-office-of-special-investigations-afosi) for immediate counterintelligence review<sup>30</sup>. After a YouTube video surfaced, Reid requested an informal review of Elizondo's conduct by AFOSI<sup>31</sup>, an organisation responsible for providing investigative support to the Office of the Secretary of Defense, including investigation of assigned personnel<sup>32</sup>. Reid was separately pursuing options to investigate Elizondo's possible mishandling of sensitive information<sup>33</sup>, and on 2017-12-21 met with AFOSI agents to initiate a formal inquiry into the matter<sup>34</sup>; he also opened an inquiry through AFOSI into how the [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) videos had been released<sup>35</sup>. Reid seized Elizondo's computers and files from his office and questioned every one of his former employees after the resignation<sup>36</sup>, and Elizondo later received a call from an agent of the Defense Counterintelligence Security Agency (DCSA), an agency overseen by Reid, regarding allegations that he had improperly declassified the videos<sup>37</sup>.

Employees filed complaints about Reid's conduct with the Department of Defense Inspector General<sup>38</sup>, and the DoD Office of Inspector General investigated Reid over a litany of issues after a complaint from a female department employee worked its way through official channels<sup>39</sup>. Elizondo said the investigation examined allegations including a sexual relationship with a subordinate employee, sexual harassment, and fostering a hostile work environment<sup>40</sup>, and concluded that Reid had violated Joint Ethics Regulations by creating the appearance of an inappropriate relationship or preferential treatment with a female subordinate, and by mishandling Controlled Unclassified Information<sup>41</sup>. Elizondo also said Reid had withheld his resignation from Secretary of Defense [James Mattis](/people/james-mattis), hoping he would never need to reveal it<sup>42</sup>. Reid was subsequently reassigned from OUSD(I)<sup>43</sup> to the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) as a "special advisor"<sup>44</sup>, after the inspector general investigation into the allegations against him had concluded<sup>45</sup>.
