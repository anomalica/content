Christopher K. Mellon is a former senior US national security official who served as Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence under both the Clinton and George W. Bush administrations,<sup>1</sup> a position described as the third highest intelligence role at [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon).<sup>2</sup> In that capacity he oversaw all [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) intelligence activities and [Special Access Programs](/matters/special-access-programs),<sup>3</sup> and was only one step removed from the Secretary of Defense.<sup>4</sup> Prior to his DoD roles, Mellon served as a staffer on the [Senate Intelligence Committee](/organisations/senate-intelligence-committee), eventually rising to minority staff director,<sup>5</sup> and earlier in his career served as a legislative assistant to Senator [William Cohen](/people/william-cohen), whom he accompanied to the Pentagon when Cohen became Secretary of Defense.<sup>6</sup> His Senate Intelligence Committee work required him to review top-secret Sensitive Compartmented Information projects inside Special Access Programs.<sup>7</sup> He is a prominent member of the Mellon family, described as an heir to one of America's oldest financial and industrial dynasties.<sup>8</sup>

Mellon's engagement with [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) began during his Senate committee work and continued through his senior DoD role.<sup>9</sup> In early 2017, while serving as an unpaid consultant for the Office of Naval Intelligence,<sup>10</sup> Mellon was introduced to [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) by [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan), a mutual contact from the CIA.<sup>11</sup> Mellon visited Elizondo's Pentagon office, where Elizondo presented him with what Mellon described as incontrovertible evidence that strange unidentified aircraft were routinely violating sensitive US military airspace, craft lacking any discernible markings or means of propulsion.<sup>12</sup> Following that initial meeting, Mellon concluded that the [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) were not experimental US aircraft, a position informed by his own extensive contacts and access to classified programmes.<sup>13</sup> He later recalled that after being briefed on the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program), he expressed frustration that despite having overseen all DoD Special Access Programs, he had had zero visibility into the UAP topic.<sup>14</sup>

Concerned that the UAP issue was not receiving adequate institutional attention, Mellon collaborated with Elizondo and [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) on a strategy to bring the matter before Congress, concluding that the only route to change was to engage the press in order to draw congressional interest.<sup>15</sup> Mellon arranged for a journalist to receive a padded envelope containing compact discs encoded with three unclassified UAP videos, drawing on his knowledge of where the footage had been stored.<sup>16</sup> Those videos -- the [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video), [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video), and [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video) -- were subsequently reported on by [Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean) and [Bryan Bender](/people/bryan-bender), with the resulting New York Times articles quoting Mellon, [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), and Elizondo.<sup>17</sup> Mellon also attempted to arrange a meeting between Elizondo and Secretary of Defense James Mattis, and worked to develop a broader plan to bring UAP information to Congress and the public in the event of Elizondo's resignation.<sup>18</sup> On the afternoon of the [Elizondo Pentagon Resignation 2017](/events/elizondo-pentagon-resignation-2017), Mellon met with Elizondo, Semivan, Puthoff, and Kean at a hotel near the Pentagon.<sup>19</sup>

In late 2017, Mellon joined Elizondo and Puthoff as a co-founder of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science), taking the title of national security affairs adviser.<sup>20</sup> The organisation described its mission as encompassing fresh approaches to UAP science and engineering, educating journalists and the public, and producing film, television, and publishing content.<sup>21</sup> Mellon and Elizondo co-produced a television programme for the History Channel under the condition that it be authentic, with no scripted drama, conspiracy theories, or unverified witnesses.<sup>22</sup> In late 2020, Mellon departed [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) along with Elizondo, Steve Justice, and subsequently Puthoff.<sup>23</sup> After leaving the organisation, Mellon continued working his relationships on Capitol Hill to advance the UAP disclosure effort.<sup>24</sup>

A central strand of Mellon's advocacy concerned legislative action. He proposed that an oversight committee request an unclassified public report on UAP from the Director of National Intelligence as a means of elevating the issue without new public expenditure.<sup>25</sup> During the COVID-19 pandemic, Mellon and Elizondo drafted UAP language for insertion into legislation, and subsequently managed to insert a requirement into the [COVID Relief Bill UAP Language 2020](/matters/covid-relief-bill-uap-language-2020) stipulating that the DoD release a UAP report the following summer.<sup>26</sup> Much of the wording in Senate Report 116-233 on UAP was attributed directly to Mellon.<sup>27</sup> He also facilitated classified briefings for members of Congress on UAP, including references to exotic retrieved materials; the first such briefing on retrieved unexplained objects was provided to staff of the [Senate Armed Services Committee](/organisations/senate-armed-services-committee) on 2019-10-21 and to staff of the [Senate Intelligence Committee](/organisations/senate-intelligence-committee) two days later.<sup>28</sup> Mellon, Elizondo, and Stratton constructed a multi-year strategy called the Five Pillars of Engagement to educate Congress and address legal hurdles facing UAP disclosure.<sup>29</sup> Participants in these congressional engagement efforts included pilots [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) and [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich), whom Mellon arranged to meet through official debriefings, and senator [Kirsten Gillibrand](/people/kirsten-gillibrand), among others.<sup>30</sup>

Mellon has made a number of public statements on the substance of UAP evidence. He stated that the UAP observed during the [2015 Atlantic Coast UAP Sightings](/events/2015-atlantic-coast-uap-sightings) were encountered by Navy pilots nearly every time they conducted training in restricted airspace off the US east coast, and that warning signs were posted at one naval base of the potential for mid-air collisions with unidentified aircraft.<sup>31</sup> He noted that information provided to [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) acknowledged eleven near mid-air collisions between military aircraft and UAP since 2004.<sup>32</sup> He stated with a high degree of confidence, based on his positions in the DoD and his knowledge of classified programmes, that the observed UAP are not US technology.<sup>33</sup> In May 2020 he stated that some UAP-related material samples had different isotopic ratios from anything normally found on Earth, raising the question of whether they formed in a different solar system.<sup>34</sup> In January 2023 he acknowledged having spoken with several credible people who claimed the US has evidence of alien technology in its possession,<sup>35</sup> and a 2023 article reported that he had been instrumental in arranging classified briefings for officials that included references to exotic retrieved craft of what witnesses described as non-human origin, in connection with the [Reverse engineering of recovered craft of non-human origin](/topics/reverse-engineering-of-recovered-craft-of-non-human-origin) issue.<sup>36</sup> [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) DoD Threat Scenario Slides](/documents/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-dod-threat-scenario-slides) found on Mellon's website stated that the United States was incapable of defending itself against some of the observed technologies.<sup>37</sup>
