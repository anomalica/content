Christopher K. Mellon is a former United States Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence who became one of the most prominent public advocates for congressional and government transparency on unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP). He is part of the Mellon family associated with Carnegie Mellon University.<sup>1</sup> Mellon served as deputy assistant secretary of defense for intelligence in the Clinton and George W. Bush administrations.<sup>2</sup> He began his career as a staffer to the [US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](/organisations/us-senate-select-committee-on-intelligence),<sup>9</sup> where his duties included reviewing top-secret Sensitive Compartmented Information projects inside Special Access Programs<sup>16</sup> and monitoring [USA, Nevada, Area 51](/places/nevada-area-51-usa) on the committee's behalf.<sup>17</sup> He later served as Minority Staff Director of the same committee.<sup>65</sup> He had previously worked as a legislative assistant for [William Cohen](/people/william-cohen);<sup>11</sup> when Cohen became Secretary of Defense, he brought Mellon onto his staff at the Pentagon as one of his trusted principals in 1997.<sup>34,15</sup> As Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security and Information Operations, the third-highest intelligence position at the Pentagon,<sup>12</sup> Mellon was one step removed from the Secretary of Defense<sup>44</sup> and oversaw all Department of Defense intelligence activities and Special Access Programs.<sup>45</sup> He was one of the founding authors and a champion of the bill that established the United States Special Operations Command, a proposal he fought long and hard to defend against opponents who considered a new Combatant Command unnecessary.<sup>56,57</sup> Mellon was in the Pentagon when American Airlines Flight 77 struck the building on 2001-09-11.<sup>60</sup>

During his time on the Senate Intelligence Committee staff, Mellon led inquiries attempting to explain the mass Belgian Wave sightings of 1989-1990, working exhaustively with the Pentagon and intelligence services.<sup>8,9</sup> He concluded there was no secret US triangular craft programme matching what witnesses had seen.<sup>10</sup> Separately, he was ordered by then Senate Appropriations Committee chairman Robert Byrd to investigate claims that the United States was secretly developing a black high-technology aerospace project known as Aurora.<sup>17</sup>

Mellon first met [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) at a closed-door Pentagon meeting in early 2017.<sup>62</sup> Mellon asked how he could be read onto the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip),<sup>39</sup> and Elizondo told him he would first need a specified list of security clearances, which Mellon obtained within a week.<sup>40,36</sup> Elizondo then spent three hours briefing Mellon on legacy UAP material in a [Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF)](/topics/sensitive-compartmented-information-facility-scif);<sup>37</sup> Mellon afterwards pledged his loyalty to the AATIP effort and became a trusted member of the team.<sup>38</sup> Mellon later gave Elizondo permission to share his name with officials who could help reach [James Mattis](/people/james-mattis),<sup>44</sup> and he met with Elizondo, [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) and journalist [Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean) for a four-hour meeting in a hotel lobby near the Pentagon that led to Elizondo's introduction to Kean.<sup>48,49,25</sup> Mellon was present at the 2017 launch of To The Stars Academy of Arts & Sciences<sup>19</sup> and went on to chair its National Security Policy and Scientific Advisory Board.<sup>64</sup> When Elizondo resigned from the Pentagon in 2017 to join [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa), he did so to rejoin [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), Semivan and Mellon.<sup>47</sup> The organisation's team also included [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice)<sup>66</sup> and [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge), who met Mellon through a cold call.<sup>67</sup> After months of speculation, Mellon, Elizondo and Justice departed TTSA, a change DeLonge took months to confirm formally to the Securities and Exchange Commission.<sup>20,21</sup>

Mellon and Elizondo, later joined by [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton), concluded that changing how the Pentagon handled UAP required pressuring [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) to force the issue, and began engaging with contacts on Capitol Hill.<sup>23,24,25</sup> They funnelled credible members of the military and intelligence community with UAP knowledge to congressional offices,<sup>26</sup> and Mellon drafted UAP report legislative language with Elizondo during the COVID-19 pandemic.<sup>27</sup> Mellon and Stratton developed a multiyear strategy, dubbed the Five Pillars of Engagement, to educate Congress and address disclosure hurdles;<sup>28,33</sup> this work drew support from Harry Reid, a longtime benefactor of Mellon and Elizondo's advocacy.<sup>34</sup> Mellon proposed that a congressional oversight committee request an unclassified public UAP report from the Director of National Intelligence, and he drafted and posted online a version of that reporting requirement.<sup>35,36</sup> Bipartisan senators and a representative worked with Mellon and Elizondo to craft UAP language for the National Defense Authorization Act.<sup>37</sup> Mellon has been instrumental in arranging classified UAP briefings for members of Congress and other officials, including references to exotic retrieved materials,<sup>32</sup> spending nearly two decades in the US intelligence community across this work.<sup>33,34</sup>

Mellon has spoken at length about the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), which he describes as involving the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) warship USS Nimitz, worth approximately five billion US dollars and crewed by thousands,<sup>75</sup> operating off San Diego, California with the escort USS Princeton.<sup>76</sup> He recounts that an unidentified aircraft with a small radar cross-section appeared on the USS Princeton's radar heading toward the battle group,<sup>77</sup> that the four aviators involved observed the object had no wings or exhaust,<sup>79</sup> and that it was white, oblong and approximately 12 metres long.<sup>80</sup> Mellon describes the object ascending to roughly 24,000 metres before dropping at supersonic speed to a full stop about 15 metres above the ocean.<sup>81</sup> He says he met with one of the pilots involved and confirmed the account.<sup>68</sup> Mellon has also stated that he is aware of recovered materials with isotopic ratios different from terrestrial metals<sup>72</sup> and, when asked by Glenn Beck in May 2020, said some materials he knew of had genuinely extraordinary properties<sup>71</sup> - though he has separately said that during his time overseeing Special Access Programs and visiting Area 51, he never saw an enormous spending line or evidence for craft such as the TR3B, and never encountered anything so orthogonal to conventional science that it would suggest an origin elsewhere. He has said he is aware of a number of well-placed officials who shared detailed information with him about an alleged non-human craft recovery programme,<sup>70</sup> while also noting that some potential sources distrust the leadership of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office established by Congress.<sup>71</sup>

Mellon has consistently argued, in his own assessment, that the anomalous objects observed surveilling US carrier battle groups are not American, Russian or Chinese technology,<sup>60</sup> considering it necessary to entertain the possibility of an alien origin given their apparent absence from any known terrestrial arsenal.<sup>61</sup> He has described naval aviators as highly credible observers - holding top-secret clearances, drug-tested, and trained in aeronautics<sup>58</sup> - and has said that the extent of UAP activity revealed by Navy aviators over restricted airspace was staggering,<sup>52</sup> noting that [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) F-22 fighters with superior sensors operated in the same training areas.<sup>15</sup> He has pointed out that the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) considers it a priority to avert strategic surprise, yet extraordinary vehicles surveilled a carrier battle group without action being taken to determine their origin.<sup>62</sup> Mellon also investigated the physics patents of Salvatore Pais and found disputes over their viability, with no indication of testing or prototypes.<sup>63</sup> He has said the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) is represented among the TTSA team alongside a prominent aerospace engineer, former senior Pentagon officials and an experimental physicist.<sup>71</sup>
