L. Ron Hubbard founded Dianetics and, later, the [Church of Scientology](/organisations/church-of-scientology).<sup>1</sup> Before that, in the mid-1940s, he was among the most active members of the Los Angeles branch of the Ordo Templi Orientis (OTO), alongside rocket engineer [Jack Parsons](/people/jack-parsons).<sup>2</sup> Hubbard came to Parsons' mansion in [California Pasadena](/places/california-pasadena-usa) in 1945,<sup>3</sup> and by 1946 the two men had carried out a variety of magical workings, mostly sexual rituals intended to achieve short-term goals.<sup>4</sup>

These workings culminated in the so-called Babalon Working, in which Hubbard was Parsons' partner while himself serving in naval intelligence during World War II.<sup>5</sup> As part of the ritual, the pair re-enacted a series of seances originally performed in the sixteenth century by [John Dee](/people/john-dee), Royal Astrologer to Queen Elizabeth I.<sup>6</sup> They meditated in the California desert for eleven days in an attempt to conjure an elemental spirit.<sup>7</sup> According to Dark Mission, the rituals also drew on Dee's Enochian calls, or angelic language, and specifically invoked the Egyptian god Osiris.<sup>8</sup> Dark Mission further states that the pair's magical practice, carried out in Southern California as the [NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)](/organisations/nasa-jet-propulsion-laboratory-jpl) was being founded nearby, had roots in ancient Egyptian tradition and invoked Osiris, Horus, and the rest of the Egyptian pantheon.<sup>9</sup> The same source states that [Aleister Crowley](/people/aleister-crowley) became alarmed on learning that Hubbard and Parsons were attempting to produce a Moonchild,<sup>10</sup> considering neither man experienced enough in the methods and consequences of his Thelemic sex magick to attempt it safely.<sup>11</sup>

In 1946 Hubbard left for New York with Parsons' money and his wife, Sara Northrup, then travelled on to Florida with the boat, the woman, and the remainder of Parsons' cash.<sup>12</sup> Northrup was the sister of Parsons' wife Helen Parsons and had been Parsons' lover before her involvement with Hubbard; the boat was a sailing vessel named the Harpoon.<sup>13</sup> Dark Mission also states that a Bill of Sale produced by the Church of Scientology shows Parsons had in fact sold the Harpoon to Hubbard.<sup>14</sup> The same source states that Parsons summoned the Martian god Bartzabel in an attempt to conjure a storm against the fleeing pair.<sup>15</sup> Hubbard and Northrup did meet a fierce storm at sea and were forced back to Miami, where Parsons had them arrested.<sup>16</sup> Hubbard subsequently married Northrup and went on to found the Church of Scientology.<sup>17</sup>

Dark Mission also sets out a different account of this period, according to which the Church of Scientology states that Hubbard was in fact sent by the [United States Navy (USN)](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) to infiltrate and break up a black magic cult operating in Los Angeles.<sup>18</sup> According to Mike Bara, church members sought to correct what they viewed as mischaracterisations of Hubbard and Parsons in Dark Mission's original treatment,<sup>19</sup> arguing that Hubbard had been sent to infiltrate and disrupt Parsons' activities at 1003 Orange Grove Avenue rather than being a willing participant in Parsons' pursuits within the OTO.<sup>20</sup> Bara wrote that documents supplied by the church inclined him toward the interpretation that Hubbard had been sent to infiltrate the Ordo Templi Orientis (O.T.O.) rather than being a willing participant,<sup>21</sup> and that Hubbard was, on the church's account, a Navy intelligence officer at some point during his service career.<sup>22</sup> Bara further judged that Hubbard was residing in the area and still an active naval officer while befriending Parsons and infiltrating the OTO,<sup>23</sup> that he would have made an ideal agent for such an infiltration,<sup>24</sup> and that the OTO's Pasadena headquarters was damaged for years by his actions,<sup>25</sup> never fully recovering from the harm done to Parsons.<sup>26</sup>

Bara cited Navy documents showing Hubbard was assigned to intelligence duties in 1941-07,<sup>27</sup> and that he remained in the Navy reserves until 1950-10.<sup>28</sup> Hubbard was officially listed as a patient at Oak Knoll Naval Hospital in [San Francisco](/places/california-san-francisco-usa) until 1945-12.<sup>29</sup> Dark Mission states that a telegram from Hubbard to the Navy dated 1945-10-13 places him in Hollywood, near Parsons and the OTO lodge in Pasadena,<sup>30</sup> and that minutes from a Federation of American Scientists meeting on 1945-11-15 record both Hubbard and Parsons in attendance.<sup>31</sup> Hubbard had an intelligence background<sup>32</sup> and attended one of the first university courses on nuclear physics ever taught in the United States.<sup>33</sup> [Richard C. Hoagland](/people/richard-c-hoagland) has said he considers it plausible that Hubbard was an [Office of Naval Intelligence](/organisations/office-of-naval-intelligence) informant sent to infiltrate and destabilise the OTO and the Federation of American Scientists, after reviewing documentation supplied by the Church of Scientology,<sup>34</sup> and now holds that the weight of the evidence supports the church's version of events over the less flattering alternative account.<sup>35</sup>

Jacques Vallée has linked Parsons and Hubbard to the same process of extraterrestrial-superiority belief.<sup>36</sup> Parsons collaborated with Crowley and Hubbard while both launching rockets and engaging in provocative rituals in the Los Angeles desert.<sup>37</sup> In 2003-03, Bill Chalker said that he and Diane Harrison were contacted by an overseas source who suggested the Gundiah case had more to do with a misguided secret Scientology mission to find a buried spaceship, as described in the Mission Earth science fiction series by Scientology's founder, L. Ron Hubbard.<sup>38</sup> Harrison has noted that Hubbard lived out his last years on a remote US property he called Whispering Winds - sharing its name with the Gundiah property - which he had described as a quiet place, a perfect place to hide.<sup>39</sup>
