Harry Reid served as a United States Senator for Nevada from 1987 to 2017, rising to become Senate Majority Leader, and served on the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence.<sup>1</sup> During his tenure, Reid visited [Area 51](/places/area-51) air force base in Nevada and developed a long-standing interest in space phenomena.<sup>1</sup> According to Reid, his interest in UFOs was prompted by his friendship with entrepreneur [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow).<sup>2</sup> Around 2007, Bigelow informed Reid that an official from the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) had approached him seeking to visit his Utah ranch, where Bigelow conducted research.<sup>3</sup> Shortly afterwards, Reid met directly with DIA officials and learned they wished to establish a formal UFO research programme.<sup>4</sup>

Reid subsequently worked to secure congressional funding for what would become the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip). He approached senators [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens) and [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye) to secure US$22 million for a UAP study,<sup>5</sup> summoning both to a secure room in the Capitol to discuss the proposed programme.<sup>6</sup> Reid later described that meeting as "one of the easiest meetings I ever had."<sup>7</sup> The [Pentagon Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) UFO Program, 2007–2017](/matters/20072017-pentagon-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-ufo-program) was initially largely funded at Reid's direction;<sup>8</sup> at his direction, the Defence Appropriations Acts of Fiscal Years 2008 and 2010 appropriated $22 million for the DIA to assess long-term foreign advanced aerospace threats.<sup>9</sup> The majority of that funding went to an aerospace research company operated by Robert Bigelow, a billionaire entrepreneur and longtime friend of Reid.<sup>10</sup> Reid, Stevens, and Inouye did not seek a public debate on the Senate floor about the funding, which Reid described as "black money" drawn from the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon)'s classified programmes budget.<sup>11</sup>

By 2009, Reid judged that the [AATIP programme](/matters/20072017-pentagon-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-ufo-program) had produced sufficiently significant results to warrant heightened security protections, citing "several highly sensitive, unconventional aerospace-related findings."<sup>12</sup> On 2009-06-24, Reid wrote to then Deputy Secretary of Defense [William Lynn III](/people/william-lynn-iii) requesting that the programme be designated a DoD [Special Access Program](/matters/special-access-programs).<sup>13</sup> This request, formalised in the [Reid Harry 2009 AATIP Special Access Program Memo](/documents/reid-harry-2009-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-special-access-program-memo), was ultimately rejected by the Pentagon.<sup>14</sup> The [2009 Harry Reid SAP Request](/events/2009-harry-reid-sap-request) subsequently became a point of public controversy during disputes over the role of [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) in AATIP, with Reid's memo cited by some as evidence of Elizondo's involvement and with an official letter from Reid said to counter Pentagon denials of that role.<sup>19,20</sup>

Reid was publicly supportive of the AATIP programme following its disclosure. In 2017, he stated: "I'm not embarrassed or ashamed or sorry I got this thing going. I think it's one of the good things I did in my congressional service."<sup>15</sup> He also expressed the view that UFO sightings went underreported within the military because service members feared being laughed at or stigmatised.<sup>16</sup> On the question of the objects' origin, Reid maintained a cautious position, stating: "If anyone says they have the answers now, they're fooling themselves. We do not know."<sup>17</sup> Reid separately told *The New Yorker* that he had been informed for decades that Lockheed held retrieved materials, and that he had sought classified approval to examine them but had been denied.<sup>18</sup>

Reid supported UAP disclosure efforts in the final years of his life, during which he was battling pancreatic cancer. He died on 2021-12-28, one day after President Biden signed UAP legislation into law.<sup>22</sup> Senator Chuck Schumer subsequently stated that he honoured the legacy of his mentor Harry Reid by continuing to fight for transparency on unexplained phenomena.<sup>23</sup>
