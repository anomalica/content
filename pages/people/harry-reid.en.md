Harry Reid was born the son of a rock miner in Searchlight, Nevada,<sup>1</sup> and worked as a United States Capitol Police Officer while putting himself through law school.<sup>2</sup> He served in the US House of Representatives from 1983<sup>3</sup> and in the [United States Senate](/organisations/united-states-senate) from 1987 to 2017,<sup>4</sup> rising to Senate Majority Leader over his thirty years in the chamber.<sup>5</sup> He served two years on the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence<sup>6</sup> and was for years a member of the so-called Gang of Eight, entitled to be briefed on highly classified intelligence matters including Waived Unacknowledged Special Access Programs.<sup>7</sup> As senator for Nevada, the state that housed Area 51,<sup>8</sup> he visited the air force base during his time in office, a site associated with speculation about strange glowing craft and future aerospace technology.<sup>9</sup>

Reid has long had an interest in space phenomena<sup>10</sup> and has long admitted a strong interest in Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena.<sup>11</sup> In 1989, during his first term in the Senate, he was the first person the journalist [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp) told about crashed-saucer accounts he had gathered outside his newsroom.<sup>12</sup> Knapp said Reid told him he was interested in hearing more, beginning a private conversation between the two that continued for the next three decades.<sup>13</sup> Knapp kept Reid informed of [UFO](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) developments he was pursuing as a journalist, and Reid in turn helped him obtain information otherwise inaccessible.<sup>14</sup> After the [National Institute for Discovery Science](/organisations/national-institute-for-discovery-science) began its own investigations, Knapp introduced Reid to the organisation.<sup>15</sup> Russia's ongoing interest in UFOs was one of the topics of interest to Reid, [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) and the Institute,<sup>16</sup> and Knapp said this information made a lasting impression on Reid and became a key factor in a secretive program launched a few years later.<sup>17</sup> Reid said his own interest in UFOs came from Bigelow.<sup>18</sup>

Around 2008, Reid received a letter from a senior [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) scientist - according to the book *In Plain Sight*, likely a physicist never publicly named - whose worldview had been transformed by events he witnessed at Skinwalker Ranch.<sup>19</sup> Bigelow sent Reid the letter.<sup>20</sup> Reid did not name the scientist but said he had visited the ranch himself, and that what the scientist saw there convinced him it was a real phenomenon needing investigation.<sup>21</sup> Reid said the scientist was clearly rattled by his purported experience and told him it was time such phenomena were properly investigated,<sup>22</sup> and Reid claims the account prompted him to seek congressional funding for a study into UAP.<sup>23</sup> [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) has said he suspects Reid was being deliberately vague, and knew more from the classified intelligence world than the letter alone suggested.<sup>24</sup>

Reid approached Senators [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye) and [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens) to secure funding for a study into the UAP mystery.<sup>25</sup> He summoned the two senators to a secure room in the Capitol,<sup>26</sup> where, Reid said, [John Glenn](/people/john-glenn), the astronaut and former senator, had years earlier told him the federal government should be looking seriously into UFOs.<sup>27</sup> According to Reid, Stevens said "I've been waiting to do this since I was in the Air Force,"<sup>28</sup> and told him he had seen a UAP himself during a wartime flight and that they needed to look into it.<sup>29</sup> None of the three senators wanted a public debate on the Senate floor about the funding;<sup>30</sup> Reid later said, "This was so-called black money. Stevens knows about it, Inouye knows about it. But that was it, and that's how we wanted it."<sup>31</sup> Bigelow's team began its UAP investigations before DIA funding arrived in July 2008, when Reid guaranteed the black-budget funding for the program that became the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap).<sup>32</sup> At his direction, the Defense Appropriations Acts of Fiscal Years 2008 and 2010 appropriated 22 million US dollars for the DIA to assess long-term and over-the-horizon foreign advanced aerospace threats to the United States.<sup>33</sup> The programme was supported by then-DIA director Lieutenant General Michael D. Maples and funded through the efforts of Reid, Stevens and Inouye.<sup>34</sup> The [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) was itself initially largely funded at Reid's request.<sup>35</sup> Most of that money went to an aerospace research company run by Bigelow.<sup>18</sup> Knapp has said, however, that the 22 million US dollars Reid secured did not fund AATIP directly, but instead went to a separate effort managed by the DIA.<sup>36</sup> [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) has said the United States Air Force and most of the US intelligence community refused to support the congressionally funded investigation.<sup>37</sup> Stevens, Reid's other backer, died in a plane crash two years after backing the funding.<sup>38</sup>
