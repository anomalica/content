Harry Reid served as a United States Senator from Nevada from 1987 to 2017, rising to the position of Senate Majority Leader.<sup>1</sup> Before entering politics, he worked as a United States Capitol Police officer while putting himself through law school.<sup>2</sup> As senator for Nevada -- the state that houses Area 51 -- he developed an early familiarity with classified aerospace programmes that, by his own account, contributed to his interest in unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP).<sup>3</sup> He stated that he had long had an interest in space phenomena,<sup>4</sup> and that this interest was further shaped by his friendship with [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow), the billionaire aerospace entrepreneur.<sup>5</sup>

The immediate catalyst for congressional action came in 2007, when Bigelow told Reid that an official with the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) had approached him seeking to visit Bigelow's research ranch in Utah.<sup>6</sup> Reid subsequently met with DIA officials and learned they wished to establish a research programme on [unidentified flying objects (UFOs)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo).<sup>7</sup> He then convened a private meeting with fellow senators [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens) and Daniel K. Inouye in a secure room in the United States Capitol.<sup>8</sup> Stevens, who had himself reported seeing UAP as a pilot during World War Two, endorsed the initiative.<sup>9</sup> The three senators jointly controlled congressional spending for black-budget [Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) programmes<sup>10</sup> and chose not to seek a public Senate floor debate on the funding.<sup>11</sup>

At Reid's direction, the Defense Appropriations Acts for fiscal years 2008 and 2010 appropriated USD 22 million for the [DIA](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) to assess long-term and over-the-horizon foreign advanced aerospace threats, forming the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap).<sup>12</sup> That funding was programmed for the years 2008 to 2012.<sup>13</sup> The majority of the money was directed to an aerospace research company run by Bigelow.<sup>14</sup> A successor initiative, the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), emerged from this work and was also supported by Reid.<sup>15</sup> He later succeeded in authorising an additional USD 10 million for AATIP through a separate request.<sup>16</sup> Reid and his fellow supporting senators took what one account characterised as a secular, science- and intellect-based approach to the UAP problem,<sup>17</sup> though the US Air Force and most components of the intelligence community declined to support the congressionally funded investigation.<sup>18</sup>

On 2009-07-24 Reid sent a memorandum to William Lynn III, then Deputy Secretary of Defense, requesting that AATIP be granted Special Access Program (SAP) status, citing substantial progress and several highly sensitive, unconventional aerospace-related findings.<sup>19</sup> The Harry Reid memo to William Lynn III on Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) Special Access Program status (2009-07-24) was later authenticated by the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod),<sup>20</sup> and the 2009 Harry Reid SAP Request became a significant document in subsequent debates over AATIP's structure and personnel. The request for SAP designation was denied by the Pentagon.<sup>21</sup> The memo was later cited by some as bearing on the question of [Luis Elizondo's](/people/luis-elizondo) role in AATIP; earlier releases had redacted the names of Luis D. Elizondo and Hal Puthoff from the document.<sup>22</sup> Reid also separately asked that the KONA BLUE Prospective Special Access Programme be established, alongside Senator Joseph Lieberman, with the promise of additional funding.<sup>23</sup>

Reid retired from Congress in 2017.<sup>24</sup> Following the December 2017 New York Times reporting on AATIP, he expressed pride in having initiated the programme and described it as one of the good things he had done in his congressional service.<sup>25</sup> He acknowledged publicly that he did not know where the observed objects had come from, stating that anyone claiming to have answers was fooling themselves.<sup>26</sup> In later years he stated his belief that both the government and the private sector held actual retrieved physical materials, and that such information should be disclosed.<sup>27</sup> Journalist [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp), who received the unofficial report on the 2004 USS Nimitz incident from Reid, also obtained names of crew involved in that encounter through the document.<sup>28</sup> Reid visited Area 51 during his Senate term<sup>29</sup> and served on the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence.<sup>30</sup> He had also recounted that for decades he was told that Lockheed held retrieved materials, and that he sought but was denied classified approval to examine them.<sup>31</sup> Senator Chuck Schumer later stated that he honoured Reid's legacy by fighting for transparency on unexplained phenomena.<sup>32</sup>

Reid had been battling pancreatic cancer for three years in his final period, stating he would keep fighting until President Biden signed the UAP disclosure legislation.<sup>33</sup> He died peacefully in his sleep on 2021-12-28, one day after President Biden signed the UAP bill into law.<sup>34</sup>
