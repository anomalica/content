Robert Weiss, also referred to in sources as Rob Weiss, was executive vice president of [Lockheed Martin Skunk Works](/organisations/lockheed-martin-skunk-works)<sup>1</sup>, serving as its executive vice-president and general manager until 2019<sup>2</sup>. He has appeared in published accounts of contacts between the aerospace industry and figures pushing for UAP disclosure, principally musician [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge).

According to In Plain Sight, the 2016 leak of [John Podesta](/people/john-podesta)'s emails, catalogued here as the Podesta email leak correspondence with Tom DeLonge, contained a string of exchanges between Podesta and DeLonge revealing plans for a Google Hangout meeting between DeLonge, two US Air Force generals, and Weiss<sup>3</sup>. The book records the proposed attendee list as DeLonge, Podesta, Weiss, Hillary Clinton campaign assistant Milia Fisher, and retired Major General Michael Carey<sup>4</sup>. It further states that the correspondence shows DeLonge in contact with one of America's most senior private aerospace chief executives, a campaign manager for the Hillary Clinton presidential campaign, and two senior recently retired US Air Force generals, with UAP discussed among them<sup>5</sup>. Separately, according to Jason Samosa, presenter of The UFO Cover-Up Leads to One Secret Society — and One Final Event, the Podesta correspondence describes a meeting involving [William McCasland](/people/william-mccasland), Carey, Weiss and Podesta<sup>6</sup>.

In the book In Plain Sight, journalist Ross Coulthart said it was "a fair bet" that the Lockheed executive DeLonge met at a families' event was Weiss<sup>7</sup>, and the book separately identifies Weiss as probably the executive DeLonge refers to by the pseudonym 'BossMan', with whom DeLonge says he held a one-on-one conversation about the potential disclosure of the US government's purported secret UAP programme<sup>8</sup>. According to DeLonge, the executive he called 'BossMan', probably Weiss, told him that [Lockheed Martin](/organisations/lockheed-martin) could not be involved in any project associated with the UAP topic because there had never been any evidence that it existed<sup>9</sup>. DeLonge said he responded by invoking [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell), the sixth man to walk on the Moon, arguing that if Mitchell was telling every kid in the world the topic was real, there was a problem, and asking for time to address credibility issues<sup>10</sup>. DeLonge said he asked for a five-minute meeting alone with 'BossMan', and it was agreed that all other executives and engineers would leave the room<sup>11</sup>.

In that meeting, held around March 2016, DeLonge said he told 'BossMan' that he understood the national security implications of the UAP subject and was not naive to it<sup>12</sup>, and that over the previous thirty years there had been a programme to indoctrinate people to the idea that UFOs or UAP might be real<sup>13</sup>. DeLonge told Weiss that he knew about "some bad things" the government had done regarding the UAP subject<sup>14</sup>, prompting Weiss to demand to know what kind of bad things he meant<sup>15</sup>. DeLonge replied that if he were allowed to pursue his disclosure work, he would need advisers to guide him so that he did not misinform people<sup>16</sup>. DeLonge regards this exchange as the breakthrough point in gaining access to military and intelligence gatekeepers on the UAP subject<sup>17</sup>, and said that in the months that followed, progress on his project accelerated rapidly<sup>18</sup>. Within two weeks of the meeting, he said he was introduced to senior military and government figures, including US Air Force generals and intelligence officials, high-ranking [NASA](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) officials, and senior officials at the [White House](/organisations/the-white-house)<sup>19</sup>.
