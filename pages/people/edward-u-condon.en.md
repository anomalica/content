Edward U. Condon was a physicist and former Director of the National Bureau of Standards who served as scientific director of an 18-month study of flying saucers funded under a 325,000 US dollar contract between the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) and the [University of Colorado](/organisations/university-of-colorado), beginning in April 1968.<sup>1</sup> Condon led the Condon Committee,<sup>2</sup> based in Colorado Boulder, where the investigation - headed by a physicist then close to retirement - received a sizable grant, with its report due in 1969.<sup>3</sup> Condon and his team of scientists were recruited by the USAF to conduct an objective and independent investigation into [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap), work that became known as the [Condon Report](/documents/condon-report).<sup>4</sup> Separately, the University of Colorado UFO Project began, in 1967, spending 512,000 US dollars given to Condon by [The Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon).<sup>5</sup>

In 1967, [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee) visited the University of Colorado physics department and had lunch with Condon and the staff of the University of Colorado UFO Project.<sup>6</sup> Vallée said he told Condon that he believed the problem of [Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) could be solved scientifically.<sup>7</sup> Condon said he compared the UFO problem to the existence of God, a matter he believed science had nothing to say about.<sup>8</sup> Vallée said he disagreed, believing science could have analysed the observations and reached a conclusion.<sup>9</sup> Vallée later argued that Condon and his team could have conducted a serious scientific study of UFOs in 1967 but did not, and that it was now too late for science to resolve the social and political consequences of the spreading belief in space contact.<sup>10</sup>

Condon and his team, chosen for their presumed impartiality, produced the Condon Report, which concluded that no evidence existed for the reality of UFOs.<sup>11</sup> According to *In Plain Sight*, the 1968 report concluded that further study of UAP could not be justified<sup>12</sup> and rejected the extra-terrestrial hypothesis.<sup>13</sup> The report gave the USAF the justification it sought to close [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book).<sup>14</sup> According to *Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs*, the Condon Committee inquiry - the Air Force-funded UAP study at the University of Colorado chaired by Condon - declared the UAP topic devoid of scientific merit.<sup>15</sup> In a paper, [Harry Turner](/people/harry-turner) criticised the 1968 Condon Committee report, the USAF-funded study of UFOs directed by Condon.<sup>16</sup>

Condon fired the minority group from the Condon Committee after being outraged by the publication of an internal memorandum, and afterwards ran the project without serious consideration of the possible reality of the UFO phenomenon.<sup>17</sup> He later destroyed the Condon Committee's project files, feeling strongly about the uselessness of studying UFOs.<sup>18</sup> Three days before his death in March 1974, Condon was still urging a physicist friend to drop his own study of UFOs.<sup>19</sup> When told that a documentary about UFOs was being prepared, he advocated that all the footage be burned.<sup>20</sup>
