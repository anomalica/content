Ryan Graves is a former [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) pilot who holds the rank of Lieutenant and served approximately a decade, including two deployments in Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Inherent Resolve.<sup>1</sup> In 2014 he was an [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Foxtrot pilot assigned to Strike Fighter Squadron 11 (VFA-11), the Red Rippers, stationed at Naval Air Station Oceana in Virginia Beach.<sup>2</sup> He subsequently became one of the most publicly prominent military Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) witnesses, testifying before Congress and founding the advocacy organisation Americans for Safe Aerospace.

The sightings that first drew Graves's attention began after radar upgrades were made to his squadron's jets, whereupon aviators started detecting unknown objects in their airspace — initially attributed to radar errors but later correlated with multiple onboard sensors including infrared and visual identification.<sup>3</sup> His squadron began seeing UAP hovering over restricted airspace southeast of Virginia Beach in 2014 following those radar updates.<sup>4</sup> The most serious early incident occurred during a training mission in Warning Area W-72, approximately 16 kilometres off the coast of Virginia Beach, when two [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) Super Hornets were split by a UAP.<sup>5</sup> The object was described as a dark grey or black cube inside a clear sphere,<sup>6</sup> came within approximately 15 metres of the lead aircraft,<sup>7</sup> and was estimated to be between approximately 1.5 and 4.5 metres in diameter.<sup>8</sup> On a separate occasion during the [2015 Atlantic Coast UAP Sightings](/events/2015-atlantic-coast-uap-sightings), two fighters flying approximately 30 metres apart experienced a translucent sphere with a cube inside passing through the gap between them, described as resembling a giant soap bubble with the points of the cube touching the outside of the sphere.<sup>9</sup>

Graves described the UAP consistently as exhibiting characteristics beyond the performance envelope of any known aircraft: no visible wings, flaps, engines, control surfaces, or means of propulsion,<sup>10</sup> no exhaust plume,<sup>11</sup> and the ability to hold position completely motionless in Category 4 hurricane-force winds of approximately 220 kilometres per hour.<sup>12</sup> The same objects would accelerate to supersonic speeds of Mach 1.1 to 1.2 in erratic and rapid behaviours.<sup>13</sup> They also outlasted fighter jets, operating continuously throughout the day,<sup>14</sup> with pilots training off the Atlantic coast reporting sightings every day for at least a couple of years.<sup>15</sup> After the 2014 Virginia Beach UAP encounter, Graves's squadron submitted a safety report but received no official acknowledgement, and no further reporting mechanism was offered.<sup>16</sup> Encounters grew so frequent that aircrew began discussing the UAP risk as part of regular preflight briefs.<sup>17</sup>

Graves also provided accounts relating to the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) and the USS Theodore Roosevelt. He told both [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) and the [History Channel](/organisations/history-channel) programme [Unidentified Inside Americas UFO Investigation](/documents/unidentified-inside-americas-ufo-investigation) that the publicly released Gimbal footage was a portion of a longer, higher-resolution video he had viewed aboard the Roosevelt.<sup>18</sup> He stated the on-board version showed a cluster of smaller craft flying in an inverted V-shape in front of the larger Gimbal object, a portion absent from the publicly released clip.<sup>19</sup> The [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) itself depicts an object rotating through a 90-degree angle while stationary in a hover.<sup>20</sup> During the 2015 USS Roosevelt UAP Encounters, the advanced targeting forward-looking infrared (FLIR) recording shows a single rotating object, while the pilots referred to a fleet of objects not visible on the FLIR, which Graves witnessed in the debrief as radar data on the situational awareness page.<sup>21</sup> When the 2017 New York Times UAP report was published, Graves recognised the voices and the video itself, having been present when it was taken and when it was debriefed.<sup>22</sup> Graves and other USS Theodore Roosevelt pilots were eventually summoned to give confidential briefings to members of Congress.<sup>23</sup>

Graves founded Americans for Safe Aerospace as a venue for UAP witnesses who lacked a safe intake process.<sup>24</sup> By the time of his 2023 Congressional testimony, more than thirty witnesses had come forward to the organisation and almost 5,000 Americans had joined it.<sup>25</sup> On 2023-07-26 Graves testified as Executive Director of Americans for Safe Aerospace before the House Oversight Committee UAP Hearing 2023, appearing alongside [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch) and [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor).<sup>26</sup> The three witnesses concurred that UAP are having an impact on training ranges and need to be treated as a legitimate issue.<sup>27</sup> During the hearing Graves warned that the low-tech objects shot down during Balloongate 2023 in February 2023 should not be conflated with the advanced UAP his colleagues had witnessed.<sup>28</sup> He also appeared in Episode 4 of [Unidentified Inside Americas UFO Investigation](/documents/unidentified-inside-americas-ufo-investigation) on the [History Channel](/organisations/history-channel) in June 2019, alongside [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) and [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor).<sup>1</sup>

Beyond his own testimony, Graves advanced several institutional recommendations. He suggested that NASA's existing Aviation Safety Reporting System be used as a short-term trusted platform for pilot UAP reporting,<sup>29</sup> and has worked with the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (AIAA) to help establish a UAP integration committee to bring engineering expertise to the problem.<sup>30</sup> He drew attention to the gap in civilian reporting infrastructure, noting that commercial pilots were reaching out to Americans for Safe Aerospace because they were having UAP experiences similar to military pilots but had no reporting system available to them.<sup>31</sup> Graves also relayed to the 2023 House Oversight UAP Hearing an account of the 2003 Vandenberg Air Force Base UAP sighting, passed to him by a witness who approached Americans for Safe Aerospace, in which a large red square object estimated to be approximately 90 metres across approached the base from the ocean, hovered at low altitude over a launch facility, remained for about 45 seconds, and then departed rapidly over the mountains. He has been a consistently attributed source in published UAP accounts, including by [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), and Sean Cahill, and his accounts form part of the primary source record for the Atlantic UAP Cube-Sphere Encounter and the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) reporting period. The AN/APG-79 Radar and ATFLIR Pod were among the sensor systems Graves cited as having tracked and imaged the UAP, with radar detecting objects at distance and the FLIR providing electro-optical or infrared imagery once in sufficient proximity.<sup>21</sup>
