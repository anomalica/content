Luis Elizondo is a former US military intelligence officer who describes himself as a "career spy"<sup>1</sup> and who spent some twenty years running military intelligence operations worldwide, including in Afghanistan, the Middle East, and Guantanamo.<sup>2</sup> By his own account, he had not considered [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) (UAP) seriously until 2008,<sup>3</sup> when he was asked to join a [Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) programme called the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program) (AATIP).<sup>4</sup> According to accounts from within the programme, he was personally hired in 2008 by James Clapper, then US Deputy Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.<sup>5</sup> Elizondo is described in public records as having been based at the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) from 2008 until his retirement in 2017.<sup>6</sup> He retired as an official there,<sup>6</sup> and AATIP is said to have been transferred from the Defense Intelligence Agency to the Office of the Secretary of Defense under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence when Elizondo took it over as director in 2010.<sup>7</sup>

Elizondo claims to have directed AATIP from the fifth floor of [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon)'s C Ring,<sup>8</sup> where the programme's stated mission was to collect and analyse information involving anomalous aerial vehicles (AAV), known colloquially as UFOs.<sup>9</sup> He took over the programme in 2010 with a focus on the national security implications of UAP documented by US service members.<sup>10</sup> Government funding for the programme dried up in 2012,<sup>11</sup> but Elizondo and a small number of colleagues continued the work informally,<sup>12</sup> during which time he worked with officials from the US Navy and the [CIA](/organisations/cia).<sup>13</sup> Elizondo resigned from [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) in October 2017, citing excessive secrecy and internal opposition to the UAP investigation.<sup>14</sup> His resignation letter, dated 2017-10-04<sup>15</sup> and bearing the apparent seal of the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi),<sup>16</sup> was addressed to then-Defence Secretary [Jim Mattis](/people/jim-mattis) and questioned why more attention was not being devoted to the UFO issue.<sup>17</sup> The letter alluded to internal Pentagon opposition and strongly implied Elizondo was leaving because the Department of Defense was not treating the matter as the threat he believed it to be.<sup>18</sup> Before departing, he arranged for three US Navy UAP videos to be declassified.<sup>19</sup>

Elizondo's public emergence followed a 2017 article in [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times), "Glowing Auras and 'Black Money': The Pentagon's Mysterious U.F.O. Program", which launched him into wider public view.<sup>20</sup> Three reporters shared bylines on that piece and met Elizondo at a nondescript Washington hotel prior to publication.<sup>21</sup> Several days after his resignation he joined [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) (TTSA),<sup>22</sup> alongside [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) and former [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) official [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon).<sup>23</sup> TTSA collapsed in late 2020, with Elizondo among those who departed alongside Mellon, Steve Justice, and subsequently Puthoff.<sup>24</sup> Elizondo later published a book, *Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs*, and his personal website is luiselizondo-official.com.<sup>25</sup> He is married to [Jennifer](/people/elizondo-jennifer-wife) and has two daughters named Taylor and Alex;<sup>26</sup> his parents are [Luis D. III](/people/luis-d-iii-father-elizondo) and [Janise Elizondo](/people/janise-elizondo-mother).<sup>27</sup>

The question of whether Elizondo genuinely directed AATIP remains contested. Pentagon spokeswoman Dana White confirmed to Politico that AATIP existed and was run by Elizondo,<sup>28</sup> and [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), as an [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program](/matters/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program) (AAWSAP) and AATIP contractor and senior adviser, stated that he attended meetings, provided briefings, and met with staff under the aegis of Elizondo's leadership until Elizondo resigned.<sup>29</sup> An email documenting the transfer of Elizondo's AATIP responsibilities to [Neill Tipton](/people/neill-tipton) in 2017 was eventually released following a successful Freedom of Information Act appeal;<sup>30</sup> until 2024-05 the [Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) had asserted that all of Elizondo's emails had been deleted.<sup>31</sup> Conversely, Pentagon spokesman Christopher Sherwood stated that Elizondo had no assigned responsibilities for AATIP while he worked in OUSDI,<sup>32</sup> and separately stated that Elizondo interacted with the Defense Intelligence Agency office managing AATIP while the programme was ongoing but did not lead it.<sup>33</sup> Critics, including journalist Keith Kloor, noted that Elizondo did not supply documentary materials directly validating his role, and that his resignation letter did not mention AATIP or his claimed directorship of it.

In his public statements following resignation, Elizondo described what he called "The Five Observables" - characteristics most commonly seen in UAP: positive lift or anti-gravity, instantaneous acceleration, hypersonic velocity, low observability, and trans-medium travel.<sup>34</sup> He told CNN that AATIP had identified aircraft capable of extreme manoeuvrability beyond healthy G-forces, hypersonic velocities, low observability, and positive lift seemingly defying aerodynamics,<sup>35</sup> and stated his personal belief that "there is very compelling evidence that we may not be alone."<sup>36</sup> He characterised the UAP under investigation as performing manoeuvres of 600 to 700 G-forces,<sup>37</sup> flying at approximately 21,000 kilometres per hour,<sup>38</sup> with no obvious signs of propulsion, no wings, and no control surfaces.<sup>39</sup> In a 2021 interview with GQ Magazine, Elizondo stated his strong belief that the US government is in possession of exotic material associated with UAPs,<sup>40</sup> and during a May 2019 Fox News interview with Tucker Carlson he stated, when asked whether the US government holds debris from a UFO: "simply put, yes."<sup>41</sup> He worked alongside [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton), credited [David Grusch](/people/david-grusch) for coming forward publicly, and facilitated meetings between US Navy pilots - including [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) - and congressional oversight committees, with [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) describing the US Air Force as notably unhelpful in that process. The [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) was among the materials declassified and released in connection with the broader UAP disclosure effort in which Elizondo was a central figure, alongside the [UAP Legacy Programs](/matters/uap-legacy-programs) debate that followed. Elizondo also acknowledged [UAP Biological Effects](/matters/uap-biological-effects) as a subject of concern within AATIP's remit, and the wider disclosure effort he participated in contributed to the passage of the [UAP Disclosure Act](/matters/uap-disclosure-act) and related legislative milestones. Associates from TTSA including [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) and [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) were credited by Elizondo in his book's acknowledgements for their roles in bringing the UAP topic to the media. The [Orb UAP Phenomenon](/concepts/orb-uap-phenomenon) and questions about [Nonhuman Intelligence](/concepts/nonhuman-intelligence) were themes Elizondo addressed in later interviews, including his 2021 statement to GQ that UAPs appear to be "intelligently controlled by something, because they are responding and reacting to human actions." Elizondo's accounts have also been cited in the context of the [Australian Department of Defence](/organisations/australian-department-of-defence) and its own UAP-related inquiries, reflecting the international scope of interest his disclosures generated.
