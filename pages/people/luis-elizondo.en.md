Luis Elizondo is a former intelligence officer with the United States government.<sup>1</sup> Accounts describe a career spent running military intelligence operations in Afghanistan, the Middle East and Guantanamo over twenty years,<sup>2</sup> and separately as having served in Afghanistan, the Middle East and Latin America,<sup>3</sup> as a trained senior Special Agent holding top-secret compartmentalised clearances,<sup>4</sup> in a career spanning work as a counterintelligence special agent, a case officer and an intelligence practitioner.<sup>5</sup> In 2008, James Clapper hired him into the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi),<sup>6</sup> where he remained based until his retirement in 2017.<sup>7</sup> He was tasked to join the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap),<sup>8</sup> and is described as having led the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), a secretive Pentagon unit established at the urging of Senator Harry Reid to study [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap).<sup>9</sup> In 2010 he took over the programme, focusing on the national security implications of UAP documented by US service members.<sup>10</sup>

AATIP's mission, according to Elizondo, was to collect and analyse information involving anomalous aerial vehicles (AAV).<sup>11</sup> According to In Plain Sight, the programme's mission was to conduct scientific-based intelligence investigations of UAP incursions into controlled US airspace.<sup>12</sup> Elizondo has described AATIP as a loose-knit mix of scientists, electro-optical engineers, avionics specialists and intelligence experts, often working part-time,<sup>13</sup> and according to David Fravor, [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) was also part of the programme, led by Elizondo out of the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod).<sup>14</sup> Elizondo has reported that the AAVs studied by AATIP had been observed reaching 600 to 700 G-forces<sup>15</sup> and flying at approximately 21,000 kilometres per hour,<sup>16</sup> could evade radar,<sup>17</sup> could fly through air and water and possibly space,<sup>18</sup> showed no obvious signs of propulsion, wings or control surfaces,<sup>19</sup> and appeared to defy the natural effects of Earth's gravity.<sup>20</sup> AATIP determined that a real phenomenon was present in the sky, though its nature, method of operation, intent and full capabilities remained unknown.<sup>21</sup> In relation to the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), Elizondo asked David Fravor what he thought the unidentified craft were doing there,<sup>22</sup> and suggested to him that the objects could have been "prepping the battlefield" by observing US military operations.<sup>23</sup> Elizondo has said the object disappeared instantaneously, with a radar operator reporting five seconds later that it was waiting at the aircraft's cap point,<sup>24</sup> and described it as having no visible propulsion, no obvious signs of lift, no wings, no rudders, no control surfaces, no ailerons, no elevators, no cockpit and no rivets;<sup>25</sup> he has said he considers the object to have responded intelligently to Fravor's actions.<sup>26</sup>
