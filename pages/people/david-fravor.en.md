David Fravor is a retired Commander in the United States Navy who served as a naval aviator for 18 years before retiring after 24 years of total military service.<sup>1</sup> Before entering the Navy, Fravor was enlisted in the Marines, who recommended him for the Naval Academy.<sup>2</sup> He holds an undergraduate degree in oceanography<sup>3</sup> and an MBA from the University of Houston.<sup>4</sup> His early flying career was spent on [A-6 Intruder (aircraft)](/objects/a-6-intruder-aircraft) aircraft on the west coast, stationed at Naval Air Station Whidbey Island, Washington.<sup>5</sup> He subsequently attended the Navy Fighter Weapons School — commonly known as Top Gun — in 1997, Class 4,<sup>6</sup> graduating with honours according to [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo).<sup>7</sup> Fravor later commanded [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41), known as the Black Aces, which comprised 12 aircraft and several hundred personnel.<sup>8</sup>

In November 2004, at the time of the events for which he is principally known, Fravor had held command of VFA-41 for only one month, making him the most junior Commanding Officer at the O-5 level in the Navy at that time.<sup>9</sup> On 2004-11-14, he served as pilot of an [F-18F Super Hornet (aircraft)](/objects/f-18f-super-hornet-aircraft) with the callsign FASTEAGLE 01.<sup>10</sup> [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41) was attached to Carrier Air Wing 11 and embarked aboard [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz), which was conducting a workup cycle in preparation for a combat deployment to the Persian Gulf in support of ground forces in Iraq.<sup>11</sup> During a scheduled air-to-air training exercise, Fravor's two-aircraft flight was handed off to [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) and received vectors towards an unknown contact.<sup>12</sup> [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) had reportedly been tracking anomalous objects on its Aegis combat system for approximately two weeks prior to this date.<sup>13</sup>

Upon arriving at the intercept location off the coast between San Diego and Baja California, Fravor's first visual indication of the contact was a disturbance on the ocean surface below — frothy, boiling-looking water — beneath an object he would later describe in detail.<sup>14</sup> All four aircrew in the two [F-18F Super Hornet (aircraft)](/objects/f-18f-super-hornet-aircraft) observed a small white object moving abruptly over the [Water Disturbance](/objects/water-disturbance) with no rotors, rotor wash, or visible flight control surfaces.<sup>15</sup> Fravor described the [AAV](/objects/aav) as approximately 14 metres long, shaped like an elongated egg or "Tic Tac", and uniformly white across its entire body with no wings, windows, markings, or visible propulsion.<sup>16</sup> He stated it appeared to hover, using the phrase "holding like a Harrier".<sup>17</sup> Fravor detached the wingman aircraft — FASTEAGLE 02, flown by [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) — to hold high cover at approximately 6,100 metres while he descended to intercept.<sup>18</sup> As he descended, the [Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV)](/concepts/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav) appeared to reorient its longitudinal axis toward his aircraft and began to climb to meet him,<sup>19</sup> before accelerating away at supersonic speed and disappearing. [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) subsequently reported the object had reappeared at Fravor's pre-briefed combat air patrol point.<sup>20</sup> Fravor assessed that the [AAV](/objects/aav) had travelled approximately 97 kilometres in under one minute.<sup>21</sup>

Fravor confirmed that no video footage was captured during his visual encounter; all four crew members observed the object with the naked eye only.<sup>22</sup> A subsequent flight, launched after Fravor's debriefing, included [Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood), who recorded the forward-looking infrared (FLIR) video of the [AAV](/objects/aav) that was later released by the US Government.<sup>23</sup> Fravor stated that a radar tape from the [APG-73 Radar](/objects/apg-73-radar) — which reportedly showed active jamming of the aircraft's radar during Underwood's intercept — was never publicly released and its whereabouts remain unknown.<sup>24</sup> The [2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter) was never officially investigated and none of Fravor's crew were ever formally questioned in the immediate aftermath.<sup>25</sup> It was not until 2009 that Jay Stratton, described by Fravor as part of the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) led by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), contacted Fravor to investigate the incident.<sup>26</sup>

Fravor's public profile grew substantially from 2016 onwards. [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) contacted Fravor in 2016,<sup>27</sup> and [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) subsequently worked with journalists to publish articles in the [2017 NYT Pentagon UAP Article](/documents/2017-nyt-pentagon-uap-article) in December 2017, bringing the Nimitz incident to wide public attention.<sup>28</sup> Fravor was interviewed on Fox News on 2017-12-20,<sup>29</sup> and later participated in a *60 Minutes* story on UAP alongside [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich), and Christopher Mellon.<sup>30</sup> In July 2023, Fravor delivered sworn written and oral testimony at the [Fravor House Oversight Committee Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) Testimony, 2023](/events/2023-fravor-house-oversight-committee-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-testimony) alongside [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) and [David Grusch](/people/david-grusch).<sup>31</sup> In that testimony, Fravor assessed the [AAV](/objects/aav) as far superior in performance to his [F/A-18F](/objects/fa-18f) and as not operating under any known aerodynamic principles applicable to objects flying in Earth's atmosphere,<sup>32</sup> and called for greater congressional oversight of any programmes relating to UAP technology. Fravor has stated that he came forward publicly in part because a friend told him he was the one person who could not be discredited, and that his testimony would lend credibility to reporting on the subject.<sup>33</sup> He has consistently characterised himself not as a UAP enthusiast but as an expert witness on the basis of direct observation.<sup>34</sup>
