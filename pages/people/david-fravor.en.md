David Fravor is a retired [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) aviator who served for 18 years<sup>1</sup> and held the rank of Commander. Originally enlisted as a United States Marine Corps (USMC) member,<sup>2</sup> he was recommended by the Marine Corps to attend the United States Naval Academy, which he completed.<sup>3</sup> His ambition to fly developed in 1969, after watching Neil Armstrong walk on the moon during the Apollo 11 Moon landing shortly before his fifth birthday.<sup>4</sup> After receiving his wings he was initially assigned to A-6 Intruder aircraft on the west coast,<sup>5</sup> subsequently flying single-seat F/A-18 Hornet aircraft until the end of 2001 before transitioning to F/A-18F Super Hornet variants.<sup>6</sup> He attended the Navy Fighter Weapons School, widely known as Top Gun, completing Class 4 in 1997,<sup>7</sup> and became a qualified Strike Fighter Weapons and Tactics Instructor.<sup>8</sup>

Fravor served as Commanding Officer of [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41), known as the Black Aces,<sup>9</sup> flying the two-seat F/A-18F in that role.<sup>10</sup> At the time of the encounter described below he had accumulated 16 years of flying experience.<sup>11</sup>

On 2004-11-14, Fravor was the pilot of FastEagle 01 and Commanding Officer of [VFA-41](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41) when his flight was airborne from [USS Nimitz (CVN-68)](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68) on a training mission that was subsequently cancelled and redirected to real-world tasking.<sup>12, 13</sup> The flight comprised four aircrew across two F/A-18F aircraft.<sup>14</sup> [USS Princeton (CG-59)](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59), an Aegis cruiser, vectored the flight toward an unidentified radar contact it had been tracking for approximately two weeks — the 2004-11-14 flight being the first occasion aircraft had been airborne to investigate.<sup>15</sup> This event is documented as the 2004 USS Nimitz Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) incident and is also referred to as the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), the Tic Tac Sighting, and the 2004 USS Nimitz Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) encounter.

Upon reaching the contact area, Fravor's first indication of the unknown object was a visual disturbance on the sea surface beneath it.<sup>16</sup> He described this disturbance as frothy waves and foam, almost as if the water was boiling, localised directly below what he would identify as the [anomalous aerial vehicle (Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV))](/topics/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav).<sup>17</sup> His [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) was not equipped with a forward-looking infrared (FLIR) pod,<sup>18</sup> and his AN/APG-73 radar, set to Range While Search on the 150 kilometre scale, showed a clean display with no targets.<sup>19</sup> Fravor described the AAV as an elongated egg or "Tic Tac" shape, approximately 14 metres in length, uniformly white across its entire body.<sup>20</sup> It was hovering in a manner he compared to that of a Harrier using Vertical/Short Takeoff and Landing thrust vectoring,<sup>21</sup> oriented north-to-south and making minor displacements in both axes while maintaining a consistent altitude.<sup>22</sup> Fravor descended from approximately 3,000 metres at approximately 650 kilometres per hour to take a close visual pass on the object.<sup>23</sup> As he manoeuvred, the AAV appeared to reorient its axis toward the approaching aircraft, at which point the water disturbance ceased.<sup>24</sup> As Fravor completed his manoeuvre, the AAV ascended rapidly and moved aft of the F/A-18F at supersonic speed.<sup>25</sup> His flight subsequently searched for both the AAV and the water disturbance but found nothing.<sup>26</sup> During the intercept Fravor attempted an unsuccessful helmet lock using the Joint Helmet Mounted Cueing System<sup>27</sup> and later stated he had not thought to use its recording capability as it was rarely used for that purpose.<sup>28</sup>

No video was recorded during Fravor's encounter; all four aircrew observed the object visually.<sup>29</sup> [Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood) subsequently obtained forward-looking infrared footage on a later sortie, and when Fravor's crew viewed that recording they confirmed it matched what they had seen.<sup>30</sup> [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) was among the aircrew on the flight. Following the engagement, Fravor reported to the Carrier Intelligence Center and discussed the AAV contact with the Carrier Air Wing Intelligence Officer,<sup>31</sup> who, while not initially inclined to take the report seriously, acknowledged having no reason to question an account from such an experienced and well-regarded squadron commanding officer.<sup>32</sup> Fravor's crew were not asked to sign any non-disclosure agreement.<sup>33</sup> His account of the Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) contact has been corroborated by several other pilots involved in the wider incident.<sup>34</sup> The encounter has attracted sustained interest from researchers and commentators, and Fravor has discussed it publicly in multiple forums, including with journalist [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp) and in a 2020 interview with [Lex Fridman](/people/george-knapp). It has also been referenced in connection with figures including [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves), and [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch) in the broader context of [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) disclosure discussions, and with the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) and the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod). The involvement of [Jim Slaight](/people/jim-slaight) has also been noted in connection with the wider Nimitz carrier strike group events of that period.
