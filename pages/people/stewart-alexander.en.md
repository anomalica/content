Stewart Alexander is a British physical medium who has given his life to physical mediumship for close to fifty years<sup>1</sup>, since developing an interest in the subject in 1968 at the age of twenty-two<sup>2</sup>. He has always lived as a part-time medium, running a business with his wife seven days a week while raising two children<sup>3</sup>, and today spends much of his time with his two grandchildren<sup>4</sup>. He kept his mediumship separate from his home and business life to such an extent that his two sons did not know of his involvement until he gave them copies of his 2010 memoir, An Extraordinary Journey<sup>5</sup>. Despite publishing that book, he has remained a private person who prefers to avoid publicity<sup>6</sup>.

At the time of his first sitting with White Feather, Stewart Alexander worked as a design engineer at a large international engineering company<sup>7</sup>. He had not set out to become a medium himself, but hoped only to help create the conditions in which phenomena might occur<sup>8</sup>. During Stewart Alexander's first paranormal experience with White Feather at his brother's home circle, attended only by his brother and sister-in-law after he had sat in the group for months without any phenomena occurring<sup>9</sup>, Stewart Alexander was entranced without warning by an entity calling himself White Feather, who became his principal spirit guide<sup>10</sup> and went on to be his longtime spirit guide, the first spirit control to make himself known to him<sup>11</sup>. When his brother switched off the red light, Stewart Alexander felt every nerve and muscle in his body twitch and jump, and experienced something entering his body while he found himself outside it<sup>12</sup>; he then heard himself speaking the words "I come, speak, brother, sister" from a distance<sup>13</sup>. Whenever White Feather controls him, his left hand becomes almost deformed, curling in on itself as if from severe arthritis, though without pain<sup>14</sup>.

White Feather gradually developed the ability to speak through Stewart Alexander while he was in trance<sup>15</sup>, and it took over ten years before he could speak independently through a levitated trumpet<sup>16</sup>. A second guide, Christopher (Stewart Alexander's spirit guide), arrived saying he had died as a young boy<sup>17</sup>, with the job of relaxing sitters and making them laugh<sup>18</sup>. Walter Stinson joined the team in 1992 and took charge of developing the physical phenomena<sup>19</sup>; he had worked with his sister Margery Crandon in her séance room after his death and intended to continue that work through Stewart Alexander<sup>20</sup>. Freda completed the team in 1996<sup>21</sup>, and Dr. Barnett has served as a physical healer for many sitters, able to speak independently of the medium and materialise fully during séances<sup>22</sup>. Stewart Alexander describes this team as close friends who became part of weekly sittings<sup>23</sup>. His current home circle was formed in 1988<sup>24</sup>, and he has sat with the same small group for almost thirty years<sup>25</sup>. He gave his first public séance in 1992<sup>26</sup>, and during larger public sittings, sitters were invited to test him in the light to confirm he was genuinely in a deep trance<sup>27</sup>. The physical manifestations in his séances rest on an energy substance referred to as ectoplasm, which the spirit team use to manipulate objects and to partially or fully materialise<sup>28</sup>.

Persistent doubts about the authenticity of the voices speaking through him were relieved when Stewart Alexander attended a séance with the English medium Leslie Flint in the early 1970s<sup>29</sup>, then internationally celebrated as one of the finest direct-voice mediums in the world<sup>30</sup> and a virtual stranger who knew nothing of Stewart Alexander's own mediumistic development<sup>31</sup>. Stewart Alexander's grandmother had raised him and his siblings after their parents' marriage collapsed<sup>32</sup>, and he had sat alone with her at her hospital bedside on the day she died, holding her hand, in a moment known to no one else<sup>33</sup>. During Stewart Alexander's séance with Leslie Flint where his grandmother's voice was heard, held two or three weeks after her death<sup>34</sup>, a voice identified as hers mentioned that same private moment at her bedside<sup>35</sup>. During Stewart Alexander's séance with Leslie Flint where White Feather confirmed his own left-hand deformity, Stewart Alexander asked White Feather, speaking independently through Flint, whether he had had any physical deformity during his earthly life<sup>36</sup>; White Feather replied "Do you mean my left hand?", confirming the same deformity Stewart Alexander experiences when controlled by him<sup>37</sup>.

[Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean) attended two séances with Stewart Alexander's circle in April 2015 and returned for two more in August 2015<sup>38</sup>, part of Leslie Kean's séances with Stewart Alexander (2015-04 - 2015-08). At her first sitting, Stewart Alexander was secured to his chair with cable ties threaded through the wooden arms, removable only with a wire cutter<sup>39</sup>, and Kean confirmed they were locked tightly enough that neither hand could slide free<sup>40</sup>. Directed by Walter Stinson, Kean held Stewart Alexander's hand and felt the locked cable tie against his wrist<sup>41</sup>; his arm then suddenly moved upward, carrying her hand with it and producing a snapping sound<sup>42</sup>, and she found the tie remained locked and unbroken on the chair arm even though her hand had been inside it a moment before<sup>43</sup> - Walter Stinson then passed the still-closed tie through the solid arm of the chair and told her it was hers to keep<sup>44</sup>. Novelist [Whitley Strieber](/people/whitley-strieber) personally witnessed the levitation of spirit trumpets during the 1988 (era) private weekly séance of Stewart Alexander witnessed by Whitley Strieber<sup>50</sup>; a trumpet came up to his face and rubbed against his nose<sup>51</sup>, and he examined both the room and the trumpet before and after the séance, seeing it clearly in the dim red light<sup>52</sup>, noting that the trumpet - made of paper and unconnected to any wires - could not have been manipulated by magnets<sup>53</sup>. Strieber said the séances involved no trickery, since there was no motive for it<sup>54</sup>. While sitting as medium in a circle at Alan Crossley's home, part of Alan Crossley's dry-carpet incident, Stewart Alexander and others watched a vase of flowers topple onto the floor, spilling water onto the carpet<sup>55</sup>; when Crossley tried to wipe up the water, the carpet was found totally dry<sup>56</sup>. In 2014, Reville Mohr, a sceptic who did not believe in mediumship or the afterlife, flew from Australia with his sister Leone Holdsworth to attend a sitting with Stewart Alexander<sup>57</sup>. In 2016, Kevin Kussow emailed Stewart Alexander for advice<sup>58</sup>, as part of Kevin Kussow's book test through Stewart Alexander (2016-03); Stewart Alexander had never met Kussow and knew nothing about his home<sup>59</sup>. During Sitting where a grieving woman told Stewart Alexander "Thank you for saving my life", a woman travelled over 320 kilometres to attend a séance at the home of Stewart Alexander's circle leader<sup>60</sup>; she came into communication with her deceased partner about two hours into the sitting<sup>61</sup>, and afterwards embraced Stewart Alexander, telling him "Thank you for saving my life."<sup>62</sup> Stewart Alexander has interviewed countless people who sat with mediums Alec Harris, Minnie Harrison, and Helen Duncan<sup>63</sup>, and Kath Matthews was a medium who became his first mentor<sup>64</sup>. Writing in 2010, David Fontana said that, aside from a few unconvincing accusations by ill-informed individuals, Stewart Alexander's long career had been free from attempts to cast doubt on the genuine nature of the phenomena associated with his mediumship<sup>65</sup>.
