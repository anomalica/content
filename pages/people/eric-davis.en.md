Eric Davis is an American physicist who began his career in UAP-related research in 1996 when he joined the [National Institute for Discovery Science](/organisations/national-institute-for-discovery-science), the organisation founded by entrepreneur Robert Bigelow.<sup>1</sup> He had previously been on secondment from the University of Maryland at a US Air Force fighter wing in South Korea when he encountered an advertisement in a physics magazine that led him to the role.<sup>1</sup> Davis subsequently became one of the field investigators assigned to the [NIDS Skinwalker Ranch Investigation](/matters/nids-skinwalker-ranch-investigation) at [Skinwalker Ranch](/places/skinwalker-ranch) in Utah, where he reported a series of anomalous observations. In September 1996, he observed a craft with amber light descend below tree level and remain at ground level for half an hour,<sup>2</sup> and in November 1996 he and [Colm Kelleher](/people/colm-kelleher) watched a similarly lit object hurtle out from above a nearby bluff and execute a sharp high-speed 90-degree turn directly above them.<sup>3</sup> Davis, Kelleher, and the ranch manager also reported observing a large creature jump from a tree and walk away after the ranch manager fired multiple shots into it at point-blank range without any apparent effect, and without the creature leaving footprints.<sup>4</sup>

Davis later worked as a contractor for both the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program) (AATIP), operating alongside [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) at [EarthTech International](/organisations/earthtech-international), the company Puthoff had founded.<sup>5, 6</sup> Together, Davis and Puthoff constructed a gravity detection device of sufficient sensitivity to detect the gravitational field of an automobile parked in an adjacent car park.<sup>7</sup> Following an organisational relocation, Davis was among only three contractors — alongside Puthoff and [William Livingston](/people/william-livingston) — who remained involved with [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) and the AATIP programme.<sup>8</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) described Davis and Puthoff as having careers operating behind the scenes on the nation's most classified programmes.

In 2018, Davis gave a series of public statements on UAP matters. On 2018-06-24 he was interviewed by [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp) on [Coast to Coast AM](/organisations/coast-to-coast-am),<sup>9</sup> during which he stated that the BAASS and AATIP investigation into the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) had concluded the objects were real vehicles under intelligent control and an advanced technology not made on Earth by humans.<sup>10</sup> In the same interview he stated that the US government holds crash retrievals of [Non-Human Craft Recovery Programs](/matters/non-human-craft-recovery-programs) and that existing laboratory diagnostic technologies and material sciences were not advanced enough to understand what had been recovered,<sup>11</sup> and that the threat designation within AATIP reflected documented interference by the [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) with military operations.<sup>12</sup> In July 2020, Davis told [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) that the US held recovered physical UAP samples whose origin examination had failed to determine, saying "We couldn't make it ourselves."<sup>13</sup> He additionally told the newspaper that in March 2020 he had delivered a classified briefing to a [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) agency about retrievals from "off-world vehicles not made on this earth."<sup>14</sup> Davis also briefed staff from the [Australian Department of Defence](/organisations/australian-department-of-defence) and Senate committee members regarding UAP and what sources describe as a legacy programme,<sup>15</sup> and told Congress in private briefings that the propulsion of the Tic Tac, Go Fast, and Gimbal UAPs was consistent with off-world technology.<sup>16</sup>

Davis is widely known in UAP research circles as the alleged author of the document referred to as the [Wilson Davis Memo](/documents/wilson-davis-memo), also known as the "EWD Notes".<sup>17</sup> The document purports to record a private meeting between Davis and [Tom Wilson](/people/tom-wilson) — at the time a recently retired Director of the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) — held on 2002-10-16 in a parked car in a [Las Vegas](/places/las-vegas) office building car park, during the [Wilson Davis Las Vegas Meeting 2002](/events/wilson-davis-las-vegas-meeting-2002).<sup>18, 19</sup> Davis's handwritten notes of the meeting, dated 2002-10-16, are held in the archive of astronaut [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell).<sup>18</sup> [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), when asked about the document at an Arlington Institute address in February 2020, described [Thomas Wilson](/people/thomas-wilson) as "one of the joint chiefs of staff interviewed by my senior scientist colleague Eric Davis,"<sup>20</sup> and Davis is identified in UAP research literature as the document's primary author.<sup>21</sup> In a June 2020 letter, Admiral [Tom Wilson](/people/tom-wilson) issued a strong denial that any such meeting with Davis had taken place,<sup>22</sup> stating that the conversations attributed to him concerning Special Access Programs related to UFOs are "pure fiction."<sup>23</sup> Davis told journalist Steven Greenstreet that the notes "were leaked out of Ed Mitchell's estate"<sup>24</sup> and described them as "purportedly classified information," stating he was not at liberty to confirm or verify any aspect of the document.<sup>25</sup> Independent observers noted that neither Puthoff nor Davis had denied the memorandum's authenticity when given opportunities to do so.<sup>26</sup>

Davis also responded publicly to statements by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) regarding recovered UAP technology. When [James Iandoli](/people/james-iandoli) asked Davis about Elizondo's claim that the US government possesses recovered crashed and landed UAP technology hardware, Davis endorsed the statement as "1,000% accurate," adding that his national security non-disclosure agreements prevented him from commenting further.<sup>27</sup> Davis was hired by the [Aerospace Corporation](/organisations/aerospace-corporation) in December 2019.<sup>28</sup> A paper by Davis found within [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell)'s archive addressed UAP behaviour, describing mimicry techniques that Davis proposed the phenomenon employs, including entering the atmosphere with the appearance or trajectory of a meteor, behaving as pseudo-stars sitting stationary over certain regions, or mimicking the aggregate features of man-made aircraft.<sup>29</sup>
