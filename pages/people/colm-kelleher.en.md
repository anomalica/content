Colm Kelleher is a biochemist who served as an administrator of the [National Institute for Discovery Science](/organisations/national-institute-for-discovery-science) (NIDS), a privately funded organisation that investigated reports of anomalous phenomena.<sup>1</sup> He joined NIDS from a cancer research centre at the USA, Colorado, University of Colorado.<sup>2</sup> He later served as deputy administrator of [LLC Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies](/organisations/llc-bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies) (BAASS) during the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap).<sup>3</sup> The programme's database, based in [USA, Nevada, Las Vegas](/places/nevada-las-vegas-usa), incorporated reports from civilian organisations and foreign governments alongside new investigations carried out by boots-on-the-ground teams that Kelleher, as the programme's manager, dispatched.<sup>4</sup>

As part of his work for NIDS, Kelleher wrote reports on strange occurrences at Skinwalker Ranch and other parts of Utah and New Mexico.<sup>5</sup> In November 1996, [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) was sitting with Kelleher on the ranch house's back porch when a lit craft came out from above a nearby bluff and executed a sharp high-speed 90-degree turn directly overhead.<sup>6</sup> On another occasion, Davis alerted Kelleher and ranch manager Terry Sherman, and all three saw a huge creature jump down in front of them, far larger than any wildcat, comparable in size to a bear or a cow.<sup>7</sup> Kelleher himself said that he witnessed a great deal of anomalous phenomena at the ranch, none of which was captured on the numerous cameras set up around the property.<sup>8</sup>

Kelleher worked with Gabe Valdez, a New Mexico State Police officer with strong links to the Jicarilla Apache Tribe in USA, New Mexico, Dulce, during the 1997-09 National Institute for Discovery Science (NIDS) Dulce investigation.<sup>9</sup> According to In Plain Sight, Kelleher's resulting NIDS report contains interviews with multiple members of the Jicarilla Apache community who said that in September 1997 they had witnessed hovering UAP and had found a mysterious ventilation shaft dug into Mount Archuleta.<sup>10</sup>

In 2004, Kelleher said that there was information from the previous several years indicating that pieces of technology had certainly been recovered.<sup>11</sup> William Livingston flew to California with a team that included Kelleher and [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee) to meet [Garry Nolan](/people/garry-nolan).<sup>12</sup>

Kelleher was also involved in the 2000-09 Gary Lowrey alien claw discovery, agreeing to look into the case.<sup>13</sup> According to Hair of the Alien: DNA and Other Forensic Evidence for Alien Abductions, Kelleher, after consultation with [Roger Leir](/people/roger-leir), who had already made extensive enquiries into the case, felt the claw sample could be a good candidate for DNA study.<sup>14</sup> The same source states that Kelleher described the investigation as going far beyond the analyses usually afforded anomaly cases, evolving into a major molecular biology research project in its own right.<sup>15</sup> According to that account, six separate rounds of subsequent DNA analysis, using different and sometimes very novel approaches, were carried out, including a [polymerase chain reaction](/topics/polymerase-chain-reaction) (PCR) technique using novel primers targeting the most conserved DNA sequences on Earth, to resolve the case.<sup>16</sup> The same source further states that painstaking DNA analyses and bioinformatics methodology, carried out over a 12-month period by highly qualified teams of experts in three countries, established that the biological specimen found in the Gary Lowrey house was a mundane terrestrial mollusc.<sup>17</sup>
