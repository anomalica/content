Aleister Crowley was an English occultist who proclaimed himself the wickedest man in the world<sup>1</sup> and led the Ordo Templi Orientis (OTO). He was a member of numerous secret societies, including a 33rd-degree Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite Freemason.<sup>2</sup> He expressed his principal magical invocations in the Enochian language, which he held had to be pronounced in a specific way to be effective, and which are still used today by his many disciples.<sup>3</sup> According to Dark Mission, Crowley claimed that his Book of the Law (Crowley) had been dictated to him by an entity named Aiwass.<sup>4</sup>

[Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee) noted that occultists from [John Dee](/people/john-dee) and Giacomo Casanova to Crowley have often doubled as spies.<sup>5</sup> Vallée said Crowley was himself a minor espionage figure, and that his file at the Intelligence Service is said to have read "Use only with the most extreme caution".<sup>6</sup> According to Dark Mission, Crowley was considered a possible Nazi spy.<sup>7</sup> Vallée said Crowley's influence on [Adolf Hitler](/people/adolf-hitler) was more imaginary than real.<sup>8</sup>

[Jack Parsons](/people/jack-parsons), a rocket engineer at the [California Institute of Technology (Caltech)](/organisations/california-institute-of-technology-caltech), fell in with Crowley's OTO at their temple in Los Angeles in December 1938.<sup>9</sup> He was initiated into the OTO in 1939 and made a strong impression on fellow members, including Jane Wolfe, an actress who had spent time with Crowley.<sup>10</sup> After several years as a member of Crowley's Pasadena lodge, Crowley appointed Parsons to head Crowley's Southern California centre while Parsons was simultaneously employed by Caltech to develop rockets for the US Army.<sup>11</sup> By the early 1940s Parsons was a rising figure in the Ordo Templi Orientis (O.T.O.) and exchanged frequent correspondence with Crowley,<sup>12</sup> and the two worked together to expand the membership of the Los Angeles lodge.<sup>13</sup>

According to Dark Mission, Crowley and Parsons believed mankind was living in the Aeon of Osiris, a dark time when men were ruled by arbitrary laws that denied them their proper birthright.<sup>14</sup> The same source states that in a letter dated 1946-02-23, Parsons wrote to Crowley that he had obtained his "elemental", referring to Marjorie Cameron.<sup>15</sup> It further states that Crowley became alarmed when he learned that [L. Ron Hubbard](/people/l-ron-hubbard) and Parsons were attempting to produce a "Moonchild",<sup>16</sup> and that he considered neither man experienced enough in the methods and consequences of his Thelemic sex magick to safely raise one.<sup>17</sup> Paul Rydeen wrote that Parsons claimed the mantle of Antichrist for himself as the magickal heir prophesied by Crowley in the Book of the Law.<sup>18</sup>

Crowley wanted to remove Parsons as head of the OTO in America after the Babalon Working episode, but Parsons continued to lead the OTO for a few more years because of his strong support among the Pasadena members, before eventually quitting.<sup>19</sup> Karl Germer reported directly to Crowley.<sup>20</sup> Right after World War II, a branch of Crowley's OTO flourished in [Los Angeles](/places/california-los-angeles-usa).<sup>21</sup> Parsons collaborated with Crowley and Hubbard while both launching rockets and engaging in provocative rituals in the Los Angeles desert.<sup>22</sup> Mike Bara said the [Church of Scientology](/organisations/church-of-scientology) was most concerned with conveying that none of its teachings are based on Crowley's Thelemic beliefs,<sup>23</sup> and that it deserved the benefit of the doubt on that claim.<sup>24</sup>
