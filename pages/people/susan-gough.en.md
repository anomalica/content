Susan Gough is a spokesperson for the United States [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod), who has served as the Pentagon's public-facing voice on matters concerning the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) and related unidentified aerial phenomena. In a 2019 exchange with The Black Vault, Gough's office said it was unable to provide a full copy of the Department of Defense's response to Senator [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), stating that the Public Affairs office does not release Congressional correspondence.<sup>1</sup>

The exchange concerned a 2009 memo from Reid to Deputy Secretary of Defense William Lynn III on the Special Access Program status of AATIP. Gough said the memo was authentic.<sup>2</sup> She said [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) had no assigned responsibilities for AATIP during his time in the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi).<sup>3</sup> She said the Department of Defense had received the memo and had responded to Reid.<sup>4</sup> Gough further said that the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) administered AATIP and that Elizondo was never assigned to the DIA.<sup>5</sup> She said Elizondo had interacted with the DIA office managing the programme while it was still running, but had not led it.<sup>6</sup>

Gough has also spoken on other UAP-related matters. She said that an intelligence report had formally attributed some military sightings of [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) to surveillance by China using relatively ordinary drone technology.<sup>7</sup> Separately, in connection with the 2023 'Balloongate' Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) shoot-down controversy, Gough admitted that the Pentagon had known, in the months before the Super Bowl-week shoot-downs, that it was aware of potentially dangerous balloons or drones in American airspace.<sup>8</sup>
