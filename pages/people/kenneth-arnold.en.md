Historians of the modern flying saucer phenomenon typically date its emergence to the start of the Cold War and to Kenneth Arnold's 1947 sighting of nine flat, saucer-like discs near Mount Rainier, Washington.<sup>1</sup> The event is recorded as the [Kenneth Arnold Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) sighting](/events/kenneth-arnold-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-sighting), also referred to as the 1947 Mount Rainier sighting. Arnold said he was a businessman whose sighting became known in the press as "flying saucers",<sup>2</sup> and that he had taken his plane up as a private pilot to search for a downed US military transport aircraft, hoping to win a $5,000 reward, when it occurred.<sup>3</sup>

According to Arnold's own account, he first saw a brilliant flashing light in the distance, which he worried might be another aircraft,<sup>4</sup> then made out a series of bright flashes north of Mount Rainier from a distance of 30 to 40 kilometres.<sup>5</sup> He watched the objects fly toward the mountain and pass in front of its snow-covered slopes,<sup>6</sup> describing their shape as convex, with one object appearing crescent-shaped.<sup>7</sup>

On 23 June 1947, Arnold saw nine large circular objects flying in formation that periodically flipped as they passed near Mount Rainier.<sup>8</sup> As an experienced pilot accustomed to judging the speed of distant aircraft,<sup>9</sup> he estimated the objects were travelling at approximately 2,700 kilometres per hour.<sup>10</sup> He compared their flight to the "tail of a Chinese kite"<sup>11</sup> and described the objects themselves as "saucer-like aircraft".<sup>12</sup> When later asked by a reporter how they had flown, Arnold said their motion was like saucers skipping over water.<sup>13</sup> The account was picked up by several media outlets, and the term "flying saucer" emerged from it.<sup>14</sup>

Project SAUCER investigated the account Arnold provided,<sup>15</sup> and segments of the American public have been interested in the UFO/UAP topic since the term "flying saucer" emerged following the sighting.<sup>16</sup> Chris Ramsay has said that the sighting, together with the [Roswell Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) crash](/events/roswell-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-crash) and the Aztec crash, prompted government and military interest that led to the formation of [Project GRUDGE](/projects/project-grudge), which later became [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book).<sup>17</sup> Major Murphy has said that General James Doolittle's involvement in a 1946 investigation in Sweden - a year before Arnold's sighting, and before the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) itself existed - reflected considerable interest in the topic among senior military figures even before the creation of Project Blue Book.<sup>18</sup>

Luis Elizondo has said that after the sighting - which took place a couple of weeks before the 1947 Roswell Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) crash - Arnold and his family allegedly experienced balls of light appearing in their home.<sup>19</sup>
