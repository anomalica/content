Tom DeLonge is an American musician best known as the front man of the punk-rock band [Blink-182](/organisations/blink-182).<sup>2</sup> Born in Poway, near San Diego, California,<sup>3</sup> he developed an interest in [UFO](/topics/ufo)s during the band's touring years, reading books on the subject while travelling.<sup>6</sup> This interest found early public expression in June 1999, when Blink-182 released their album *Enema of the State*, which included a song titled "Aliens Exist".<sup>5</sup> DeLonge subsequently created a website called Strange Times as a clearinghouse for UFO news and research.<sup>7</sup> He has recounted a personal experience near [Area 51](/places/area-51), north of Tonopah, Nevada, in which he and two friends camped in the desert and he claims to have awoken at around 3 am to hear what sounded like approximately twenty voices outside his tent, felt completely paralysed, and experienced approximately three hours of lost time.<sup>8</sup> In early 2015, DeLonge left Blink-182<sup>9</sup> and, according to [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), turned his attention fully to UAP research and began crisscrossing the country on a learning tour meeting with sympathetic former government officials.<sup>10</sup>

DeLonge has described a series of meetings with defence and intelligence figures that he claims preceded the formation of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science). [Lockheed Martin](/organisations/lockheed-martin) confirmed to journalists that its [Lockheed Martin Skunk Works](/organisations/lockheed-martin-skunk-works) division did meet with DeLonge,<sup>11</sup> stating he had reached out with interest in collaborating on a documentary about secret machines and advanced development projects,<sup>12</sup> though Skunk Works ultimately decided not to move forward with the project.<sup>13</sup> DeLonge has claimed he was subsequently invited to a second meeting inside a secure concrete bunker inside Area 51,<sup>14</sup> and that within two weeks of his Skunk Works meeting he was introduced to senior military brass, intelligence officials, high-ranking NASA bureaucrats, and senior White House officials.<sup>15</sup> He has further claimed he was summoned to [Colorado Springs](/places/colorado-springs), headquarters of [US Air Force Space Command](/organisations/us-air-force-space-command), to meet a general and a colonel who approved his access and media awareness campaign. DeLonge also claims that after making revelations on [Coast to Coast AM](/organisations/coast-to-coast-am) he was approached by someone from an unnamed agency and interrogated for two days.

DeLonge's contacts with [John Podesta](/people/john-podesta) became a matter of public record through the [Podesta DeLonge Email Exchange](/objects/podesta-delonge-email-exchange) revealed by [Wikileaks](/organisations/wikileaks). He sent emails to Podesta on 2015-09-24,<sup>16</sup> 2015-10-26,<sup>17</sup> and 2016-02-23.<sup>18</sup> Leaked emails revealed plans for a Google Hangout meeting that would have included DeLonge, Podesta, [Rob Weiss](/people/rob-weiss) of Lockheed Martin Skunk Works, two US Air Force generals, and others.<sup>19</sup> A leaked email dated 2016-01-24 listed meeting attendees as DeLonge, Podesta, Weiss, a Clinton campaign assistant, and retired Major General [Michael Carey](/people/michael-carey).<sup>20</sup> Carey also wrote an endorsement for DeLonge's 2016 thriller *SeKret Machines: Chasing Shadows*.<sup>21</sup>

On 2017-10-11, DeLonge formally announced, as president and chief executive, the launch of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) (TTSA) on a Seattle stage.<sup>23</sup> The organisation's advisory board included [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice), an aerospace engineer who had spent thirty-one years at Lockheed Martin Skunk Works,<sup>24</sup> and [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), who had left [The Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) and joined DeLonge along with Chris Mellon, Justice, and others.<sup>25</sup> DeLonge served as executive producer of the History Channel television series *Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation*.<sup>26</sup> Two weeks after the TTSA launch, DeLonge appeared on [The Joe Rogan Experience](/organisations/the-joe-rogan-experience) -- episode 1029, recorded on 2017-10-26<sup>29</sup> -- where he gave a 100-minute interview discussing his claimed secret meetings with defence and intelligence officials.<sup>27</sup> He also gave a four-hour interview with [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp) about his UAP briefings.<sup>28</sup> During the [DeLonge Joe Rogan Interview](/events/delonge-joe-rogan-interview), DeLonge claimed TTSA had a 60 per cent chance within approximately 36 months of demonstrating a major breakthrough in anti-gravity propulsion, and a definite propulsion breakthrough within eight years.<sup>30</sup>

TTSA's trajectory shifted over subsequent years. In 2019, DeLonge sold six [Art's Parts Samples](/objects/arts-parts-samples) to TTSA for US$35,000, as disclosed to the Securities and Exchange Commission.<sup>31</sup> DeLonge subsequently announced he had refocused TTSA's operations, scaling back science and technology commercialisation and placing greater emphasis on its entertainment business.<sup>32</sup> In December 2020, he called Elizondo to end their business relationship, stating TTSA had not brought in the money he had expected or needed.<sup>33</sup> Despite this rupture, Elizondo has credited DeLonge -- alongside [Kari DeLonge](/people/kari-delonge), DeLonge's sister and a TTSA spokesperson -- with bringing the UAP topic to the media through TTSA,<sup>35</sup> and has stated that he gives DeLonge credit for his role in the UAP disclosure movement.<sup>34</sup> [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) similarly identified DeLonge and Elizondo as the two main players in TTSA's founding in congressional testimony submitted in 2023. The [DeLonge George Knapp Interview 2016](/events/delonge-george-knapp-interview-2016), conducted on [Coast to Coast AM](/organisations/coast-to-coast-am) on 2016-03-27, and the [DeLonge Skunk Works Meeting](/events/delonge-skunk-works-meeting) and [DeLonge General Airport Meeting](/events/delonge-general-airport-meeting) remain among the key events described in DeLonge's account of how he assembled his network of government contacts prior to TTSA's launch.
