Tom DeLonge is an American musician from Poway, near San Diego, California,<sup>3</sup> best known as the front man of the rock band [Blink-182](/organisations/blink-182).<sup>1</sup> He departed the band in early 2015, in part to devote himself to researching UAP.<sup>2</sup> His interest in the subject pre-dated that departure by some years: [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) later recounted that DeLonge had begun reading about UFOs on long van rides with the band before it achieved fame.<sup>5</sup> Blink-182's June 1999 album *Enema of the State* included a track entitled "Aliens Exist", an early public signal of DeLonge's preoccupations.<sup>4</sup> DeLonge has also described a personal experience near [Area 51](/places/area-51), north of Tonopah, Nevada, in which he claims to have awoken at around 3 am to hear what sounded like approximately twenty voices outside his tent, to have felt completely paralysed, and to have lost approximately three hours of time; one of the two friends present corroborated hearing voices around the tent.<sup>6,7</sup>

DeLonge's efforts to engage the defence and intelligence establishment began with an approach to [Lockheed Skunk Works](/organisations/lockheed-skunk-works), the advanced-development division of [Lockheed Martin](/organisations/lockheed-martin). [Lockheed Martin](/organisations/lockheed-martin) confirmed to the publication *The War Zone* that multiple Skunk Works members met with DeLonge after he reached out with interest in collaborating on a documentary focused on secret machines and advanced development projects.<sup>9,8</sup> The company ultimately decided not to participate in the documentary.<sup>10</sup> DeLonge has claimed that a second meeting took place inside a secure concrete bunker at [Area 51](/places/area-51), requiring passage through four layers of security including armed guards, electronic code-entry systems, white-noise hallways, and rotary-lock doors.<sup>11</sup> He further claimed that within two weeks of the [Skunk Works meeting](/events/delonge-skunk-works-meeting) he was introduced to senior military officers, intelligence officials, senior NASA bureaucrats, and White House officials,<sup>12</sup> and that a general at an airport restaurant told him: "It was the Cold War and we found a lifeform."<sup>13</sup>

DeLonge also made contact with [John Podesta](/people/john-podesta) through the [Podesta DeLonge Email Exchange](/objects/podesta-delonge-email-exchange). Emails published by [Wikileaks](/organisations/wikileaks) show he wrote to Podesta on 2015-09-24, 2015-10-26, and 2016-02-23.<sup>14</sup> Leaked emails further revealed plans for a meeting that would have brought together DeLonge, Podesta, [Rob Weiss](/people/rob-weiss) from Lockheed Skunk Works, and two US Air Force generals.<sup>15</sup> A separate leaked email dated 2016-01-24 listed actual attendees as DeLonge, Podesta, Weiss, a Clinton campaign assistant, and retired Major General [Michael Carey](/people/michael-carey).<sup>16</sup> Ross Coulthart, in his account of this period, assessed that the Podesta email material corroborated DeLonge's claims that senior US government defence officials had briefed him on UAP matters.<sup>17</sup>

On 2017-10-11, DeLonge formally announced, as president and CEO, the establishment of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) (TTSA) at a launch event in Seattle.<sup>18</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) subsequently left the Pentagon and joined TTSA alongside [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice), and others.<sup>19</sup> TTSA's stated mandate encompassed new approaches to UAP science and engineering, public education, and the production of film, television, and publishing content.<sup>20</sup> Prior to the launch, DeLonge had conducted an extensive tour of meetings with former government officials, and [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) later described him as having crisscrossed the country in a learning tour before forming the organisation.<sup>21</sup> DeLonge articulated his own rationale for his communications role in an interview on [The Joe Rogan Experience](/organisations/the-joe-rogan-experience), stating that the military has no mechanism to produce films or appear on television and that he therefore served a function in public communication.<sup>22</sup> That interview, recorded on 2017-10-26 and lasting approximately 100 minutes, covered DeLonge's claimed meetings with defence and intelligence officials.<sup>23</sup> He also gave a four-hour interview with [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp) on the same themes.<sup>24</sup>

During public appearances, DeLonge made a series of claims attributed to government sources. He told [Joe Rogan](/people/joe-rogan) that the [Roswell UAP Crash](/events/roswell-uap-crash) had involved a recovered craft and that the US Air Force was created ninety days later as a consequence.<sup>25</sup> He claimed that a metamaterial sample in TTSA's possession lost mass when radiated with terahertz frequencies and would float if subjected to sufficient terahertz radiation.<sup>26</sup> In 2019, DeLonge sold six [Art's Parts Samples](/objects/arts-parts-samples) to TTSA for US$35,000, a transaction disclosed to the Securities and Exchange Commission.<sup>27</sup> DeLonge also served as executive producer of the History Channel series *[Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation](/objects/unidentified-inside-americas-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-investigation)*, in which he was prominently featured.<sup>28</sup>

In the period following TTSA's launch, the organisation's direction shifted. DeLonge announced a decision to scale back science and technology commercialisation efforts and place greater emphasis on TTSA's entertainment activities.<sup>29</sup> In December 2020, he contacted [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) shortly before Christmas to end their professional relationship, citing TTSA's failure to generate the revenue he had anticipated.<sup>30</sup> Despite the difficulties, Elizondo subsequently stated publicly that he credited DeLonge and Kari DeLonge for their role in bringing the UAP topic to broader media attention through TTSA.
