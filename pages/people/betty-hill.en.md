Betty Hill and her husband [Barney Hill](/people/barney-hill) were an interracial couple, both active in the civil rights movement,<sup>1</sup> who lived in New Hampshire USA.<sup>2</sup> On the night of 1961-09-19, driving home along a rural road in what became known as the 1961 Betty and Barney Hill interrupted journey and UFO abduction September 19, part of the wider [1961 Betty and Barney Hill Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) abduction](/events/1961-betty-and-barney-hill-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-abduction) case, the couple reported an unidentified [UFO](/topics/ufos-uaps) sighting. According to American Cosmic, they saw a light that at first resembled a falling star but seemed to move differently;<sup>3</sup> they stopped the car and used binoculars to try to identify it,<sup>4</sup> and when the light came toward them, almost filling the windscreen, a frightened Barney Hill got out of the car with a pistol he was carrying.<sup>5,6</sup> Messengers of Deception recounts that the object appeared to change from looking like a star to looking like a disk as it neared them,<sup>7</sup> and that further along the road the couple lost awareness of their actions, next remembering themselves driving past a point roughly 100 kilometres to the south.<sup>8</sup> Betty Hill's account describes figures visible at the windows of the object during the sighting.<sup>9</sup> Their dog, Delphi, was frightened during the encounter,<sup>10</sup> and Betty stayed in the car for most of the sighting while Barney was outside.<sup>11</sup> Two days later, according to American Cosmic, Betty Hill telephoned nearby Pease Air Force Base;<sup>12</sup> Major Paul W. Henderson returned her call the following day,<sup>13</sup> and she described what they had seen without mentioning any beings.<sup>14</sup> Barney Hill later said that what they had reported to the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) was a disk with windows.<sup>15</sup>

The case first reached investigators through a six-page report from the [National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP)](/organisations/national-investigations-committee-on-aerial-phenomena-nicap) describing the sighting,<sup>16</sup> and when NICAP interviewed the couple it accepted their story completely.<sup>17</sup> [Benjamin Simon](/people/benjamin-simon), who later treated the Hills, considered it one of NICAP's greatest mistakes that its interviewer never examined silver-dollar-sized spots the couple said were still visible on the back of their car;<sup>18</sup> those spots eventually washed away.<sup>19</sup>

Betty and Barney Hill began experiencing anxiety dreams and stress-related problems severe enough that they sought the help of Boston psychiatrist Benjamin Simon in January 1964.<sup>20</sup> Betty Hill said she had written down her nightmares in November 1961.<sup>21</sup> Simon's hypnotherapy sessions began from the point in Canada, Quebec, Montreal where the couple had been at the top of their journey home,<sup>22</sup> and he hypnotised them one at a time, starting with Barney.<sup>23</sup> In what became known as Betty and Barney Hill's 1964 hypnosis sessions with Benjamin Simon, their accounts matched each other up to the point of the sighting, but from there Betty's account of an abduction grew far more vivid and detailed while Barney's remained minimal.<sup>24</sup>

During the account, the leader aboard the object told Betty Hill he would say where they came from if she said where she came from, then showed her a mask containing a map of stars.<sup>25</sup> In Betty Hill's hypnotic account she and Barney were taken aboard the object, with Barney, his eyes closed, half-dragged by two figures while Betty walked with her eyes open, led by the apparent leader of the group;<sup>26</sup> she described a needle being inserted into her navel during an examination.<sup>27</sup> She later drew a version of the star map for Simon, showing a number of stars connected by lines, one set representing trade routes and another representing expeditions - what became known as Betty Hill's star map.<sup>28</sup> Benjamin Simon concluded that Betty Hill's abduction story derived from her nightmares, and said he was satisfied he did not need to accept a genuine visitation from outer space as an explanation.<sup>29</sup> The therapist who treated the Hills did not believe in UFOs.<sup>30</sup>

[Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee), who spent hours with the couple and discussed the case with Simon,<sup>31</sup> wrote that Betty and Barney disagreed on several details of the appearance of the occupants they described.<sup>32</sup> Vallée said he believed the Hills really saw a UFO and that something unknown stopped their car and took control of their minds, and that they were not lying or "nuts."<sup>33</sup> He did not, however, believe any contactee had been physically aboard a saucer, proposing instead a nonphysical experience controlled by a system acting on human consciousness, which he said the Soviets term "psychotronic."<sup>34</sup> Betty Hill later told [Marjorie Fish](/people/marjorie-fish) that she had drawn the star map under post-hypnotic suggestion in 1964.<sup>35</sup> According to Messengers of Deception, Fish, an Ohio schoolteacher, built a model of nearby sunlike stars and matched it to the map Betty Hill had described,<sup>36</sup> and researchers including Stanton Friedman and David Saunders considered Fish's model to indicate that the object came from Zeta Reticuli.<sup>37</sup> Saunders, a psychologist formerly with the Condon Committee, said he could find no major point to quibble with in Fish's interpretation.<sup>38</sup> Vallée disagreed, calling the star map "a map to nowhere" that proved nothing about an extraterrestrial origin.<sup>39</sup>

Betty Hill became a UFO celebrity and a rallying point for believers in extraterrestrial visitation, appearing frequently on talk shows and television.<sup>40</sup> The Betty and Barney Hill abduction case and the 1957 case of [Antonio Villas-Boas](/people/antonio-villas-boas) are widely recognised among researchers in the field as the two watershed first abduction reports.<sup>41</sup> On 1967-12-03, the 1967 Herb Schirmer Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) abduction, Ashland Nebraska was later regarded as comparable to the abduction accounts of Betty Hill, Barney Hill, and the fishermen involved in the 1973 Pascagoula, Mississippi Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) abduction.<sup>42</sup> Betty Hill, in Portsmouth, and [Charlie Hickson](/people/charlie-hickson), in Pascagoula, each took a lie detector test related to their accounts and passed.<sup>43</sup> In late 2003, chemist Phyllis Budinger circulated an analysis of a large pink stain found on the dress Betty Hill had worn during the 1961 encounter, part of what became Betty Hill's 1961 dress Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) analysis.<sup>44</sup> The Anomaly Physical Evidence Group (APEG), led by [Bill Chalker](/people/bill-chalker), received dress samples along with control biological samples,<sup>45</sup> and undertook specialised ribosomal polymerase chain reaction (PCR) DNA profiling to seek non-human DNA alongside standard assessments;<sup>46</sup> the DNA found was connected to Betty and Barney Hill, with some of the stained areas found to be due to spider's blood.<sup>47</sup> Betty Hill had died by December 2004, before that analysis was completed.<sup>48</sup> Decades later, author Whitley Strieber said he had no conscious memory of reading about the Hill case in Look Magazine before undergoing his own hypnosis.<sup>49</sup>
