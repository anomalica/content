Edwin C. May worked at the [Cognitive Sciences Laboratory](/organisations/cognitive-sciences-laboratory-the-laboratories-for-fundamental-research) for approximately nine years before becoming its director in 1986.<sup>1</sup> Together with his colleagues, he was directly responsible for the laboratory's accomplishments from that year until [Project STARGATE](/projects/project-stargate) was terminated in November 1995.<sup>2</sup> He was also a director of [The Stargate Project](/projects/the-stargate-project).<sup>3</sup>

Much of what is recorded about May comes through [Joseph McMoneagle](/people/joseph-mcmoneagle). McMoneagle has said that, in an interview on Put to the Test (ABC Special), May noted a near-perfect match between some of McMoneagle's drawings and the intended target.<sup>4</sup> McMoneagle has also said that May's statements in that interview reflected a target hit rate of about sixty to sixty-five percent for his [remote viewing](/topics/remote-viewing-rv).<sup>5</sup> On McMoneagle's account, May said he produces spectacular detail roughly twenty percent of the time - which May called as close to a miracle as scientists can get<sup>6</sup> - and that this specifically referred to near-perfect drawings, something McMoneagle says rarely happens in remote viewing.<sup>7</sup> McMoneagle has further said that out-of-context editing of May's statement in the ABC Special interview produced a strongly negative reaction among other remote viewers, which he considered grossly unfair to both himself and May.<sup>8</sup>

Julia Mossbridge has said that May frequently discusses [precognitive remote viewing](/topics/precognitive-remote-viewing), and that there is a large effect size for it, especially among gifted remote viewers.<sup>9</sup> According to the documentary "I LEFT MY BODY - The Gateway Experience (Part 3)", in research conducted by May and McMoneagle, the higher the entropy of a target and the greater the risk to human life, the clearer the target reportedly became to the viewer.<sup>10</sup> [Chris Ramsay](/people/chris-ramsay) has said he expects May would attribute the strongest remote-viewing signal to physical contrast, on the basis that a high-entropy target yields a more accurate reading.<sup>11</sup>

Ramsay has also related an account, given to him by McMoneagle and May, of a session in which experimenters placed ping-pong balls over the eyes to produce a Ganzfeld effect, inducing an [out-of-body experience (OBE)](/topics/out-of-body-experience-obe) before asking McMoneagle to view a picture sealed in an envelope in another room.<sup>12</sup> Separately, Mossbridge has recounted hearing a story - which she believes came from May, though possibly from McMoneagle - that in the 1990s Russian counterparts told their American counterparts "you killed all your psychics", a remark she connects to [Pat Price](/people/pat-price) and possibly others.<sup>13</sup> Commentator Jesse Michels has suggested that May may also have been part of the Lone Stars group.<sup>14</sup>
