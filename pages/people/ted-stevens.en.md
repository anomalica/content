Ted Stevens was a Republican United States Senator for Alaska who, alongside [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye), sat among the top members of a defense spending subcommittee and used that position to support funding for the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip).<sup>1</sup> Accounts of the programme's origins describe [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) summoning Stevens and Inouye to a secure room in the Capitol to discuss the funding.<sup>2</sup> Reid later approached the two senators to secure $22 million for a study into the UAP mystery.<sup>3</sup>

Stevens had served as a pilot in the Army's air force during World War II, flying transport missions over China,<sup>4</sup> having flown US military cargo over the Himalayas from India to China with the Army Air Corps.<sup>5</sup> According to Reid, during the meeting on the funding Stevens recounted having been tailed by a strange aircraft of no known origin, which followed his plane for miles.<sup>6</sup> Stevens told Reid he had seen a UAP during one wartime flight and that they needed to take a look at this.<sup>7</sup> Stevens also said he once saw a "foo fighter" while flying a mission during the war.<sup>8</sup>

Reid said that none of the three senators wanted a public debate on the Senate floor about the AATIP funding.<sup>5</sup> Reid described the arrangement as "so-called black money," saying "Stevens knows about it, Inouye knows about it. But that was it, and that's how we wanted it," in reference to the classified portion of the [United States Senate](/organisations/united-states-senate) defense appropriations he oversaw with Stevens and Inouye.<sup>9</sup> The successor programme, the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap), was supported by then-[Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) Director Lieutenant General Michael D. Maples and funded through the efforts of Reid, Stevens, and Inouye.<sup>10</sup> Together, the three senators controlled congressional spending for black-budget Pentagon programmes.<sup>11</sup>

Stevens died in a plane crash in 2010, two years after backing the UAP funding alongside Reid.<sup>12</sup>
