Marco Rubio is a US Senator from Florida who has been one of Congress's most consistent advocates for a coordinated federal response to [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap). In December 2020, while serving as head of the [US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](/organisations/us-senate-select-committee-on-intelligence), Rubio pressed the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)](/organisations/office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence-odni) and the Pentagon to deliver Congress an unclassified report on UAP within a month.<sup>1</sup> Having received classified briefings on the subject, he called for a detailed analysis of the phenomena.<sup>2</sup>

Rubio said publicly that there is a stigma attached to discussing UAP on Capitol Hill, and that some of his Senate colleagues take the topic seriously while others react dismissively.<sup>3</sup> He said the stigma should not prevent the government from finding an answer to what the objects are,<sup>3</sup> and called for a formal process to take UAP reports seriously and continuously catalogue and analyse incoming data.<sup>4</sup>

Following the February 2023 2023 'Balloongate' Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) shoot-down controversy, Rubio said this marked the first time in [North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD)](/organisations/north-american-aerospace-defense-command-norad)'s history that a decision had been made to shoot down an object over American airspace, noting that all three downed objects remained unidentified.<sup>5</sup> He said the Pentagon's UAP office had recorded hundreds of similar airborne cases that were not met with the same response.<sup>6</sup> He also expressed concern that the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) had not been sharing information on those cases with investigators and scientists at the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro),<sup>7</sup> and said the underlying worry was whether an adversary had developed a capability that US systems, built to detect missiles and aeroplanes rather than smaller low-altitude objects, were not designed to see.<sup>8</sup> Rubio separately criticised the government's handling of the Chinese Spy Balloon episode involving China and pushed for answers on unidentified objects more broadly.<sup>9</sup>

On legislation, Rubio worked with Democratic Senator Kirsten Gillibrand to establish an office coordinating the reporting and response to unidentified objects,<sup>6</sup> and he and Mark Warner, his predecessor as chair of the Senate Intelligence Committee, gave strong public support to the UAP provisions of the [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023 (FY2023 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA))](/documents/national-defense-authorization-act-for-fiscal-year-2023-fy2023-national-defense-authorization-act-ndaa), which was signed into law by President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden) in December 2022.<sup>10, 11</sup> As acting chairman of the Senate Intelligence Committee, he supported a proposal for a UAP transparency report despite criticism from those opposed to greater openness,<sup>12</sup> and was later named chief sponsor, as chair of the committee, of the bill requiring a Department of Defense UAP report as part of the FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act.<sup>13</sup> He also joined Republican senators Roy Blunt and Lindsey Graham, and Democratic senators Gillibrand and Martin Heinrich, together with Democratic representative Ruben Gallego and assistance from [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) and [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), in crafting UAP-related language for the NDAA.<sup>14</sup> In the summer of 2023, Rubio, Chuck Schumer, Mike Rounds and Gillibrand sponsored the UAP Disclosure Act.<sup>15</sup>

According to [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch), Rubio and Warner were nervous about escalating their confrontation with the intelligence community bureaucracy over the UAP issue.<sup>16</sup> Rubio has continued to figure in UAP-related developments in the Trump administration: he was seated beside Pete Hegseth at a cabinet meeting on 2026-08-11 at which [Donald Trump](/people/donald-trump) made remarks about releasing extraterrestrial information.<sup>17</sup>
