According to The Fatima Secret, the Prophecy of the Popes tradition described John XXIII, pope from 1958 to 1963, by the epithet "Pastor et nauta" ("shepherd and seafarer").<sup>1</sup> He came from the shepherds' village of Italy, Bergamo, Sotto il Monte and herded sheep as a boy,<sup>2</sup> and before his election he had served as patriarch of Venice Italy.<sup>3</sup> On 1959-01-25, before he had read the Third Secret of Fatima, he announced his intention to convene an ecumenical council.<sup>4</sup>

The person responsible for keeping the Third Secret envelope safe had earlier asked John XXIII whether he wished to see it; he replied that he would let them know when to bring it to him.<sup>5</sup> On 1959-08-17, at Castel Gandolfo Italy, the pope's summer residence, John XXIII's opening of the Third Secret envelope began when he received a messenger from [Rome Italy](/places/rome-italy) asking whether he had brought the document.<sup>6</sup> Father Paul Philippe delivered the sealed envelope to him there, its seal still intact.<sup>7</sup><sup>8</sup> He waited four days, until Friday, 1959-08-21, before breaking the seal,<sup>9</sup> and read the text in a closed room in the presence only of a Vatican interpreter and his confessor, Monsignor Alfredo Cavagna.<sup>10</sup> He read slowly and with trembling hands, needing the interpreter's help since he did not speak Portuguese,<sup>11</sup> and as he took in its content his face turned white as chalk.<sup>12</sup> According to The Fatima Secret, some of those closest to him reported that he almost fainted with horror when he opened it.<sup>13</sup> The seal had in fact been broken six months before the 1960 announcement that the secret would not be published.<sup>14</sup>

Three men then discussed the secret's contents until late at night; John XXIII decided he could not and would not publish it, certain it did not concern his own papal reign.<sup>15</sup> He placed Lúcia (Fatima seer)'s letter back in its envelope along with a personal note explaining his reasoning, told his closest advisors of its contents and his decision, and made them all swear to secrecy.<sup>16</sup> He kept the Fatima message on his desk for the rest of his time in office, until his death on 1963-06-03.<sup>17</sup> Monsignor Loris Capovilla later recalled that when Paul VI asked him what John XXIII's opinion had been, he could only answer that the decision had been "I will leave it to my successors", to which Paul VI replied, "I shall do that too."<sup>18</sup> On 1977-05-13, [Vatican](/organisations/vatican) Radio announced that neither John XXIII nor Paul VI considered it right to reveal the Third Secret to the world at large.<sup>19</sup> In total, five popes, [Pius XII](/people/pius-xii), John XXIII, Paul VI, John Paul I, and [John Paul II](/people/john-paul-ii), kept the secret under lock and key.<sup>20</sup> Unlike his predecessor, who had left the envelope unopened out of respect for a mistakenly interpreted 1960 date, John XXIII went to Castel Gandolfo in August 1959 for his summer holiday and decided to open it.<sup>21</sup> After his own papacy, three further popes followed: Paul VI (1963-1978), John Paul I (1978), and John Paul II, pope since 1978.<sup>22</sup> When John XXIII died, Conchita (Garabandal seer) told her mother there would be three more popes.<sup>23</sup>

Rome was preparing for the Second Vatican Council in early 1959, and John XXIII said he did not want to listen to "prophets of doom."<sup>24</sup> Vatican II opened on 1962-10-11 with an address in which he again raised his voice against the prophets of doom who forever proclaimed that the end of the world stood before them;<sup>25</sup> by that point he had already read Lúcia's Third Secret.<sup>26</sup> He trembled on reading it and, rather than spread fear, wanted to instil a state of optimism in the Church ahead of the council.<sup>27</sup> Early in 1959 a senior Vatican official had suggested that Sister Lúcia be allowed to address the world by radio, but John XXIII declined;<sup>28</sup> according to The Fatima Secret, his private secretary later wrote that it seemed he personally preferred her to remain silent.<sup>29</sup> At the Fatima 2000 Conference (1995-11), Malachi Martin, formerly secretary to Bea (Cardinal), recalled that the cardinal was shaken and annoyed that John XXIII would not publish the secret.<sup>30</sup> Martin also said that John XXIII later told him the secret's contents himself.<sup>31</sup> Father José Geraldes Freire, an authority on Fatima, said that John XXIII gave the message great importance, and that some of his major decisions, otherwise found inexplicable, were influenced by it.<sup>32</sup> On 1962-10-25, during the [Cuban Missile Crisis (1962-10)](/events/cuban-missile-crisis-1962-10), John XXIII appealed to the world, pleading for peace;<sup>33</sup> Michael Hesemann has said it remains unknown whether the Fatima message played any part in the crisis's diplomacy.<sup>34</sup>

A separate account states that part of the Fatima message was not due to be revealed until 1960, when John XXIII opened the sealed envelope without publishing the secret.<sup>35</sup> [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee) said a source he trusted received a report from one of John XXIII's secretaries, describing how senior figures were brought into the pope's presence for that 1960 opening.<sup>36</sup> Vallée said that, according to this account, the cardinals left the pope's office with looks of deep horror on their faces, and one prelate approached by the secretary gently pushed him aside and walked on with the expression of someone who had seen a ghost.<sup>37</sup> Vallée said this indirect information sheds light on the reactions of senior Catholic Church officials to the prophecy, though not necessarily on its actual contents.<sup>38</sup>

According to a message purportedly from UMMO, John XXIII was named among a group of public figures said to belong to an extraterrestrial expeditionary presence on Earth, alongside Bertrand Russell, Ernesto (Che) Guevara, Helder Camara, Emmanuel Mounier, and Albert Schweitzer, among others.<sup>39</sup> The Mass at St Peter's Basilica has been celebrated in Latin near his incorruptible body.<sup>40</sup> According to American Cosmic, Tyler D. had never previously heard of the Catholic tradition of incorruptible saints before viewing the pope's preserved body.<sup>41</sup> [Diana Walsh Pasulka](/people/diana-walsh-pasulka) and Tyler D. later discussed the preserved body the day after Tyler D.'s mother's funeral, two weeks after they returned from Rome, Italy.<sup>42</sup> The fact that they had been close to it together was, for Tyler D., a source of comfort following his mother's death.<sup>43</sup>
