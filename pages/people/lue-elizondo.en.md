Lue Elizondo is a career counterintelligence officer who served as manager of the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), launched from the [Office of the Undersecretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-undersecretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi).<sup>1</sup> He kept the [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) (UAP) investigation running for several years despite declining interest from [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) leadership, until his frustration became too much to bear.<sup>2</sup> His public disclosure of AATIP's existence was later credited as a major factor in the creation of the Unidentified Aerospace Phenomena Task Force and, subsequently, the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office.<sup>3</sup>

Elizondo's father, Luis D. Elizondo III, fought alongside Fidel Castro against the Cuban dictator Fulgencio Batista in his youth,<sup>4</sup> attended the Havana Military Academy,<sup>5</sup> and later joined the resistance and Brigade 2506 after Castro aligned with the Soviet Union, taking part in the US-led Bay of Pigs Invasion.<sup>6</sup> He spent two years in Castro's prisons as a consequence.<sup>7</sup> The family later settled in Sarasota, Florida, where the elder Elizondo opened an Italian restaurant, Michelangelo's, on St. Armands Circle.<sup>8</sup> Elizondo entered the premedical program at the University of Miami, double-majoring in microbiology and immunology,<sup>9</sup> working as a bouncer at sports bars and nightclubs to help pay for his studies.<sup>10</sup> After college he joined the [United States Army](/organisations/united-states-army),<sup>11</sup> beginning his career at a recruiter's office in Miami in 1995,<sup>12</sup> at the age of twenty-three.<sup>13</sup> He enlisted as a grunt rather than accept an officer's commission, in order to avoid assignment to the medical corps,<sup>14</sup> and trained as an Army counterintelligence special agent, an occupational specialty known as 97-Bravo.<sup>15</sup>

While working as a bouncer, Elizondo dated Jennifer, who worked as a cocktail waitress at the same sports bar<sup>16</sup> and who taught and interpreted for hearing-impaired children in the Miami-Dade public school system as an American Sign Language specialist.<sup>17</sup> The couple later married, and Elizondo's first Army assignment took him to [South Korea](/places/south-korea) shortly after Jennifer conceived their first child.<sup>18</sup> There he worked with the Korean Central Intelligence Agency, the Korean National Police, and a Special Projects surveillance team.<sup>19</sup> His second posting was to the 902nd Military Intelligence Group, headquartered at Fort Meade, Maryland,<sup>20</sup> with a duty station at Fort Huachuca, Arizona,<sup>21</sup> where he conducted counterintelligence investigations in Nevada, California, and Arizona to protect emerging US government technologies including laser weapons, unmanned aerial vehicles, and rocket engines,<sup>22</sup> and worked to shield sensitive technology at defence contractors including TRW, Raytheon, Boeing, and Lockheed from foreign intelligence services.<sup>23</sup> During this period, two [Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI)](/organisations/air-force-office-of-special-investigations-afosi) agents told him that a contractor who claimed he could make aircraft invisible in fact worked for the Air Force on a classified programme, and requested Elizondo's file on the man.<sup>24</sup>

Lieutenant Colonel Michael Seage recommended Elizondo for a position in the Army's "Great Skills" programme,<sup>25</sup> and Eugene Lessman flew from Hanover, Virginia, to meet him about it,<sup>26</sup> telling him, according to Elizondo, that the programme was also known as Grey Fox.<sup>27</sup> Lessman, who also recruited soldiers into Project Stargate,<sup>28</sup> trained Elizondo in Remote Viewing at an old motel in Sierra Vista, Arizona,<sup>29</sup> using an old training manual he had been given,<sup>30</sup> after the [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) had already pulled funding for Project Stargate.<sup>31</sup> Practitioners were taught to report what came to them without judgement, a discipline known as avoiding analytical overlay.<sup>32</sup> According to Elizondo, he once used remote viewing while running counterintelligence operations in the Middle East to develop a strong unease about a location scheduled for investigation by five of his subordinates the next day; an allied team that went in his place instead encountered an improvised explosive device.<sup>33</sup> He also took part in a group remote-viewing session with four other trained practitioners in a secure facility at the Pentagon, directed at a suspected terrorist held in a cell thousands of miles away.<sup>34</sup> According to Elizondo, the detainee later told his lawyers that the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) had sent five angels to disrupt his sleep, a story that subsequently appeared in a newspaper article about a secret CIA harassment programme.<sup>35</sup> Elizondo later taught John Robert how to remote view.<sup>36</sup>

Elizondo went on to work at the [Advanced Aerospace Weapons System Application Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap), the contractor-run effort associated with AATIP.<sup>37</sup> [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) and [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski) briefed him on the programme inside a [Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF)](/topics/sensitive-compartmented-information-facility-scif),<sup>38</sup> a room designed to block sound, radio, and electromagnetic waves.<sup>39</sup> The programme's chief scientist was [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff),<sup>40</sup> a physicist and engineer<sup>41</sup> who had previously managed the CIA's Stargate program.<sup>42</sup> Puthoff commissioned thirty-eight Defense Intelligence Research Documents,<sup>43</sup> technical studies deliberately framed to apply to any conceivable weapon system rather than UAP specifically, so as to disguise the programme's true focus<sup>44</sup> — a tactic Elizondo later reused to keep AATIP viable.<sup>45</sup> Elizondo's career path had briefly crossed with Puthoff's earlier, when Elizondo was a young soldier, though the two first met in person at a programme dinner in Roslyn, Virginia.<sup>46</sup> Elizondo had been invited to that dinner by [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) and Jay Stratton.<sup>47</sup> [James R. Clapper](/people/james-r-clapper) served as Elizondo's boss at OUSDI during this period.<sup>48</sup> Robert Bigelow's firm, Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), was the prime contractor for the programme;<sup>49</sup> Bigelow himself was a billionaire hotelier, developer, and aerospace magnate.<sup>50</sup> Bigelow's team also ran a separate research contract at Skinwalker Ranch, Utah, beginning in 2008.<sup>51</sup> The effort initially enjoyed a good degree of support from Defense Intelligence Agency leadership,<sup>52</sup> but as new executive leadership settled in, an increasing number of detractors emerged within the agency's senior ranks,<sup>53</sup> and within weeks of the leadership transition Lacatski found himself spending most of his time defending the programme rather than conducting research, as senior leadership moved to end it.<sup>54</sup>

Elizondo left government service and was subsequently offered the role of chief of security and special programs at [To The Stars Academy (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-ttsa) by Tom DeLonge, at a salary lower than he had earned at [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) and requiring relocation to California.<sup>55</sup> He has since drawn comparisons between publicly released military footage and historical spaceflight, likening the manoeuvring of the object in the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) to the docking manoeuvres of the Apollo Lunar Module approaching the lunar orbiter.<sup>56</sup> According to author Whitley Strieber, Elizondo went public under the US Whistleblower Protection Act, referencing the existence of nonhuman bodies.<sup>57</sup> Elizondo has described his own feeling about the secrecy officials are compelled to maintain regarding nonhuman visitors as "somber".<sup>58</sup> Within the Pentagon, some deeply compartmented programmes were referred to as "ultrablack" or "purple novas" — projects so secret that not even the secretary of defense or the president would know of them unless they stumbled across them by accident.<sup>59</sup> He has recounted being shown archival material describing old reports from US Marine Corps personnel stationed at Cuba Guantanamo Bay of unexplained lights descending in the no-man's-land between the US and Cuban military zones,<sup>60</sup> and a Russian booklet of maps pinpointing two UAP crash sites along the Ural Mountains.<sup>61</sup>
