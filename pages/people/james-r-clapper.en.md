James R. Clapper is a former US intelligence official who has held some of the country's most senior intelligence posts, including Deputy Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and director of the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia).<sup>1</sup> He also served as head of intelligence for the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) and as undersecretary of defense for intelligence before becoming Director of National Intelligence (DNI),<sup>2</sup> a post he held from 2010 to 2017.<sup>3</sup>

In 2008, Clapper personally hired [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) into the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), the office that acts as principal staff for the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) on sensitive intelligence-related matters and oversees a cluster of agencies including the DIA and the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa).<sup>4</sup> Clapper subsequently served as Elizondo's supervisor within OUSDI.<sup>5</sup> Elizondo has said that Clapper was one of his upper-level supervisors during what he considered the golden era of the office.<sup>6</sup>

According to the *Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP), Volume I*, [William Lynn III](/people/william-lynn-iii) declined to designate the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) as a DoD Special Access Program, acting on a recommendation Clapper made in a 2009-11-17 memorandum to the Deputy Secretary of Defense that such a designation was not justified.<sup>7</sup>

[Barack Obama](/people/barack-obama) asked Clapper to become the new DNI,<sup>8</sup> and Clapper received a phone call from Obama during a meeting with Elizondo, becoming the next Director of National Intelligence shortly afterwards.<sup>9</sup> As DNI, Clapper had Charles McCullough III reporting directly to him in 2011.<sup>10</sup> Devon Woods did not get along with Clapper,<sup>11</sup> but when Clapper became DNI he offered Woods a job at the DIA regardless of their disagreements.<sup>12</sup> Woods accepted the position of deputy director of the DIA after Clapper became DNI.<sup>13</sup>

While waiting in the greenroom at [Cable News Network (CNN)](/organisations/cable-news-network-cnn) before an on-camera interview, Elizondo and Jennifer were approached by Clapper.<sup>14</sup> Clapper told Elizondo that he was surprised [The Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) had admitted it had a [UAP](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) programme, and that he was proud of him.<sup>15</sup>

John Brennan served as Obama's [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) director while Clapper was his director of national intelligence.<sup>16</sup> The two men appear together in the documentary film Age of Disclosure, in which they tacitly endorse the idea that [UFOs](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) are real.<sup>17</sup> Jesse Mitchells has said that Clapper personally talked about having helped set up UFO tracking programmes at [Area 51](/places/nevada-area-51-usa).<sup>18</sup> In the same film, Brennan and Clapper tacitly endorse the idea that a multi-generational legacy programme has conducted crash retrieval and reverse engineering operations for decades.<sup>19</sup>
