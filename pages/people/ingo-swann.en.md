Ingo Swann was an American artist and psychic credited with helping develop the systematic methodology behind [Remote Viewing (RV)](/topics/remote-viewing-rv). According to Joseph McMoneagle, Swann suggested using map coordinates to identify a remote-viewing target, an approach that gave rise to the coordinate targeting system,<sup>1</sup> and he is separately credited with originating Controlled Remote Viewing (CRV) while working at [SRI International](/organisations/sri-international).<sup>2</sup> Joseph McMoneagle also stated that Swann did not leave behind an all-inclusive document describing the training system he developed.<sup>3</sup> Swann worked with the SRI International laboratory<sup>4</sup> and was one of the original participants in [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) evaluations of remote viewing in the early 1970s.<sup>5</sup> He is described as a renowned artist and psychic often credited with developing the first systematic approach to remote viewing in the 1970s.<sup>6</sup>

Swann was approached by scientists at the [Stanford Research Institute (SRI)](/organisations/stanford-research-institute-sri) to take part in research into psychic phenomena,<sup>7</sup> where he worked alongside [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) and Russell Targ.<sup>8</sup> There he developed [Coordinate Remote Viewing](/topics/coordinate-remote-viewing).<sup>9</sup> In 1973 he began working with SRI scientists to explore the possibility of remote viewing planetary objects,<sup>10</sup> and was tasked with remote viewing the planet Jupiter.<sup>11</sup> During that 1973 session he described a previously unknown ring around the planet.<sup>12</sup> Luis Elizondo said that Swann claimed to have remote viewed Jupiter and described its thin rings before they were confirmed by unmanned space probes years later.<sup>13</sup> NASA's [Pioneer 10](/objects/pioneer-10) spacecraft later confirmed Swann's remote-viewing findings about Jupiter when it flew past the planet in the 1970s.<sup>14</sup> Swann recalled that Puthoff told him he was the first to find a ring around the planet.<sup>15</sup>

Swann was also asked by the CIA to remote view a secret facility in the Soviet Union believed to be used for manufacturing nuclear weapons,<sup>16</sup> and his descriptions of the facility were later confirmed by satellite imagery.<sup>17</sup>

Within [Project STARGATE](/projects/project-stargate), Swann trained personnel for the [United States Army](/organisations/united-states-army). [Ed Dames](/people/ed-dames) was selected as one of five prototype Army members trained by Swann during 1983 and 1984.<sup>18</sup> Dames described Swann as his mentor and said he later became Swann's protege.<sup>19</sup> Dames also said that Swann asked Puthoff who was funding the training, because it was classified.<sup>20</sup> One of the first training targets Swann set for Dames was the Washington Monument.<sup>21</sup>

[Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) told [Thomas Wilson](/people/thomas-wilson) about Swann's story.<sup>22</sup> According to Swann's memoir, as described in "Hair of the Alien: DNA and Other Forensic Evidence for Alien Abductions", the writer recounts covert meetings between 1975 and 1977 with a shadowy group of individuals that led to Swann's participation in remote-viewing evidence of intelligence on the moon, an account associated with Ingo Swann's 1975-1977 remote viewing episodes.<sup>23</sup> His planetary work is also associated with the Jupiter remote viewing session.
