David Charles Grusch is a former [US Air Force](/organisations/us-air-force) intelligence officer who served for fourteen years in active duty, the [Air National Guard](/organisations/air-national-guard), and the Reserve, reaching the rank of Major.<sup>5</sup> He built a career across the [United States Intelligence Community](/organisations/united-states-intelligence-community), holding senior positions at both the [National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)](/organisations/national-reconnaissance-office-nro) and the [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA)](/organisations/national-geospatial-intelligence-agency-nga). From 2016 to 2021 he served at the NRO as a Senior Intelligence Officer, leading the production of the NRO director's daily briefing.<sup>3</sup> In that capacity he was rated as a GS-15 civilian, the military equivalent of a full colonel.<sup>4</sup> From 2019 to 2021 he served concurrently, in his NRO reservist capacity, as the NRO's representative to the [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAP Task Force)](/projects/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uap-task-force).<sup>1</sup> From late 2021 to July 2022 he joined the NGA in a civilian role, serving as the agency's co-lead for [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) and trans-medium object analysis, cleared at the Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information level and designated the agency's Senior Technical Advisor for UAP analysis.<sup>6,7</sup> He sat on the [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)](/organisations/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-task-force-uaptf) throughout this period and helped author the task force's 2019 security classification guide.

In 2019, the UAPTF Director tasked Grusch with identifying all [Special Access Programs (SAP)](/topics/special-access-program-sap) and controlled access programmes needed to satisfy the task force's congressionally mandated mission.<sup>8</sup> In the course of that work he was informed of a multi-decade [UAP crash retrieval and reverse engineering programme](/projects/uap-crash-retrieval-and-reverse-engineering-program) and was denied access when he requested it.<sup>9</sup> His subsequent investigation centred on interviews with high-level intelligence officials, some of whom he described as directly involved with the programme, and his testimony was based on information from individuals who shared evidence in the form of photography, official documentation, and classified oral testimony. In 2021 he confidentially provided classified information to the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) Inspector General concerning the withholding of UAP-related information from [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress). In May 2022, acting through his attorney [Charles III McCullough](/people/charles-iii-mccullough) — senior partner of [Compass Rose Legal Group](/organisations/compass-rose-legal-group) in Washington and the original [Inspector General of the Intelligence Community](/organisations/inspector-general-of-the-intelligence-community), confirmed by the United States Senate in 2011<sup>29</sup> — he formally filed a [Grusch Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG) whistleblower complaint (2022)](/documents/grusch-intelligence-community-inspector-general-icig-whistleblower-complaint-2022) under [Presidential Policy Directive 19 (PPD-19)](/documents/presidential-policy-directive-19-ppd-19), alleging that the US government was operating with a level of secrecy that exceeded congressional oversight.<sup>10,11</sup> The [Office of the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG)](/organisations/office-of-the-intelligence-community-inspector-general-icig) found the complaint credible and urgent in July 2022,<sup>12</sup> and a summary was immediately submitted to the Director of National Intelligence and the relevant congressional intelligence committees.<sup>13</sup>

The substance of Grusch's allegations centred on three interlocking claims. First, he alleged that recoveries of partial fragments through to intact craft of non-human origin had been made for decades by the US government, its allies, and defence contractors,<sup>20</sup> and that analysis of these objects — based on vehicle morphologies, material science testing, unique atomic arrangements, and radiological signatures — had determined them to be of exotic, non-human origin.<sup>21</sup> Second, he alleged that [reverse engineering of recovered craft of non-human origin](/topics/reverse-engineering-of-recovered-craft-of-non-human-origin) had been conducted within multiple agencies by nesting UAP activities inside conventional special access programmes without appropriate reporting to oversight authorities,<sup>22</sup> characterising this as a decades-long, publicly unknown competition with near-peer adversaries to retrieve and exploit such material for asymmetric national defence advantage.<sup>23</sup> Third, he alleged that the information about these programmes had been illegally withheld from Congress,<sup>15</sup> and that the operation had been illegally shielded from proper congressional oversight, with him personally targeted and harassed as a result of his investigation.<sup>14</sup> He also alleged that the programmes were funded in part through misappropriation of funds and that individuals on them had raised concerns with him about violations of Federal Acquisition Regulation contracting rules. Beginning in 2022, he provided Congress with hours of recorded classified information transcribed into hundreds of pages. He suffered months of retaliation and reprisals beginning in 2021,<sup>16</sup> and filed a separate complaint alleging illegal retaliation for his confidential disclosures.

Grusch's public statements were cleared for open publication by the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) Prepublication and Security Review on 2023-04-04 and 2023-04-06.<sup>17</sup> He left government service on 2023-04-07, citing a desire to advance government accountability through public awareness.<sup>18</sup> On 2023-07-26 he testified under oath at the [2023 House Oversight UAP Hearing](/events/2023-house-oversight-uap-hearing), appearing alongside [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) and [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor).<sup>24</sup> In that public session he stated that the US government is in possession of UAP, based on his having interviewed over forty witnesses over four years,<sup>25</sup> and testified — relaying assessments of people with direct knowledge still on the programme — that non-human biologics came with some of the recovered craft.<sup>26</sup> He described having spent eleven and a half hours in closed sessions with both intelligence committees, providing them with the specific names and titles of individuals with firsthand knowledge, the facilities involved, and the corporations and divisions participating in the programmes. He described the retaliation he experienced as "very brutal and unfortunate"<sup>27</sup> and characterised the administrative tactics used to silence career government members as "administrative terrorism".<sup>28</sup> Members of the [US House Committee on Armed Services (HASC)](/organisations/us-house-committee-on-armed-services-hasc) and the [US Senate Committee on Armed Services (SASC)](/organisations/us-senate-committee-on-armed-services-sasc) received congressional notifications through the Periodic Program Review process, while the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) — whose then-director [Sean Kirkpatrick](/people/sean-kirkpatrick) Grusch had spoken with in a classified conversation in April 2022 — publicly maintained that no credible evidence of extraterrestrial activity existed, a characterisation Grusch disputed.

Grusch's credibility was attested to by several colleagues. [Karl E. Nell](/people/karl-e-nell), a recently retired Army Colonel who served as the Army's liaison to the UAPTF from 2021 to 2022 and worked alongside Grusch, characterised him as "beyond reproach".<sup>29</sup> Several members of the recovery programme independently corroborated information Grusch had provided to the Inspector General's office. Grusch consistently preferred the term [Non-Human Intelligence](/topics/non-human-intelligence) over "extraterrestrial" to avoid denoting a specific origin and to keep the analytical aperture open to explanations not yet understood from a biological or astrophysics perspective.<sup>30</sup> He has stated publicly that he placed US government awareness of non-human intelligence as far back as the 1930s, and that he is personally aware of individuals participating in reverse engineering programmes for non-terrestrial craft who would be willing to testify in closed session with appropriate assurances that they would not be administratively punished for breaking their non-disclosure agreements.
