Pat Price was one of the leading psychics in the [Stanford Research Institute (SRI)](/organisations/stanford-research-institute-sri)'s [remote viewing](/topics/remote-viewing-rv) programme during the 1970s. In 1973 he gave physicist [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) an unsolicited package of remote-viewing notes describing four alleged "UFO bases" on Earth, an episode recorded as the 1973 Pat Price remote-viewing of alleged "UFO bases" for Hal Puthoff.<sup>1</sup> [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee) said that the SRI experiments with Price and [Ingo Swann](/people/ingo-swann) - including Ingo Swann's 1975-1977 remote viewing episodes - suggested that remote viewing operates on an addressing scheme.<sup>2</sup> According to Bill Chalker, Puthoff was uncertain what to make of Price's notes but confident of his remarkable psychic abilities.<sup>3</sup>

One of the four sites Price identified lay just west of the secret US intelligence facility at Pine Gap in central Australia.<sup>4</sup> Chalker said Puthoff was intrigued that the associated remote-viewing location was Mt. Zeil.<sup>5</sup> Price's notes named four alleged underground bases in total: Mt. Zeil, Mt. Hayes in Alaska, Mt. Perdido in the Spanish Pyrenees, and Mount Inyangani in Zimbabwe.<sup>6</sup> According to Hair of the Alien: DNA and Other Forensic Evidence for Alien Abductions, Mt. Perdido lay near the Basque territories.<sup>7</sup>

By the early 1980s, Price's remote-viewing notes had reached army officer Skip Atwater, who used Price's targets in a blind, controlled fashion while training and assessing US Army remote viewers.<sup>8</sup> Working blind, Atwater's own viewers reported hidden underground facilities with an alleged alien or extraterrestrial connection at the same four localities Price had identified a decade earlier.<sup>9</sup>

According to Luis Elizondo, Price also remote-viewed a secret [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa) location in West Virginia while working from a couch on the US west coast, correctly describing identifying labels on file folders locked in a subterranean cabinet there.<sup>10</sup>

Julia Mossbridge said she recalled a story, which she believed came from [Edwin C. May](/people/edwin-c-may) but possibly from [Joseph McMoneagle](/people/joseph-mcmoneagle), that in the 1990s Russian counterparts told American researchers "you killed all your psychics" - a remark she understood to refer to Price and possibly other American psi researchers.<sup>11</sup>
