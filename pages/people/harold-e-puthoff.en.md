Harold E. Puthoff, known widely as Hal Puthoff, is an American theoretical physicist and engineer whose career has spanned laser physics, parapsychology research, and advisory roles on classified US government programmes concerning unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP). He earned his PhD from [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university) in 1967<sup>1</sup>, and his professional background spans decades of research at General Electric, Sperry Corporation, the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa), Stanford University, and [SRI International](/organisations/sri-international)<sup>2</sup>. He went on to serve almost every major US government entity, including the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) and the NSA, as a senior scientific advisor<sup>3</sup>, publishing numerous papers on quantum physics, lasers, and space propulsion<sup>4</sup> and holding patents in the laser, energy, and communications fields<sup>5</sup>.

In the late 1960s, Puthoff led the development of a remote-viewing technique at Stanford University<sup>6</sup>. As a Stanford researcher and NSA employee, he and [Russell Targ](/people/russell-targ) were approached by the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia), which told them Russia had a remote-viewing programme<sup>7</sup>; the resulting work, Project Stargate, became successful enough that Puthoff reported directly to the CIA director and the White House on a regular basis<sup>8</sup>. He worked for both the NSA and the CIA<sup>9</sup> and, according to Tom DeLonge, created the CIA's psychic spy programme<sup>17</sup>. During this period, [Ingo Swann](/people/ingo-swann) worked with Puthoff and Targ<sup>15</sup>, and Puthoff told Swann that the US Army was funding his remote-viewing training<sup>16</sup>. In 1973, [Pat Price](/people/pat-price) gave Puthoff an unsolicited package of remote-viewing notes describing alleged "UFO bases" on Earth<sup>10</sup>, prompting Puthoff to contact a CIA station chief he knew at Pine Gap to ask if anything unusual was happening in the area<sup>11</sup>. Puthoff and Targ's Outbounder remote-viewing protocol was published in an Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) journal in 1976<sup>12</sup>, and the two were directly responsible for the Cognitive Sciences Laboratory's work from 1972 to 1986<sup>13</sup>. Bill Chalker's account of the episode describes Puthoff as a theoretical physicist who led controversial CIA and Defense Department research into "psychic spies"<sup>14</sup>.

Puthoff later founded EarthTech International and, before joining the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP)))](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap) effort, had previously managed the CIA's Stargate programme<sup>18</sup>. According to [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), before Elizondo joined AAWSAP/AATIP, Puthoff had already conceived and commissioned studies including invisibility cloaking, traversable wormholes and stargates, antigravity, brain-machine interfaces, and [warp drive](/topics/warp-drive)<sup>19</sup>. Puthoff subsequently worked as a contractor and senior advisor to the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip)<sup>20</sup>, and continued attending meetings, providing briefings and Proposed Program Plans, and meeting with staff under Elizondo's leadership until Elizondo resigned<sup>21</sup>. Puthoff himself has said that AATIP was a follow-on, semi-official programme structured differently from AAWSAP<sup>22</sup>, and that AATIP was in fact a nickname for AAWSAP rather than an independent programme in its own right<sup>23</sup>; he described Elizondo as basically the leader of AATIP<sup>24</sup>. Separately, he served as a senior scientist for Bigelow Aerospace examining activity funded by the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip)'s Defense Intelligence Agency backers<sup>25</sup>, and on Bigelow's behalf commissioned around 36 papers from experts worldwide on future developments to the year 2050, without disclosing any connection to extraterrestrials or UAP<sup>26</sup>. He has stated that the Kona Blue Special Access Program followed on from AAWSAP<sup>27</sup>, that AAWSAP itself was a classified programme intended to obtain answers about UAP<sup>28</sup>, and that the so-called "legacy program" had access to crash material and biologics<sup>29</sup> but was ultimately shut down because its materials could never be placed in front of scientists capable of properly evaluating them<sup>30</sup>.

Puthoff possessed a piece of material allegedly recovered from the 1947 Roswell Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) crash<sup>31</sup>, and Elizondo has recounted that Puthoff told him a UAP crash occurred at Roswell, New Mexico in 1947 and that it was covered up by the government<sup>32</sup>, and that four deceased nonhuman bodies were recovered from the crash<sup>33</sup>. In materials analysis, Puthoff produced a 1997 paper on samples reportedly ejected from the Ubatuba object<sup>34</sup>, finding the recovered magnesium to be of exceptional purity and 6.7% denser than ordinary pure magnesium<sup>35</sup>, and wrote that a pure Mg26 isotope result would be at least anomalous and possibly evidence of extraterrestrial manufacture<sup>36</sup>. He was similarly involved in examining Art's Parts (alleged Roswell metal samples): a 2018 TTSA disclosure to the US Securities and Exchange Commission revealed the organisation had retained his EarthTech International Inc. to prepare plans on materials analysis and beamed energy propulsion launch systems<sup>36</sup><sup>37</sup>, and a further 2019 disclosure stated that Tom DeLonge sold six such samples to TTSA for $35,000, with Puthoff to carry out the analysis<sup>38</sup>. Puthoff said no evidence could be found that anyone had ever made a sample like the Art's Parts bismuth/magnesium-zinc material<sup>39</sup>; in a 2012 letter to Linda Moulton Howe he stated that tests on the samples had produced no interesting or anomalous outcome under various applied fields<sup>40</sup>, though he later told a 2018 Las Vegas conference that the bismuth/magnesium samples turned out to be an excellent microscopic waveguide for terahertz-frequency electromagnetic radiation<sup>41</sup>.

In 2017, Puthoff joined Elizondo and [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) in launching [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa)<sup>42</sup>, a commercial venture aiming to raise money for UAP research<sup>42</sup>, and he was the organisation's third co-founder<sup>43</sup>. Reporting on Puthoff's role in AATIP described him as an engineer who had researched extrasensory perception (ESP) for the CIA and later worked as an AATIP contractor<sup>28</sup>, and coverage of a 2009 memo from Senator Harry Reid confirmed that Puthoff was well established to have worked within AATIP<sup>35</sup>, noting that his name (along with Elizondo's) had at one point been redacted from the memo's public release. Puthoff was later described as co-founder of [To The Stars Academy (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-ttsa), where Elizondo was employed<sup>37</sup>. A week after Tom DeLonge disclosed the departures of Mellon, Elizondo, and [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice) from TTSA, he also disclosed Puthoff's resignation as a director<sup>44</sup>; Puthoff subsequently transitioned to the company's scientific advisory board<sup>45</sup>. According to Elizondo, TTSA's roster also included [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan)<sup>46</sup>, and Puthoff was involved with Mellon as part of the organisation<sup>47</sup>.

Puthoff maintained a laboratory of approximately 560 square metres at his office in Austin, Texas<sup>48</sup>, where [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) worked for him<sup>49</sup> as an astrophysicist and contractor on AAWSAP/AATIP<sup>50</sup>, and later as chief scientist for Puthoff's Institute for Advanced Studies at Austin<sup>51</sup>. Davis was, at the time of an alleged 2002 meeting with [Thomas Wilson](/people/thomas-wilson), working for Puthoff as his boss<sup>52</sup>; asked in a February 2020 address about the resulting "Admiral Wilson memo," Puthoff said it had been leaked on the internet and that Wilson was one of the joint chiefs of staff interviewed by Davis, declining to comment further because it concerned potentially ongoing programmes<sup>53</sup>. Puthoff and Davis also built a gravity-measuring device together, and his academic output extended into propulsion physics, including papers on [warp drive](/topics/warp-drive), traversable wormholes, and time machines<sup>54</sup>, and he has been described as an expert in zero-point energy theory, which posits that harnessable energy exists within the vacuum of space<sup>55</sup>. He estimated that generating and sustaining a space-time warp bubble would require energy in the range of 3.2 to 5.6 terahertz<sup>56</sup>. Puthoff's work intersected with the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter) and broader Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena research through his continued advisory role, and his findings on Remote Viewing at SRI International remain a reference point for the field's early scientific study of anomalous perception.
