Harold E. Puthoff is an American physicist and engineer whose career has spanned classified government research, theoretical physics, and UAP investigation over more than five decades. He earned his PhD from [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university) in 1967<sup>2</sup> and built a professional background that included positions at General Electric, Sperry, the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa), Stanford University, and SRI International.<sup>3</sup> He published widely on quantum physics, lasers, and space propulsion and held patents in the laser, energy, and communications fields.<sup>4</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) has described him as a physicist, engineer, and chief scientist on highly classified government projects for over fifty years.

Puthoff's early intelligence-community work centred on parapsychology. While a researcher at Stanford University and an employee of the NSA, he and his colleague Russell Targ were approached by the [CIA](/organisations/cia), which informed them that the USSR had a remote-viewing programme and that the United States needed to develop one in response.<sup>6</sup> Puthoff subsequently pioneered the [Stargate Program](/matters/stargate-program) and remote-viewing research at Stanford University in the late 1960s.<sup>7</sup> The programme grew to the point where Puthoff reported directly to the director of the CIA and the White House on a regular basis.<sup>8</sup> His prominence in this field was reflected by an appearance on the cover of Time magazine, which, according to Elizondo, pitched hardened scientists against him for decades.<sup>9</sup>

Puthoff later transitioned to UAP-related government contracting. He served as chief scientist for the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program](/matters/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program) (AAWSAP)<sup>10</sup> and worked with and supported the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program) (AATIP).<sup>14</sup> In that capacity he conceived and commissioned 38 [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) Defence Intelligence Research Documents](/documents/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap-defence-intelligence-research-documents), each drawn from a top scientist expert on technology concepts applicable to the UAP problem, including invisibility cloaking, traversable wormholes, antigravity, brain-machine interfaces, and warp drives.<sup>11</sup> He also founded [EarthTech International](/organisations/earthtech-international), which was separately retained by AATIP to prepare plans on materials analysis and beamed energy propulsion launch systems.<sup>12,13</sup> He worked alongside [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) throughout much of this period as a contractor for the AAWSAP and AATIP programmes. Puthoff subsequently sought and obtained permission to publish all but one of his AATIP papers in the Journal of the British Interplanetary Society.<sup>15</sup>

Puthoff is the principal architect of the [UAP Warp Bubble Theory](/matters/uap-warp-bubble-theory), which he presented to AATIP team members including Elizondo and [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) at [the Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon) in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF). He proposed that all six UAP observables — hypersonic velocities, instantaneous acceleration, low observability, transmedium travel, [anti-gravity propulsion](/concepts/anti-gravity-propulsion), and [UAP biological effects](/matters/uap-biological-effects) — could be the product of a single technology: the warping of [space-time](/concepts/space-time-warping) in a localised area to create a bubble around a craft.<sup>16,18</sup> He argued that all six observables are consistent with [Albert Einstein](/people/albert-einstein)'s theory of [general relativity](/concepts/general-relativity)<sup>17</sup> and stated that with sufficient energy technology a vehicle could compress space in front of it whilst stretching it behind, theoretically enabling faster-than-light travel relative to an outside observer.<sup>19</sup> He identified zero-point energy — free energy theoretically available in the quantum vacuum — as a candidate mechanism for generating the required energy levels<sup>21,22</sup> and co-wrote papers proposing a new paradigm for interstellar travel touching on warp drives, traversable wormholes, and time machines.<sup>23</sup> He concluded that explaining UAP observables had moved from a theoretical challenge to a technological one.<sup>20</sup>

Puthoff has been involved in the analysis of physical materials associated with UAP claims. He examined samples known as the [Art's Parts Samples](/objects/arts-parts-samples), and in a 2012 letter to journalist [Linda Moulton Howe](/people/linda-moulton-howe) acknowledged that testing at that time had not yielded an interesting or anomalous outcome.<sup>28</sup> By 2018 he was reporting to a Las Vegas conference that the bismuth and magnesium samples turned out to be an excellent microscopic waveguide for very high frequency electromagnetic radiation at terahertz frequencies.<sup>29</sup> He was also reported to be in possession of material allegedly recovered from the [Roswell UAP Crash](/events/roswell-uap-crash) — described as an intricate piece with multiple microscopic layers of interlacing bismuth and magnesium.<sup>30</sup> He told Elizondo that the 1947 Roswell crash was real<sup>31</sup> and theorised that the craft may have been brought down by an [electromagnetic pulse](/concepts/electromagnetic-pulse) produced as a by-product of nearby atomic-bomb testing. Puthoff also authored a paper titled "Ultraterrestrial Models", published in the Journal of Cosmology, which enumerates possibilities for UAP beings and posits that an unidentified phenomenon interacting with the current human population is highly likely not constituted exclusively of members of that population.<sup>32</sup>

In October 2017, Puthoff co-founded [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) (TTSA) alongside [Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) and former Department of Defense official [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon),<sup>24</sup> serving as a director and its third co-founder following his previous CIA and Defence Department research.<sup>25</sup> Puthoff, Mellon, and [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) had urged Elizondo to join TTSA, describing its directive as encompassing fresh approaches to UAP science and engineering, educating journalists and laypeople, and generating film, television, and publishing content about UAP. TTSA collapsed in late 2020 with the departure of multiple key figures including Puthoff.<sup>26</sup> The [Wilson Davis Memo](/documents/wilson-davis-memo) has been connected to Puthoff through multiple accounts: sources suggest he may have sent a copy to [Edgar Mitchell](/people/edgar-mitchell),<sup>14</sup> and when asked about the document at an Arlington Institute address in 2020-02, Puthoff stated that Admiral Wilson was among the joint chiefs of staff interviewed by his senior scientist colleague [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis).<sup>16</sup> Neither Puthoff nor Davis, according to one anonymous researcher cited by journalist Ross Coulthart, denied the memorandum's authenticity despite being given multiple opportunities to do so.
