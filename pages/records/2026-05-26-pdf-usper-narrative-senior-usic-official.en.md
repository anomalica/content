Formally catalogued as ODNI-UAP-D001, USPER Narrative, Senior USIC Official and dated 2026-05-26, it was collected as part of the PURSUE collection of UAP-related records administered by the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)](/organisations/office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence-odni). The document describes a helicopter mission conducted in late 2025 during which the officer and two pilots encountered multiple Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) over a military test range, an event catalogued separately as the Late 2025 test range UAP helicopter encounter.

The account begins with the officer, a colleague, and two pilots departing the Joint Operations Center (JOC) in early evening daylight.<sup>1</sup> The stated purpose was to investigate loud thuds heard in the mountains that had coincided with UAP sightings reported over several prior nights,<sup>2</sup> with the specific aim of searching remote terrain for debris or objects that might explain the orb-like sightings.<sup>3</sup> The team flew a low-altitude nap-of-the-earth route through the mountain range for several hours.<sup>4</sup> Debris spotted on the ground was inspected on multiple descents but identified each time as remnants from rockets and other projectiles accumulated during years of weapons testing on the range.<sup>5</sup> The team also discovered a large cave entrance with no visible end in sight;<sup>6</sup> finding no safe landing spot, the officer instructed the pilot to orbit it several times before noting its location and pressing on.<sup>7</sup>

After dropping off the colleague at a rendezvous point and refuelling at a prepositioned tanker, the JOC radioed with a request to search a nearby mountain for debris spotted by a ground team.<sup>8</sup> By this time the sun had set, and the pilots switched to forward-looking infrared (FLIR) and night vision goggles (NVG) while the officer continued with the naked eye.<sup>9</sup> The mountain search yielded no findings, but as the team began returning to the JOC the JOC messaged that radar had detected hits several miles up range, in the same area where UAP activity had been observed on prior nights.<sup>10</sup> The officer relayed this to the pilots and the helicopter altered course to intercept, initiating a sequence of encounters that the narrative states lasted over an hour.<sup>11</sup> Ground teams reported observing a UAP on FLIR described as super-hot, low to the ground, and moving east then south at high speed, and that it subsequently split into two and changed direction.<sup>12</sup> A ground team further radioed that the UAP had risen from the ground, approached within approximately three metres of the helicopter, dropped below it, and then sped away.<sup>13</sup> The pilots simultaneously observed through NVG what appeared to be the same object split into two as a smaller object emerged before accelerating out of sight.<sup>14</sup> The helicopter briefly pursued but broke off, unable to match the object's speed.<sup>15</sup>

Directed by the JOC to further radar detections, the crew hovered at approximately 210 metres above ground level (AGL).<sup>16</sup> From this position the officer and pilots observed countless orange orbs swarming in all directions against the backdrop of a mountain for several minutes before the display faded.<sup>17</sup> Redirected again by the JOC on fresh radar hits and hovering at approximately 210 metres AGL,<sup>18</sup> the crew then witnessed two large orbs flare up side-by-side, stationary and just above the rotor disc to their right, visible through both NVG and the naked eye.<sup>19</sup> The orbs were oval-shaped, orange with a white or yellow centre, and emitted light in all directions.<sup>20</sup> After a few seconds a third orb flared up below the pair, followed by a fourth, forming a total of four or five orbs in a T formation beneath the original two.<sup>21</sup> The orbs then dimmed in reverse order and remained stationary until they vanished; the officer noted the entire event lasted 10 to 15 seconds.<sup>22</sup> The officer did not photograph the objects, stating that attention was focused on assessing what they were and whether they posed a threat.<sup>23</sup>

Shortly afterwards the JOC reported that several fighter jets had launched on a training mission in the operating area and requested the team's assistance in identifying the UAP. The team spotted the jets entering visual range at approximately 7,000 metres AGL, identifiable by their blinking navigation lights.<sup>24</sup> The same type of orbs then appeared directly above the jets, flaring up one at a time in a horizontal formation and matching the jets' speed and flight path.<sup>25</sup> These orbs dimmed sequentially after 10 to 15 seconds, and the pattern repeated several times as the jets transited the airspace before eventually landing.<sup>26</sup> The officer remarked to the pilots that the orbs appeared to be pursuing the fighter jets. Additional orbs were observed forming a distinct triangle formation before vanishing.<sup>27</sup> Running low on fuel, the pilots returned to the JOC.<sup>28</sup> The narrative concludes with the officer noting that the crew were virtually speechless following the observations, and that the officer entered the JOC for a brief debrief before departing.<sup>29</sup>
