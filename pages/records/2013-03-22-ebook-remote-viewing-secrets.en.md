Remote Viewing Secrets: A Handbook presents [Remote Viewing](/topics/remote-viewing-rv) as the production of information about a distant place, event, person, object, or concept while the target remains inaccessible to the viewer and other participants.<sup>1</sup> The handbook treats protocol as central: it defines a protocol as a detailed plan for an experiment or procedure,<sup>2</sup> and distinguishes protocols from the methods and techniques applied within them. It describes Controlled Remote Viewing (CRV), Extended Remote Viewing (ERV), Scientific Remote Viewing and Technical Remote Viewing as named methods.<sup>3</sup> It also warns that Front-Loading (remote viewing)—providing information about a target in advance—can introduce overlay and make psychic information difficult to distinguish from reasoning.<sup>4</sup>

The historical account centres on [Joseph McMoneagle](/people/joseph-mcmoneagle)'s description of his involvement with [Project STARGATE](/projects/project-stargate), where he says he was among the original remote viewers recruited for intelligence purposes.<sup>5</sup> He describes the [Cognitive Sciences Laboratory The Laboratories for Fundamental Research](/organisations/cognitive-sciences-laboratory-the-laboratories-for-fundamental-research) at [SRI International](/organisations/sri-international) as conducting experimentation and supporting intelligence operations from 1972 to 1995.<sup>6</sup> The source also records research on [Anomalous Cognition (AC)](/topics/anomalous-cognition-ac), including S. James P. Spottiswoode's reported association between trial performance and Local Sidereal Time. One database of 1,468 trials showed a reported 340% effect-size increase near 13.5 hours of local sidereal time,<sup>7</sup> while an independent database of 1,015 trials showed a reported 450% increase in the same period.<sup>8</sup> The handbook separately reports a small overall correlation between AC and Geomagnetic Fluctuations (GMF) across 2,879 trials.<sup>9</sup>

For UAP research, the handbook matters chiefly because it records explicit limits on applying remote viewing to anomalous subjects. McMoneagle considers UFOs poor targets and says that, in his experience, UFO-related statements obtained in this way have a chance of being correct “very close to zero”.<sup>10</sup><sup>11</sup> The Tunguska event, in Russia, Siberia, Tunguska Basin, is offered as an example of a remote-viewing interpretation, but the source identifies the proposed explanation as conjecture that has not been verified.<sup>12</sup> Alongside these cautions, its emphasis on blind targeting, feedback, evaluation, and retaining all session data—including material judged inaccurate or irrelevant—provides a framework for assessing claims and preserving an auditable research record.<sup>13</sup>
