This source is a 2023-06-05 article presenting allegations by former intelligence official [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch) about covert programmes that he said possessed retrieved craft of non-human origin.<sup>1</sup> It also reports supporting statements attributed to other intelligence officials and places the allegations in the context of [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) oversight of [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap).

## Subject and principal allegations

The article states that Grusch gave Congress and the Office of the Inspector General of the Intelligence Community (ICIG) classified information about deeply covert programmes that he said possessed intact and partially intact craft of non-human origin.<sup>1</sup> Grusch also said that information about these alleged recovery programmes had been illegally withheld from Congress.<sup>2</sup>

According to the source, other active and retired intelligence officials with knowledge of the alleged programmes independently provided similar information about Grusch's account, both on and off the record.<sup>3</sup> The article describes Grusch as a veteran of the [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA)](/organisations/national-geospatial-intelligence-agency-nga) and the [National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)](/organisations/national-reconnaissance-office-nro).<sup>4</sup>

Grusch said that the recovery of partial fragments through to intact vehicles had continued for decades and into the present, involving the government, its allies and defence contractors.<sup>5</sup> He said analysis had identified the recovered objects as exotic in origin, which he defined as involving non-human intelligence, whether extraterrestrial or of unknown origin. His stated basis included vehicle morphologies, material-science testing, unique atomic arrangements and radiological signatures.<sup>6</sup>

## UAP task-force context

According to the article, Grusch served as the NRO's representative to the [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force](/projects/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-task-force-uaptf) from 2019 to 2021.<sup>7</sup> The source describes the task force as having been established to investigate objects formerly called unidentified flying objects, or UFOs, and officially termed UAP.<sup>8</sup> It reports that the task force was led by the Department of the Navy under the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, and that it was later reorganised and expanded into the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro).<sup>9</sup>

The article also identifies Karl E. Nell as a recently retired Army colonel and aerospace executive who served as the [United States Army](/organisations/united-states-army) liaison to the task force from 2021 to 2022 and worked with Grusch.<sup>10</sup> Nell said that Grusch was “beyond reproach”.<sup>11</sup>

## Background of the contributors

The source describes Christopher Mellon as having spent nearly twenty years in the US Intelligence Community and as having served as Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence. It also reports that he had worked with Congress on UAP for years.<sup>12</sup> Mellon said that current and former officials had shared detailed information with him about the alleged recovery programme, including its history, governing documents and the alleged location of a recovered craft.<sup>13</sup>

The article describes Jonathan Grey as a generational officer of the US Intelligence Community with a Top Secret clearance who worked at the [National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)](/organisations/national-air-and-space-intelligence-center-nasic), where UAP analysis was his focus.<sup>14</sup> Grey said that he considered the non-human-intelligence phenomenon real and that humanity was not alone.<sup>15</sup> He also said that alleged craft retrievals were not limited to the United States but represented a global phenomenon.<sup>16</sup>

The source identifies [Garry Nolan](/people/garry-nolan) as a professor in the Department of Pathology at [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university) who had applied laboratory technologies to the analysis of purported exotic materials.<sup>17</sup> Nolan said that studying even small samples of such material could lead to benefits that were then inconceivable.<sup>18</sup>

## Disclosure and oversight

The article reports that Grusch prepared numerous UAP briefings for Congress and helped draft UAP language for the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023 (FY2023 NDAA).<sup>19</sup> According to the source, the FY2023 NDAA was spearheaded by [Kirsten Gillibrand](/people/kirsten-gillibrand) and [Marco Rubio](/people/marco-rubio), and was signed into law by [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden) in December 2022.<sup>20</sup>

The article says that the relevant provision allowed people with information about UAP to inform Congress without retaliation, regardless of previous non-disclosure agreements.<sup>21</sup> It also reports that the law required information on material retrieval, material analysis, reverse engineering, research and development involving UAP, both current and historical.<sup>22</sup>

In statements cleared for publication by the Pentagon in April 2023, Grusch asserted that UFO “legacy programmes” had been concealed within multiple agencies by nesting UAP activities in conventional secret access programmes without appropriate reporting to oversight authorities.<sup>23</sup> He separately reported to Congress what he described as a decades-long, publicly unknown competition with near-peer adversaries to locate UAP crashes and landings, retrieve material and reverse-engineer it for national-defence advantages.<sup>24</sup>

The article states that Congress had not been provided with physical materials relating to wreckage or other non-human objects.<sup>25</sup> It also reports that current members of the alleged recovery programme spoke to the ICIG and corroborated information Grusch had supplied in his classified complaint.<sup>26</sup>

## Complaint and alleged retaliation

Grusch was represented by Charles McCullough III, a senior partner at the [Compass Rose Legal Group](/organisations/compass-rose-legal-group) in [USA, Washington, D.C.](/places/washington-dc-usa).<sup>27</sup> The article identifies McCullough as the first Inspector General of the Intelligence Community and reports that he filed a reprisal complaint on Grusch's behalf with the ICIG in May 2022.<sup>28</sup>

According to the article, the Unclassified complaint of David Charles Grusch stated that UAP-related classified information had been withheld or concealed from Congress by elements of the Intelligence Community to thwart Congressional oversight of the UAP programme.<sup>29</sup> The source states that Grusch's testimony for the classified complaint was provided under oath.<sup>30</sup> The ICIG found the complaint “credible and urgent” in July 2022, according to the article.<sup>31</sup>

The source further reports that a summary of the complaint was submitted to Avril Haines, then Director of National Intelligence, and to the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence and [House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence](/organisations/house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence).<sup>32</sup> The article says that a whistleblower reprisal investigation was launched and that Grusch began communicating with Congressional intelligence-committee staff in private closed-door sessions. Grusch said he left government on 2023-04-07 to advance government accountability through public awareness.<sup>33</sup>

## Responses and implications presented

Nell said that Grusch's assertion about a covert terrestrial arms race focused on reverse-engineering technologies of unknown origin was fundamentally correct, and that at least some of those technologies derived from non-human intelligence.<sup>34</sup> Mellon described the matter as an oversight challenge for Congressional committees and said he believed Congressional leaders were capable of addressing it.<sup>35</sup>

[Andre Carson](/people/andre-carson) said that whistleblowing was essential to governmental checks and balances and that federal employees should not be discouraged from coming forward by fear of retaliation.<sup>36</sup> The article reports that Carson presided over the first open Congressional hearing on UAP since 1968 in May 2022.<sup>37</sup>
