According to Relationships with Inhabitants of Celestial Bodies, dated 1947-06, [J. Robert Oppenheimer](/people/j-robert-oppenheimer) and [Albert Einstein](/people/albert-einstein) considered relations with extraterrestrial beings both legally familiar in principle and difficult in practice.<sup>1</sup><sup>2</sup> The document distinguishes between accepting the possibility of mutual understanding and determining the principles on which such relationships would rest.<sup>3</sup> It presents communication through a language, together with a psychology sufficiently similar to that of humans, as a necessary first condition.<sup>4</sup> Its proposed legal framework is a new field called Law Among Planetary Peoples, which would require changes so fundamental that it might no longer be regarded as international law.<sup>5</sup>

The document considers political recognition, settlement and conflict. It states that culturally and politically organised extraterrestrial beings would have a right to recognition as independent and sovereign peoples, while allowing only a necessary degree of legitimate defence if they became an imminent threat to Earth.<sup>6</sup><sup>7</sup> It also discusses whether a human population could become culturally independent on another celestial body, with living conditions on the moon or Mars needing to support stable and partly economically independent life.<sup>8</sup> In considering beings who might settle on Earth, it uses the term extraterrestrial biological entity (EBE).<sup>9</sup> Possible roles for the [United Nations (UN)](/organisations/united-nations-un) include tacit approval of tutelage, although the document also argues that the UN would not automatically have authority over humanity.<sup>10</sup><sup>11</sup>

A further subject is the legal status of celestial territory. The document defines [res nullius](/topics/res-nullius) as something belonging to nobody and proposes that an inhabited body would instead be res communis, with equal rights for celestial states.<sup>12</sup><sup>13</sup> It also addresses unidentified spacecraft, stating that their presence in Earth's atmosphere was accepted de facto by the US military and linking unidentified space vehicles with security anxiety among major powers.<sup>14</sup><sup>15</sup> The possibility of mistaking such a craft for a missile is presented as a route to accidental nuclear war, and the situation is described as extremely perilous.<sup>16</sup><sup>17</sup> The document identifies both men as being based in USA, New Jersey, Princeton; an unnamed annotator additionally recorded an approach to George Marshall and a conversation with Gordon concerning its prospects for presidential consideration.<sup>18</sup><sup>19</sup><sup>20</sup><sup>21</sup>
