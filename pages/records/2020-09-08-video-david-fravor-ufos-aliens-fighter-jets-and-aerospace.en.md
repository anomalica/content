This source is a podcast interview with [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor), a former [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) pilot and commander of [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41). It matters to UAP research because it presents Fravor's account of the 2004 USS Nimitz UFO incident, alongside discussion of the aircraft, radar, video, and official-investigation context associated with the case. According to the source record, Fravor was a United States Navy pilot for 18 years and commanded VFA-41, also known as the Black Aces.<sup>1</sup><sup>2</sup>

## Background and aviation career

According to Lex Fridman, Fravor personally saw and chased a [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) in 2004. The source identifies that encounter as the Tic Tac, more formally the USS Nimitz UFO incident.<sup>3</sup><sup>4</sup> Fridman said that Fravor's account, corroborated by several other pilots, was the most credible UFO sighting in history that he was aware of.<sup>5</sup>

Fridman also said that Fravor was a graduate of the United States Navy Fighter Weapons School (Top Gun). Fravor said he attended the school in Class 4 of 1997.<sup>6</sup><sup>7</sup> The interview discusses the school's training practices, including the distinction between its depiction in the film Top Gun (film) and Fravor's description of training as not being competitive between students in that manner.

Fravor described a flying career that began in the two-seat A-6 Intruder, continued with single-seat [F/A-18 Hornet](/objects/fa-18-hornet) aircraft, and shifted after 2001 to the two-seat [F/A-18F Super Hornet](/objects/fa-18f-super-hornet).<sup>8</sup> He said that he flew the two-seat Super Hornet while commanding VFA-41.<sup>9</sup> The interview uses this background to explain Fravor's familiarity with aircraft performance, crew coordination, radar systems, and visual observation.

## Account of the 2004 incident

According to Fridman's account in the interview, the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) detected several objects on 2004-11-10 at an altitude of approximately 8,500 metres and a speed of approximately 190 kilometres per hour off the California coast.<sup>10</sup> The source says that the ship detected the objects intermittently for about a week before Fravor's encounter on 2004-11-14.<sup>11</sup>

Fridman described the object associated with the incident as white, Tic Tac-shaped, approximately 12 metres long, and without wings.<sup>12</sup> The object was observed during a flight involving two [F/A-18F Super Hornet](/objects/fa-18f-super-hornet) aircraft, with two crew members in each aircraft, after a training mission had been cancelled.<sup>13</sup> The initial sighting was not recorded on video.<sup>14</sup>

The source describes the USS Princeton as a guided-missile cruiser equipped with an [AN/SPY-1 radar](/objects/anspy-1-radar). Before the encounter, the ship provided bearing, range, and altitude calls for an unidentified radar contact at approximately 80, 64, and 48 kilometres.<sup>15</sup> Fravor said the [AN/APG-73 radar](/objects/anapg-73-radar) used on Navy fighter aircraft at the time was mechanically scanned, and considered the later AN/APG-79 radar significantly better.<sup>16</sup>

Fravor said that the incident occurred off [USA, California, San Diego](/places/california-san-diego-usa), near Mexico, on a clear day with unrestricted visibility, no clouds, and calm seas.<sup>17</sup> The crew first noticed white water on the ocean surface in a cross-like shape approximately the size of a Boeing 737. The disturbance appeared to be caused by something roughly 5 to 6 metres below the surface, although the crew did not see an object beneath the water.<sup>18</sup> [Jim](/people/jim), the weapon systems operator in the other aircraft, was the first to draw attention to the white water.<sup>19</sup>

Fravor then observed a small white object with a Tic Tac shape below his aircraft, which was at approximately 6,100 metres.<sup>20</sup> He said that the object moved abruptly between directions without gradual deceleration, behaviour he considered unlike that of a helicopter.<sup>21</sup> Fravor descended towards it while his wingman remained at a higher altitude to observe from another perspective.<sup>22</sup>

According to Fravor, the object appeared to react to his approach by turning west and beginning to ascend. The four crew members in both aircraft subsequently lost sight of it at the same time.<sup>23</sup> Fravor said he approached to within approximately 0.8 kilometres, and that the object accelerated and disappeared from view in less than half a second as he closed in.<sup>24</sup> The acceleration and disappearance occurred at approximately 3,700 metres, about 2,400 metres below the other aircraft.<sup>25</sup>

The source records Fravor's estimate that the observation, from the first sighting of the white water to the object's disappearance, lasted approximately five minutes. After the object disappeared, the white water also disappeared, leaving no visible trace.<sup>26</sup> Fravor said that the object later reappeared at his flight's combat air patrol point, approximately 97 kilometres away, but that his flight could not find it again using radar.<sup>27</sup>

## Video and associated records

The video associated with the incident was recorded by [Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood) using a forward-looking infrared (FLIR) system.<sup>28</sup> The source says that Underwood recorded the object after taking off with his pilot, following Fravor's account of the initial sighting. Only the FLIR targeting-pod tape has been publicly released; other recorded data has not.<sup>29</sup>

Fravor said that the original FLIR video is approximately 90 seconds long and is the entire original recording rather than an excerpt.<sup>30</sup> He also said that the released version does not include the aircraft's radar display because that footage would reveal information about how the radar behaved while being jammed.<sup>31</sup>

In the interview, Fravor described the object as having no visible exhaust, propulsion mechanism, or heat signature.<sup>32</sup> He also described two small protrusions on its underside resembling antennas, with nothing visible on its top.<sup>33</sup> Small surface details, he said, were visible only when the video was enlarged in television mode and were generally absent from versions circulated online.

## Interpretations and disagreement

Fravor said that the object was a hard, solid physical object rather than a light projection, and that all four pilots involved would confirm that view.<sup>34</sup> He linked this assessment to the different positions of the observing aircraft and to the object's apparent response to his approach. He also said that the object was never aggressive or offensive towards his aircraft, although it departed abruptly when he approached too closely.

The interview includes an alternative interpretation from [Mick West](/people/mick-west). According to the source record, West suggested that Fravor may have miscalculated the object's size and distance.<sup>35</sup> West also argued that the apparent rapid movement off the left side of the FLIR screen could have resulted from the vision-based tracking system losing the object rather than the object rapidly departing.<sup>36</sup> Fravor disputed that explanation after repeatedly reviewing the original tapes.<sup>37</sup>

Fravor said that the incident was unlike anything else he had encountered during his flying career. At the time of the incident, he estimated that he had approximately 3,100 flight hours accumulated over 18 years of flying.<sup>38</sup> He also said that he was unable to lock the object on radar despite trying.

## AATIP and the unofficial report

The interview discusses the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip). Fravor said that [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) ran AATIP at the Pentagon, while Fridman described the programme as tasked with investigating UFOs and [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap).<sup>39</sup>

Fravor said that, around 2009, a government employee contacted him to say that the incident would be investigated. He described the resulting document as the Unofficial Official Report on the Nimitz Tic Tac incident, using that name because it was never made formally official.<sup>40</sup> According to Fravor, [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) provided the report to [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp), making it public. The circulated version was redacted, although unredacted versions also existed.<sup>41</sup>

The interview also records Fravor's view that the report was fairly well written but contained errors, and that he did not help write it. He said that official situation reports filed after events aboard a ship could become third-hand accounts as information was passed along and written up by others.

## Related UAP discussion

Fravor said that he did not consider himself a UFO enthusiast, but believed it statistically very unlikely that Earth was the only planet in the universe with life. He considered it possible that an advanced civilisation might observe humanity, particularly around nuclear technology, and that the object involved in the USS Nimitz incident did not have a conventional propulsion system.

The interview also discusses other cases and proposed explanations. Fravor described the 1997 Phoenix Lights as unexplainable, while reporting that the [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) attributed it to flares dropped by an A-10 aircraft. He disputed the flare explanation because, in his view, flares would not burn for as long as the reported lights lasted.

Fravor further said that the To The Stars Academy of Arts & Science (TTSA) was founded with a mission of treating UFOs as technology to be studied and reverse-engineered using physics-based knowledge rather than as extraterrestrial “little green men”. He also suggested that the United States government should release physical evidence, such as the Roswell wreckage, if recovered craft technology exists.

## Significance as a source

The interview is primarily a first-person account by a military aviator of the visual observation, aircraft manoeuvres, radar context, and subsequent video associated with the 2004 USS Nimitz UFO incident. It also records competing interpretations, including West's tracking-loss explanation and Fravor's rejection of it. The source therefore preserves both testimony and disagreement about the event rather than presenting a single agreed explanation.
