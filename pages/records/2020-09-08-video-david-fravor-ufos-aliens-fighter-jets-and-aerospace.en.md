Episode 122 of the [Lex Fridman Podcast](/organisations/lex-fridman-podcast), published 2020-09-08, features host [Lex Fridman](/people/lex-fridman) in a wide-ranging conversation with retired US Navy commander [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor). Fridman introduces his guest as a Navy pilot of 18 years and commanding officer of [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41), also known as the [Black Aces Squadron](/organisations/black-aces-squadron), a squadron of 12 aircraft consisting of several hundred people.<sup>1</sup> Fridman describes Fravor as one of the people who with his own eyes saw and chased a UFO in 2004, an event he refers to as the [Tic Tac Sighting](/events/tic-tac-sighting),<sup>2</sup> and states that from his perspective as a scientist the account is the most credible UFO sighting in history.<sup>3</sup> The episode was sponsored by [Athletic Greens](/organisations/athletic-greens), [ExpressVPN](/organisations/expressvpn), and [BetterHelp](/organisations/betterhelp), and Fridman noted it was likely the longest episode of the podcast to that date.<sup>28</sup>

The first portion of the episode covers Fravor's aviation career. He attended the [Navy Fighter Weapons School](/organisations/navy-fighter-weapons-school) as [Top Gun Class 4 1997](/events/top-gun-class-4-1997),<sup>4</sup> having previously been enrolled in the [Strike Fighter Weapons and Tactics Instructor (SFTI) Programme](/programmes/strike-fighter-weapons-and-tactics-instructor-sfti-programme). He describes his early career flying the [A-6 Intruder (aircraft)](/objects/a-6-intruder-aircraft), a side-by-side two-seat bomber built in the 1960s capable of all-weather low-altitude night operations using terrain-mapping radar,<sup>5</sup> and the formative working relationship he developed with his first fleet bombardier navigator, [Chris Sato](/people/chris-sato), a graduate of [MIT](/organisations/mit) who later moved to [Apple Inc](/organisations/apple-inc).<sup>6</sup> Fravor was subsequently selected as commanding officer of [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41) after fleeting up from the executive officer position under then-Commander [Weitzel (Vice Admiral)](/people/weitzel-vice-admiral),<sup>7</sup> and when he commanded the squadron it flew the two-seat [F-18F Super Hornet (aircraft)](/objects/f-18f-super-hornet-aircraft). The episode includes detailed discussion of cockpit systems including the [APG-73 Radar](/objects/apg-73-radar), the [ATFLIR Pod](/objects/atflir-pod), the [Joint Helmet Mounted Cuing System](/objects/joint-helmet-mounted-cuing-system), and [Sensor Fusion (aviation)](/concepts/sensor-fusion-aviation), as well as [Digital Flight Controls (aircraft)](/concepts/digital-flight-controls-aircraft), [3D Spatial Awareness in Aviation](/concepts/3d-spatial-awareness-in-aviation), and [Carrier Landing Night Operations](/concepts/carrier-landing-night-operations).

The central portion of the episode concerns the [Nimitz Carrier Strike Group Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV) Detection Matter, 2004-11-10 to 2004-11-16](/events/2004-11-10-to-2004-11-16-nimitz-carrier-strike-group-anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav-detection-matter). Fridman describes the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) as beginning to detect objects flying at approximately 8,500 metres altitude at about 190 kilometres per hour off the coast of California from 2004-11-10.<sup>8</sup> On 2004-11-14, Fravor's training mission was cancelled and replaced with a real-world tasking from the Princeton; the intercept involved four crew members across two [F/A-18F](/objects/fa-18f) aircraft.<sup>9</sup> No video was recorded during Fravor's visual intercept — all four crew members observed the Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV) directly.<sup>10</sup> Fravor describes the object as smooth and white, with no wings, no visible propulsion, and no windows,<sup>11</sup> and observed no rotor wash on the water surface below, eliminating helicopter as an explanation for its hovering behaviour.<sup>12</sup> He descended to approximately 4,600 metres during the intercept,<sup>13</sup> approached to approximately 800 metres from the object,<sup>14</sup> and estimated the total visual observation at approximately five minutes<sup>16</sup> before the AAV accelerated and disappeared in less than approximately half a second.<sup>15</sup> [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) flew the second aircraft and maintained a higher altitude throughout, providing an independent viewing angle.

Following Fravor's flight, [Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood) launched and recorded what became the [Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV) Forward Looking Infrared (FLIR) Recording 2004-11](/documents/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav-forward-looking-infrared-flir-recording-2004-11).<sup>17</sup> Fravor explains that when Underwood locked his [APG-73 Radar](/objects/apg-73-radar) onto the AAV, the radar indicated it was being jammed across every available radar mode,<sup>18</sup> and that the original recording is approximately one minute and thirty seconds long — claims that it is ten minutes are false.<sup>19</sup> Fravor recounts the [Fravor CVIC Tape Incident](/events/fravor-cvic-tape-incident), in which [Carrier Intelligence Center (CVIC)](/organisations/carrier-intelligence-center-cvic) officers collected the FLIR tapes, and how he subsequently retrieved them. The tape had been available informally online for years before official release; Fravor's weapons systems officer sent him a link to the footage on strangeland.com around 2008, and it later migrated to [YouTube](/organisations/youtube). The episode also addresses the [Nimitz Unofficial Investigation](/events/nimitz-unofficial-investigation): approximately five years after the incident, around 2009, Fravor received a call from a government employee conducting what he describes as the unofficial official investigation.<sup>20</sup> The resulting report was subsequently obtained by [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) and passed to [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp), who published it with redactions.<sup>21</sup> The investigator was identified by Fravor as an original member of the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), which was run at the Pentagon by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo).<sup>22</sup>

The later sections of the episode address broader UAP context and sceptical responses. Fridman raises [Mick West](/people/mick-west)'s hypothesis that Fravor and the other aircrew may have miscalculated the size and distance of the AAV;<sup>23</sup> Fravor disputes this, estimating the object at approximately 40 feet (approximately 12 metres) long — roughly the size of an [F/A-18F](/objects/fa-18f) Hornet — based on 16 years of experience judging aircraft dimensions from the air, and notes that all four witnesses returned with the same assessment.<sup>24</sup> Fravor expresses the opinion that the AAV was not US-developed technology, reasoning that an advance of that magnitude would typically be preceded by detectable academic research at leading universities.<sup>25</sup> The episode also covers the [2015 Atlantic Coast UAP Sightings](/events/2015-atlantic-coast-uap-sightings), in which pilots confirmed radar contacts were real by slewing a targeting pod onto them and detecting a heat signature,<sup>26</sup> and the [Virginia Beach UAP Near-Miss](/events/virginia-beach-uap-near-miss), in which an object described as a cube inside a sphere passed between two aircraft at approximately 30 metres clearance.<sup>27</sup> Additional discussion touches on the [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) and its principals including [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge), [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), and [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice); the [Rendlesham Forest Incident](/events/rendlesham-forest-incident); [Project Blue Book](/documents/project-blue-book); [Bob Lazar](/people/bob-lazar), whom Fravor met at the [McMinnville UAP Festival](/events/mcminnville-uap-festival); and the [SpaceX](/organisations/spacex) development programme, with Fravor drawing on his acquaintance with a [Fravor Friend Unnamed SpaceX Employee](/people/fravor-friend-unnamed-spacex-employee) who worked on the [SpaceX Falcon 1 Development](/programmes/spacex-falcon-1-development) project.
