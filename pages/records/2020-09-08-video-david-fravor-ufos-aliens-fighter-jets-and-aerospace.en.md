Episode 122 of the Lex Fridman Podcast, published 2020-09-08, is a conversation between host Lex Fridman and retired [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) commander [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor), running to approximately three hours and fifty-five minutes. Fridman introduces [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) as a pilot of 18 years<sup>1</sup> who commanded [Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41)](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41), a unit of 12 aircraft and several hundred personnel,<sup>2</sup> and describes the 2004 sighting as the most credible he is aware of, corroborated by several other pilots.<sup>3,4</sup> The episode is the primary published source for [Fravor](/people/david-fravor)'s extended first-hand account of the 2004 USS Nimitz UFO incident and is separately catalogued as Lex Fridman Podcast #122 (David Fravor interview).

The first hour surveys [Fravor](/people/david-fravor)'s career. Originally enlisted as a United States Marine Corps recruit,<sup>6</sup> he was recommended by the Corps for the United States Naval Academy, which he completed,<sup>7</sup> motivated by watching Neil Armstrong walk on the Moon in 1969 just before his fifth birthday.<sup>8</sup> After receiving his wings he was assigned to A-6 Intruder bombers on the west coast<sup>9</sup> before progressing to single-seat F/A-18 Hornet aircraft and, from the end of 2001, to the two-seat F/A-18F Super Hornet,<sup>10</sup> which he flew as commander of [VFA-41](/organisations/strike-fighter-squadron-41-vfa-41).<sup>11</sup> He attended Top Gun Naval Flight School (Navy Fighter Weapons School) in 1997 as a member of Class 4.<sup>5</sup> Extended discussion covers the culture and hierarchy of the school, the mechanics of carrier landings, the capabilities of the F/A-18F Super Hornet's radar and sensor fusion systems, the AN/APG-73 Radar carried at the time of the 2004 encounter, and a comparison with the more advanced AN/APG-79 Radar used during the 2014-2015 US East Coast UAP encounters. [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) also discusses the EA-6B Prowler as a derivative of the A-6 Intruder and the EA-18G Growler as its replacement, references the [SR-71 Blackbird](/objects/sr-71-blackbird) as an engineering marvel developed at [Lockheed Martin Skunk Works](/organisations/lockheed-martin-skunk-works) by Kelly Johnson, and recounts reaching Mach 1.78 in a Super Hornet on a check flight off Pebble Beach, at which speed the aircraft was still accelerating when the run was halted by available fuel and airspace near the Monterey Peninsula.<sup>12</sup>

The central section of the episode is [Fravor](/people/david-fravor)'s account of the 2004 USS Nimitz UFO incident. The [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) first detected unidentified objects flying at roughly 8,500 metres at approximately 190 kilometres per hour beginning 2004-11-10,<sup>13</sup> with tracking continuing for about a week before aircraft were sent to investigate.<sup>14</sup> On 2004-11-14 [Fravor](/people/david-fravor)'s flight of four crew in two F/A-18F Super Hornets launched on a training mission that was cancelled and redirected to investigate the contact.<sup>15</sup> The encounter occurred roughly 130 to 160 kilometres off the California coast<sup>16</sup> on a clear day with five to ten knots of wind.<sup>17</sup> Looking down from altitude the crews noticed anomalous cross-shaped white water on the ocean surface, approximately the size of a Boeing 737.<sup>18</sup> The crews were approximately 6,000 metres above the object when they first saw it:<sup>19</sup> a smooth, white, Tic Tac-shaped craft with no wings, no visible propulsion, and no windows,<sup>20</sup> moving north-south and east-west in abrupt motions with no apparent inertia.<sup>21</sup> [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) descended at approximately 560 kilometres per hour<sup>22</sup> and closed to within about 800 metres of the object,<sup>23</sup> at which point it accelerated and disappeared in less than half a second.<sup>24</sup> The total interaction lasted approximately five minutes and was observed by four trained military personnel.<sup>25</sup> When [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) looked back for the white water it had also vanished, leaving only blue ocean.<sup>26</sup> The [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) subsequently reported that the object had reappeared at the flight's combat air patrol point approximately 100 kilometres away.<sup>27</sup>

There is no video from [Fravor](/people/david-fravor)'s own encounter; [Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood) recorded the FLIR1 (Nimitz Tic Tac UAP video) on a subsequent flight after [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) told him to obtain footage.<sup>28</sup> When [Underwood](/people/chad-underwood)'s radar locked onto the object it could not maintain a lock and indicated it was being jammed.<sup>29</sup> The object showed no exhaust plume, no infrared signature, and no heat signature in the forward-looking infrared (FLIR) footage;<sup>30</sup> however, on the original tape viewed in television mode, two small protrusions were visible on the underside of the craft.<sup>31</sup> [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) states the publicly released video is the complete footage, approximately one minute and thirty seconds long.<sup>32</sup> After the flight, intelligence officers from the Carrier Intelligence Centre took the recording tapes; [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) subsequently retrieved them from an ordinary filing cabinet and made two copies onto new 8-millimetre tapes.<sup>33</sup> No officials arrived to conduct interviews or collect material in person, contrary to widely circulated rumours.<sup>34</sup> Around 2009 a government employee contacted [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) to inform him of an investigation, producing what he refers to as the unofficial official report; the report later became public after Senator [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) passed it to journalist [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp).<sup>35,36</sup> The author of that report was an original member of the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), which was run at the Pentagon by Luis (Lou) Elizondo;<sup>37</sup> [AATIP](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip)'s original work was exempt from the Freedom of Information Act under a signed [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) memorandum that [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) personally saw.<sup>38</sup>

The later portions of the episode address the 2014-2015 US East Coast UAP encounters, during which the Go Fast (US military UAP video) and Gimbal (US military UAP video) were recorded, with at least 60 personnel observing the objects on radar.<sup>39</sup> One object described during those encounters resembled a cube inside a translucent sphere and passed within approximately 30 metres of an aircraft.<sup>40</sup> [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) and Fridman discuss sceptical analyses by Mick West of the released footage, as well as Jeremy Corbell's counter-arguments regarding the Go Fast video's infrared polarity. On the question of origin, [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) states he does not believe the Tic Tac was of human manufacture, reasoning that a technological leap of that magnitude would have begun to surface in the open literature over the 16 years since the encounter;<sup>41</sup> separately, radar operators reported tracking objects descending from above approximately 24,000 metres, hovering at around 6,100 metres for three or four hours, and then ascending again.<sup>42</sup> As of the recording [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) estimated he had spoken to approximately 23 people in total about the incident,<sup>43</sup> and he proposes that privately funded research—potentially by figures such as Jeff Bezos and [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk) through entities outside government contracting constraints—could advance propulsion research at universities, citing the model of SpaceX delivering the SpaceX Dragon faster and more cheaply than established contractors.<sup>44</sup> The episode also covers [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science (TTSA)](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa), co-founded by [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) and [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), whose stated mission was to examine unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) as a technological rather than extraterrestrial question, and which [Fravor](/people/david-fravor) describes consulting with frequently without being a member.
