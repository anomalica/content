"The Media Loves This UFO Expert Who Says He Worked for an Obscure Pentagon Program. Did He?" is an investigative article by [Keith Kloor](/people/keith-kloor), published on 2019-06-01 in advance of the [History Channel](/organisations/history-channel) premiere of [*Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation*](/objects/unidentified-inside-americas-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-investigation). The show opened with a 2017 headline from [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) about the Pentagon's UFO programme<sup>1</sup> and its narrator declared that the [2017 NYT Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) Story Publication](/events/2017-nyt-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-story-publication) had "shocked the world" and that [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) had secretly run the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) for eight years.<sup>2</sup> The programme follows Elizondo as he re-investigates UFO incidents he says he learned of while heading AATIP at the Pentagon,<sup>3</sup> and portrays him as having quit because he was frustrated by what he described as a cover-up.<sup>4</sup>

The [2019 Elizondo AATIP Credibility Controversy](/matters/2019-elizondo-aatip-credibility-controversy) is the central subject of Kloor's reporting. Kloor states that he found no discernible evidence that Elizondo ever worked for a government UFO programme, much less led one.<sup>5</sup> [Pentagon Public Affairs](/organisations/pentagon-public-affairs) spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) confirmed to Kloor that AATIP existed and did pursue research into unidentified aerial phenomena,<sup>6</sup> but stated that Elizondo "had no responsibilities with regard to the AATIP program" while working in the [Office of Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSD(I))](/organisations/office-of-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi) up to his resignation on 2017-10-04.<sup>7</sup> Sherwood confirmed he had consulted with OUSD(I) leadership who remained in post from the period when Elizondo started working there.<sup>23</sup> He also acknowledged that Elizondo had worked for other organisations within the [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) besides OUSD(I).<sup>24</sup>

The only official confirmation Kloor could locate asserting that Elizondo led AATIP was a 2017-12 article in [Politico](/organisations/politico) by [Bryan Bender](/people/bryan-bender), in which [Dana White](/people/dana-white), then serving as Pentagon spokesperson and a Trump administration political appointee, confirmed to Bender that the programme existed and was run by Elizondo.<sup>10</sup><sup>11</sup> However, White subsequently resigned from the Pentagon amid an internal investigation into charges of misconduct,<sup>13</sup> and Sherwood told Kloor he could not confirm her statement.<sup>12</sup> [Kari DeLonge](/people/kari-delonge), a public relations representative for [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science), had written in the [Greenewald–Kari DeLonge Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) Email Exchange](/events/greenewald-kari-delonge-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-email-exchange) — initiated by UFO researcher [John Greenewald Jr.](/people/john-greenewald-jr) — that Elizondo took over AATIP as Director in 2010 and ran it from the [Office of the Secretary of Defense](/organisations/office-of-the-secretary-of-defense).<sup>8</sup> Kari DeLonge did not respond to multiple requests for comment from Kloor,<sup>53</sup> and [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge), co-founder and interim chief executive of [To the Stars Academy](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science), is listed as executive producer of *Unidentified*.<sup>N</sup>

Kloor examines the documentary record Elizondo has made available. The [Elizondo Resignation Letter](/documents/elizondo-resignation-letter), dated 2017-10-04 and addressed to then-Defence Secretary [Jim Mattis](/people/jim-mattis), bears the apparent seal of the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense](/organisations/office-of-the-secretary-of-defense) and alludes to internal Pentagon opposition to investigating UFOs that Elizondo wrote had posed an "existential threat to our national security."<sup>21</sup> The letter does not, however, mention AATIP by name or describe Elizondo's role as its director.<sup>22</sup> Kloor also observes that although Elizondo provided reporters with DoD performance evaluations and other materials,<sup>55</sup> he had not supplied any memorandums, emails about deliverables, or paperwork directly connecting him to AATIP.<sup>20</sup> Elizondo arranged for the Pentagon to release cockpit camera videos from F-18 fighter jets shortly before he resigned,<sup>14</sup> and at the [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) Conference 2018-07 Elizondo Talk](/events/unidentified-flying-object-ufo-conference-2018-07-elizondo-talk) told the audience that no paper trail for the programme would be found.<sup>16</sup> By 2018, Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests seeking details of AATIP and the scope of Elizondo's role were returning nothing.<sup>15</sup>

The article also scrutinises the media ecosystem surrounding Elizondo and To the Stars Academy. [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), a former deputy assistant secretary of defense for intelligence and the company's national security affairs adviser, appeared on [Fox News](/organisations/fox-news)'s *Fox and Friends* days before the [Unidentified Season 1 Premiere 2019](/events/unidentified-season-1-premiere-2019), asserting that UFOs exist and raising questions about their origin and technology.<sup>28</sup> Kloor notes that [Fox News](/organisations/fox-news) did not disclose Mellon's affiliation with To the Stars Academy or that *Unidentified* was produced by the same company.<sup>29</sup> Las Vegas television journalist [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp), who received unofficial AATIP documents and had been a vocal defender of Elizondo and DeLonge,<sup>17</sup> appeared in the first episode of the show. Kloor reports that Knapp had purchased stock in To the Stars Academy without always disclosing this to his audience;<sup>18</sup> in the [Knapp George TTSA Stock Purchase](/events/knapp-george-ttsa-stock-purchase) Knapp acknowledged to [The Intercept](/organisations/the-intercept) buying 400 shares in 2018, describing it as support for the company's work rather than an investment.<sup>19</sup> On The New York Times podcast [NYT The Daily Podcast](/organisations/nyt-the-daily-podcast), Pentagon correspondent [Helene Cooper](/people/helene-cooper) described Elizondo in the [NYT Daily Podcast Elizondo Cooper Discussion 2017](/documents/nyt-daily-podcast-elizondo-cooper-discussion-2017) as a "spooky, secretive guy" but "completely credible,"<sup>25</sup> and said she believed him after he showed her documents, pictures, and military videos during their meeting.<sup>26</sup> Kloor notes that one of the three reporters who shared bylines on the [2017 NYT Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) Story Publication](/events/2017-nyt-advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip-story-publication) was freelancer [Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean), who had previously written that she was privileged to welcome Mellon into the UFO organisation to which she belonged. The [Securities and Exchange Commission](/organisations/securities-and-exchange-commission) filing for To the Stars Academy, cited in the article, classifies the company as a "Motion Picture & Video Tape Production" concern rather than a scientific research body.<sup>27</sup>
