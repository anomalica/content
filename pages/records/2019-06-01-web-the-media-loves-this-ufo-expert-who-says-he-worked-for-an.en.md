"The Media Loves This UFO Expert Who Says He Worked for an Obscure Pentagon Program. Did He?" is an investigative article published on 2019-06-01 by journalist [Keith Kloor](/people/keith-kloor), examining the claims of [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) regarding his purported role directing a secret Pentagon [UFO](/topics/ufo) research programme. The article was prompted by the premiere of [Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation](/documents/unidentified-inside-americas-ufo-investigation) on the [History Channel](/organisations/history-channel), in which the narrator states that for eight years a clandestine US government programme investigating UFOs was run by Elizondo.<sup>3</sup> The [Glowing Auras and 'Black Money': The Pentagon's Mysterious UFO Program (New York Times, 2017-12-16)](/documents/2017-12-16-glowing-auras-and-black-money-the-pentagons-mysterious-ufo-program-new-york-times) article in [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) had first launched Elizondo into the public eye,<sup>1</sup> where he was described as a former military intelligence case officer<sup>2</sup> who quit [The Pentagon](/objects/the-pentagon) frustrated by what he characterised as a cover-up.<sup>4</sup> In the show, Elizondo re-investigates [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) incidents he says he learned of while running the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) at the Pentagon.<sup>5</sup>

Kloor's central finding is that no discernible evidence exists that Elizondo ever worked for a government UFO programme, much less led one.<sup>6</sup> The Pentagon spokesperson [Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood) confirmed to Kloor that AATIP existed and did pursue research and investigation into UAP,<sup>7</sup> but stated that Elizondo had no responsibilities with regard to AATIP while he worked in the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), up until his resignation effective 2017-10-04.<sup>8</sup> Sherwood told Kloor he had spoken with OUSDI leadership, including individuals still there from the time Elizondo began working in the office, to establish this point.<sup>18</sup> Elizondo was based at OUSDI from 2008 until his retirement in 2017.<sup>19</sup> The article notes that Elizondo does not appear to have supplied any materials validating his connection to AATIP -- no memorandums, no emails, and no paperwork connecting him to the programme.<sup>17</sup> His [Luis Elizondo resignation letter to James Mattis (2017-10-04)](/documents/luis-elizondo-resignation-letter-to-james-mattis-2017-10-04) does not mention AATIP or his role as its director,<sup>16</sup> though it does allude to internal opposition at the Pentagon to investigating UFOs and strongly implies he was leaving because the Pentagon was not taking the threat seriously.<sup>29</sup>

The article examines conflicting official statements about Elizondo's role. In December 2017, Pentagon spokeswoman [Dana White](/people/dana-white) had confirmed to [Politico](/organisations/politico) that AATIP existed and was run by Elizondo;<sup>13</sup> however, Sherwood told Kloor he could not confirm that statement.<sup>14</sup> [Kari DeLonge](/people/kari-delonge) -- sister of [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) and a spokesperson for [To The Stars Academy of Arts & Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-science) -- told researcher [John Greenewald](/people/john-greenewald) that AATIP was initially run out of the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia) but that when Elizondo took it over in 2010 as director, he ran it from the [Office of the Secretary of Defense (OSD)](/organisations/office-of-the-secretary-of-defense-osd) under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.<sup>15</sup> Greenewald, who runs [The Black Vault](/organisations/the-black-vault) archive of Freedom of Information Act documents, had grown increasingly sceptical when his requests and conversations with Pentagon representatives failed to verify Elizondo's claims.<sup>20</sup>

The article profiles the network surrounding Elizondo. Elizondo joined To The Stars Academy several days after resigning from the [Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/department-of-defense-dod) in October 2017,<sup>9</sup> a company co-founded by Tom DeLonge,<sup>10</sup> a musician of [Blink-182](/organisations/blink-182) fame, who serves as its interim chief executive. To The Stars raised more than $2 million from investors and was originally hyped as exploring the outer edges of science,<sup>11</sup> but its [Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)](/organisations/securities-and-exchange-commission-sec) filing classifies it as a "Motion Picture & Video Tape Production" concern.<sup>12</sup> [Christopher Mellon](/people/christopher-mellon), a former deputy assistant secretary of defense for intelligence in the Clinton and George W. Bush administrations, works for To The Stars Academy as national security affairs adviser. [Fox News](/organisations/fox-news) coverage did not mention Mellon's connection to To The Stars Academy, nor that Unidentified was produced by the same company Elizondo and Mellon work for.<sup>28</sup> Tom DeLonge claimed publicly that he is the military's chosen vessel for UFO disclosure.<sup>27</sup> [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp), a Las Vegas television journalist at [KLAS-TV](/organisations/klas-tv) and a vocal defender of Elizondo, purchased 400 shares of To The Stars Academy stock in 2018,<sup>21</sup> informed his employer, and subsequently placed the shares in a trust to be donated to charity.<sup>22</sup>

The article also examines how the news media handled Elizondo's claims. [Helene Cooper](/people/helene-cooper), [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times)'s Pentagon correspondent and one of the reporters on the original 2017 story, described Elizondo on [The Daily (New York Times podcast episode on the Pentagon UFO program)](/documents/the-daily-new-york-times-podcast-episode-on-the-pentagon-ufo-program) as a spooky, secretive figure but completely credible.<sup>23</sup> She acknowledged afterwards that doubts crept in, but said what mattered most was whether the Pentagon's UFO programme was real.<sup>24</sup> Elizondo arranged for the Pentagon to release videos taken from cockpit cameras of [F-18 fighter jet](/objects/f-18-fighter-jet)s just before he quit,<sup>25</sup> and subsequently appeared on cable news describing hypersonic, gravity-defying unidentified aerial vehicles that had encroached on military training areas in restricted airspace.<sup>26</sup> Public records examined by Kloor show a series of home addresses for Elizondo near intelligence facilities in [USA, Puerto Rico, Toa Baja](/places/puerto-rico-toa-baja-usa) -- the site of the surveillance programme known as [Echelon](/projects/echelon) -- and in [USA, Georgia, Grovetown](/places/georgia-grovetown-usa), close to [USA, Georgia, Fort Gordon](/places/georgia-fort-gordon-usa), home of the [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy)'s principal signals intelligence facilities.<sup>30</sup> These records are consistent with a signals intelligence career at OUSDI, but do not corroborate Elizondo's claims about AATIP. [Bryan Bender](/people/bryan-bender) of [Politico](/organisations/politico) and [Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean), a co-author of the original New York Times story, are also mentioned in relation to the wider coverage. The article was published by [The Intercept](/organisations/the-intercept).
