The article discusses the television series [Unidentified: Inside America's UFO Investigation](/documents/unidentified-inside-americas-ufo-investigation), which, according to the source, follows Elizondo as he re-investigates incidents he says he learned about while running AATIP. <sup>1</sup> According to the article, the series premiered on a Friday and was promoted as “groundbreaking nonfiction”.<sup>1</sup> [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) is described as the programme's executive producer, and the article says that he co-founded and served as interim chief executive of To The Stars Academy.<sup>4</sup><sup>5</sup>

## The disputed programme and Elizondo's role

[Christopher Sherwood](/people/christopher-sherwood), identified in the article as a Pentagon spokesperson, said that AATIP existed and conducted research and investigation into unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP).<sup>2</sup> Sherwood also said that Elizondo had no responsibilities relating to AATIP while working in the [Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI)](/organisations/office-of-the-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-ousdi), up to his resignation effective 2017-10-04.<sup>3</sup>

This account was contrasted with a statement attributed to [Dana White](/people/dana-white). The article says that White confirmed to [Politico](/organisations/politico) that AATIP existed and was run by Elizondo.<sup>6</sup> Sherwood said he could not confirm White's statement.<sup>7</sup> The article identifies [Bryan Bender](/people/bryan-bender) as Politico's defence editor and reports his view that Elizondo and Chris Mellon represented an unusually well-placed team of former government officials speaking publicly about UFOs.<sup>6</sup>

[Kari DeLonge](/people/kari-delonge) gave a different account of the programme's administrative history. According to the article, she said that AATIP was initially run from the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia), before Elizondo took it over in 2010 and ran it from the Office of the Secretary of Defense under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.<sup>8</sup> The article says that [John Greenewald](/people/john-greenewald) asked Kari DeLonge specifically where Elizondo worked within the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) when he ran AATIP.<sup>9</sup>

The article reports that Greenewald had become frustrated by the lack of tangible information about AATIP and Elizondo's role, and that Elizondo did not respond to his interview requests.<sup>10</sup><sup>11</sup> It further says that, by mid-2019, researchers were having difficulty determining what AATIP had done and the scope of Elizondo's role, while Freedom of Information Act requests had produced little information.<sup>12</sup>

## Documents and resignation

The article says Elizondo provided reporters, including [Keith Kloor](/people/keith-kloor), with recent annual Defense Department performance evaluations and the Elizondo resignation letter to James Mattis (2017-10-04), which bore the apparent seal of OUSDI.<sup>13</sup> Kloor said that Elizondo had not supplied memorandums, emails or other paperwork connecting him to AATIP, despite providing other documents intended to establish his professional background.<sup>14</sup>

The article also reports that Kloor found no mention of AATIP or of Elizondo's role as its director in the resignation letter.<sup>15</sup> According to the account, the letter alluded to internal opposition at the Pentagon to investigating UFOs that Elizondo wrote had menaced Navy pilots and posed an “existential threat to our national security”.<sup>15</sup> The article presents this as Elizondo's implied explanation for leaving the Pentagon, rather than as an independently established account of the programme.

## Public presentation and released videos

According to the article, Elizondo arranged for the Pentagon to release cockpit-camera videos from [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) fighter jets shortly before he left the DoD.<sup>16</sup> Sherwood said that the videos had been released for research purposes and not for general public release.<sup>4</sup> Elizondo claimed that the footage was “compelling evidence” of phenomena he could not identify.<sup>16</sup>

The article describes Elizondo's later public profile, including his appearances discussing hypersonic and gravity-defying unidentified aerial vehicles that he said had entered military training areas in restricted airspace. It also reports that he described himself as a “career spy” and said that, before December 2017, he had lived “in the shadows”. According to the source, these descriptions were part of Elizondo's own presentation of his background rather than independently documented findings.

In July 2018, Elizondo told a UFO conference audience that people who had associated him with the fringe were wrong and that “all along, you were right”.<sup>3</sup> According to the article, the conference was the first public forum in which he laid out AATIP's history and objectives.<sup>3</sup>

## Journalistic responses

The article records differing reactions among journalists who encountered Elizondo and the material he provided. [Helene Cooper](/people/helene-cooper) described him as a “spooky, secretive guy” but also as “completely credible”.<sup>17</sup> She said that he showed her documents, pictures and military videos of potential UFOs.<sup>17</sup> Cooper found the material persuasive at the time, but said that doubts emerged after she left the meeting.<sup>5</sup><sup>18</sup> She said that the central reporting question was whether the Pentagon's UFO programme was real.<sup>19</sup>

Kloor's account was more sceptical. The article says he tried to contact Elizondo repeatedly by email and mobile telephone without receiving a response, and that Chris Mellon also did not respond to a request for comment. It reports that Kloor regarded Sherwood's denial of Elizondo's AATIP responsibilities as contradicting an email from a To The Stars Academy spokesperson.

The source also discusses [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp), a Las Vegas television journalist who had defended Elizondo and Tom DeLonge and had challenged critics of To The Stars Academy. According to the article, Knapp had purchased shares in the company. Knapp acknowledged to [The Intercept](/organisations/the-intercept) buying 400 shares in 2018 to support the company and its work, and said he had disclosed the purchase publicly and informed his employer, [KLAS-TV](/organisations/klas-tv).<sup>20</sup>

## To The Stars Academy

The article describes To The Stars Academy as initially promoted as a UFO research company intended to explore the “outer edges of science”.<sup>2</sup> It also reports that the organisation raised more than 2 million US dollars from investors.<sup>21</sup> A Securities and Exchange Commission filing is described as identifying the company as a “Motion Picture & Video Tape Production” concern.

The source reports that some observers in the UFO community suspected Tom DeLonge was being used as an unwitting front for a black-ops project, while DeLonge himself claimed to be the military's chosen vessel for UFO disclosure. These statements are presented in the article as claims and suspicions, not as established facts.<sup>22</sup> The article also says that, several years before the To The Stars Academy rollout, DeLonge had created a website called Strange Times as a clearing house for UFO news and conspiracies.

Greenewald, who was initially enthusiastic about To The Stars Academy, became increasingly sceptical after he was unable to verify many of Elizondo's claims through FOIA requests and discussions with Pentagon representatives.<sup>23</sup> The article says that Greenewald contacted Kari DeLonge for more information about Elizondo's involvement in AATIP, while Sherwood maintained that Elizondo had no AATIP responsibilities in OUSDI.

The source therefore presents the question in its title through competing testimony and incomplete documentation. It records claims that AATIP existed and investigated UAP, claims that Elizondo ran it, and statements from a Pentagon spokesperson denying that he had responsibilities for it; it does not resolve the disagreement.
