*Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs* is a memoir by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), published in 2024 by [William Morrow](/organisations/william-morrow). The record captured here covers the book as image-only pages rather than extracted text, encompassing the front matter, foreword, author's note, introduction, and the first eighteen or so chapters, along with appendix material and photo captions. A disclaimer reproduced within the source states that public-release clearance by the [Department of Defense](/organisations/department-of-defense) does not imply endorsement or factual accuracy of its contents.<sup>1</sup> The volume contains 25 chapters together with acknowledgements, an appendix, an index, a photo section, and an about-the-author section.<sup>2</sup> Elizondo dedicated the book to his wife [Elizondo Jennifer (wife)](/people/elizondo-jennifer-wife) and daughters [Taylor Elizondo](/people/taylor-elizondo) and [Alex Elizondo (daughter)](/people/alex-elizondo-daughter).

The foreword, dated 2024-05-16, was written by [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), who describes himself as a former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and former Minority Staff Director of the [Senate Intelligence Committee](/organisations/senate-intelligence-committee).<sup>3,16</sup> Mellon opens by asserting that Elizondo played a central and indispensable role in changing how humanity views [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/concepts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena).<sup>4</sup> He describes the environment Elizondo operated in: from 1970 to 2017 the [US Air Force](/organisations/us-air-force) maintained that no UFO had ever indicated a threat to national security or technology beyond present knowledge, a position unchanged since the closure of [Project Blue Book](/documents/project-blue-book), and a climate of hostility traceable to the [CIA](/organisations/cia)'s Robertson Panel, which Mellon says advised enlisting the [Walt Disney Company](/organisations/walt-disney-company) and mass media in a campaign to debunk UAP reports.<sup>5,6,7</sup> An Air Force-funded study at the [University of Colorado](/organisations/university-of-colorado) chaired by physicist [Edward Condon](/people/edward-condon) concluded the topic was devoid of scientific merit and provided the Air Force the excuse it sought to shut down Project Blue Book.<sup>7</sup> Mellon was serving as an unpaid consultant for the [Office of Naval Intelligence](/organisations/office-of-naval-intelligence) in 2017 when mutual CIA friend [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) introduced him to Elizondo, and the two met in Elizondo's [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon) office.<sup>8,9</sup> There Elizondo presented what Mellon describes as incontrovertible evidence that unidentified aircraft lacking discernible markings or means of propulsion were routinely violating sensitive military airspace.<sup>10</sup> Mellon recounts detail from the [2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter): the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) detected objects descending from low earth orbit, dropping to approximately 6,000 metres altitude before instantaneously accelerating,<sup>11</sup> and Commander [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) observed from his F/A-18 a craft approximately 14.6 metres long, wingless, and white, which he described as "not from this world" — a sighting corroborated by six naval aviators, multiple radar platforms, and an advanced infrared targeting system.<sup>12,13</sup> Mellon also describes the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program](/matters/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program) (AAWSAP) as having been funded with 22 million US dollars earmarked by Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) in 2008, yet notes that the [Department of Defense](/organisations/department-of-defense) worked to kill it at the earliest opportunity.<sup>14,15</sup>

The author's note, dated April 2024, explains that Elizondo chose the title *Imminent* because he views the reality of UAP as something now upon humanity rather than still approaching.<sup>17,18</sup> The introduction establishes Elizondo's central claims: that he joined the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon-organisation) in late 2008 and was shortly recruited into a highly sensitive programme investigating UAP,<sup>19</sup> and that the craft he studied are not made by humans and that humanity is not the only intelligent life in the universe.<sup>20</sup> Chapter 2 of the book is titled "Colares", chapter 7 "The Tic Tac", and chapter 15 "USS Roosevelt", signalling the case studies that structure the memoir's narrative arc.<sup>21</sup>

Chapters 1 and 2 describe Elizondo's recruitment into the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip). In early 2009, [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) and a colleague visited his office to recruit him for a programme at the [Defense Intelligence Agency](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency), which Stratton described as the AAWSAP that would later become AATIP.<sup>22,23</sup> Elizondo was then taken to meet [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski), whom he describes as one of the US government's top rocket scientists holding a doctorate in engineering.<sup>24</sup> The programme enjoyed the support of DIA Director Lieutenant General [Michael D. Maples](/people/michael-d-maples) and was funded through the efforts of senators [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens), and [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye).<sup>25</sup> After accepting the role, Elizondo attended a dinner at a hotel in [Roslyn Virginia](/places/roslyn-virginia) where [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) was introduced as the prime AAWSAP contractor through his firm [Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies).<sup>26</sup> [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) also attended as chief scientist; he had earned his PhD from [Stanford University](/organisations/stanford-university) in 1967 and had previously managed the CIA's [Stargate Program](/matters/stargate-program).<sup>27</sup> The dinner also included Brazilian General [Paulo Roberto Uchôa](/people/paulo-roberto-uchôa) and his daughter; his father [Alfredo Moacyr Uchôa](/people/alfredo-moacyr-uchôa) had founded the [Brazilian Center for UAP Studies](/organisations/brazilian-center-for-uap-studies) in the 1970s.<sup>28</sup>

Chapter 2 covers the [Colares Incidents](/events/colares-incidents) in detail. UAP reported along the coast of northeastern [Brazil](/places/brazil) during the mid-1970s ranged in size from baseball-sized orbs to craft described as large enough to transport a city's population, in shapes including discs, spheres, triangles, and cylinders.<sup>29</sup> In 1977 the intelligence branch of the Brazilian Air Force sent twenty investigators and physicians to [Colares Brazil](/places/colares-brazil) under Lieutenant Colonel [Uyrange Hollanda](/people/uyrange-hollanda), who captured footage and hundreds of photographs — one craft estimated at approximately ninety metres long.<sup>30</sup> Dr. [Wellaide Cecim Carvalho](/people/wellaide-cecim-carvalho) treated approximately forty victims that year; twenty-three had burns featuring two central puncture wounds, and all tested had low haemoglobin levels.<sup>31</sup> Researcher [Robert Pratt](/people/robert-pratt) interviewed 514 witnesses, and [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallée) independently verified the incidents; the total evidence base runs to more than 3,500 case files.<sup>32</sup> Brazilian officials subsequently shut down the investigation, classified the files until the 1990s, and Hollanda died of apparent suicide shortly after their release.<sup>33</sup> The [Chupa-Chupa Phenomenon](/matters/chupa-chupa-phenomenon) described by witnesses — beams of light that burned or incapacitated victims — drew comparisons, within the AAWSAP analysis, to [UAP Biological Effects](/matters/uap-biological-effects) documented elsewhere.

Later chapters recorded in this source detail the [AATIP UAP Six Observables](/matters/aatip-uap-six-observables) framework. Elizondo identifies five primary performance characteristics — hypersonic velocity, [Instantaneous Acceleration](/concepts/instantaneous-acceleration), [Low Observability](/concepts/low-observability), [Transmedium Travel](/concepts/transmedium-travel), and [Anti-Gravity Propulsion](/concepts/anti-gravity-propulsion) — and adds a sixth, biological effects, which he states had not been publicly discussed by the US government at the time of writing.<sup>34,35</sup> In a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) briefing, Puthoff then proposed that all six observables could be explained by a single technology: the warping of space-time in a localised bubble around a craft, consistent with [Albert Einstein](/people/albert-einstein)'s general relativity and related to concepts including [Warp Drive](/concepts/warp-drive), [Zero-Point Energy](/concepts/zero-point-energy), and [Space-Time Warping](/concepts/space-time-warping).<sup>36</sup> The source also documents Elizondo's formal resignation on 2017-10-04, with his letter addressed directly to Secretary of Defense [Jim Mattis](/people/jim-mattis) citing opposition within the Department to further UAP research despite overwhelming evidence.<sup>37</sup> On 2017-12-16 the [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) broke the AATIP story, followed immediately by [Politico](/organisations/politico) and other outlets, catalysing the public phase of the UAP disclosure campaign that Elizondo and [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) had planned in the event of his resignation.<sup>38</sup> The [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video), [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video), and [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video) were formally cleared for unlimited public distribution via a [DoD Form 1910](/documents/dod-form-1910) submission Elizondo made before leaving the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon), and these videos became central exhibits in subsequent congressional engagement and the eventual passage of the [UAP Disclosure Act](/matters/uap-disclosure-act).
