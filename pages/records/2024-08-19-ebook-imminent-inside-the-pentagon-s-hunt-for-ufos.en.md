*Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs* is a source account concerning United States investigations into [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap). It presents the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) as a highly sensitive intelligence programme and connects its work with military encounters, alleged crash-retrieval activities, and later congressional oversight.<sup>1</sup>

## Scope and subject

The account focuses substantially on Lue Elizondo, whose testimony describes his work in and around the USA, Washington DC, Pentagon. Elizondo said that he was recruited into a sensitive programme investigating UAP, also known as [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/ufos-uaps) reports, and that he spent nearly a decade working on the subject.<sup>1</sup> The source also discusses Christopher Mellon, Harold Puthoff, [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton), [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan), and [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski) as participants or associated figures in the account.

A recurring theme is the relationship between UAP reporting and institutional secrecy. According to the source record, the [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) abandoned [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book) in 1970 after maintaining that reports could be explained by hysteria, hoaxes, publicity-seeking, psychological conditions, or misidentification of natural objects.<sup>2</sup> The source also records claims that, by 2017, many military and commercial personnel feared professional consequences if they reported UAP observations.<sup>3</sup>

## The 2004 USS Nimitz encounter

The account treats the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), also called the “Tic Tac” incident, as a particularly important case. Elizondo described it as a “gold standard” because of the investigation and the quality of the information collected.<sup>4</sup>

According to the source record, the encounter involved three forms of evidence: radar data from airborne and shipborne systems, forward-looking infrared (FLIR) imagery from externally mounted targeting pods, and testimony from fighter pilots.<sup>2</sup> The vessels associated with the account include the [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68), the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59), and USS Roosevelt in later comparisons. The source describes the Tic Tac as an elongated oval or cigar-shaped object approximately 14 metres long.<sup>5</sup>

The account further states that the object in the video had no wings, air intake, exhaust plume, cockpit, or distinguishable control surfaces.<sup>6</sup> It also describes the object as difficult to track by radar and as displaying rapid manoeuvres. Dave Fravor, [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich), and other [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) personnel are presented as witnesses to the encounter.

## The AATIP observables

The source describes five performance characteristics, called “the observables”, that [AATIP](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) used to categorise reported UAP capabilities. These were hypersonic velocity, instantaneous acceleration, low observability, transmedium travel, and antigravity.<sup>3</sup> The account presents the first four as flight-related characteristics and antigravity as the apparent ability to counter the effects of gravity.

Elizondo also described biological effects as a sixth observable, rather than as a flight characteristic.<sup>7</sup> The source discusses reported injuries, medical observations, and alleged effects on people associated with UAP encounters, while attributing these accounts to witnesses, researchers, or participants rather than establishing them independently.

## AAWSAP, contractors, and programme continuity

According to the source record, [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) earmarked $22 million in [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) funding in 2008 for a UAP investigation conducted by an aerospace contractor.<sup>8</sup> The resulting Advanced Aerospace Weapons System Application Program (AAWSAP) is described as producing a substantial report on the 2004 USS Nimitz encounter.<sup>9</sup>

The source says that Elizondo referred to a successor initiative as [AATIP](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip).<sup>10</sup> It also describes Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), owned by [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow), as the primary contractor for AAWSAP, and discusses research at USA, Utah, Skinwalker Ranch. Other associated researchers include Puthoff, [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis), William "Will" Livingston, and Garry P. Nolan.

## Public disclosure and congressional activity

Elizondo said that, after his resignation, he and Mellon launched an effort to bring UAP information to the [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress), the press, and the wider public.<sup>11</sup> The account describes the subsequent release of three videos: the GoFast video, the [GIMBAL video](/documents/gimbal-video), and footage associated with the Tic Tac encounter.

The source connects this activity with the development of a broader interagency effort. According to the source record, [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) was tasked with building a whole-of-government UAP task force, which later became the Pentagon's UAP Task Force.<sup>12</sup> The account describes participation by personnel from intelligence and civilian agencies, including the [Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)](/organisations/federal-bureau-of-investigation-fbi), the [National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)](/organisations/national-reconnaissance-office-nro), and [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa).

The Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena report is described as identifying 144 military UAP incidents between 2004 and 2021-06.<sup>13</sup> The source also states that a May 2022 congressional hearing addressed more than 400 cases logged during the previous year.<sup>14</sup> The May 2022 Congressional UAP hearing included testimony from Ronald Moultrie and other [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) witnesses.

In 2022-07, the DoD announced the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) as a permanent UAP office required to report to Congress.<sup>15</sup> The source subsequently discusses the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), including provisions described as requiring reporting on UAP capture, recovery, reverse-engineering, and health effects.<sup>16</sup>

The account also covers the UAP Disclosure Act. According to the source record, the proposed legislation included provisions for collecting government records and directing the National Archives to preserve material concerning unidentified anomalous phenomena, technologies of unknown origin, and nonhuman intelligence (NHI).<sup>17</sup>

## Alleged crash retrievals and nonhuman technology

A substantial part of the source concerns alleged programmes for recovering and reverse-engineering crashed vehicles. The [Legacy Program](/projects/legacy-program) is described in testimony as involving elements of the United States government and defence contractors. The source also reports that David Grusch said the Pentagon's UAP Task Force had been denied access to a broad crash-retrieval programme involving alleged nonhuman-origin vehicles.<sup>18</sup>

These claims are presented as testimony, hearsay, or opinion within the source rather than as independently verified findings. The account describes the alleged recovery of nonhuman bodies and technology at the July 2023 Congressional UAP hearing, but the relevant claims concern what witnesses said about such matters.<sup>18</sup> The source similarly records statements by Puthoff, Elizondo, and others about the 1947 Roswell UAP crash, alleged materials, and biological remains.

## Overall character

*Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs* combines personal testimony, historical narrative, descriptions of military sensor cases, institutional history, and discussion of proposed legislation. Its central account follows the movement of UAP from a stigmatised subject within parts of the military and intelligence establishment towards a subject addressed through public reporting, congressional hearings, and dedicated government offices. The source distinguishes between documented administrative actions and attributed claims about the nature, origin, and capabilities of the phenomena.
