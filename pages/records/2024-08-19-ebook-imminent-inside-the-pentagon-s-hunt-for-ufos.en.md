"Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs" is a 2024 book by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), with a foreword by [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), according to its contents page.<sup>1</sup> The same page lists 25 chapters together with an Acknowledgments section, an Appendix, an Index, a Photo Section, and an About the Author section.<sup>2</sup> Mellon's foreword is dated 2024-05-16.<sup>3</sup> Elizondo dedicates the book to his wife Elizondo Jennifer (wife) and daughters Taylor Elizondo and Alexandria,<sup>4</sup> and to people who have had [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) (UAP) encounters or who are aware of facts they feel should be publicly known.<sup>5</sup>

In the foreword, Mellon states that Elizondo played a central and indispensable role in changing the way humanity views UAP.<sup>6</sup> Mellon writes that he first met Elizondo at a closed-door Pentagon meeting early in 2017,<sup>7</sup> after [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan), a mutual friend from the CIA, brought Elizondo to his attention.<sup>8</sup> During that meeting, Mellon recounts, Elizondo showed him authentic military gun-camera UAP videos,<sup>9</sup> presenting what Mellon describes as incontrovertible evidence that strange, unmarked aircraft with no discernible means of propulsion were routinely violating sensitive US military airspace.<sup>10</sup> Mellon's foreword also recounts the 2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter, describing how the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59) detected a large number of manoeuvring objects appearing to descend from low earth orbit,<sup>11</sup> with [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) subsequently observing a wingless, white craft approximately 14.6 metres long.<sup>12</sup>

Elizondo states that he began a new job at the Pentagon in late 2008 and was shortly afterwards recruited into a highly sensitive intelligence programme investigating UAP,<sup>13</sup> on which he says he worked for nearly a decade.<sup>14</sup> According to Elizondo, [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) told him that he had helped create the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP), which would later become the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) (AATIP).<sup>15</sup> The contents page indicates that individual chapters address particular incidents: Chapter 2, titled "Colares", covers the Colares Incidents;<sup>16</sup> Chapter 7, titled "The Tic Tac", covers the Tic Tac Sighting;<sup>17</sup> and Chapter 15, titled "USS Roosevelt", covers the 2015 USS Roosevelt UAP Encounters.<sup>18</sup>

The book reproduces a resignation letter that Elizondo says he addressed directly to Secretary of Defense [Jim Mattis](/people/jim-mattis), effective 2017-10-04,<sup>19</sup> after more than 22 years of government service,<sup>20</sup> stating that there appeared to be a direct correlation between UAP and US nuclear and military capabilities.<sup>21</sup> Elizondo writes that, working with Mellon and [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff), he selected three unclassified military videos for declassification and release: the FLIR1 Video, the [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video), and the [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video).<sup>22</sup> He recounts that on 2017-12-16 [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) published the story online, revealing AATIP's existence, followed within seconds by Politico and then the Washington Post.<sup>23</sup>

Elizondo describes a subsequent campaign, conducted with Mellon, to bring UAP before Congress; he writes that language they drafted was incorporated into a Senate report directing the Director of National Intelligence to submit an unclassified UAP report within 180 days.<sup>24</sup> He states that this effort continued through the establishment of the [UAP Task Force](/organisations/uap-task-force) and culminated in the NDAA FY2023 UAP Language, signed into law by President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden) on 2022-12-23.<sup>25</sup> The book also covers a July 2023 congressional hearing at which witnesses, including [David Grusch](/people/david-grusch), testified about an alleged hidden programme to recover and reverse-engineer vehicles of nonhuman origin.<sup>26</sup>

According to its "About the Author" section, Elizondo was formerly head of AATIP,<sup>27</sup> and before that role oversaw counterespionage and counterterrorism investigations worldwide for the Department of Defense.<sup>28</sup> The same section describes him as an Army veteran who served in South Korea, Latin America, the Caribbean, Afghanistan and elsewhere in the Middle East,<sup>29</sup> and a graduate of the University of Miami, where he studied microbiology and immunology.<sup>30</sup>
