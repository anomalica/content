NASA-UAP-D008, *Apollo 12 Medical Debriefing - Tape 12, 1969* records discussion associated with the [Apollo 12](/projects/apollo-12) mission. Charles Conrad said that he and other crew members had been asked to experiment with observing the [astronaut light flash phenomenon].(/topics/astronaut-light-flash-phenomenon)<sup>1</sup> He also said that Buzz Neal had discussed two related phenomena in a letter.<sup>2</sup> According to the record, astronauts on earlier spaceflights had reported seeing flashes inside a spacecraft at night while their eyes were closed.<sup>3</sup> It also relays an unnamed source's description of the flashes as a known effect involving discharge across the eye caused by gamma rays or cosmic particles.<sup>4</sup> In the same context, the record states that NASA photographed astronauts' retinas during earlier crewed missions to the Moon, finding no evidence of cosmic ray strikes.<sup>5</sup>

The debriefing preserves Conrad's description of the appearances and perceived movement of the flashes. He distinguished between a flash-like shape and a streak.<sup>6</sup> He said that he perceived more instances in his left eye than in his right eye.<sup>7</sup> According to the record, his sleeping position on the left side of the spacecraft was suggested as a reason for that difference.<sup>8</sup> Conrad further described one flash travelling a short distance along a handrail above the MVP while he was looking directly at the location.<sup>9</sup> He characterised the streaks as horizontal,<sup>10</sup> and had the impression that they appeared roughly in the same place, slightly above the centre of his field of view, moving from right to left.<sup>11</sup> He recalled that they appeared at roughly 30 degrees from horizontal.<sup>12</sup> The source matters to UAP research because it brings together a crew member's contemporaneous account, discussion of related observations, and a proposed radiation-related explanation within a medical debriefing context.
