The [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) source record states that the audio tape describing the sighting was released four hours and 24 minutes into the flight.<sup>1</sup> According to the record, the crew of Gemini 7 (spacecraft) reported several objects at the start of the second orbital revolution.<sup>2</sup> The objects were reported in the area of Antigua USA.<sup>3</sup> The record says that the relevant portion of the tape had not been available for release because of a mechanical breakdown,<sup>4</sup> identified as a line breakdown between Building 30 and the NASA news centre in Building 6.<sup>5</sup> According to the record, NASA Mission Control (Houston) dubbed a copy from the control centre’s master tape for release.<sup>6</sup> The released material refers to particles, an unidentified object and the Titan II GLV booster.<sup>7</sup>

[Frank Borman](/people/frank-borman) said that the crew had a bogey at 10 o’clock high and reported it to NASA Mission Control.<sup>8</sup> According to the record, Mission Control asked whether the object was the Titan II GLV booster or an actual sighting.<sup>9</sup> Borman described several objects as looking like debris and characterised the report as an actual sighting.<sup>10</sup> He also reported the Titan II GLV booster in sight.<sup>11</sup> Borman said that what looked like hundreds of small particles passed from the left, at an estimated distance of approximately 5 to 6.5 kilometres from Gemini 7.<sup>12</sup> He described the object near the spacecraft as resembling a vehicle that had disintegrated.<sup>13</sup> According to the source record, NASA Mission Control confirmed the particles were approximately 5 to 6.5 kilometres away,<sup>14</sup> and asked whether they were additional to the booster and the bogey at 10 o’clock high.<sup>15</sup>

Jim Lovell said that the booster appeared on his side of Gemini 7 as a brilliant body against the Sun, with a black background and particles around it.<sup>16</sup> He placed it at his 2 o’clock position, ahead of Gemini 7 and slowly tumbling.<sup>17</sup> According to the record, the third unidentified object mentioned in the conversation was the bogey of the 1965 Gemini 7 unidentified object sighting.<sup>18</sup> The particles were described as appearing to head into a polar orbit,<sup>19</sup> while the record identifies Borman as the crew member who reported the bogey.<sup>20</sup> For Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) research, the excerpt is significant as a released record containing separate references to particles, an unidentified object and an identifiable launch-related object, alongside the crew’s descriptions and Mission Control’s questions.
