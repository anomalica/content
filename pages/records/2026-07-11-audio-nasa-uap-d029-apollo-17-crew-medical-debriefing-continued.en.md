This source is a continuation of a crew medical debriefing concerning [Apollo 17](/events/apollo-17). Harrison Schmitt discussed the practical use of mission documentation, saying that the Apollo 17 Flight Data File for both the Command and Service Module (CSM) and the Lunar Module (LM) was excellent, with few possible improvements.<sup>1</sup> He identified a specific weakness in the supplement to the Apollo 17 Flight Plan: splitting logging pages was, in his view, a mistake, because the paper was too thin to maintain the division and the pages tended to fall out.<sup>2</sup><sup>3</sup> He recommended either retaining a single set of logging pages or using heavier paper.<sup>4</sup>

The debriefing also records how reference materials functioned in practice. Schmitt called the Apollo 17 Cuff Checklist excellent and said its photo maps were useful for determining the crew's position at a lunar surface station, although he used them less than expected; he regarded the checklist chiefly as a learning aid rather than a surface reference.<sup>5</sup><sup>6</sup> He praised the Apollo 17 Timeline Book, reporting no problems with it.<sup>7</sup> Schmitt said that Ronald Evans carried the general flight-planning burden for himself and Eugene Cernan, while he considered the Apollo 17 Flight Plan excellent and its numerous requirements and experiments competently integrated.<sup>8</sup>

For UAP research, the source is notable for Schmitt's account of visual light flashes rather than for an identification of their cause. He said that flashes occurred almost continuously when he was dark-adapted during the flight, and that he may have seen one on the lunar surface during the first revolution.<sup>9</sup><sup>10</sup> He further reported that no visible flashes occurred while the crew wore blindfolds for the ALPMET experiment, that they returned that evening, and that the phenomenon was absent for all three crewmen only during that interval.<sup>11</sup><sup>12</sup> The same record places this account alongside ordinary medical observations: Schmitt reported weight loss,<sup>13</sup> considered his medical logs generally valid at five and a half to six hours of sleep per night,<sup>14</sup> and described reduced awareness of limb position in zero gravity when motionless.<sup>15</sup>
