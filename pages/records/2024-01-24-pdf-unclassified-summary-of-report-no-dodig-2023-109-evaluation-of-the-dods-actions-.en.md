The Unclassified Summary of Report No. DODIG-2023-109 is an unclassified summary of an evaluation of the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod)'s actions regarding [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/ufos-uaps). According to the summary, the U.S. Department of Defense Inspector General determined that the Department had not used a coordinated approach to detect, report, collect, analyse and identify UAP incidents.<sup>1</sup>

## Historical context

According to the summary, military pilots have historically reported many UAP sightings.<sup>2</sup> The [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) and [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) were described as being at the forefront of developing policies, procedures and mechanisms for reporting UAP.<sup>3</sup> The summary also states that the Department of Defense relied heavily on its Services and Components to detect, report, collect, analyse and identify UAP from the 1940s onwards.<sup>4</sup>

According to the summary, the Air Force Chief of Staff established [Project Sign](/projects/project-sign) in December 1947 to investigate the first recorded observations of [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/ufos-uaps).<sup>5</sup> Air Force personnel investigated 243 reported UAP sightings between 1947 and 1949.<sup>6</sup> In 1952, the Air Force Director of Intelligence initiated [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book) to investigate UFOs; Air Force personnel subsequently investigated over 12,000 reported UFO sightings between 1952 and 1969.<sup>7</sup><sup>8</sup>

The summary states that [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) directed the Department of Defense in 2020, through the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022 (FY 2022 NDAA), to develop, fund and staff a formal interagency entity for studying, collecting data on and reporting UAP.<sup>9</sup> The Department established the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) on 2022-07-20.<sup>10</sup>

## Successive reporting organisations

According to the summary, the Department established the UAP Task Force (UAPTF) in August 2020 to develop standardised reporting requirements for UAP encounters.<sup>11</sup> In June 2021, the UAP Task Force and the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)](/organisations/office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence-odni) issued the congressionally mandated Preliminary Assessment on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena.<sup>12</sup>

A November 2021 memorandum directed the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD(I&S)) to establish the [Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG)](/organisations/airborne-object-identification-and-management-synchronization-group-aoimsg) as the successor to the UAP Task Force. Its purpose was to synchronise efforts across the Department of Defense and with other federal departments and agencies to detect, identify and characterise objects of interest in special-use airspace.<sup>13</sup> The summary states that AOIMSG did not reach operational capability and was disestablished on 2022-07-15.<sup>14</sup><sup>15</sup>

In July 2022, AARO was established to implement duties previously assigned to AOIMSG.<sup>16</sup> The summary also describes the FY 2022 NDAA as expanding the meaning of UAP to include the water and space domains.<sup>17</sup> The James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023 (FY 2023 NDAA) is described as including additional requirements for a secure method of reporting UAP incidents without exposure to a nondisclosure agreement or reprisal.

The summary cites the Preliminary Assessment's description of UAP as objects that are not immediately identifiable and lack a single explanation.<sup>18</sup> It also records five potential explanatory categories identified in that assessment: airborne clutter, natural atmospheric phenomena, United States Government or industry developmental programmes, foreign adversary systems, and an “other” category.<sup>19</sup>

## Findings on coordination and policy

According to the summary, the Inspector General found that DoD Components had developed varying processes for collecting, analysing and identifying UAP incidents.<sup>20</sup> The Inspector General also found that the Department had not issued a comprehensive UAP response plan identifying roles, responsibilities, requirements and coordination procedures for those activities.<sup>21</sup> The Department's response was described as uncoordinated and concentrated within individual Military Departments.<sup>22</sup>

Officials from the Military Services and the Military Department Counterintelligence Organizations (MDCOs) said that they were waiting for comprehensive Department of Defense guidance before developing their own UAP guidance.<sup>23</sup> The Inspector General concluded that existing UAP processes largely excluded the role of geographic combatant commands.<sup>24</sup>

The Inspector General recommended that the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, in coordination with the AARO Director, issue a Department of Defense policy integrating UAP roles, responsibilities, requirements and coordination procedures into existing intelligence, counterintelligence and force-protection policies and procedures.<sup>25</sup> The recommendation also called for methods to address UAP incidents while aligning with policies and procedures protecting United States persons' civil liberties.<sup>26</sup>

The Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security agreed with the recommendation to issue a Department of Defense UAP policy.<sup>27</sup> The Inspector General considered the recommendation resolved but open pending the provision of the issued policy.<sup>28</sup>

## Scope and method

According to the summary, the Inspector General conducted the evaluation from May 2021 through June 2023.<sup>29</sup> The work included reviewing Presidential and Department of Defense policies, directives and guidance concerning intelligence gathering, counterintelligence, force protection and civil-liberty protections for United States persons.<sup>30</sup> The evaluation therefore addressed both the organisation of UAP reporting and the policy context governing related information-collection and protective activities.
