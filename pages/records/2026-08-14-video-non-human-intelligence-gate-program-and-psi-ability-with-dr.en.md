This episode is a conversation between Julia Mossbridge and [Chris Ramsay) (/people/chris-ramsay) about childhood gifted education, anomalous cognition and theories of consciousness. Mossbridge described memory gaps associated with childhood counselling, saying that she remembered the route to the room but not what occurred after the door closed, and did not know whether a drug or [hypnotic regression](/topics/hypnotic-regression) could explain the loss.<sup>1</sup> Ramsay said that Mossbridge had been in the Students on Active Research (SOAR) gifted programme,<sup>2</sup> while Mossbridge said that some testing in such programmes did not appear to concern giftedness or benefit pupils and may have involved deliberate memory removal.<sup>3</sup> The episode presents these accounts as personal testimony rather than as independently established findings.

The discussion then turns to psi, [precognition](/topics/precognition) and [remote viewing](/topics/remote-viewing-rv). Mossbridge distinguished remote viewing from an [out-of-body experience](/topics/out-of-body-experience-obe), describing the body as a sensor rather than treating the practitioner as physically travelling elsewhere.<sup>4</sup> She reported an association between unconditional love and [precognitive remote viewing](/topics/precognitive-remote-viewing) in an in-person study of 36 participants, followed by an online replication involving 305 participants.<sup>5</sup><sup>6</sup> The episode also discusses the use of large language models (LLMs) in scoring: Mossbridge said they produced larger effects and reduced positional bias, while retaining human bias embedded in the model.<sup>7</sup><sup>8</sup> She places these results within a proposed informational substrate, described as timeless and spaceless and containing information about the universe.<sup>9</sup>

For UAP research, the significance of the source lies in its emphasis on consciousness alongside craft. Mossbridge said the Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies (SCU) had agreed to study consciousness as well as vehicles.<sup>10</sup> Ramsay recounted waking [out-of-body experiences](/topics/out-of-body-experience-obe), including one after meditation in which he walked around his room before hearing a voice and returning to his body;<sup>11</sup> Mossbridge offered a brain-based interpretation in which the experience occurs within the brain's spatial construction.<sup>12</sup> She further divided [time travel](/topics/time-travel) into mental, informational and physical forms,<sup>13</sup> linking the distinction to American Electrodynamics, Applied Love Labs and the Time Machine (timemachine.love) project.<sup>14</sup><sup>15</sup>
