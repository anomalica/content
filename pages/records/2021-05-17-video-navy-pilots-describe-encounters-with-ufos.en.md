"Navy Pilots Describe Encounters with UFOs" is a segment broadcast by [60 Minutes](/organisations/60-minutes) in May 2021, presented by [Bill Whitaker](/people/bill-whitaker). The report brought together testimony from active and former US Navy pilots, a former senior Pentagon intelligence official, and a sitting senator to examine a series of UAP encounters spanning more than a decade and the institutional response — or lack of one — that followed. The US government, the segment noted, had by that point officially stated for the record that UAPs are real.<sup>1</sup>

The programme introduced [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), who spent 20 years running military intelligence operations worldwide<sup>2</sup> before being asked in 2008 to join the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/organisations/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program), a programme buried within the [Pentagon](/organisations/pentagon) and funded to the value of $22 million,<sup>3</sup> sponsored by then Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid).<sup>4</sup> AATIP's stated mission was to collect and analyse information involving anomalous aerial vehicles (AAVs), commonly known as UFOs or UAPs.<sup>5</sup> Elizondo took over its leadership in 2010.<sup>6</sup> In the segment he described UAPs exhibiting performance characteristics without known precedent: forces of 600 to 700 G,<sup>7</sup> speeds of approximately 20,900 kilometres per hour,<sup>8</sup> no wings, no control surfaces, and no obvious signs of propulsion,<sup>9</sup> the apparent ability to traverse air, water, and possibly space,<sup>10</sup> and the capacity to evade radar.<sup>11</sup>

Former US Navy pilot [Ryan Graves](/people/ryan-graves) described the [Virginia Beach UAP Sightings](/matters/virginia-beach-uap-sightings): starting in 2014, his [F/A-18C](/objects/fa-18c) squadron began encountering UAPs hovering over restricted airspace southeast of [NAS Oceana](/places/nas-oceana) and [Warning Area W-72](/places/warning-area-w-72).<sup>12</sup> The objects were confirmed simultaneously by radar and infrared sensor returns,<sup>13</sup> and Graves stated pilots saw them every day for at least a couple of years.<sup>14</sup> The [2019 Atlantic UAP Photographs](/documents/2019-atlantic-uap-photographs) taken in that area were later confirmed by the Pentagon as images of objects it could not identify.<sup>15</sup> In 2015, members of Graves' squadron captured the [2015 Jacksonville UAP Video](/events/2015-jacksonville-uap-video) off the coast of Jacksonville, Florida, showing an object rotating<sup>16</sup> and moving against a westward wind of approximately 220 kilometres per hour.<sup>17</sup> The [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video) and [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video) from the [2015 Atlantic Coast UAP Sightings](/events/2015-atlantic-coast-uap-sightings) were also referenced in the segment.

The programme devoted substantial attention to the [2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter) in the [SOCAL Operating Area](/places/socal-operating-area). In November 2004 the [Nimitz Carrier Strike Group](/organisations/nimitz-carrier-strike-group) was training approximately 160 kilometres southwest of San Diego.<sup>18</sup> For a week prior to 2004-11-14, the advanced radar aboard the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) had been detecting multiple [AAV (object)](/objects/aav-object)s descending from approximately 24,000 metres in less than a second during the [AAV Detection Period](/events/aav-detection-period).<sup>19</sup> On 2004-11-14, [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) and [Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) — each with a weapons systems officer in the back seat — were diverted from their training mission to investigate the contacts.<sup>20</sup> They found an area of roiling white water approximately the size of a Boeing 737, above which Fravor observed a small white tic-tac-shaped object — the [Tic Tac Sighting](/events/tic-tac-sighting) — moving erratically.<sup>21</sup> The [Water Disturbance (object)](/objects/water-disturbance-object) beneath it was visible to both pilots. The object was approximately the size of Fravor's [F/A-18C](/objects/fa-18c), with no markings, no wings, and no exhaust plumes.<sup>22</sup> All four aircrew observed it for roughly five minutes<sup>23</sup> before it disappeared; seconds later, the [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz) and the USS Princeton reacquired the target approximately 97 kilometres away.<sup>24</sup> The encounter was later investigated by AATIP as part of the [AAV Intercept](/events/aav-intercept), and the [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video) recording of the object became one of the most discussed pieces of evidence in the public domain. Dietrich, a graduate of the [Navy Fighter Weapons School](/organisations/navy-fighter-weapons-school) like Fravor,<sup>ref37</sup> stated this was the first time she had spoken publicly about the encounter and that she felt vulnerable because her aircraft had nothing to defend itself with against the object.

After AATIP's funding was eliminated in 2012,<sup>25</sup> Elizondo continued the mission informally before resigning from the Pentagon in 2017, having first obtained declassification of three UAP videos — the FLIR1, [Go-Fast Video](/documents/go-fast-video), and [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video) — in the [AATIP FLIR GoFast Gimbal Declassification](/events/aatip-flir-gofast-gimbal-declassification) and [Elizondo Pentagon Resignation 2017](/events/elizondo-pentagon-resignation-2017).<sup>26</sup> [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), who had served as Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence under two presidents,<sup>27</sup> subsequently acquired those videos and in 2017 leaked them to [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) in what became the [AATIP Video Leak to NYT](/events/aatip-video-leak-to-nyt).<sup>28</sup> Mellon stated with a high degree of confidence that the objects were not US technology.<sup>29</sup> Together, Elizondo and Mellon brought their accounts to newspapers, the [History Channel](/organisations/history-channel), and members of Congress, with the explicit strategy of generating public pressure that would compel the Department of Defense to treat UAPs as a serious national security matter.

By the time the segment aired, institutional attitudes had begun to shift. In August 2020 the Pentagon re-established a successor programme, the [UAP Task Force](/organisations/uap-task-force), in the [UAPTF Establishment 2020](/events/uaptf-establishment-2020),<sup>30</sup> and US service members were encouraged to report encounters. Senator [Marco Rubio](/people/marco-rubio), while chair of the [Senate Intelligence Committee](/organisations/senate-intelligence-committee), had asked the [Director of National Intelligence](/organisations/director-of-national-intelligence) and the Pentagon to deliver an unclassified UAP report to Congress,<sup>31</sup> describing anything entering restricted airspace without authorisation as a potential threat. The segment also noted that the US government had publicly ignored UAP reports following the closure of [Project Blue Book](/documents/project-blue-book) in 1969 and the [Project Blue Book Termination 1969](/events/project-blue-book-termination-1969), and that [Americans for Safe Aerospace](/organisations/americans-for-safe-aerospace) had subsequently advocated for greater transparency. An extended version of the interview was published by [60MinutesOvertime](/organisations/60minutesovertime). The [60 Minutes UAP Segment May 2021](/events/60-minutes-uap-segment-may-2021) was among the most prominent mainstream media treatments of UAP testimony at the time, framing the issue not as a matter of fringe speculation but of unresolved national security concern.
