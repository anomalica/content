"Claims that UFO information was inappropriately withheld from Congress deemed 'credible,' 'urgent'" is an opinion article published on 2023-06-09 in [The Hill](/organisations/the-hill), authored by [Marik von Rennenkampff](/people/marik-von-rennenkampff), a former Obama administration appointee at the [Department of Defense](/organisations/department-of-defense)<sup>30</sup> and former analyst with the [US Department of State Bureau of International Security and Nonproliferation](/organisations/us-department-of-state-bureau-of-international-security-and-nonproliferation).<sup>29</sup> The article analyses the [2023 ICIG Credible and Urgent Finding on Grusch Complaint](/events/2023-icig-credible-and-urgent-finding-on-grusch-complaint), in which the [Intelligence Community Inspector General](/organisations/intelligence-community-inspector-general) found that [David Grusch](/people/david-grusch)'s assertion that UFO-related information had been inappropriately concealed from Congress was both urgent and credible.<sup>1</sup> The article forms part of the broader [2023 Grusch UFO Whistleblower Allegations](/matters/2023-grusch-ufo-whistleblower-allegations) matter. [The Hill](/organisations/the-hill), in which it was published, and [NewsNation](/organisations/newsnation) are both owned by [Nexstar Media Group](/organisations/nexstar-media-group).<sup>9</sup>

Rennenkampff describes Grusch as a former high-level intelligence official<sup>2</sup> who was represented by [Compass Rose Legal Group](/organisations/compass-rose-legal-group), a firm whose managing partner co-signed the [Grusch ICIG Complaint](/objects/grusch-icig-complaint) submitted to the current Inspector General.<sup>4</sup> The article notes that Grusch had been represented until recently by a lawyer who previously served as the intelligence community's first inspector general, a Senate-confirmed position.<sup>3</sup> In a 2023-06-09 press release, the firm clarified that it "took no position and takes no position" on the substance of the information provided to the Inspector General.<sup>5</sup> Current and former officials are reported to have vouched for Grusch and corroborated the broad outlines of his allegations,<sup>6</sup> and Grusch spoke to Congress for hours, generating hundreds of pages of transcripts.<sup>7</sup> The [Grusch Congressional Disclosure](/events/grusch-congressional-disclosure) was accompanied by a [Grusch NewsNation Interview 2023](/events/grusch-newsnation-interview-2023), in which Grusch gave an exclusive interview to [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) of NewsNation.<sup>8</sup>

The article notes that Grusch provided classified evidence to Congress and two inspectors general alleging that UFO information was improperly withheld,<sup>14</sup> though Grusch claims the evidence supporting his allegations is highly classified<sup>13</sup> and Rennenkampff acknowledges that Grusch has not provided proof of his allegations publicly.<sup>11</sup> Rennenkampff observes that knowingly making false statements to the Inspector General carries the risk of financial penalties and imprisonment,<sup>10</sup> and opines that Grusch appears to be proceeding "by the book" by using the legally protected whistleblower process rather than leaking classified information.<sup>15</sup> The article also references corroboration from [Michael Shellenberger](/people/michael-shellenberger), described as an author and political commentator, whose sources confirmed the broad outlines of Grusch's allegations<sup>17</sup> and provided descriptions of vehicles allegedly recovered by a surreptitious UFO retrieval and reverse engineering effort — matters related to both [UAP Reverse Engineering Legacy Programs](/matters/uap-reverse-engineering-legacy-programs) and [Non-Human Craft Recovery Programs](/matters/non-human-craft-recovery-programs).<sup>18</sup>

The [Department of Defense](/organisations/department-of-defense) denied that its new UFO office had uncovered the sort of activity alleged by Grusch,<sup>12</sup> with the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO))](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) stating in 2023-06 that it had "not discovered any verifiable information to substantiate claims that any programs regarding the possession or reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial materials have existed in the past or exist currently."<sup>19</sup> Grusch contends that staff of previous UFO analysis programmes were not "read in" to information regarding the alleged activities,<sup>20</sup> and Rennenkampff opines that AARO, like its predecessor organisations, may simply not be privy to the alleged UFO retrieval programme.<sup>21</sup> The article further notes that Grusch's statements about the retrieval of exotic craft of non-human origin were approved by the [Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review](/organisations/defense-office-of-prepublication-and-security-review),<sup>22</sup> an office that scrutinises books, film scripts, and statements by former officials to ensure no sensitive information is inadvertently disclosed.<sup>23</sup>

The article concludes by highlighting the legislative context provided by the [FY2023 National Defense Authorization Act](/objects/fy2023-national-defense-authorization-act), a major defence bill signed by President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden) in 2022-12 that establishes whistleblower protections for individuals with knowledge of UFO programmes involving material retrieval, analysis, reverse engineering, and research and development — relating directly to [UAP Whistleblower Protection Legislation](/matters/uap-whistleblower-protection-legislation).<sup>24</sup> The [House Oversight Committee](/organisations/house-oversight-committee) vowed to hold a hearing on UFO-related matters following Grusch's allegations,<sup>16</sup> a commitment that would later materialise as the [House Oversight Committee UAP Hearing 2023](/events/house-oversight-committee-uap-hearing-2023).
