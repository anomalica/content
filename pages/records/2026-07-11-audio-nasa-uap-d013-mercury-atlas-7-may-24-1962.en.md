NASA-UAP-D013, Mercury Atlas 7, May 24, 1962, is a source concerning [Scott Carpenter](/people/scott-carpenter)'s account during the Mercury-Atlas 7 spaceflight. The source title identifies the [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa), while the claims describe Carpenter's observations and communications during the flight. Carpenter said that he observed white particles below the Mercury-Atlas 7 spacecraft.<sup>1</sup> He said that the particles appeared to be travelling at exactly his own speed,<sup>2</sup> and that one particle drifted away and moved faster than his spacecraft.<sup>3</sup>

The account also records Carpenter's description of the number and appearance of the particles. He said that he had not seen great numbers, but had seen a few.<sup>4</sup> He described their motion as random,<sup>5</sup> and considered them to look exactly like snowflakes.<sup>6</sup> The source therefore contains both descriptions of apparent movement relative to the spacecraft and a visual comparison made by the observer. These details are presented as Carpenter's unverified account rather than as independently established characteristics of the particles.

The source further preserves an exchange with Ground Control. Carpenter said that, when asked whether he had tried a return, he responded negatively because he needed to get within scanner limits first.<sup>7</sup> He also said that he needed to adjust the spacecraft's attitude to within those limits.<sup>8</sup> Later in the account, he reported seeing more particles that definitely looked like snowflakes.<sup>9</sup> Its significance for UAP research is therefore its concentration of a firsthand astronaut report, visual description, relative-motion observations and contemporaneous operational communication in a single record concerning the Mercury-Atlas 7 flight.
