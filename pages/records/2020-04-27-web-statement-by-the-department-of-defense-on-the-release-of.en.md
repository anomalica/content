The *Statement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos* is a 2020 statement concerning the authorised release of three unclassified videos recorded by the [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn). It is notable in UAP research because it addresses the videos’ prior public circulation, their security review and the continued characterisation of the phenomena as unidentified.

According to the [Statement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos](/records/2020-04-27-web-statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of), the Department of Defense (DoD) authorised the release of three unclassified Navy videos.<sup>1</sup> The statement dates one of the videos to November 2004<sup>2</sup> and two of them to January 2015.<sup>3</sup>

The statement says that the three videos had already been circulating in the public domain following unauthorised releases in 2007 and 2017.<sup>4</sup> It also says that the [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) had previously acknowledged that the circulating footage was indeed Navy footage.<sup>5</sup>

According to the statement, the Department of Defense (DoD) conducted a thorough review and determined that authorising the release would not reveal sensitive capabilities or systems.<sup>6</sup> The statement further says that the release would not impinge on subsequent investigations of military airspace incursions involving [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap).<sup>7</sup>

The stated purpose of the release was to clarify public misconceptions about whether the circulating footage was real and whether there was additional material associated with the videos.<sup>8</sup> The statement nevertheless says that the aerial phenomena observed in the videos remained characterised as unidentified.<sup>9</sup>
