The Statement to Congress records [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp)’s account of his involvement in [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) reporting and investigation. Knapp says that his interest in UFO secrecy began in 1987,<sup>1</sup> and that he subsequently wrote hundreds of UFO-related news stories and series.<sup>2</sup> He states that, since 1969, the official position of the United States military had been that UFOs posed no threat to national security and were not worthy of further study.<sup>3</sup> Against that background, he refers to Freedom of Information Act documents which, he says, showed that high-ranking military officers regarded the subject as “serious business”.<sup>4</sup>

Knapp describes a long-running exchange with [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), whom he says he first told in 1989 about accounts of crashed saucers and secret investigations. He also discusses [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow) and the creation of the National Institute for Discovery Science (NIDS) in 1996.<sup>5</sup> The statement then recounts Knapp’s spring 1993 visit to Moscow Russia, where he says he interviewed more than a dozen military officials, intelligence operatives and scientists.<sup>6</sup> He attributes accounts of a Russian military investigation, including 45 reported encounters between Russian warplanes and UFOs, to Colonel Boris Sokolov.<sup>7</sup><sup>8</sup> In the later United States section, Knapp says the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) was launched from the Office of the Undersecretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSDI) and managed by Lue Elizondo.<sup>9</sup> He distinguishes it from the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap), whose contract he says went to a subsidiary of [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace).<sup>10</sup> Knapp describes AAWSAP as a broad study that catalogued more than 200,000 cases,<sup>11</sup> beginning with the 2004 USS Nimitz Tic Tac UAP incident.<sup>12</sup> He says the programme lasted 27 months rather than the five years planned by the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia),<sup>13</sup> after which AATIP was created from its remnants.<sup>14</sup> The statement concludes that Knapp regards Elizondo’s public exposure of AATIP as contributing to the creation of the Unidentified Aerospace Phenomena Task Force (UAP Task Force) and subsequently the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro).<sup>15</sup>
