The Fatima Secret is a book by Michael Hesemann centred on the Fatima apparitions (1917). Hesemann says that he wrote it to present evidence for a higher intelligence behind Fatima and other apparitions,<sup>1</sup> while also describing it as non-religious and not intended to prove any religious doctrine.<sup>2</sup> The account begins with the report that three children saw a “beautiful lady” at [Fatima Portugal](/places/fatima-portugal) during the summer of 1917,<sup>3</sup> and states that the apparition delivered three secrets.<sup>4</sup> It follows the experiences of Lúcia (Fatima seer), [Jacinta Marto](/people/jacinta-marto), and [Francisco Marto](/people/francisco-marto), combining their reported visions with witness testimony, institutional history and later commentary.

A major part of the source concerns the Miracle of the Sun (1917-10-13). The account reports that more than seventy thousand people were present and that the sun appeared to change colour and move in the sky.<sup>5</sup> It also records a second-hand description of a large disk moving in front of the sun, with apparent activity visible on its surface.<sup>6</sup> The book then traces the development of the First Secret of Fatima, the Second Secret of Fatima, and the Third Secret of Fatima, including the latter’s eighty-year period of public concealment.<sup>7</sup> The Third Secret was eventually published on 2000-06-26,<sup>8</sup> after [John Paul II](/people/john-paul-ii), the [Vatican](/organisations/vatican), and church officials had associated it with twentieth-century persecution, the papacy and the fate of the Church. Angelo Sodano’s interpretation identifies the figure in white as the pope,<sup>9</sup> whereas Joseph Ratzinger describes the vision as a private revelation that should not be read literally.<sup>10</sup>

For UAP research, the source is notable because it places reports of luminous spheres, disks, atmospheric displays and mass observation alongside religious interpretations. Hesemann says that Marian apparitions and [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) reports show parallels, but cautions against explaining one poorly understood phenomenon through another.<sup>11</sup> The book also discusses the [Marian apparitions](/topics/marian-apparitions) at Fatima in relation to later claims, including the 1984 consecration of Russia to Mary,<sup>12</sup> and presents Hesemann’s view that UFO phenomena and Marian apparitions may represent incursions of another world into everyday life.<sup>13</sup> Its treatment of photographed miracles and scientifically examinable weeping statues further illustrates the source’s interest in evidence that can be documented as well as interpreted.<sup>14</sup>
