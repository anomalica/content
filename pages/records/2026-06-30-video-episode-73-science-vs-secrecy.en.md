Episode 73: Science vs. Secrecy records discussion of a Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) forum held in the USA, Washington DC, Kennedy Caucus Room of the [United States Senate](/organisations/united-states-senate), as described by [Hal Puthoff].<sup>1</sup> [Avi Loeb](/people/avi-loeb) said that the US government was cooperating with scientists to understand observed orbs.<sup>2</sup> He presented two alternatives for the objects under investigation: human-made or non-human-made; he described the first as a potential national-security breach and the second as potentially the biggest discovery in the history of science.<sup>3</sup> Loeb also used the Vela nuclear-detection satellite program as an example of military sensing producing scientific discoveries: he described its 1960s purpose as detecting gamma-ray flashes from nuclear explosions and its later detection of such flashes.<sup>4</sup> He said that he had found examples of interstellar meteors in a declassified US government satellite database.<sup>5</sup>

The episode gives particular attention to disclosure mechanisms and programme history. [UAP Gerb](/people/uap-gerb) said that legal interrogatives directed at the MITRE Corporation and other institutions were more effective for pursuing UAP disclosure than released files and videos.<sup>6</sup> Puthoff described himself as a senior scientist for [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace) working on the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), examining activity from a [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia)-funded programme.<sup>7</sup> He said that related corporate materials were handled under Independent Research and Development and were unavailable through Freedom of Information Act requests.<sup>8</sup> He characterised the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap) as a classified effort to obtain answers about UAP, while describing AATIP as a differently structured follow-on programme.<sup>9</sup> He further said that AAWSAP began in 2008 and was separate from the legacy program, which he associated with access to crash material and biologics but insufficient scientific expertise.<sup>10</sup>
