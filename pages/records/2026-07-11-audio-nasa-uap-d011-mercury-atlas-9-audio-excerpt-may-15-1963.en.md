Cooper said that he saw a brilliant blue band of light at sunrise in the east, with a haze layer beneath it.<sup>1</sup> He also described small luminescent particles drifting away from the spacecraft and appearing brilliant white.<sup>2</sup> At another point, he said that he had not seen the recovery beacon.<sup>3</sup> He reported considerable noise that sounded as if something were departing from the spacecraft,<sup>4</sup> and inferred that a recovery beacon had departed because doors sounded as though they had blown open.<sup>5</sup>

The excerpt also records operational communications. According to the source record, ground control reported that the telemetry readings looked good on the ground.<sup>6</sup> Cooper said that the television control switch was off,<sup>7</sup> and reported Direct Current (DC) readings of 24 volts on the main bus and 28.5 volts on an isolated circuit.<sup>8</sup><sup>9</sup> [Walter Schirra](/people/walter-schirra), serving as ground control, reported fuel computations from Hawaii USA of 88 percent on automatic control and 98 percent on manual control.<sup>10</sup><sup>11</sup> Cooper’s onboard instruments instead indicated 96 percent on automatic control and 102 percent on manual control.<sup>12</sup><sup>13</sup> He subsequently described a clear orbital view of the entire state of Florida USA.<sup>14</sup> Later observations concerned a brownish or reddish-brown light below the spacecraft, which he said appeared to be at considerable altitude above the ground.<sup>15</sup><sup>16</sup> After firing the pitch-down thruster, Cooper described a shower of small firefly-like particles.<sup>17</sup> He characterised the reddish-brown light as flashing, bright and discernible,<sup>18</sup> estimated its distance at approximately 16 to 19 kilometres,<sup>19</sup> and compared its brightness with that of a second-magnitude star.<sup>20</sup>
