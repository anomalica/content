*In Plain Sight* (2021) is a non-fiction book by [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart), an award-winning Australian investigative journalist who previously worked on [60 Minutes](/organisations/60-minutes) and the Sunday Night programme. The book draws on Coulthart's interviews with hundreds of witnesses — military personnel, government insiders, scientists, and civilians — alongside declassified documents and leaked materials, to survey what he argues is decades of deliberate suppression of UAP evidence by governments, principally the United States. The source record in the Anomalica knowledge graph encompasses the Prologue, Chapters 1 and 2, and a substantial body of material from subsequent chapters, giving the record its full breadth across events spanning the 1930s to 2023.

The opening section of the book is anchored in the experience of [Annie Farinaccio](/people/annie-farinaccio), who in late 1991 witnessed a long diamond-shaped craft hovering approximately 30 metres overhead near the [Harold Holt Naval Communications Station](/places/harold-holt-naval-communications-station) on Western Australia's [North West Cape](/places/northwest-cape).<sup>1</sup> The craft followed her vehicle for approximately 1 kilometre before apparently landing in nearby scrub.<sup>2</sup> [Two Australian Federal Police officers](/organisations/australian-federal-police) told Farinaccio the same object had followed them the previous night,<sup>3</sup> and two days later two American military police escorted her into the top-secret section of the US base for questioning, where officials claimed the object was a weather balloon.<sup>4,5</sup> A police officer warned her during the interrogation to stop talking "before you get us all killed".<sup>6</sup> Photographs taken by the officers were confiscated after development inside the base.<sup>7</sup> Coulthart notes that declassified files in the Australian National Archives record anomalous sightings at [North West Cape](/places/northwest-cape) over many decades, reported by soldiers, tourists, a senior American officer, and a local fireman.<sup>8</sup> The [Harold Holt Naval Communications Station](/places/harold-holt-naval-communications-station) is described as the most powerful transmission station in the Southern Hemisphere, with twelve antenna tower arrays reaching a highest point of 388 metres, and its original primary role was to send nuclear launch orders to American Polaris submarines.

The first two chapters survey UAP history from the 1930s through to the late 1960s, establishing what Coulthart frames as a long pattern of serious sightings and official suppression. In 1930, [Royal Australian Air Force](/organisations/royal-australian-air-force) Squadron Leader [George Jones](/people/george-jones) was sent to investigate mystery aircraft at Warrnambool, Victoria, and concluded they did not belong to any known power.<sup>9,10</sup> Jones later rose to Air Marshal and Chief of Air Staff.<sup>11</sup> Aviator [Francis Chichester](/people/francis-chichester) reported seeing a dull grey-white airship over the Tasman Ocean in 1931,<sup>12</sup> and from 1946 onwards the pace of sightings accelerated dramatically: Sweden reported more than 2,000 ghost rocket sightings with hundreds corroborated by radar,<sup>13</sup> and Sweden's Air Intelligence Service reportedly concluded the phenomenon "cannot be credited to any presently known culture on earth".<sup>14</sup> The [1946 Swedish Ghost Rockets](/events/1946-swedish-ghost-rockets) were followed in February 1947 by three independent sightings of a formation of five egg-shaped, wingless and tailless craft over southern Australia spanning approximately 1,000 kilometres,<sup>15</sup> with a New South Wales farmer estimating their speed at approximately 1,600 kilometres per hour.<sup>16</sup> [Kenneth Arnold](/people/kenneth-arnold) then sighted his famous objects near Mount Rainier on 1947-06-24, estimating their speed at approximately 2,700 kilometres per hour,<sup>17</sup> observed from approximately 30 to 40 kilometres distance.<sup>18</sup> The [Kenneth Arnold Sighting](/events/kenneth-arnold-sighting) triggered a cascade of reports, and on 1947-07-05 ranch foreman Mac Brazel found crash debris approximately 120 kilometres from [New Mexico Roswell](/places/new-mexico-roswell-usa).<sup>19</sup> [Jesse Marcel](/people/jesse-marcel) described a debris field approximately 1,200 metres long and 60 to 90 metres wide with a gouge up to 150 metres long.<sup>20</sup> On 1947-07-08, Colonel William Blanchard ordered [Walter Haut](/people/walter-haut) to issue a press release announcing recovery of a flying disc,<sup>21</sup> followed three hours later by General Ramey's retraction claiming the debris was a weather balloon.<sup>22</sup> The 1994 [Roswell Air Force 1994 Report](/documents/roswell-air-force-1994-report) admitted the weather balloon story was false, substituting [Project MOGUL](/matters/project-mogul) as the explanation,<sup>23</sup> while a 1995 General Accounting Office review found every Roswell Army Airfield document from the crash period had been destroyed without authorisation.<sup>24</sup> Haut's [Walter Haut Roswell Affidavit 2002](/documents/walter-haut-roswell-affidavit-2002) testified that he was shown child-sized bodies with abnormally large heads<sup>25</sup> and an egg-shaped craft approximately 3.6 to 4.6 metres in length in a hangar.<sup>26</sup> Coulthart also notes that Allied bomber pilots over Europe regularly reported [Foo Fighters WWII Sightings](/matters/foo-fighters-wwii-sightings) — wingless glowing objects that could "turn on a dime" — with Axis pilots equally concerned.<sup>27</sup>

The book's Australian material is especially detailed. The [Westall UFO Sighting](/events/westall-ufo-sighting) of 1966-04-06 is covered at length: more than 100 children and adult teachers witnessed several hovering UAPs at [Westall High School](/places/westall-high-school) in Melbourne.<sup>28</sup> Witness [Colin Kelly](/people/colin-kelly) described three craft approximately 5.5 metres across, silvery, shaped like two inverted saucers with a dome on top.<sup>29</sup> [Terry Peck](/people/terry-peck) saw a saucer close up in [The Grange Westall](/places/the-grange-westall), felt its heat through her hand,<sup>30</sup> watched it lift off the ground, tilt on its side, and shoot off at incredible speed.<sup>31</sup> All three craft were observed turning on their axis at 45 degrees before disappearing silently into the horizon.<sup>32</sup> Military jeeps and trucks arrived at the school and students were warned at an assembly not to speak about what they had seen.<sup>33</sup> Researcher [Shane Ryan](/people/shane-ryan) subsequently interviewed 122 witnesses who clearly saw a flying saucer,<sup>34</sup> yet no government document on the incident has ever surfaced from any archive despite strenuous efforts by multiple researchers.<sup>35</sup> The book also covers the [North West Cape 1973 UAP Sighting](/events/north-west-cape-1973-uap-sighting): on 1973-10-25, [Bill Lynn](/people/bill-lynn), an Australian fire captain working for the US Navy, observed a large black sphere approximately 9 metres in diameter hovering at approximately 300 metres before taking off at tremendous speed in a northerly direction after approximately 4 minutes,<sup>36,37</sup> an incident that Coulthart found entirely absent from declassified Royal Australian Air Force sighting records held by the Australian National Archives. The [Kaikoura UAP Sighting](/events/kaikoura-uap-sighting) in New Zealand in December 1978 is also examined: reporter [Quentin Fogarty](/people/quentin-fogarty) reported Wellington radar detected an object approximately 1.6 kilometres behind the aircraft,<sup>38</sup> air traffic controller [John Cordy](/people/john-cordy) tracked solid radar contacts parallel to the plane for approximately 64 kilometres at approximately 4,300 metres altitude,<sup>39</sup> yet the [Royal New Zealand Air Force](/organisations/royal-new-zealand-air-force) concluded witnesses were confused by unusual atmospheric phenomena and failed to interview Cordy or other key witnesses.<sup>40</sup> The [Valentich Disappearance](/events/valentich-disappearance) in October 1978 is also presented: [Frederick Valentich](/people/frederick-valentich) reported a huge illuminated shiny metal craft hovering above his Cessna over [Bass Strait](/places/bass-strait) before his transmission ended with strange pulsing sounds.<sup>41,42</sup>

The book's later chapters shift to the United States government's post-Cold War engagement with the UAP question. The [2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter](/events/2004-nimitz-uap-encounter) is treated in considerable depth. On 2004-11-14, the [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz) carrier strike group was conducting training approximately 100 kilometres off the California coast when radar operator [Kevin Day](/people/kevin-day) observed 14 unidentified objects on his screen, confirmed by the [USS Theodore Roosevelt](/objects/uss-theodore-roosevelt) radar, plummeting from approximately 24,000 metres to approximately 15 metres above the ocean in approximately 0.78 of a second.<sup>43,44,45</sup> [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) described the [Tic Tac Sighting](/events/tic-tac-sighting) object as a featureless giant white craft with no wings, engines, exhaust, or markings hovering just above the ocean surface.<sup>46</sup> Weapons systems officer [Jim Slaight](/people/jim-slaight) stated it "rifled out of sight in a split second" with acceleration no human could have survived.<sup>47</sup> The [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz) radioed Fravor that a UAP was already waiting at the prearranged cap point — a latitude and longitude not transmitted on an open channel.<sup>48</sup> The following morning, Day found all communications from the encounter had been wiped while date and time stamps remained,<sup>49</sup> and [Gary Voorhis](/people/gary-voorhis) was ordered to turn over all data tapes and erase everything including blank tapes contrary to normal procedure.<sup>50</sup> The [Tic Tac Data Bricks Confiscation](/events/tic-tac-data-bricks-confiscation) further removed evidence from the scene.

The book draws on a wide array of insider testimony regarding alleged crash retrieval programmes and the suppression of UAP information within the US national security apparatus. [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) told [George Knapp](/people/george-knapp) that the US government holds crash retrievals of non-human craft and that laboratory technologies were not advanced enough to understand the material.<sup>51</sup> In July 2020, Davis told [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times) he had given a classified briefing in March 2020 about retrievals from "off-world vehicles not made on this earth".<sup>52</sup> The [Wilson Davis Memo](/documents/wilson-davis-memo) — a 15-page document allegedly recording a 2002-10-16 meeting between Davis and [Defence Intelligence Agency](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency) Vice Admiral [Thomas Wilson](/people/thomas-wilson) in a [Las Vegas](/places/las-vegas) car park — claims the secret program "has recovered technological hardware not of this Earth and not made by human hands",<sup>53</sup> though Wilson issued a strong denial in June 2020 that any such meeting occurred.<sup>54</sup> Former US Navy Director of Science and Technology Development [Nat Kobitz](/people/nat-kobitz) confirmed to Coulthart that he had been read into a programme involving crash retrievals,<sup>55</sup> and described visiting [Wright Patterson Air Force Base](/places/wright-patterson-air-force-base) to examine a piece of material approximately 0.9 metres by 1.2 metres with a bulkhead-to-skin bond showing no visible join that he could not explain using any known manufacturing process.<sup>56</sup> On the legislative side, the book tracks [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge)'s founding of [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science) on 2017-10-11,<sup>57</sup> the subsequent release of the [FLIR1 Video](/documents/flir1-video) and [Gimbal Video](/documents/gimbal-video) in December 2017,<sup>58</sup> and the eventual collapse of TTSA in late 2020 with the departure of [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo), [Steve Justice](/people/steve-justice), and [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff).<sup>59</sup> Elizondo's "Five Observables" framework — positive lift, instantaneous acceleration, hypersonic velocity, low observability, and trans-medium travel — is presented as a synthesis of consistent UAP characteristics documented across multiple military encounters.<sup>60</sup> The book closes on the legislative breakthrough represented by [FY2023 NDAA UAP Section 1673](/matters/fy2023-ndaa-uap-section-1673), signed into law by President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden) on 2022-12-23, and the [First Open Congressional UAP Hearing 2022](/events/first-open-congressional-uap-hearing-2022) on 2022-05-17, at which [Ronald S. Moultrie](/people/ronald-s-moultrie) and [Scott W. Bray](/people/scott-w-bray) acknowledged UAPs posed flight safety and security risks and that 400 unresolved incidents were under investigation.<sup>61,62</sup>
