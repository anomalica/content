In Plain Sight is a 2023 source by [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) examining historical and contemporary reports of [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap). Its stated approach combines case narratives, declassified records, official investigations, witness testimony and competing explanations, while repeatedly distinguishing reported claims from established conclusions.<sup>1</sup><sup>2</sup>

## Scope and approach

The source ranges across Australia, New Zealand, the United States, Europe and the former Soviet Union. It treats UAP reports as a subject involving eyewitness accounts, radar and other sensor records, military procedures, intelligence programmes, alleged recovered materials and public policy. Its treatment of disputed cases is not uniformly affirmative: the author states that some central questions remain unresolved and that the [Roswell incident](/events/roswell-incident-1947) should remain open to investigation rather than being assigned a definitive explanation.<sup>2</sup>

## Historical and Australian cases

The source places Australian and New Zealand reports within a longer historical context, stating that recorded sightings of strange objects in their skies date to the period of early European settlement.<sup>3</sup> It also discusses declassified files held by the National Archives of Australia, which it says contain reports of unexplained objects at North West Cape made by soldiers, tourists, a senior American officer and a local fireman over several decades.<sup>4</sup>

A prominent Australian case is the 1991 North West Cape sighting involving [Annie Farinaccio](/people/annie-farinaccio) and two Australian Federal Protective Service officers. According to Farinaccio's account as presented in the source, she saw a long diamond-shaped craft with a truncated rear edge and rows of lights.<sup>5</sup> The account further states that the object followed the officers' vehicle for about one kilometre before rising and appearing to land in scrub a few hundred metres from the road.<sup>6</sup> The source also describes subsequent questioning at the United States Naval Communication Station Harold E. Holt, including the claim that the witnesses were told the object was a weather balloon.

The 1978 Kaikoura case in New Zealand is presented as a multi-sensor and multi-witness incident. The source says that lights following a cargo aircraft were seen by several people and detected on radar.<sup>7</sup> It also discusses the disagreement between official explanations and accounts from air-traffic controllers, including John Cordy, who considered the radar returns to represent solid objects rather than atmospheric effects.

The 1966 Westall school incident is another major Australian case. The source reports that more than 100 children and teachers saw several hovering objects after going onto the school sports field.<sup>8</sup> Accounts attributed to witnesses describe silvery, saucer-shaped objects, movement towards the nearby Grange bushland and later military or police activity. The source presents these accounts alongside claims of warnings not to discuss the incident, while noting that no official government explanation has been offered for what the witnesses reported.

## Official investigations and secrecy

A recurring theme is the contrast between public explanations and the existence of continuing official interest. [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book) is described as having investigated 12,618 civilian and military reports, with most assigned prosaic explanations but a small percentage remaining classified as unexplained.<sup>9</sup><sup>10</sup> The source also states that the 1968 Condon Report rejected the extra-terrestrial hypothesis and concluded that further UAP study could not be justified.<sup>11</sup>

The source nevertheless discusses alleged or documented investigations outside the public-facing Blue Book process. Declassified United States Department of Defense and United States Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) files are said to show a covert worldwide investigation and recovery programme.<sup>12</sup> In this context, Project Moon Dust is described as a 1961 operation intended to locate, recover and deliver descended foreign space vehicles.<sup>13</sup> The source connects this subject with alleged reports from Pakistan, Nepal, Bolivia and other locations, while the provenance and interpretation of individual reports remain disputed.

The source also discusses the alleged 2002 meeting between [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) and Tom Wilson, as recorded in the document known as The Admiral Wilson Memo (EWD Notes).<sup>14</sup> The document purports to describe Wilson's attempts to investigate alleged special-access projects involving recovered technology. Wilson later categorically denied that the conversation occurred and denied visiting Las Vegas in 2002 or meeting Davis.<sup>15</sup><sup>16</sup> Coulthart's assessment, as reported in the source, is that the document cannot be authenticated conclusively and should not by itself support the allegations associated with it.<sup>17</sup>

## The 2004 Nimitz encounter and later military cases

The 2004 USS Nimitz Tic Tac UAP encounter receives extensive treatment. Coulthart describes it as probably the most well-verified UAP event in history.<sup>18</sup> The account centres on radar observations by the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton-cg-59), visual observations by [David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) and other aircrew, and later footage recorded by [Chad Underwood](/people/chad-underwood).

According to the source, [Kevin Day](/people/kevin-day) calculated that some radar tracks descended from approximately 24,000 metres to altitudes as low as approximately 15 metres above the ocean in about 0.78 seconds.<sup>19</sup> Fravor described the visually observed object as a featureless white craft roughly the length of an FA-18 Super Hornet, with no visible engines, wings, exhaust or markings.<sup>20</sup> The source also reports that the FLIR1 video (Tic Tac footage) showed the object making a sudden movement to the left and leaving the screen.<sup>21</sup>

The source distinguishes between reported observations and interpretations. It attributes to Davis the conclusion that the Tic Tac was a legitimate unidentified object rather than human-made technology, but presents this as Davis's assessment rather than as an independently established finding.<sup>22</sup> It also notes that the [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) and the Pentagon continued to characterise the objects in the released footage as unidentified. The April 2020 Pentagon release of three UAP videos is described as the first public confirmation by the Pentagon that the three videos were authentic.<sup>23</sup><sup>24</sup>

The source places the Nimitz case alongside the 2014–2015 USS Theodore Roosevelt East Coast UAP encounters. It reports that pilots equipped with advanced radar detected objects on radar, infrared systems and by direct observation.<sup>25</sup> One account describes the [Gimbal video](/documents/gimbal-video) object as remaining stationary in winds of approximately 220 kilometres per hour before accelerating to speeds above supersonic.<sup>26</sup> These reports are used to illustrate the source's recurring interest in apparent low observability, unusual acceleration, prolonged station-keeping and possible operation across different environments.

## Government programmes, materials and public policy

The source discusses the relationship between Advanced Aerospace Weapons Systems Applications Program (AAWSAP) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), including the claim that much of the work was carried out under the latter name. It also covers later public activity by To The Stars Academy of Arts & Sciences (TTSA), including its release of UAP videos and its work on alleged materials.

The discussion of materials includes Art's Parts (alleged Roswell metal samples), described as layered bismuth and magnesium-zinc samples. The source reports that a 2019 filing disclosed the sale of six samples by [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) to TTSA for $35,000.<sup>27</sup> It also reports that no evidence had been publicly presented to substantiate the claim that a sample lost mass under terahertz radiation.<sup>28</sup> The source therefore presents both the extraordinary interpretations associated with the samples and the evidential limitations surrounding them.

The policy discussion culminates in the National Defense Authorization Act 2023 (NDAA). The source says that the Act created a secure means for current and former government employees and contractors to submit UAP-related information to the Pentagon's UAP office and relevant congressional oversight committees.<sup>29</sup> It further reports that the Act requires information about material retrieval, material analysis, reverse engineering, research and development, detection, tracking, testing and security protection to be included in reporting.<sup>30</sup> The source also states that authorised disclosures of concealed UAP programmes received protection against reprisal.<sup>31</sup>

## Evidential status

In Plain Sight combines official records, declassified documents, interviews, witness testimony and commentary from participants in UAP investigations. Many of the claims it presents are explicitly attributed to witnesses, researchers, former officials or anonymous sources. The source also records official denials, alternative explanations and unresolved questions, including disputes over the authenticity of the EWD Notes and the interpretation of alleged recovered materials.

Its central evidential distinction is between an unexplained observation and a demonstrated explanation. Radar or video evidence may establish that an observation was recorded, but the source does not treat that alone as proof of extra-terrestrial origin. The work consequently presents UAP research as an unresolved field in which the status of individual cases depends on the quality, provenance and independent corroboration of the available evidence.
