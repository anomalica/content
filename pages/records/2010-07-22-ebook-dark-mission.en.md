[Dark Mission] is a source centred on the investigations and interpretations of [Richard C. Hoagland](/people/richard-c-hoagland) and Mike Bara. Its principal case study is Cydonia, where the Face on Mars, the D&M Pyramid, the City, the Fortress, the Tholus, the Cliff and the Crater Pyramid are described as the Cydonia Complex. The source recounts how Hoagland and the Independent Mars Investigation began examining Viking imagery in 1983, and presents later Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) imagery as supporting his interpretation of the region as containing an ancient Martian arcology.<sup>1</sup><sup>2</sup><sup>3</sup>

A second major strand concerns alleged lunar anomalies in imagery from Apollo 10, [Apollo 12](/projects/apollo-12), Apollo 14, Apollo 15, Apollo 16 and [Apollo 17](/events/apollo-17), alongside Lunar Orbiter III, Surveyor 6 and the Lunar Crater Observation and Sensing Satellite (LCROSS). The source describes the Shard as a vertical feature extending at least 2.4 kilometres above the lunar surface and identifies the Tower/Cube as a second anomaly beyond the horizon.<sup>4</sup><sup>5</sup> It also reports Hoagland’s interpretation of LCROSS thermal imagery as evidence for surviving ancient lunar domes.<sup>6</sup> These observations are connected in the source to a wider ancient lunar dome hypothesis, including alleged glass-like structures, geometric frameworks and unusual light-scattering effects.

The book further links the imagery claims to criticism of [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa), the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), image-processing practices and the release of planetary data. Hoagland and Bara are reported as treating Cydonia’s geometry, mission dates and celestial alignments as evidence for the Ritual Alignment Model, while the source also presents the associated hyper-dimensional physics theory as a framework rejected by most modern physicists.<sup>7</sup><sup>8</sup> Hoagland states that five of eight predictions in the Hoagland–Torun model had been confirmed or supported by initial observations.<sup>9</sup> The source therefore matters as a substantial record of a particular strand of planetary-anomaly research, preserving its evidential claims, proposed explanations and disputes over interpretation.
