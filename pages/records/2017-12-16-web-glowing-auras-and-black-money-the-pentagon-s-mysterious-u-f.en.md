This 2017-12-16 article reports on the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), a US government effort that investigated reports of [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) sightings.<sup>1</sup> It is notable for describing the programme’s funding, activities and disputed status through accounts attributed to officials, participants and records obtained by [The New York Times](/organisations/the-new-york-times).

## Programme and funding

According to the article, AATIP was run by military intelligence official [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) from the fifth floor of the [The Pentagon](/organisations/the-pentagon)’s C Ring.<sup>2</sup> The article also reports that, within the roughly $600 billion annual Department of Defense (DoD) budgets described there, AATIP’s $22 million allocation was difficult to find.<sup>3</sup>

The article reports that AATIP began in 2007 and that parts of it remained classified.<sup>4</sup> It attributes the programme’s initial funding largely to a request from [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid), then the Senate majority leader.<sup>5</sup> Most of the money reportedly went to an aerospace research company run by [Robert Bigelow](/people/robert-bigelow), described as a billionaire entrepreneur and longtime friend of Reid.<sup>6</sup> The article says that Bigelow was also working with [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) to produce expandable craft for human use in space.<sup>7</sup>

According to the article, Bigelow believes that UFOs have visited Earth.<sup>8</sup> Working with his Las Vegas-based company, AATIP produced documents describing aircraft that appeared to move at very high velocities without visible propulsion.<sup>9</sup> The article further reports that AATIP documents described aircraft hovering without an apparent means of lift, and that programme officials studied videos of encounters between unknown objects and American military aircraft.

## Investigated incidents and materials

The article states that, in August 2017, AATIP released a video of a whitish oval object, described as about the size of a commercial aeroplane, being chased by two Navy [F/A-18F Super Hornet](/objects/fa-18f-super-hornet) fighters from the aircraft carrier [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz-cvn-68) off [USA, California, San Diego](/places/california-san-diego-usa) in 2004. The account concerns the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter).<sup>10</sup>

The article reports that AATIP funding was used for programme management, research and assessments of the threat posed by the objects.<sup>11</sup> It says that contracts obtained by The New York Times showed a congressional appropriation of just under $22 million beginning in late 2008 and continuing through 2011.<sup>12</sup> The funding reportedly went to [Bigelow Aerospace](/organisations/bigelow-aerospace), which hired subcontractors and solicited research.<sup>13</sup>

According to the article, buildings in Las Vegas were modified under Bigelow’s direction to store metal alloys and other materials that Elizondo and AATIP contractors said had been recovered from unidentified aerial phenomena.<sup>14</sup> AATIP researchers also reportedly studied people who said they had experienced physical effects during encounters with unidentified aerial objects, examining them for physiological changes.<sup>15</sup> The article identifies [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) as an engineer who had conducted [extrasensory perception (ESP)](/topics/extrasensory-perception-esp) research for the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) and later worked as an AATIP contractor.<sup>16</sup>

AATIP recordings reportedly included footage from a Navy F/A-18F Super Hornet showing an aircraft surrounded by a glowing aura, travelling at high speed and rotating as it moved.<sup>17</sup> In a recorded encounter, a Navy pilot can be heard saying, “There’s a whole fleet of them.” The article says that Defense officials declined to release the location and date of that incident.<sup>18</sup>

## Dispute over the programme’s status

In response to questions from The New York Times, Pentagon officials acknowledged AATIP’s existence in December 2017 and said that it had begun as part of the [Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)](/organisations/defense-intelligence-agency-dia).<sup>19</sup> The article reports that officials said the programme had ended after five years, in 2012.<sup>20</sup> Thomas Crosson, a Pentagon spokesman, said that higher-priority issues merited funding and that making a change was in the best interests of the DoD.<sup>21</sup>

Elizondo gave a different account. He said that the only part of AATIP that had ended was its government funding, which dried up in 2012.<sup>22</sup> The article says that, after 2012, he worked with officials from the [United States Navy](/organisations/united-states-navy-usn) and the CIA on AATIP. It reports that he continued working from his Pentagon office until October 2017, when he resigned in protest at what he characterised as excessive secrecy and internal opposition.<sup>23</sup>

In the Elizondo resignation letter to Jim Mattis (2017-10-04), addressed to Jim Mattis, Elizondo wrote, “Why aren’t we spending more time and effort on this issue?”<sup>24</sup> The article reports that he called for more serious attention to accounts from the Navy and other services involving unusual aerial systems interfering with military weapon platforms and displaying beyond-next-generation capabilities.<sup>25</sup> Elizondo also said that the AATIP effort continued and that he had a successor whose name he declined to provide.<sup>12</sup>

## Congressional involvement

The article attributes AATIP’s origins to Reid’s interest in space phenomena and to his relationship with Bigelow. It reports that Reid said his interest in UFOs came from Bigelow, who told him in 2007 that a DIA official had approached Bigelow about visiting his research ranch in Utah USA. Reid said that he subsequently met DIA officials and learned that they wanted to start a UFO research programme.

According to Reid’s account in the article, he then summoned [Ted Stevens](/people/ted-stevens) and [Daniel K. Inouye](/people/daniel-k-inouye) to a secure room in the Capitol. Stevens and Inouye were described as senior members of a defence spending subcommittee who supported AATIP. The article reports that Reid said neither he, Stevens nor Inouye wanted a public debate on the Senate floor about the programme’s funding.

By 2009, the article says, Reid had decided that AATIP had made extraordinary discoveries and argued for heightened security around it.<sup>26</sup> In the Reid letter to William Lynn III (2009), Reid reportedly told [William Lynn III](/people/william-lynn-iii), then a deputy defense secretary, that progress had been made in identifying highly sensitive, unconventional aerospace-related findings. He requested that AATIP become a restricted special access programme limited to a small number of officials.<sup>27</sup> The request was denied.<sup>28</sup>

A 2009 2009 Pentagon AATIP briefing summary, prepared by the programme’s director at the time, reportedly stated that what had been considered science fiction was now science fact and that the United States was unable to defend itself against some of the technologies discovered.<sup>29</sup> The article presents these statements as claims in the briefing summary rather than as independently established findings.

## Earlier Air Force investigations

The article places AATIP in the context of earlier investigations by the [United States Air Force](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf). It reports that the Air Force began a series of studies in 1947 that investigated more than 12,000 claimed UFO sightings before the series officially ended in 1969.<sup>30</sup> [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book), one of the studies in that series, began in 1952.<sup>31</sup>

According to the article, the Air Force’s investigation, including Project Blue Book, concluded that most sightings involved stars, clouds, conventional aircraft or spy planes.<sup>32</sup> It also reports that 701 of the more than 12,000 investigated sightings remained unexplained.<sup>33</sup> [Robert C. Seamans Jr.](/people/robert-c-seamans-jr), secretary of the Air Force when Project Blue Book ended, said in a memorandum that the project could no longer be justified on grounds of national security or in the interest of science.<sup>34</sup>

## Responses and later activity

The article includes cautionary and sceptical responses. Sara Seager, an astrophysicist at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)](/organisations/massachusetts-institute-of-technology-mit), cautioned that not knowing an object’s origin did not mean that it came from another planet or galaxy.<sup>35</sup> She also said that reports of genuinely unusual phenomena could sometimes warrant serious investigation.

[James E. Oberg](/people/james-e-oberg), described as a former NASA space shuttle engineer and an author of books on spaceflight who often debunks UFO sightings, expressed doubt about AATIP. He said that prosaic events and human perceptual traits could account for such stories, while also saying that he welcomed UFO research and that there could be a significant discovery in it.<sup>36</sup>

The article reports that Elizondo later joined Puthoff and [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon), described as a former DoD official and former deputy assistant secretary of defense for intelligence, in the commercial [To the Stars Academy of Arts and Science](/organisations/to-the-stars-academy-of-arts-and-science-ttsa).<sup>37</sup> It says that the three spoke publicly about their efforts as the organisation sought funding for UFO research. Elizondo said that he and his government colleagues had determined that the phenomena studied by AATIP did not appear to originate from any country, while Reid said that he did not know where the objects came from and that anyone claiming to have the answers was fooling themselves.
