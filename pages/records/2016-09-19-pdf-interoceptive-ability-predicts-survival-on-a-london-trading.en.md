Interoceptive Ability Predicts Survival on a London Trading Floor is a field study of interoception, which the source defines as sensing physiological signals originating inside the body.<sup>1</sup> According to the source, Narayanan Kandasamy, Sarah N. Garfinkel, Lionel Page, Ben Hardy, Hugo D. Critchley, Mark Gurnell and John M. Coates quantified heartbeat detection skills among traders working on a United Kingdom, England, London trading floor.<sup>2</sup> The study recruited 18 male traders engaged in high-frequency trading; they bought and sold futures contracts and held positions for seconds or minutes, or at most a few hours.<sup>3</sup><sup>4</sup> Its central method was the heartbeat detection task, assessed through two procedures.<sup>5</sup> In the counting task, participants silently counted perceived heartbeats during brief intervals, while pulse oximetry recorded the actual number of beats for comparison.<sup>6</sup><sup>7</sup> In the second procedure, they judged whether ten auditory tones were synchronised with their heartbeats or delayed relative to them, completing 15 trials.<sup>8</sup><sup>9</sup> Results were compared with 48 age-matched, non-trading male controls drawn from studies at the University of Sussex.<sup>10</sup>

According to the source, traders had a higher mean heartbeat-counting score than controls: 78.2 compared with 66.9.<sup>11</sup> Within the trader sample, heartbeat-counting scores predicted profit and loss, and also predicted the number of years a trader had survived in the market.<sup>12</sup><sup>13</sup> Model selection produced a model containing heartbeat detection score and RMSSD, a measure of heart-rate variability, that explained 45.4% of the variance in P&L; the corresponding model for years of survival explained 60.5% of the variance.<sup>14</sup><sup>15</sup> The source also reports that the physiological variables associated with heartbeat detection included heart rate, heart-rate variability and body mass index. It explicitly states that, because the work was conducted in the field, it could not establish causation.<sup>16</sup>

The study places these results within the somatic marker hypothesis, citing Antoine Bechara and Antonio Damasio's account of physiological changes accompanying risky choices on the Iowa Gambling Task.<sup>17</sup> According to the source, such bodily signals can feed back on the brain and bias decisions, including before a person can articulate the reasons for a choice; it also discusses impaired bodily-decision coupling following ventromedial prefrontal-cortex damage.<sup>18</sup><sup>19</sup><sup>20</sup> The authors describe the findings as anomalous data for the Efficient Markets Hypothesis, particularly its strong version, which treats market performance as unaffected by individual traits or skills.<sup>21</sup><sup>22</sup> Their interpretation shifts attention towards physiological factors in financial decision-making, while the study's observational design leaves alternative explanations open.<sup>23</sup>
