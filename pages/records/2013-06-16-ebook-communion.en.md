[Communion](/documents/communion) is presented by [Whitley Strieber](/people/whitley-strieber) as the story of one person's attempt to deal with what he calls a shattering assault from the unknown.<sup>1</sup> He states that the work is a true story as far as he knows how to describe it,<sup>2</sup> and reports an elaborate personal encounter with intelligent the visitors (nonhuman beings).<sup>3</sup> The central setting is a log cabin shared by Whitley and [Anne Strieber](/people/anne-strieber) in secluded New York Ulster County, where he says the primary experiences occurred.<sup>4</sup> Whitley Strieber recalls material connected with the 1985 abduction experience December 26 from journal entries written before hypnosis or discussion with other people.<sup>5</sup> By contrast, before seeking help he says he remembered only a strange disturbance associated with the 1985 abduction experience October 4.<sup>6</sup> The account therefore places contemporaneous notes, incomplete recollection, and later recovered memories alongside one another rather than presenting a single uninterrupted narrative.

The work records the procedures through which Strieber examined these experiences. It includes transcripts of hypnotic regressions concerning the October and December 1985 episodes.<sup>7</sup> He describes a three-hour pre-interview with Dr. Donald Klein before hypnosis,<sup>8</sup> while [Budd Hopkins](/people/budd-hopkins) was present at both sessions and was permitted to ask questions only at the end.<sup>9</sup> According to the source, the session focused on October memories took place on 1986-03-01.<sup>10</sup> Strieber also records Klein's assessment that he was not suffering from psychosis.<sup>11</sup> Later, on 1986-10-31, he underwent the polygraph examination of Whitley Strieber by Ned Laurendi.<sup>12</sup> Strieber says that the examination was intended to reassure readers of his sincerity and that the work was not fiction,<sup>13</sup> while also acknowledging that a successful polygraph could not establish that his recollections were objectively correct.<sup>14</sup> The source extends this examination of experience into collective discussion: eleven people met at Hopkins's home for the 1986 colloquy April 13,<sup>15</sup> whose stated purpose was chiefly to consider coping rather than reconstruct every detail of the encounters.<sup>16</sup>
