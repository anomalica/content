NASA-UAP-D009, Apollo 17 Audio Excerpt, December 7, 1972, is an audio source concerning the Apollo 17 orbital debris sighting, December 7, 1972. The excerpt relates to the [Apollo 17](/events/apollo-17) crew and describes bright particles or fragments drifting past as the spacecraft manoeuvred.<sup>1</sup> According to the source record, a crew member reported a large number of big, bright fragments visible on a window.<sup>2</sup> They were described as jagged and angular, and as either fragmented or tumbling.<sup>3</sup> One crew member distinguished them from fluid, describing distinct pieces instead.<sup>4</sup> The source also records that most fragments appeared to tumble at a very slow rate.<sup>5</sup> A crew member took photographs at different camera settings in an attempt to capture their patterns.<sup>6</sup>

The excerpt records several possible explanations without resolving them. According to the source record, a crew member speculated that the fragments could have been curved pieces from the Saturn IVB stage (S-IVB),<sup>7</sup> chunks of ice,<sup>8</sup> or paint coming from the spacecraft or its stage.<sup>9</sup> A [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa) ground-control communicator separately recalled that, during a pre-launch inspection, a thermal protection flag possibly on the Saturn IVB stage appeared to be peeling.<sup>10</sup> The source says that the manoeuvre involving the stage was completed,<sup>11</sup> after which the fragment field became essentially static apart from slight tumbling.<sup>12</sup> It also records that a considerably faster fragment crossed the window, although this was very rare,<sup>13</sup> and that no apparent relative motion was observed between the fragments.<sup>14</sup> A plan to take two photographs about a minute apart using frame 70 is also included.<sup>15</sup> [Gene Cernan](/people/gene-cernan) described the particles as flat, flake-like fragments,<sup>16</sup> estimated some at approximately 15 centimetres across,<sup>17</sup> said most appeared to be twinkling,<sup>18</sup> and said they seemed mostly to be moving away from the spacecraft.<sup>19</sup> The source therefore preserves descriptions, estimates and proposed explanations associated with the observation, while leaving the identification of the fragments open.
