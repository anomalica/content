[Jacques Vallee](/people/jacques-vallee) presents *The Invisible College* as a personal interpretation of [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) patterns in relation to changes in human consciousness.<sup>1</sup> He describes the Invisible College as an informal body of about a hundred scientists working in five or six countries,<sup>2</sup> investigating cases, interviewing witnesses and exchanging data through international contacts.<sup>3</sup> The name was attributed to [J. Allen Hynek](/people/j-allen-hynek), who connected it with earlier natural philosophers working privately outside established doctrine.<sup>4</sup> The book also records Hynek’s invitation for Vallee to apply computer science to [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book)’s statistical procedures,<sup>5</sup> and Vallee’s examination of 10,000 reports in [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) files.<sup>6</sup> He presents this work within descriptive phenomenology, an approach concerned with the structure and quality of reported experience.<sup>7</sup>

The source gives particular weight to cases in which physical observations are accompanied by physiological, psychological or apparently paranormal effects. According to the source record, the Case of Dr. X (French physician, UFO witness) involved a medical doctor in southern [France](/places/france) and was investigated by several specialists.<sup>8</sup> Dr. X reportedly observed two disk-shaped objects and beams of light during the 1968 event,<sup>9</sup> while subsequent follow-up described triangular pigmentation appearing intermittently on both him and his son.<sup>10</sup> The 1971 Delphos, Kansas UFO landing provides another example: Ronald Johnson described a multicoloured object hovering about 0.6 metres above the ground,<sup>11</sup> and the witnesses reported a glowing ring at the site.<sup>12</sup> The record also notes that the white substance associated with the ring remained unidentified and that soil analyses found no significant difference between the ring’s interior and exterior.<sup>13</sup>

Vallee proposes that UFOs may constitute a control system,<sup>14</sup> while distinguishing technological and psychological approaches to the subject.<sup>15</sup> His The Hilltop Theory combines psychic effects, institutional and social reactions, belief formation, and the possible exploitation of those beliefs by groups such as the UMMO organization.<sup>16</sup> He suggests that reports need not represent visits from space travellers, but may involve a more complex technology involving time and space.<sup>17</sup> Contact, in his account, appears to be shaped by the phenomenon itself and marked by absurdity.<sup>18</sup> This framework matters because it places witness experience, cultural interpretation and research practice alongside physical claims, while contrasting private investigation with official conclusions such as those attributed to the Condon Committee (University of Colorado UFO study).<sup>19</sup>
