The recognition of extraterrestrial intelligence examines possible criteria for identifying technology beyond Earth. [Carl Sagan](/people/carl-sagan) considers a significant departure from thermodynamic equilibrium necessary, but not sufficient, for recognising extraterrestrial intelligence.<sup>1</sup> According to the source record, photography of Earth in reflected sunlight shows no clear sign of life until a resolution of 100 metres is achieved.<sup>2</sup> At that resolution, agricultural and urban reworking in rectangular arrays becomes apparent.<sup>3</sup> The same record states that Phobos Mars shows no rectangular-array patterns of this kind.<sup>4</sup> It also describes Earth's radio, television and radar emissions as a detectable departure from radiative equilibrium over interstellar distances.<sup>5</sup> Carl Sagan said that a civilisation with technology no more advanced than Earth's could identify the artificial character of these signals through autocorrelation techniques.<sup>6</sup>

The discussion applies these ideas to the Mariner 9 mission and the Mariner 9 photographic reconnaissance of Mars. According to the source, the mission was the first photographic investigation of the possibility of a technical civilisation there.<sup>7</sup> It obtained 7232 photographs, mostly at 1 kilometre resolution, mapping the planet from pole to pole.<sup>8</sup> Several percent of Mars was also observed at 100 metre resolution, with coverage described as adequate to detect a technical civilisation comparable to Earth's.<sup>9</sup> No signs of such a civilisation were uncovered.<sup>10</sup> The source records that Iosif Shklovskii had hypothesised in 1966 that Phobos was hollow and artificial, but that this rested on celestial-mechanical data now known to be erroneous.<sup>11</sup> Mariner 9 photography instead indicated that Phobos was natural.<sup>12</sup> For radio searches, [Carl Sagan](/people/carl-sagan) described the Arecibo radio telescope as a 305 metre semi-steerable instrument operated by Cornell University for the National Science Foundation.<sup>13</sup><sup>14</sup>

The source presents the search for extraterrestrial intelligence as both a communications problem and a question of search scale. Carl Sagan said that two Arecibo-class telescopes could communicate across the Milky Way at about 1 bit per second, and that intentional communication might be possible within about 1000 light years.<sup>15</sup><sup>16</sup> Several hundred stars had been examined at one or two frequencies, while B. Zuckerman and P. Palmer were conducting a broader search at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory (NRAO).<sup>17</sup><sup>18</sup> According to the record, the [Pioneer 10](/objects/pioneer-10) and Pioneer 11 plaques were the only direct attempts to communicate with extraterrestrial intelligence as of 1975.<sup>19</sup> The Pioneer plaque depicted hydrogen, pulsars, humans, the spacecraft and the Solar System; it was a 15 centimetre by 22.5 centimetre gold-anodised aluminium plate designed by [Carl Sagan](/people/carl-sagan), Linda Sagan and Frank Drake.<sup>20</sup><sup>21</sup> Carl Sagan said its physics and astronomy were intended to be shared with any receiving technological civilisation.<sup>22</sup> Regarding the [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) question, he reported no compelling evidence for past extraterrestrial visitation, while distinguishing that judgement from the possibility of evidence emerging in the future.<sup>23</sup><sup>24</sup>
