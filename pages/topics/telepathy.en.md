Telepathy - communication or perception between minds without recourse to the physical senses - is discussed in the source material as a form of psi, recurring across survival-of-death research, close-encounter testimony, and contemporary parapsychological research. According to the book *Surviving Death*, telepathy occurs when one mind influences or reads another, as when a person perceives someone else's thoughts.<sup>1</sup> The same source describes the Living-Agent Psi (LAP) hypothesis, which proposes that verifiable communications received by mediums, and interpreted as coming from discarnate spirits, may actually arise from the medium's own highly developed telepathy reading the minds of living people connected to the deceased person.<sup>2</sup> Under the LAP hypothesis, such telepathy is not held to be limited by the physical distance between the medium and the living source of the information.<sup>3</sup> In mental mediumship, a medium in a normal state of consciousness receives messages that she says come telepathically from a deceased person.<sup>4</sup>

Drop-in communicators - purported spirits who identify themselves through a medium without having been sought by anyone present - are cited as a specific challenge to telepathic explanations of mediumship. Alan Gauld said that at least one carefully investigated case in the literature involved a drop-in communicator making a series of correct statements whose totality could not have been obtained clairvoyantly from a single document or obituary, or telepathically from the mind of a single living person.<sup>5</sup> In the case of Runolfur Runolfsson ("Runki"), that he had been tall was confirmed by his grandson who, according to *Surviving Death*, had not known him and was not aware of the bone or other relevant facts, and so could not have been a source, through telepathy or normal channels, for all the information communicated.<sup>6</sup> [Leslie Kean](/people/leslie-kean) has argued that under the rival super-ESP hypothesis, a medium producing a drop-in communicator's biographical facts would have to cull details from various, sometimes obscure, sources in multiple locations, requiring an almost unimaginable level of telepathy and clairvoyance.<sup>7</sup> Kean has also said that for a medium to create a materialised form entirely separate from himself that could walk and talk independently, he would need telepathic information to make the form match a once-living person completely unknown to him, and would need to imbue the form with obscure verbal and physical detail proving its identity to a family member.<sup>8</sup> Erlendur Haraldsson said that telepathy among living persons and/or clairvoyance does not adequately explain the strongest drop-in communicator cases, establishing such cases as being of particular significance for the survival hypothesis.<sup>9</sup> Kean has described her first after-death communication - recorded here as Leslie Kean's first after-death communication (ADC) hearing her brother's voice (2013) - as perceived by her mind rather than heard with her ears, as though her consciousness had its own non-physical ears.<sup>10</sup> Loyd Auerbach considers perceptual insertion, a telepathic projection onto a percipient's own perceptual processes, an explanation for why only some people experience apparitions.<sup>11</sup> In one case, Auerbach concluded that either [Chris](/people/chris) or Lois had read the group's minds while at a house, or that Lois had genuinely been present in the car with them, and that telepathy by either would be a reasonable explanation.<sup>12</sup>

[Whitley Strieber](/people/whitley-strieber)'s *The Fourth Mind* situates telepathy within a broader taxonomy of siddhis (Hindu/Buddhist accomplishments): the six siddhi powers shared across major religious traditions are described as healing, clairvoyance, levitation, telepathy, astral projection, and manifestation,<sup>13</sup> with levitation and telepathy also numbered among the secondary siddhis said to be possessed by visitors.<sup>14</sup> Strieber has said that levitation, shape-shifting, telepathy, spontaneous healing, and instantaneous movement are side effects of an ability to address the universe from outside time.<sup>15</sup> According to *The Fourth Mind*, Hindu tradition relates the third eye to the pineal gland and regards it as a transceiver enabling far-seeing and telepathic communication.<sup>16</sup> Strieber has argued that humanity was once a telepathic species linked in a community of mind prior to the Younger Dryas catastrophe,<sup>17</sup> and that such worldwide social groups connected by telepathy would have contracted and disappeared as individuals struggled for local survival during that period.<sup>18</sup> He has said ancient peoples worldwide lost or intentionally abandoned old powers of mind, including levitation and telepathy, in the belief that their use had angered the gods,<sup>19</sup> and considers telepathy the lost human power most needed to restore the fractured human community, more so than levitation.<sup>20</sup> Graham Nichols has said that emotional bond or impact between participants is a key factor in the success of psychic phenomena such as [Precognition](/topics/precognition) and telepathy.<sup>21</sup>

Accounts of encounters with non-human beings frequently describe the communication involved as telepathic. Strieber's experience of "the boy," a hybrid child encountered at his cabin, was that he was telepathic,<sup>22</sup> which Strieber has suggested may relate to Klinefelter syndrome, indicating an intentional design choice rather than flawed genetic engineering.<sup>23</sup> Strieber considers that hybrids with humanoid bodies possessing powers such as telepathy but lacking the ability to speak have been created, and that many of them seem significantly flawed.<sup>24</sup> He has argued there is no known medium through which thought can be transmitted directly from one mind to another under current physics,<sup>25</sup> and has proposed that for telepathy to work, something must happen that cannot occur under the laws of reality as currently understood.<sup>26</sup> Strieber and other close encounter witnesses have reported observing the visitors communicate through mind-to-mind telepathy,<sup>27</sup> and Strieber has said he has personally experienced telepathy with the visitors, describing it as feeling natural, easy and unsurprising rather than miraculous.<sup>28</sup> [Chris Ramsay](/people/chris-ramsay) has described a recurring feature of screen memories as their absurdity and abstractness, suggesting that the beings projecting them through technology or telepathic ability may not fully understand what is normal.<sup>29</sup>
