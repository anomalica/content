Warp drive is a theoretical propulsion concept in which space itself, rather than a vehicle moving through it, is manipulated to permit apparent faster-than-light travel. The term originated with a science-fiction writer working in 1930s pulp fiction magazines<sup>1</sup>, but it has since been taken up as a subject of formal theoretical physics research, and within UAP research circles it is discussed as a candidate explanation for the observed performance characteristics of anomalous aerial vehicles.

The concept gained a rigorous theoretical footing through the work of Miguel Alcubierre, who at the age of thirty, as a PhD student in theoretical physics at Cardiff University, showed how a warp drive could work within the framework of general relativity<sup>2</sup>. Physicists writing after Alcubierre have argued, according to the book "Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs", that warp drives might be theoretically possible but not practical<sup>3</sup>. The concept has also received attention from US government-linked research institutions: facilities associated with the [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod), [National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)](/organisations/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration-nasa), and the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)](/organisations/massachusetts-institute-of-technology-mit) have given the concept of warp drive serious consideration<sup>4</sup>.

Within the Pentagon's UAP programmes, warp drive theory is associated principally with the physicist [Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff). According to Luis Elizondo, before he joined the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip), Puthoff had conceived and commissioned studies covering warp drives alongside invisibility cloaking, traversable wormholes and stargates, antigravity, and brain/machine interfaces<sup>5</sup>. Elizondo further recounts that Puthoff's own papers touched on warp drives, traversable wormholes, and time machines<sup>6</sup>.

Puthoff has offered his own theoretical account of how a warp drive might function. He said that a technology capable of warping space and time in a localised area could create a bubble around a UAP craft<sup>7</sup>. Puthoff said that, given sufficient energy, a vehicle could generate such a warp bubble to achieve faster-than-light travel by compressing space in front of the vehicle and stretching space behind it<sup>8</sup>. He also said that the warp bubble required for faster-than-light travel could only be of limited size, owing to the large amount of energy needed to sustain it<sup>9</sup>. In a 2026 documentary interview, Puthoff said that advances in physics mean warp drive is not out of the question<sup>10</sup>.
