A Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) is a secure space used to store, discuss, and view Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) belonging to a [Special Access Program (SAP)](/topics/special-access-program-sap). A SAP designation is not itself a higher security classification than top secret; it denotes that a project is compartmented, its material designated SCI, and given a codename.<sup>1</sup> Even the most senior military officials holding the highest top-secret clearance are barred from the details behind a SAP or SCI codename unless they have been formally "read into" the programme.<sup>2</sup> A SCIF is built to block sound, radio, and electromagnetic waves.<sup>3</sup> [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon)'s role on the staff of the [US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](/organisations/us-senate-select-committee-on-intelligence) required him to review top-secret SCI projects held inside SAPs, most of which remain classified.<sup>4</sup> [John Podesta](/people/john-podesta) held top-secret/SCI clearances in his earlier roles under Presidents [Barack Obama](/people/barack-obama) and [William J. Clinton](/people/william-j-clinton).<sup>5</sup> According to In Plain Sight, sources with top-secret and compartmented security clearances say that no senior military official holding a Top Secret Sensitive Compartmented Information (TS-SCI) clearance should ever be surprised at being refused information by a contractor.<sup>6</sup>

SCIFs feature repeatedly in first-hand accounts of Pentagon-based programmes investigating [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) given by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo). [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) and [James Lacatski](/people/james-lacatski) briefed Elizondo on the [Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap) and the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) inside a SCIF.<sup>7</sup>

[Harold E. Puthoff](/people/harold-e-puthoff) visited the Pentagon and met Elizondo's team in a SCIF.<sup>8</sup> Elizondo and Puthoff went on to discuss propulsion theories for UAP in a SCIF for several hours.<sup>9</sup> Elizondo has said that during that discussion the group identified a possible explanation for elevated UAP activity around nuclear sites and technologies.<sup>10</sup>

Elizondo also used a SCIF to share reports, photographs, data, and intelligence on legacy UAP efforts with Mellon over the course of three hours.<sup>11</sup> Mellon had earlier visited Elizondo's SCIF unannounced, identifying himself and expressing interest in helping AATIP.<sup>12</sup>

SCIF spaces also hosted the weekly meetings held under OPLAN Interloper, for which the [National Security Agency (NSA)](/organisations/national-security-agency-nsa) and [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) offered assets in support; some of these meetings took place at the Pentagon and others at different agencies.<sup>13</sup>

Elizondo envisioned an alternative to routine SCIF access, proposing to declassify some UAP videos and make them available to handpicked colleagues in the defence industry via a secure government server, rather than requiring them to obtain clearance and view the footage in a SCIF.<sup>14</sup> In 2023, Elizondo made multiple trips from his home to Washington DC and spent many hours in a SCIF with US Senate leaders regarding the UAP Disclosure Act.<sup>15</sup>
