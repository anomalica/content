Precognition is the term used in [Remote Viewing (RV)](/topics/remote-viewing-rv) for producing information about a target before that information actually exists in time and space, such as describing an event before it happens.<sup>1</sup> According to Surviving Death, precognition more broadly involves an awareness of future events before they occur.<sup>2</sup>

The out-of-body researcher [Robert Monroe](/people/robert-monroe) first noticed a side effect during states of deep relaxation, before any separation from the body had begun.<sup>3</sup> He initially attributed the resulting visions to material released from the unconscious mind, until a major event brought the phenomenon more strongly to his attention.<sup>4</sup> The episodes were preceded by a hissing sound localised in the forebrain, followed by a sensation of a small rectangular door hinged at one end swinging down to about 45 degrees and exposing a round hole;<sup>5</sup> Monroe retained full consciousness and sense awareness throughout, with the vision superimposed directly over outside stimuli so that he perceived both at once.<sup>6</sup> He could not produce the effect at will - it happened, or was triggered, by some non-conscious mechanism.<sup>7</sup> Monroe said the combination of the hissing sound and the valve-opening sensation reliably signalled a coming event,<sup>8</sup> a formula he said had proved accurate on 22 occasions,<sup>9</sup> and he recorded roughly a further eighteen precognitive incidents of varying significance that came to pass as previewed, allowing for minor errors of interpretation.<sup>10</sup> He used the word "precognition" - the term current in the field - for the phenomenon,<sup>11</sup> and believed that when the mind cannot truly identify something it has perceived, it substitutes the nearest equivalent drawn from its own experience.<sup>12</sup>

Graham Nichols had an out-of-body experience in 1999 during which he became convinced that a bombing was going to happen on Old Compton Street;<sup>13</sup> the experience was also witnessed by five other people at the time.<sup>14</sup> He learned of the 1999 Admiral Duncan pub bombing through television news coverage and only gradually realised that the coverage matched what he had perceived.<sup>15</sup> A few days after the bombing, Nichols said he boarded a bus with a strong feeling about a friend he had not seen for around two years, and that same friend then boarded the bus a few stops later.<sup>16</sup> Nichols said he considers emotional bond or emotional impact between participants a key factor in the success of psychic phenomena such as precognition and [telepathy](/topics/telepathy).<sup>17</sup>

Julia Mossbridge has carried out work in the study of psi (psychic functioning) and precognition.<sup>18</sup> She treats some of her own dreams as data because they have been highly evidential for her in terms of precognition,<sup>19</sup> while holding that not every dream is precognitive - any given dream, including one she had of a childhood counsellor, could instead be a psychological metaphor for how an experience felt.<sup>20</sup> In her scheme, mental [time travel](/topics/time-travel) covers precognition together with memory: knowing something about the future that cannot be known by other means.<sup>21</sup> Informational time travel, by contrast, is information sent back to a person without the use of precognition, for example by a device.<sup>22</sup> Mossbridge has studied precognition at two scales: unconscious physiological responses on the scale of 100 milliseconds to a few seconds, and conscious, deliberate [Precognitive Remote Viewing](/topics/precognitive-remote-viewing) on the scale of minutes, days and weeks.<sup>23</sup> The physiological study of precognition in the body is called presentiment.<sup>24</sup> In her measurements, photons emitted by a light source during a fixed on-period behaved as if the system already knew how long it would remain on, with both the switch-on and the future switch-off acting as boundary conditions shaping the group's behaviour;<sup>25</sup> examining only the first minute of a five-minute emission allowed the total duration to be predicted at a relatively high accuracy rate, even though the person holding the light source did not yet know when they would switch it off.<sup>26</sup> Her startup is building technology that examines the first portion of a time period in order to predict what follows.<sup>27</sup> She has also found that precognitive effects observed in remote viewing can occur over days, weeks or years, not only seconds or minutes.<sup>28</sup> Daryl Bem conducted a series of precognition studies,<sup>29</sup> none of which involved remote viewing;<sup>30</sup> Mossbridge said a subsequent meta-analysis found that the faster, unconscious results replicated better than the slower studies with a longer gap between the information and its future presentation,<sup>31</sup> and that although the work faced numerous critical takedowns, she accepts it contained some errors.<sup>32</sup> Mossbridge describes herself as gifted at precognition, telepathy and remote viewing.<sup>33</sup>

At a public event, Mossbridge said an audience member identified only as Stan B. asked what causes a precognition of an event that then fails to occur once the warning has been acted upon.<sup>34</sup> She said the conventional explanation is that the averted event feeds information back to the earlier moment and the person then moves onto a new timeline in which the event did not happen,<sup>35</sup> but she said she dislikes this branching-timeline account, calling it ugly.<sup>36</sup> She holds instead that in an averted precognition, the informational substrate already contains the intention behind the event, the giving of the warning, the fact that the person could foil the intention, and the fact that they will foil it, so that no timeline is actually changed;<sup>37</sup> under this model, precognition works with a substrate aware of what needs to happen, rather than only with events that have reached physical manifestation in time,<sup>38</sup> and acting on a precognition to prevent an event is therefore not a change to the timeline at all.<sup>39</sup> [Chris Ramsay](/people/chris-ramsay) suggested that if humankind has never, throughout history, been able to use the mind to perceive the past or future in a repeatable, measurable way, there must be some benefit to that inability.<sup>40</sup>

Precognition also features in specific case histories elsewhere in the corpus. Discussing [Betty Hill](/people/betty-hill)'s report of having identified a recently-discovered star on her Betty Hill's star map, the hypnotist Benjamin Simon said that if the claim and the map were both valid, the only explanation would be that she had precognition.<sup>41</sup> The 2017 book Surviving Death also documents decades of apparition and haunting casework. Loyd Auerbach has investigated apparitions, haunting (place memory), and poltergeist phenomena for over thirty-five years,<sup>42</sup> including an ongoing apparition case brought to him by Donna McCormick in 1985,<sup>43</sup> in which, Auerbach said, Chris (Pat's son) - the son of a woman referred to as Pat - reported that an apparition he called Lois had appeared to him daily since he first saw her and had begun communicating with him rather than disappearing.<sup>44</sup> In a separate case, McCormick said the psychic Alex Tanous and two others, presented with several photographs including one of the grandfather, each independently identified the grandfather's photograph and described him in ways matching what he had been like in life.<sup>45</sup> According to Surviving Death, [Erlendur Haraldsson](/people/erlendur-haraldsson) found that apparition cases also contained a much higher percentage of appearances from those who had suffered a violent death, compared with the general population;<sup>46</sup> Haraldsson said the same predominance of violent death also featured in cases of the reincarnation type and in mediumship, including both direct communicators and drop-ins.<sup>47</sup> The book also traces the study of deathbed visions to Sir William Barrett, a physicist who made the first attempt at a systematic scientific study of deathbed apparitions,<sup>48</sup> and records Peter Fenwick's view that deathbed-vision cases in which the visitor is also seen by other people should be classed as apparitions rather than hallucinations.<sup>49</sup> [Hereward Carrington](/people/hereward-carrington) acknowledged that mediumship phenomena can sometimes manifest spontaneously, as with After-death communication (ADC) and apparitions.<sup>50</sup> Auerbach has argued that perceptual insertion explains why only some people perceive a given apparition, since their perceptual processes are susceptible to telepathic projection,<sup>51</sup> and why genuine photographs or video of ghosts are not obtained, since apparitions have no physical form to reflect light;<sup>52</sup> he has said that, based on his investigations, he considers the evidence weighted heavily toward the survival hypothesis.<sup>53</sup>
