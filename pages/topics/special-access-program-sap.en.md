A Special Access Program (SAP) is a compartmented security control system used by the United States Department of Defense (DoD) and intelligence community to restrict information beyond standard top-secret classification. A SAP designation is not itself a higher classification than top secret; it denotes that a project is compartmented, treated as Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI), and assigned a codename.<sup>1</sup> SAPs are the mechanism by which the Defense Department conceals most of its most sensitive black-budget classified projects,<sup>2</sup> and even senior officials holding top-secret clearance are barred from knowing a SAP's or [Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF)](/topics/sensitive-compartmented-information-facility-scif)-related details unless formally "read into" it.<sup>3</sup> A subcategory, the Unacknowledged Special Access Program (USAP), goes further still: its very existence can and must be denied by those aware of it, with its true funding concealed.<sup>4</sup> Some black-budget SAP projects are compartmented so deeply that revealing their existence to anyone outside the programme is itself a criminal offence for those read in.<sup>5</sup> Bill Sweetman wrote that an unacknowledged SAP's existence qualifies as a "core secret" under United States Air Force regulations, defined as information whose compromise would cause unrecoverable failure.<sup>6</sup> [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon)'s role on the Senate Intelligence Committee staff required him to review top-secret SCI projects inside SAPs, the majority of which remain classified.<sup>7</sup>

SAPs recur throughout allegations linking UFO and UAP material to hidden defence programmes. Ahead of an April 1997 Pentagon meeting, [Steven Greer](/people/steven-greer) sent the staff of Admiral [Thomas Wilson](/people/thomas-wilson) a leaked [National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)](/organisations/national-reconnaissance-office-nro) document listing authentic SAP codenames.<sup>8</sup> Greer said Wilson was visibly upset to find codenames on that list unknown to him, despite his seniority within Defense Intelligence.<sup>9</sup> Wilson himself recalled that his visitors at the meeting raised concerns about possible special access programmes dealing with UFOs,<sup>10</sup> and he believed their objective was to solicit his help in gaining access to, or knowledge of, such alleged programmes.<sup>11</sup> Wilson stated he never acknowledged that such programmes existed, never suggested he would attempt to gain access to them, and never had knowledge of any.<sup>12</sup> This dispute resurfaced decades later around The Admiral Wilson Memo: in a 2020-06 Tom Wilson denial letter, Wilson said many people the memo named as his contacts were unknown to him, as were the alleged SAP-related conversations.<sup>13</sup> The memo itself reportedly described Wilson as having been told of "a special projects record group not belonging to usual SAP - a special subset of the unacknowledged/carve-outs/waived programs",<sup>14</sup> and recorded [Eric Davis](/people/eric-davis) asking Wilson which SAP compartment and codename the programme was hidden under.<sup>15</sup> Wilson's June 2020 letter denied the memo's account of his supposed efforts to gain such access and any meetings with contractors or programme managers.<sup>16</sup>

Attempts to obtain formal SAP protection have also featured in the 2009 Harry Reid Special Access Program request for Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP). In June 2009, Senator [Harry Reid](/people/harry-reid) wrote to the Secretary of Defense requesting that the [Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP)](/projects/advanced-aerospace-threat-identification-program-aatip) be granted SAP status.<sup>17</sup> According to a memo on the matter, this request and Reid's supporting reasoning were addressed to Deputy Secretary of Defense William Lynn III.<sup>18</sup> Ross Coulthart has speculated that Pentagon UAP briefing slides, potentially connected to the [2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter](/events/2004-uss-nimitz-uap-encounter), may have prompted Reid's request.<sup>19</sup> The request for SAP status for the AATIP investigation was rejected.<sup>20</sup>

More recent whistleblower testimony has extended SAP-related allegations into claims of oversight evasion. [David Charles Grusch](/people/david-charles-grusch) said that multiple agencies nesting UAP activities within conventional SAP and Controlled Access Program (CAP) structures, without appropriate reporting to oversight authorities, constitutes a problem.<sup>21</sup> [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) said that two damaged M1 Abrams Tank units from Camp New York, Kuwait, were sent to the Yuma Proving Ground for analysis that was classified as a Top Secret SAP, and that he never learned the findings.<sup>22</sup> Elizondo also said that he and [Jay Stratton](/people/jay-stratton) told military witnesses they worked for a Special Access Program, encouraging witnesses to describe their encounters so the programme could better conceal them in future.<sup>23</sup> Elizondo said [Christopher K. Mellon](/people/christopher-k-mellon) oversaw all [United States Department of Defense (DoD)](/organisations/united-states-department-of-defense-dod) intelligence activities and Special Access Programs,<sup>24</sup> though Mellon himself said that despite years overseeing all SAPs, he had zero visibility into the [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) topic.<sup>25</sup>

Grusch's later testimony described a broader pattern of concealment. He said he worked on programmes deliberately hidden from [Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)](/organisations/office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence-odni) oversight staff and briefed only to the White House, kept from [United States Congress](/organisations/united-states-congress) through a shell game of non-reporting.<sup>26</sup> He said officials in the current administration described being briefed by the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia) on a classified programme, then hearing a contradictory account of the same programme when briefed elsewhere.<sup>27</sup> Grusch said he was told through a back channel that Jake Sullivan's office instructed the [Inspector General of the Intelligence Community](/organisations/inspector-general-of-the-intelligence-community), serving under [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden), to halt an investigation because it was running into White House classified special access programmes.<sup>28</sup> He considered some programmes he was cleared into to defraud the taxpayer, since Congress appropriates funds against a declared set of classified programmes while a carved-out base of technology sits outside that reporting.<sup>29</sup> Grusch said he must take care not to identify technologies the federal government does not acknowledge,<sup>30</sup> and described a programme protection strategy of relocating movable archives and materials if a holding location is believed exposed.<sup>31</sup> He also said that moving recovered craft to private corporations keeps funding outside congressional oversight by using non-appropriated dollars,<sup>32</sup> and that he named several current and former officials who ran such programmes, providing the [All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)](/organisations/all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-aaro) with several leads.<sup>33</sup>

The [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023 (FY2023 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA))](/documents/national-defense-authorization-act-for-fiscal-year-2023-fy2023-national-defense-authorization-act-ndaa) addressed this landscape directly: its UAP provisions provide protections intended to ensure that no classified military and intelligence programmes, including all Special Access Programs, are compromised or made public.<sup>34</sup>
