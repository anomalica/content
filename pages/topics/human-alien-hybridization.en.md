Human-alien hybridization is a recurring theme within Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) discourse holding that non-human intelligence has interbred with, or otherwise genetically mixed with, human beings. The idea circulates through witness testimony, second-hand accounts attributed to intelligence figures, and commentary from researchers and podcast hosts, but it has not been established as fact within the sources gathered here; claims about it are consistently reported as testimony, hearsay, or opinion rather than as settled findings.

One case frequently cited in this context is that of [Peter Khoury](/people/peter-khoury), documented in the book Hair Of The Alien. [Chris Ramsay](/people/chris-ramsay) said he considers Khoury's case the strongest evidence he has seen pointing toward hybridisation and non-human intelligence interacting with humans.<sup>1</sup> A related strand concerns [alien abduction](/topics/alien-abduction) narratives: [Jesse Mitchells](/people/jesse-mitchells) said an experiencer present at a Washington DC meeting described in Shirley MacLaine's book Going Within claimed to be part of an alien hybrid programme, and that her foetus went missing about three months into the pregnancy.<sup>2</sup> Mitchells said a foetus disappearing around the three-month mark is a commonly reported element among people engaged with the alien abduction thread.<sup>3</sup>

Jason Samosa, who said he keeps a notebook folder collecting material on the topic,<sup>4</sup> said he found that a striking number of people appear to have been warned off discussing it.<sup>5</sup> Samosa said [Tom DeLonge](/people/tom-delonge) stated publicly that there is a large genetic component to the [Unidentified Flying Object (UFO)](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) phenomenon,<sup>6</sup> and that DeLonge's advisors went silent when he raised the subject of hybrids, telling him he was not supposed to discuss it.<sup>7</sup> Samosa also said [Jacques Vallée](/people/jacques-vallee)'s journals, published as Jacques Vallee's journals (Forbidden Science), recount a conversation in which Tim Taylor told [Diana Walsh Pasulka](/people/diana-walsh-pasulka) she should not talk about human-alien hybridisation.<sup>8</sup> Samosa said John Ramirez remarked in 2022 that everyone was waiting for [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) to address the subject.<sup>9</sup> Consistent with that, [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) said that, about a week before 2026-08-11, Elizondo told him on Reality Check with Ross Coulthart that people within the intelligence community had spoken to him about human-alien hybridisation.<sup>10</sup>

Other second-hand accounts reported by Samosa link the idea to specific families and lineages. He said Ryan Bledsoe recounted that [Jim Semivan](/people/jim-semivan) told him the [Christopher Bledsoe](/people/christopher-bledsoe) family are thought to be hybrids, and that their experiences may stem from carrying more, or a particular strand of, non-human DNA.<sup>11</sup> Samosa also said that in his book Imminent (book by Luis Elizondo), Elizondo wrote that people with Cherokee blood and DNA appear to report more experiences and to be more inclined toward a certain type of work, leading them into government UFO investigation because of their genetic lineage.<sup>12</sup> Separately, Samosa said an advisor quoted in the Secret Machines books suggested that instances of genocide may be a function of the phenomenon, potentially working to eliminate certain genetic strands for its own purposes.<sup>13</sup> In a related account relayed by Coulthart, Jason Reza Jorjani holds that human-alien hybrids living among humans simply want to live peacefully and happily alongside them.<sup>14</sup>

Commentators diverge on why the subject remains largely undiscussed. [Jesse Mitchells](/people/jesse-mitchells) said officials who make offhand remarks about a hybrid breeding programme or a base sighting are typically acting from cynical or opportunistic motivation and do not actually know anything about the UFO subject.<sup>15</sup> Samosa said he regards human-alien hybridisation as a late-stage disclosure topic, possibly too dangerous to discuss at all, because an ability to breed with [nonhuman intelligence (Nonhuman Intelligence (NHI))](/topics/nonhuman-intelligence-nonhuman-intelligence-nhi) would imply humans are related to it and could destabilise religion.<sup>16</sup> He further argued that revealing differing levels of non-human DNA among people would create a spectrum by which a person could be judged more or less human, and that current geopolitical conflicts make releasing such information unwise now.<sup>17</sup> Mitchells said he agrees that disclosing information about differing non-human DNA levels would not be advisable at present.<sup>18</sup> Samosa characterised the underlying data as anecdotal, originating from the group of people who have been releasing such information,<sup>19</sup> and Mitchells said that material comes specifically from people who were very senior within the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia), such as Semivan.<sup>20</sup> Mitchells suggested a sufficiently senior intelligence figure could disclose hybrid information knowing that most of the public would initially dismiss it, allowing the idea to circulate and gradually become acceptable to discuss within five to ten years, at which point academics might take it up.<sup>21</sup> Framed this way, the topic is sometimes discussed alongside the concept of the Overton window as a mechanism for gradual public acceptance of otherwise fringe claims.
