Majestic 12 (MJ-12) is the name given to an alleged secret group, said in a memo mailed to researcher Jaime Shandera to comprise twelve members tasked with dealing with "the alien problem", according to the book *Dark Mission*.<sup>1</sup> According to Jonathan Caplan, a directive dated 24 September 1947 — the Truman Directive of 1947-09-24 — directed [Vannevar Bush](/people/vannevar-bush) and James Forrestal to set up the group and report directly to President [Harry S. Truman](/people/harry-s-truman); within a week, Forrestal met Henry Tizard, then chief scientific advisor to the UK Ministry of Defence, at the start of October 1947 during the 1947-10 Forrestal-Tizard meetings.<sup>2</sup> The directive was leaked in around 1980 to two filmmakers, and the resulting material became known as the Majestic 12 documents.<sup>3</sup> The documents allegedly established Majestic 12 as the group responsible for the retrieval of non-human craft, a subject researchers term UFO crash retrieval,<sup>4</sup> though their provenance is officially disputed by the United States [Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)](/organisations/federal-bureau-of-investigation-fbi) and the US military.<sup>5</sup>

[Vannevar Bush](/people/vannevar-bush) and [Donald Menzel](/people/donald-menzel) are named as founding members of Majestic 12 in the Shandera memo, according to *Dark Mission*.<sup>6</sup> Menzel's inclusion was initially used by debunkers as evidence that the documents were forgeries, since he had been a frequent public UFO debunker who had written three books on the subject.<sup>7</sup> Research into Menzel's personal papers by nuclear physicist and UFO researcher [Stanton Friedman](/people/stanton-friedman), conducted for a book on the Majestic 12 documents, found that Menzel had led an elaborate double life,<sup>8</sup> with numerous references turning up to his participation in a variety of intelligence projects and committees, including top-secret weapons programmes, that would have made him a plausible candidate for Majestic 12.<sup>9</sup> Friedman has argued for the validity of the Majestic 12 documents and the existence of Majestic 12 itself.<sup>10</sup> According to *Dark Mission*, document researchers have also identified Theodore von Karman as a possible member based on his name appearing on other newly unearthed documents.<sup>11</sup>

Caplan has examined the typography, printing, format, watermarks and terminology of the documents, alongside corroboration from other diaries, to assess their provenance.<sup>12</sup> He has said the documents corroborate contemporary diaries not released at the time, including those of Truman, Forrestal and [Dwight D. Eisenhower](/people/dwight-d-eisenhower),<sup>13</sup> and that he examined the diaries of the head of the US Atomic Energy Commission and found they corroborate the documents.<sup>14</sup> Caplan has said he discounts the theory that the documents are the product of a counterintelligence operation, believing most of them genuine.<sup>15</sup> [Ross Coulthart](/people/ross-coulthart) has noted that debunkers claim James Jesus Angleton, head of counterintelligence at the [Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](/organisations/central-intelligence-agency-cia), fabricated the documents to disinform the Soviets into believing the Americans had recovered alien technology,<sup>16</sup> but he does not accept this theory, given the volume of material and the detailed knowledge of names, details and events it would have required.<sup>17</sup> Coulthart has pointed to a detail in one document noting that the president was at the Blair House meeting of 1949-07-14 because the White House was being rebuilt at the time, which he considers an important corroborating detail.<sup>18</sup> Caplan considers it extremely unlikely that Forrestal did not tell Tizard about Majestic 12, which he says had been set up because of a recovered craft and associated biologics,<sup>19</sup> and Coulthart has separately said he considers it extremely unlikely that Forrestal and Bush did not brief Tizard on Majestic 12 and the UFO issue at that October 1947 meeting.<sup>20</sup> Caplan believes Forrestal impressed on Tizard that Majestic 12 had to be kept secret, and that Tizard's team should limit the documents they produced, possibly for personal use only.<sup>21</sup> Caplan concludes that Majestic 12 existed and that the documents are genuine,<sup>22</sup> and Coulthart has said he considers the files authentic beyond doubt,<sup>23</sup> though he is not fully convinced that some subsequent documents were not deliberately planted disinformation.<sup>24</sup>

Caplan considers it likely that the agreement Truman referenced at the Blair House meeting relates to Majestic 12.<sup>25</sup> Many of the classic Majestic 12 documents came via Timothy Cooper, a United States Marine Corps veteran,<sup>26</sup> and Caplan has obtained key new documents that had been held by Ryan Wood, a keeper of the Majestic 12 archive.<sup>27</sup> Caplan has said he met Wood in Washington roughly two years before the interview and asked him whether Cooper had handed over all the Majestic 12 documents to Wood's father.<sup>28</sup> One of the two newly found documents typescript documents distributed among the Majestic 12 material that do not purport to be original 1940s or 1950s documents, and which he believes were forged,<sup>33</sup> including one headed
