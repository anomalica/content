Project GRUDGE was a United States Air Force investigation into unidentified flying objects, formed when [Project Sign](/projects/project-sign) was renamed<sup>1</sup>. The USAF studied unidentified aerial phenomena in 1948 and 1949 under both Project Sign and Project Grudge<sup>2</sup>, with the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf) as the operating body<sup>2</sup>. In 1952, Project Grudge was itself renamed [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book)<sup>3</sup>.

According to "In Plain Sight", the air force's first Project Grudge report announced that it would stop investigating unidentified flying objects<sup>4</sup>, and the same report attributed such sightings to mass hysteria, hoaxes or the misinterpretation of conventional objects<sup>5</sup>. Project Grudge is described as having debunked the phenomenon as the result of natural causes<sup>6</sup>.

Edward Ruppelt later said that there was a widespread view within the programme that Project Grudge operated under a directive to debunk all sightings<sup>7</sup>. [Edward Ruppelt](/people/edward-ruppelt) went on to lead its successor, Project Blue Book.

Chris Ramsay states that the [Roswell Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) crash](/events/roswell-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-crash) and the sighting by [Kenneth Arnold](/people/kenneth-arnold) - the [Kenneth Arnold Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) sighting](/events/kenneth-arnold-unidentified-flying-object-ufo-sighting) - followed by the Aztec crash, prompted government and military interest in UFOs that led to the formation of Project Grudge, which later became Project Blue Book<sup>8</sup>.

[Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) has said he conducted a deep dive into government historical records covering Project Blue Book and its predecessors, Project Sign and Project Grudge<sup>9</sup>.
