Project Sign was the [United States Air Force (USAF)](/organisations/united-states-air-force-usaf)'s first formal investigation into [UFO](/topics/unidentified-flying-object-ufo) reports. The USAF Chief of Staff established the project in 1947-12 to investigate the first recorded observations of such objects.<sup>1</sup> [Nathan Twining](/people/nathan-twining) had recommended a detailed study of the phenomena, which became known as Project Sign.<sup>2</sup> The project was originally named Project SAUCER before being formalised into an official, high-priority programme named Project Sign,<sup>3</sup> with the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) assuming control on 1948-01-23.<sup>4</sup> According to an employee of Project Sign interviewed by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the project in fact began a year earlier, in 1946, under the same informal Project SAUCER name.<sup>5</sup>

The impetus for the investigation was to determine whether the reported objects might be Soviet secret weapons or of "extra-planetary" origin.<sup>6</sup> As part of this work, Project Sign staff reviewed the military's intelligence on German weaponry and aeronautical capabilities to determine whether any had fallen into Soviet possession.<sup>7</sup> Between 1947 and 1949, USAF personnel investigated 243 reported [Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap) sightings.<sup>8</sup> [J. Allen Hynek](/people/j-allen-hynek) served as an advisor to the project;<sup>9</sup> he later said that in his first year on Project Sign the whole issue "seems utterly ridiculous".<sup>10</sup> [Edward Ruppelt](/people/edward-ruppelt), a USAF officer, was privy to the investigations.<sup>11</sup>

According to the Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena, Volume I, Project Sign concluded in 1949-02 that no definite and conclusive evidence was yet available to prove or disprove the existence of the unidentified objects as real aircraft of unknown and unconventional configuration.<sup>12</sup> The same report states that nearly all of the sightings it reviewed were attributed to misinterpretation of known objects, hysteria, hallucination, or hoax,<sup>13</sup> and that the project recommended continued military intelligence control over the investigation of all UFO sightings.<sup>14</sup> Project Sign's overall findings were inconclusive but left open the possibility of extraterrestrial origins for the craft.<sup>15</sup>

According to an unsubstantiated historical account derived from a single source, Project Sign staff in 1948-07 drafted, signed, and sent up the military chain for approval a report titled Estimate of the Situation (1948), which allegedly concluded that UFOs were of "interplanetary" origin.<sup>16</sup> The same account holds that staff who leaned towards this interplanetary explanation were subsequently purged from the organisation,<sup>17</sup> and that the report's rejection may have contributed to Pentagon leadership's loss of confidence in the Project Sign staff, leading to the establishment of Project GRUDGE (Original Organization).<sup>18</sup> Project Sign was renamed Project GRUDGE in approximately 1949-02,<sup>19</sup> bringing to a close an air force-controlled investigation that had run for one year.<sup>20</sup>

The USAF's study of UAP continued through 1948 and 1949 under Project Sign and its successor, Project GRUDGE.<sup>21</sup> Edward Ruppelt later sought to correct the mistakes of Project Sign and the original Project GRUDGE.<sup>22</sup> The reestablished Project GRUDGE (Reestablishment) reviewed all of the earlier cases from Project Sign, the original Project GRUDGE, and the ATIC interim period.<sup>23</sup> A. Ray Gordon subsequently led the CIA Special Study Group 1952, which reviewed all of ATIC's data from Projects Sign through Grudge.<sup>24</sup> Decades later, [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) conducted a review of government historical records including [Project Blue Book](/projects/project-blue-book) and its predecessors, Project Sign and Project GRUDGE.<sup>25</sup>
